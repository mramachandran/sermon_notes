# Abel

*The shepherd whose offering pleased God, the first to die, and one who "being dead yet speaketh."*

Abel was the second son of Adam and Eve and the first shepherd in Scripture. He brought God the firstborn of his flock and their fat portions, and God accepted him and his gift. His brother Cain, angry and jealous, killed him in the field. Abel never speaks a single recorded word in Genesis. Yet the New Testament calls him righteous, lists him first among the heroes of faith, and says that through his faith he still speaks. His short life raises big questions about worship, faith and justice, and it points ahead to another innocent death whose blood "speaketh better things."

## The life of Abel

*Genesis gives no dates or ages for Abel. The order below follows the text.*

**After Eden — Born to Eve.** Eve bears Abel after his brother Cain. *Genesis 4:2*

**Grown — Keeps sheep.** "Abel was a keeper of sheep, but Cain was a tiller of the ground." *Genesis 4:2*

**Later — Brings an offering.** Abel brings the firstlings of his flock and their fat. "The LORD had respect unto Abel and to his offering." *Genesis 4:4*

**In the field — Killed by Cain.** Cain talks with Abel, then rises up against him in the field and kills him. *Genesis 4:8*

**Afterward — His blood cries out.** God tells Cain that the voice of his brother's blood cries to him from the ground. *Genesis 4:10*

**Later — Remembered by Eve.** Eve names Seth as "another seed instead of Abel, whom Cain slew." *Genesis 4:25*

### The world around them

Abel belonged to the first generation born after Eden. Shepherding was one of the oldest ways of life in the ancient Near East, and it runs through the whole Bible. Abraham, Moses and David all kept sheep, and God calls himself the shepherd of his people (Psalm 23:1). The "firstlings" were the first animals born in a season, and "the fat" was seen as the richest part. The law of Moses later gave both to God (Exodus 13:12; Leviticus 3:16), but Abel offered them long before any written law.

## Key activities

*Genesis does not explain why Abel's offering was accepted. Hebrews 11:4 says it was offered by faith. Some also note that he brought the firstborn and the best parts.*

1. **Kept sheep.** He was the first shepherd named in the Bible. *Genesis 4:2*
2. **Brought the firstborn and the best.** He offered the firstlings of his flock and the fat of them. *Genesis 4:4*
3. **Offered in faith.** His gift was "a more excellent sacrifice," and God counted him righteous. *Hebrews 11:4*
4. **Was accepted by God.** The LORD had respect to Abel himself and to his offering. *Genesis 4:4*
5. **Died an innocent death.** He was killed by his own brother, the first death recorded in Scripture. *Genesis 4:8*
6. **Still speaks.** His blood cried out to God, and his faith still speaks to readers today. *Genesis 4:10; Hebrews 11:4*

### Words and places

- **Firstlings.** The firstborn animals of a flock (Genesis 4:4).
- **Had respect unto.** KJV wording for "looked on with favor" (Genesis 4:4).
- **Righteous.** In right standing with God. Jesus and John both call Abel righteous (Matthew 23:35; 1 John 3:12).
- **Blood crying out.** A picture of innocent blood calling for justice (Genesis 4:10; Hebrews 12:24).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Abel.*

### Old Testament

- **Genesis 4:2–10.** The whole account of his life and death
- **Genesis 4:25.** Eve names Seth in Abel's place
- **Echoes.** Firstborn and fat belong to God (Exodus 13:12; Leviticus 3:16)

### New Testament

- **Matthew 23:35; Luke 11:50–51.** "From the blood of Abel unto the blood of Zacharias"
- **Hebrews 11:4.** Abel heads the list of the faithful
- **Hebrews 12:24.** Jesus' blood "speaketh better things than that of Abel"
- **1 John 3:12.** Cain's works evil, "and his brother's righteous"
- **Echo: John 10:11.** The Good Shepherd lays down his life

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What did Abel bring to God, and how did God respond (Genesis 4:4)?
2. **Understand:** Hebrews says Abel "being dead yet speaketh." What does his life still say?
3. **Apply:** Abel gave God the first and best. What would giving God your first and best look like this week?
4. **Apply:** Abel was righteous and still suffered. How does his story help you when doing right does not seem to pay off?
