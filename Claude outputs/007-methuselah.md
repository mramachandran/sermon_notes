# Methuselah

*The oldest man in the Bible, son of Enoch and grandfather of Noah.*

Methuselah is famous for one thing: he lived longer than anyone else recorded in Scripture, 969 years. Genesis tells us almost nothing else about him. He was the son of Enoch, who walked with God, and the father of Lamech, who named his son Noah in hope of comfort. So Methuselah stands between two great men of faith. By the numbers in the Hebrew text of Genesis 5, he died in the same year as the flood. Genesis does not say how he died or comment on the timing. His long life is a reminder that even the longest life ends, and that what matters is how our days are spent.

## The life of Methuselah

*Ages come from Genesis 5 in the Hebrew text. The Greek Septuagint gives different numbers.*

**Enoch's 65th year — Born.** Enoch fathers Methuselah and walks with God for 300 years afterward. *Genesis 5:21–22*

**Age 187 — Father of Lamech.** Methuselah fathers Lamech. *Genesis 5:25*

**Age 369 — Grandfather of Noah.** Lamech fathers Noah at 182, naming him in hope of comfort from the cursed ground. *Genesis 5:28–29 (age by calculation)*

**The next 782 years — Raises a family.** Methuselah fathers sons and daughters. *Genesis 5:26*

**Age 969 — Dies.** "All the days of Methuselah were nine hundred sixty and nine years: and he died." *Genesis 5:27*

### The world around them

Methuselah's life spans most of the time between Enoch and the flood. By the Hebrew numbers in Genesis 5, he was born while Adam was still alive and died in the year of the flood. After the flood, recorded lifespans drop sharply. Noah's son Shem lives 600 years, Abraham 175, and Moses 120 (Genesis 11:10–11; 25:7; Deuteronomy 34:7). Christians explain the long early ages in different ways. But Genesis clearly shows a world that was changing after sin and judgment.

## Key activities

*A popular teaching says Methuselah's name means "when he dies, it shall be sent," pointing to the flood. This is not certain and is not stated in Scripture. The link between his death and the flood year comes only from adding up the ages in Genesis 5 (187 + 182 + 600 = 969).*

1. **Was raised by a man who walked with God.** Genesis says his father Enoch walked with God for 300 years after Methuselah was born. *Genesis 5:21–22*
2. **Fathered Lamech.** Through Lamech, the line continued to Noah. *Genesis 5:25, 28–29*
3. **Raised a large family.** He fathered other sons and daughters. *Genesis 5:26*
4. **Lived 969 years.** No one in Scripture is recorded as living longer. *Genesis 5:27*

### Words and places

- **Lamech.** Methuselah's son and Noah's father. He is not the Lamech of Cain's line (Genesis 4:18–24; 5:25–31).
- **Begat.** KJV wording for "fathered" (Genesis 5:25).
- **Generations.** A family line traced over time (Genesis 5:1).
- **Mathusala.** The Greek form of his name, used in Luke's genealogy (Luke 3:37).

### Read it yourself

- **Day 1.** Genesis 5:21–27. What does Genesis say about Methuselah, and what does it leave out?
- **Day 2.** Genesis 5:28–6:8. What was the world like as Methuselah's life ended?
- **Day 3.** Psalm 90. What does Moses ask God to teach us about our days?

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Methuselah.*

### Old Testament

- **Genesis 5:21–27.** This is the whole account.
- **1 Chronicles 1:3.** He is listed in the line "Henoch, Methuselah, Lamech."
- **Echoes.** Human lifespans shorten after the flood (Genesis 11:10–32). Moses writes, "So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom" (Psalm 90:12).

### New Testament

- **Luke 3:37.** He appears as "Mathusala" in the genealogy of Jesus.
- **Echoes.** "One day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day" (2 Peter 3:8).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What two things does Genesis tell us about Methuselah's life besides his age (Genesis 5:25–26)?
2. **Understand:** Methuselah lived between Enoch and Noah. What might he have learned from his father, and passed on to his grandson?
3. **Apply:** Psalm 90:12 asks God to teach us to number our days. How would you live differently if you counted your days?
4. **Apply:** What legacy of faith do you hope to leave, whether your life is long or short?
