# Nimrod

*"A mighty hunter before the LORD," the first empire-builder in Scripture.*

Nimrod appears in the Table of Nations, the list of Noah's descendants in Genesis 10. He was a great-grandson of Noah through Ham and Cush. Genesis calls him the first "mighty one in the earth" and "a mighty hunter before the LORD," a phrase that became a proverb. He founded a kingdom in Shinar, the land of Babylon, including the city of Babel. Scripture gives him only a few verses and never describes his character directly. Later tradition made him the builder of the tower of Babel, but Genesis does not say so. His story marks a new stage after the flood: the rise of cities, kingdoms and human power.

## The life of Nimrod

*Genesis gives no dates or ages for Nimrod. The order below follows the text. Scholars have tried to match him with various ancient kings, but no match is certain.*

**After the flood — Born to Cush.** Cush, son of Ham, fathers Nimrod. *Genesis 10:6–8*

**Grown — Becomes a mighty one.** "He began to be a mighty one in the earth." *Genesis 10:8*

**Known far and wide — A mighty hunter.** His skill as a hunter becomes a saying: "Even as Nimrod the mighty hunter before the LORD." *Genesis 10:9*

**In Shinar — Founds a kingdom.** His kingdom begins with Babel, Erech, Accad and Calneh in Shinar. *Genesis 10:10*

**Later — Expands north.** Nineveh, Rehoboth, Calah and Resen are built. It is unclear whether Nimrod built them or Asshur did (see next page). *Genesis 10:11–12*

### The world around them

Shinar is the Bible's name for southern Mesopotamia, the land between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers in modern Iraq. It was home to some of the world's first cities. Erech is usually identified with Uruk, and Accad with Akkad, the capital of an early empire. Nineveh, Calah and the other cities of Genesis 10:11–12 lay farther north, in Assyria. Hunting was a sign of royal power in the ancient Near East, and later Assyrian kings boasted of their hunts in carvings and inscriptions.

## Key activities

*Genesis 10:11 can be read two ways. The KJV says "Out of that land went forth Asshur," but its margin and many modern readers take it as "he went out into Assyria," meaning Nimrod. The phrase "before the LORD" is also debated: some read it as "in God's sight," others as "in defiance of God."*

1. **Rose to power.** He was the first person Genesis calls "a mighty one in the earth." *Genesis 10:8*
2. **Hunted.** He became famous as a mighty hunter, so famous that people used his name as a saying. *Genesis 10:9*
3. **Ruled a kingdom.** He founded the first kingdom named in Scripture, in the land of Shinar. *Genesis 10:10*
4. **Built cities.** His kingdom included Babel, and possibly Nineveh and the Assyrian cities too. *Genesis 10:10–12*

*Tradition.* Jewish and Christian writers, including the historian Josephus, later described Nimrod as the proud leader behind the tower of Babel. Genesis 11:1–9 does not name him.

### Words and places

- **Shinar.** Southern Mesopotamia, later called Babylonia (Genesis 10:10; 11:2).
- **Babel.** The city later known as Babylon (Genesis 10:10; 11:9).
- **Asshur.** A son of Shem, and also the name of Assyria. In Genesis 10:11 it may name either a man or a land (Genesis 10:11, 22).
- **Table of Nations.** The list of Noah's descendants in Genesis 10.

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Nimrod.*

### Old Testament

- **Genesis 10:8–12.** This is the main account.
- **1 Chronicles 1:10.** "And Cush begat Nimrod: he began to be mighty upon the earth."
- **Micah 5:6.** The prophet speaks of "the land of Nimrod," a name for Assyria.
- **Echoes.** Babel in Shinar is where people build a tower to "make us a name" (Genesis 11:1–9). Babylon later becomes the great enemy of God's people (2 Kings 25; Daniel 1–5). Nineveh is the city Jonah is sent to warn (Jonah 1:2).

### New Testament

- Nimrod is not named in the New Testament.
- **Echoes.** "Babylon the great" is a picture of proud human power set against God (Revelation 17–18). God "hath put down the mighty from their seats, and exalted them of low degree" (Luke 1:52).
