# Melchizedek

*The mysterious king of Salem and priest of the most high God, who blessed Abraham and pointed ahead to Christ.*

Melchizedek appears in only three verses of Genesis, and then he is gone. After Abram rescued Lot, this king of Salem, "the priest of the most high God," came out with bread and wine. He blessed Abram, praised God for the victory, and received a tenth of everything. Genesis gives him no parents, birth or death. Centuries later a psalm of David promises "a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek," and Hebrews builds a whole chapter on him to explain how Jesus is our great High Priest. Few people say so little and are remembered so long.

## The life of Melchizedek

*Genesis records one meeting and gives no dates or ages. It took place after Abram entered Canaan at 75 and before Ishmael was born when Abram was 86 (Genesis 12:4; 16:16).*

**After the battle — Comes out to meet Abram.** Abram returns from defeating Chedorlaomer and the kings with him. At the valley of Shaveh, the king's dale, the king of Sodom meets him, and Melchizedek brings out bread and wine. *Genesis 14:17–18*

**The same day — Blesses Abram.** He says, "Blessed be Abram of the most high God, possessor of heaven and earth." Then he blesses God, who delivered Abram's enemies into his hand. *Genesis 14:19–20*

**The same day — Receives a tenth.** Abram gives him "tithes of all." Then Abram refuses the king of Sodom's offer, swearing by "the LORD, the most high God." *Genesis 14:20–24*

**In a psalm of David — A priest for ever.** God swears to David's Lord, "Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek." *Psalm 110:1–4*

**In Hebrews — A pattern of Christ.** Hebrews quotes Psalm 110 and explains that Jesus is a high priest of Melchizedek's kind, not of Aaron's. *Hebrews 5:6–10; 6:20–7:28*

### The world around them

In Abram's day Canaan was a patchwork of small city-states, each ruled by its own king. Genesis 14 names several of them. In the ancient world, a king often also served as priest. Salem is usually identified with Jerusalem, since Psalm 76:2 sets Salem beside Zion. Centuries later Jerusalem was still ruled by a king whose name was much like Melchizedek's, Adonizedek (Joshua 10:1). "The most high God" translates the Hebrew *El Elyon*. Abram used the same title for the LORD (Genesis 14:22).

## Key activities

*Christians hold different views of who Melchizedek was. Many see him as a real Canaanite king-priest whose missing family record Hebrews uses as a picture of Christ. Some believe he was an appearance of the Son of God before the incarnation. Hebrews says he was "made like unto the Son of God" (Hebrews 7:3).*

1. **Brought bread and wine.** He met the weary victors with food and drink. *Genesis 14:18*
2. **Served the most high God.** He was a priest of the true God outside Abram's family, before Israel or the law existed. *Genesis 14:18*
3. **Blessed Abram.** He spoke a blessing over the man who carried God's promises. Hebrews notes that "the less is blessed of the better." *Genesis 14:19; Hebrews 7:6–7*
4. **Gave God the credit.** He praised God, not Abram, for delivering Abram's enemies. *Genesis 14:20*
5. **Received the tithe.** Abram gave him a tenth of all, and Hebrews says Levi in a sense paid tithes through Abraham. *Genesis 14:20; Hebrews 7:4–10*
6. **Became a pattern.** His priesthood, not based on family line, became the model for a priest who lives for ever. *Psalm 110:4; Hebrews 7:15–17*

*Tradition.* Some Jewish writers identified Melchizedek with Noah's son Shem. Genesis does not say this.

### Words and places

- **Salem.** A city generally identified with Jerusalem (Genesis 14:18; Psalm 76:2).
- **Tithe.** A tenth part given to God or his servant (Genesis 14:20; 28:22).
- **The king's dale.** A valley, probably near Salem, where Abram was met; Absalom later set up a pillar there (Genesis 14:17; 2 Samuel 18:18).
- **Order of Melchizedek.** A priesthood by God's appointment rather than by descent from Levi or Aaron (Psalm 110:4; Hebrews 7:11).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Melchizedek.*

### Old Testament

- **Genesis 14:17–24.** The only account of his life
- **Psalm 110:4.** "Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek"

### New Testament

- **Hebrews 5:5–10.** Christ "called of God an high priest after the order of Melchisedec"
- **Hebrews 6:19–20.** Jesus our forerunner, within the veil, a priest for ever
- **Hebrews 7:1–10.** His blessing and tithe show him greater than Abraham
- **Hebrews 7:11–28.** A priest "after the power of an endless life"
- **Echo: Matthew 22:41–45.** Jesus applies Psalm 110 to himself
- **Echo: Matthew 26:26–29.** Bread and wine, though not linked to Melchizedek
