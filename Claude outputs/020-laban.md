# Laban

*Rebekah's brother and Jacob's father-in-law, a shrewd host who met his match.*

Laban lived in Haran, in Padanaram, far to the north of Canaan. He was Rebekah's brother, Jacob's uncle, and the father of Leah and Rachel. He first appears welcoming Abraham's servant, who had come for a wife for Isaac. Years later he welcomed Jacob, who had fled from Esau. Laban gave Jacob work, two wives and a home, but tricked him on his wedding night and changed his wages again and again. In the end God warned Laban not to harm Jacob, and they parted with a covenant of peace. God kept his promise to Jacob even in a hard household.

## The life of Laban

*Genesis gives no dates or ages for Laban. The order below follows the text. Jacob's stay with him lasted twenty years (Genesis 31:38, 41).*

**In Haran — Welcomes Abraham's servant.** Seeing Rebekah's gold earring and bracelets, Laban runs out: "Come in, thou blessed of the LORD." *Genesis 24:28–33*

**In Haran — Lets Rebekah go.** Laban and Bethuel agree, "The thing proceedeth from the LORD," and Rebekah goes to marry Isaac. *Genesis 24:50–61*

**Many years later — Welcomes Jacob.** Laban runs to meet his fleeing nephew: "Surely thou art my bone and my flesh." *Genesis 29:13–14*

**After seven years — Swaps the brides.** Jacob serves seven years for Rachel. At the wedding Laban brings Leah instead, then gives Rachel a week later in exchange for seven more years of work. *Genesis 29:18–30*

**Jacob's twenty years — Changes the wages.** Laban admits the LORD has blessed him for Jacob's sake. He agrees to Jacob's wages, then removes the animals that would have been Jacob's share. Jacob says his wages were changed "ten times." *Genesis 30:25–36; 31:7, 41*

**On the third day — Pursues Jacob.** Jacob slips away with his family and flocks while Laban is shearing sheep. Laban chases him for seven days, but God warns him in a dream not to harm Jacob. *Genesis 31:19–35*

**Mount Gilead — Makes a covenant.** The two men set up a pillar and a heap of stones as a witness and a boundary. Early next morning Laban kisses his grandchildren and daughters, blesses them, and goes home. *Genesis 31:43–55*

### The world around them

Haran was a trading city on the Balikh River, in what is now southern Turkey. Abraham's family had lived there after leaving Ur (Genesis 11:31), and his brother Nahor's family stayed in the region. In that world a family's wealth was counted mainly in flocks and herds. Shepherds were often paid with a share of the newborn animals. Marriages were arranged between families, and a bride price or years of work could be given for a wife. Household images called teraphim were common and may have been linked to family blessing or inheritance, though scholars are not sure.

## Key activities

*In Genesis 30:27 the KJV says Laban had "learned by experience" that God blessed him through Jacob. The Hebrew word often means "to practise divination," so some translate it "I have divined." Genesis 31:53 also hints that Laban honored other gods alongside the God of Abraham.*

1. **Welcomed a stranger.** He ran to meet Abraham's servant and gave him lodging and food for his camels. *Genesis 24:29–32*
2. **Agreed to Rebekah's marriage.** He saw the LORD's hand in the servant's story and let his sister go to Isaac. *Genesis 24:50–51*
3. **Took Jacob into his home.** He received his nephew and asked him to name his wages. *Genesis 29:13–15*
4. **Deceived Jacob.** He gave Leah in place of Rachel and defended it: "It must not be so done in our country, to give the younger before the firstborn." *Genesis 29:22–26*
5. **Grew rich through Jacob.** His small flock increased greatly while Jacob cared for it. *Genesis 30:27–30*
6. **Tried to keep what was Jacob's.** He removed the spotted and speckled animals and set three days' journey between his flocks and Jacob's. *Genesis 30:31–36*
7. **Heard God's warning.** God told him in a dream, "Take heed that thou speak not to Jacob either good or bad." *Genesis 31:24, 29*
8. **Searched for his household gods.** He hunted through the tents for his missing images but did not find them, because Rachel had hidden them. *Genesis 31:30–35*
9. **Made peace.** He and Jacob swore a covenant, ate together, and agreed not to cross the boundary to harm each other. *Genesis 31:44–54*

### Words and places

- **Padanaram.** "The plain of Aram," the region of northern Mesopotamia around Haran (Genesis 28:2).
- **Teraphim or images.** Small household idols. Their exact purpose is uncertain (Genesis 31:19).
- **Galeed and Jegarsahadutha.** "Heap of witness" in Hebrew and in Aramaic. Jacob and Laban each named the heap in his own language (Genesis 31:47).
- **Mizpah.** "Watchtower," the name Laban gave the place of the covenant (Genesis 31:49).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Laban.*

### Old Testament

- **Genesis 24:29–60.** Laban welcomes the servant and sends Rebekah
- **Genesis 27:43.** Rebekah sends Jacob to "Laban my brother to Haran"
- **Genesis 29:1–31:55.** Jacob's twenty years with Laban
- **Genesis 32:4.** "I have sojourned with Laban"
- **Hosea 12:12.** "For a wife he kept sheep"
- **Echo: Genesis 27:18–29.** The deceiver deceived in turn

### New Testament

- Laban is not named in the New Testament.
- **Echo: Galatians 6:7.** "Whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap"
- **Echo: Matthew 6:24.** No one can serve God and mammon

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What did Laban say when Jacob accused him of deceit (Genesis 29:25–27)?
2. **Understand:** Laban was blessed because of Jacob, yet kept cheating him. Why didn't gratitude change him?
3. **Apply:** God saw Jacob's mistreatment (Genesis 31:42). Where do you need to trust God with something unfair?
4. **Apply:** Is there a relationship where a clear, fair boundary would help keep the peace?
