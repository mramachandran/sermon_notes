# Benjamin

*Jacob's youngest son, born in sorrow, whose safety became the test of his brothers' hearts.*

Benjamin was the youngest son of Jacob, and the second son of Rachel, who died giving birth to him on the road to Bethlehem. After Joseph disappeared, Jacob guarded him closely. Genesis never records a word Benjamin spoke, yet he stands at the story's turning point. Joseph's test came down to one question: would the brothers abandon Rachel's other son as they had abandoned Joseph? Their answer showed they had changed. His tribe later gave Israel King Saul, and much later the apostle Paul.

## The life of Benjamin

*Genesis gives no dates or ages for Benjamin. The order below follows the text. He was born after Jacob returned to Canaan, the only son of Jacob born there.*

**On the road to Ephrath — Born as Rachel dies.** Rachel has a hard labor. As she dies, she names her son Benoni, "son of my sorrow," but Jacob names him Benjamin. *Genesis 35:16–20*

**The first famine journey — Kept at home.** Jacob sends ten sons to buy grain in Egypt but keeps Benjamin back, "Lest peradventure mischief befall him." *Genesis 42:1–4*

**Back in Canaan — Demanded by Egypt.** The governor of Egypt holds Simeon and insists that Benjamin come. Jacob cries, "Joseph is not, and Simeon is not, and ye will take Benjamin away." *Genesis 42:24, 34–38*

**The second journey — Goes down to Egypt.** As the famine grows worse, Judah pledges himself for Benjamin's safety. Jacob lets him go: "If I be bereaved of my children, I am bereaved." *Genesis 43:1–15*

**In Joseph's house — Honored at the feast.** Joseph sees Benjamin and says, "God be gracious unto thee, my son." Then he leaves the room to weep. Benjamin's portion is five times larger than the others'. *Genesis 43:16–34*

**On the road home — The cup is found.** Joseph's silver cup is hidden in Benjamin's sack. When it is found, the brothers tear their clothes and all return to face the governor together. *Genesis 44:1–13*

**In Joseph's house — Reunited.** Joseph reveals himself, embraces Benjamin, and weeps. He gives Benjamin three hundred pieces of silver and five changes of clothing. *Genesis 45:1–15, 22*

### The world around them

Grain was the staple food of the ancient Near East, and a failed harvest could mean starvation. Egypt's farming depended on the yearly flooding of the Nile, not on local rain, so it often had grain when Canaan did not. Families from Canaan went down to Egypt to buy food, as Abraham had done in an earlier famine (Genesis 12:10). Meals in Egypt followed strict customs. Genesis notes that Egyptians would not eat with Hebrews (Genesis 43:32), so Joseph, his brothers and his Egyptian guests ate at separate tables.

## Key activities

*Genesis 46:21 lists ten sons of Benjamin when Jacob's family goes to Egypt, though Genesis calls him "the lad." Some think he was a grown man by then; others think the list includes descendants born later in Egypt. The lists in Numbers 26:38–41 and 1 Chronicles 8:1–5 differ somewhat.*

1. **Was born at great cost.** His birth took his mother's life. *Genesis 35:16–19*
2. **Was his father's comfort.** After Joseph was lost, Jacob held him close, saying "he is left alone." *Genesis 42:4, 38; 44:20*
3. **Went to Egypt.** He traveled with his brothers on the second journey, under Judah's pledge. *Genesis 43:8–15*
4. **Received special honor.** At Joseph's table he was given five times as much as anyone else. *Genesis 43:34*
5. **Was accused of theft.** The steward found Joseph's silver cup in his sack. *Genesis 44:12*
6. **Was defended by his brothers.** Judah offered to become a slave in his place. *Genesis 44:18–34*
7. **Wept with Joseph.** The two sons of Rachel embraced after more than twenty years apart. *Genesis 45:14–15*
8. **Received Jacob's blessing.** "Benjamin shall ravin as a wolf: in the morning he shall devour the prey, and at night he shall divide the spoil." *Genesis 49:27*

### Words and places

- **Benoni.** "Son of my sorrow," Rachel's name for him (Genesis 35:18).
- **Benjamin.** Usually explained as "son of the right hand," a place of honor. Some read it "son of the south" (Genesis 35:18).
- **Mess.** An old word for a serving of food (Genesis 43:34).
- **Ravin.** To hunt and tear prey, like a wolf (Genesis 49:27).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Benjamin.*

### Old Testament

- **Genesis 35:16–45:22.** The main account
- **Deuteronomy 33:12.** "The beloved of the LORD shall dwell in safety by him"
- **Judges 3:15; 20:16.** Ehud, and the tribe's left-handed slingers
- **Judges 19–21.** Nearly wiped out after the crime at Gibeah
- **1 Samuel 9:21.** Saul: "Am not I a Benjamite … ?"
- **Psalm 68:27.** "There is little Benjamin with their ruler"
- **1 Kings 12:21.** Benjamin stays with Judah when the kingdom divides

### New Testament

- **Acts 13:21.** Saul, "a man of the tribe of Benjamin"
- **Romans 11:1; Philippians 3:5.** Paul is "of the tribe of Benjamin"
- **Echo: 1 Corinthians 15:9.** Paul, "the least of the apostles"

4. **Apply:** Judah stood by Benjamin when it cost him. Who needs you to stand by them now?
