# Jochebed

*The mother who hid her baby, set him on the Nile, and was paid to nurse her own son.*

Jochebed was a woman of the tribe of Levi, living as a slave in Egypt when Pharaoh ordered every Hebrew baby boy thrown into the Nile. She is named only twice, in family lists, but Exodus 2 tells her story without using her name. She hid her newborn son for three months, then made a small basket, sealed it, and placed him among the reeds. God used her quiet courage to save the child who would become Moses. She was also the mother of Aaron and Miriam, so the leader, the priest and the prophetess of the Exodus all came from her home. Hebrews counts her act as faith.

## The life of Jochebed

*Only Moses' birth can be roughly dated: c. 1526 BC on the early Exodus date, or about 185 years later on the late date. Exodus 2 calls her "a daughter of Levi" and "the child's mother"; her name comes from Exodus 6:20 and Numbers 26:59.*

**In Egypt — A daughter of Levi.** She is born into the tribe of Levi during Israel's long stay in Egypt. Numbers says her mother bore her to Levi in Egypt. *Numbers 26:59*

**In Egypt — Marries Amram.** She marries Amram, a Levite of the family of Kohath. Exodus notes that she was his father's sister. *Exodus 2:1; 6:18, 20*

**Before the decree — Mother of Miriam and Aaron.** She bears a daughter, Miriam, and a son, Aaron, who is three years older than Moses. *Exodus 7:7; Numbers 26:59*

**c. 1526 BC — Hides her newborn.** Pharaoh has ordered every Hebrew son cast into the river. When she sees her baby is "a goodly child," she hides him for three months. *Exodus 1:22–2:2*

**c. 1526 BC — The basket in the reeds.** When she can hide him no longer, she makes an ark of bulrushes, seals it with slime and pitch, and lays it by the river's brink. His sister watches from a distance. *Exodus 2:3–4*

**c. 1526 BC and after — Nurses her own son.** Pharaoh's daughter finds the child. His sister offers to fetch a Hebrew nurse and brings his mother. She nurses him for wages until he is grown enough to go to the palace. *Exodus 2:5–10*

### The world around them

Exodus says a new king arose who did not know Joseph and feared how many Israelites there were. He forced them into hard labor building storage cities, then told the Hebrew midwives, Shiphrah and Puah, to kill the baby boys. The midwives feared God and refused (Exodus 1:8–21). Then Pharaoh ordered all his people to throw Hebrew sons into the Nile (Exodus 1:22). The river was Egypt's lifeline, lined with papyrus reeds. Children were usually weaned later than today, probably around age two or three, so Jochebed likely had her son for a few years.

## Key activities

*Exodus 6:20 says Amram married his father's sister. The later law forbade such marriages (Leviticus 18:12), but this was before that law was given. Some scholars also think the family list in Exodus 6 skips generations.*

1. **Saw something special in her son.** She saw that he was "a goodly child." Hebrews says both parents saw it and acted in faith. *Exodus 2:2; Hebrews 11:23*
2. **Defied the king.** She and Amram hid their son for three months and "were not afraid of the king's commandment." *Exodus 2:2; Hebrews 11:23*
3. **Built a little ark.** She took a basket of bulrushes, sealed it with slime and pitch, and laid it among the reeds. *Exodus 2:3*
4. **Trusted and waited.** She let go of her son, while his sister stood at a distance to see what would happen. *Exodus 2:3–4*
5. **Nursed him for Pharaoh's daughter.** Called by her daughter, she took her own child home and was paid to nurse him. *Exodus 2:7–9*
6. **Gave him up a second time.** When the child grew, she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, who named him Moses. *Exodus 2:10*

*Tradition.* Some Jewish rabbis identified Jochebed with Shiphrah, one of the two brave midwives, and Miriam with Puah. Exodus does not say this.

### Words and places

- **Ark.** The same Hebrew word, *tevah*, is used for Noah's ark. The Bible uses it only in these two stories (Exodus 2:3; Genesis 6:14).
- **Bulrushes.** Papyrus, a tall reed that grew along the Nile and was used for boats and paper (Exodus 2:3).
- **Slime and pitch.** Tar-like materials used to make the basket waterproof (Exodus 2:3).
- **Levi.** The third son of Jacob, whose tribe later became Israel's priests and temple servants (Genesis 29:34; Numbers 3:5–10).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Jochebed.*

### Old Testament

- **Exodus 2:1–10.** Her story, told without her name
- **Exodus 6:20.** Amram's wife, "his father's sister," mother of Aaron and Moses
- **Numbers 26:59.** Levi's daughter, mother of Aaron, Moses and Miriam
- **1 Chronicles 6:3.** Her three children listed as Amram's
- **Echo: Genesis 6–8.** Noah, too, is saved in an ark
- **Echo: 1 Samuel 1:22–28.** Hannah gives her weaned son back to God

### New Testament

- **Hebrews 11:23.** The faith of Moses' parents (she is not named)
- **Acts 7:20–21.** Moses nourished in his father's house three months
- **Echo: Matthew 2:13–16.** Another king kills baby boys; a family hides a child
- **Echo: 2 Timothy 1:5.** Faith learned from a mother and grandmother
