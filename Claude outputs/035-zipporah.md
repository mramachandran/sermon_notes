# Zipporah

*The Midianite wife of Moses who acted quickly in a strange and dangerous night.*

Zipporah was one of the seven daughters of Jethro, the priest of Midian. She met Moses when he drove off shepherds who were bullying her and her sisters at a well. Her father gave her to Moses as his wife, and she bore him two sons, Gershom and Eliezer. On the road back to Egypt, when the LORD met Moses and sought to kill him, it was Zipporah who took a flint knife and circumcised their son. Later she was sent back to her father and then brought again to Moses in the wilderness. Scripture says little about her, and several parts of her story are hard to understand. Yet at one crucial moment she saved her husband's life.

## The life of Zipporah

*Zipporah's story is not dated in Scripture. On the early Exodus date, Moses fled to Midian c. 1486 BC and returned to Egypt c. 1446 BC. On the late date, move both about 185 years later.*

**In Midian — At the well.** She and her sisters come to water their father's flock. Shepherds drive them away, but Moses stands up for them and waters their sheep. *Exodus 2:16–19*

**In Midian — Married to Moses.** Her father invites the stranger home. Moses agrees to stay, and Jethro gives him Zipporah as his wife. *Exodus 2:20–21*

**In Midian — Mother of Gershom.** She bears a son. Moses names him Gershom, saying, "I have been a stranger in a strange land." A second son, Eliezer, is born later. *Exodus 2:22; 18:3–4*

**c. 1446 BC · On the road — Circumcises her son.** Moses sets out for Egypt with his wife and sons on a donkey. At a lodging place the LORD meets him and seeks to kill him. Zipporah circumcises her son, and the LORD lets him go. *Exodus 4:20, 24–26*

**Before the Exodus — Sent back.** At some point Moses sends Zipporah and the boys back to her father. Scripture does not say when or why. *Exodus 18:2*

**c. 1446 BC · Near Sinai — Reunited.** After Israel leaves Egypt, Jethro brings Zipporah and her two sons to Moses at the mountain of God. *Exodus 18:1–6*

### The world around them

Circumcision was the sign of God's covenant with Abraham, and any male not circumcised was to be "cut off" from his people (Genesis 17:9–14). Moses, about to lead God's covenant people, had apparently not circumcised his son. Many ancient peoples practiced circumcision, but customs differed about age and meaning. Flint knives were used for the ritual long after metal tools were common (Joshua 5:2). As a Midianite, Zipporah was descended from Abraham through Keturah, so she came from a people related to Israel (Genesis 25:1–2).

## Key activities

*Exodus 4:24–26 is one of the hardest passages in the Bible. It is unclear whether the LORD sought to kill Moses or his son, whose feet Zipporah touched, and what "a bloody husband" means. Most readers agree the crisis was about the missing sign of the covenant.*

1. **Worked with her sisters.** She helped water her father's flock and was rescued by Moses at the well. *Exodus 2:16–17*
2. **Married a foreigner.** She married Moses, a fugitive who looked like an Egyptian to her family. *Exodus 2:19–21*
3. **Raised two sons.** She bore Gershom and Eliezer, whose names recall Moses' life as a stranger and God's help. *Exodus 2:22; 18:3–4*
4. **Traveled toward Egypt.** She set out with Moses and the boys on the journey back to Egypt. *Exodus 4:20*
5. **Acted in a crisis.** She "took a sharp stone, and cut off the foreskin of her son," and the LORD let Moses go. *Exodus 4:24–26*
6. **Returned to Moses.** After a time back with her father, she came to Moses in the wilderness. *Exodus 18:2–6*

*Tradition.* Some later readers, including the Jewish historian Josephus, speak of an Ethiopian wife of Moses, apart from Zipporah. Others think the "Cushite woman" of Numbers 12:1 was Zipporah herself. Scripture does not settle it.

### Words and places

- **Midian.** Desert land east of the Gulf of Aqaba, where Moses lived forty years (Exodus 2:15; Acts 7:29–30).
- **Circumcision.** The cutting of the foreskin, the sign of God's covenant with Abraham's family (Genesis 17:10–11).
- **Sharp stone.** A flint knife, used for circumcision (Exodus 4:25; Joshua 5:2).
- **Gershom.** Sounds like the Hebrew for "a stranger there" (Exodus 2:22).

### Read it yourself

- **Day 1.** Exodus 2:15–22. What do we learn about Zipporah's family?
- **Day 2.** Exodus 4:18–26. Why do you think circumcision mattered so much here?

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Zipporah.*

### Old Testament

- **Exodus 2:16–22.** She meets and marries Moses
- **Exodus 4:20–26.** The journey to Egypt and her son's circumcision
- **Exodus 18:2–6.** Brought back to Moses in the wilderness
- **Numbers 12:1.** "The Ethiopian woman"; whether this is Zipporah is debated
- **1 Chronicles 23:14–17.** Her sons counted among the Levites

### New Testament

- Zipporah is not named in the New Testament.
- **Acts 7:29.** Moses lived as a stranger in Midian, "where he begat two sons."
- **Echoes.** God commands Joshua to circumcise Israel before they enter the land (Joshua 5:2–9). Paul speaks of circumcision "of the heart" (Romans 2:28–29).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What did Zipporah do when the LORD met Moses on the road (Exodus 4:24–26)?
2. **Understand:** Why do you think God took the sign of the covenant so seriously in the family of Israel's leader?
3. **Apply:** Zipporah acted when her husband could not. When has someone close to you needed you to step in?
4. **Apply:** Is there an act of obedience you have been putting off, as Moses had? What will you do about it this week?
