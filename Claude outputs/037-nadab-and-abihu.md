# Nadab and Abihu

*Aaron's two oldest sons, who saw God on the mountain and died for offering "strange fire."*

Nadab and Abihu were the two oldest sons of Aaron, Israel's first high priest. Few were given more privilege. They climbed Sinai with Moses and the elders, saw the God of Israel, and ate and drank in his presence. They were ordained as priests and served at the first sacrifices in the tabernacle. Then, on that very day or soon after, they offered "strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not," and fire from the LORD killed them. Their story shows that God's nearness must be treated as holy.

## The life of Nadab and Abihu

*Dates are approximate (BC) and follow the early Exodus date, c. 1446 BC. On the late date, move them about 185 years later. Leviticus 10 seems to follow straight after the eighth day of the ordination (Leviticus 9:1), but the exact day is not stated.*

**In Egypt — Born to Aaron.** Aaron marries Elisheba, and she bears Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar. Nadab is the firstborn. *Exodus 6:23; Numbers 3:2*

**c. 1446 BC · At Sinai — See the God of Israel.** They are called up the mountain with Moses, Aaron and seventy elders. They see the God of Israel, and they eat and drink before him. *Exodus 24:1, 9–11*

**c. 1446 BC · At Sinai — Chosen as priests.** God tells Moses to set apart Aaron and his four sons to serve as priests, with holy garments made for them. *Exodus 28:1–4, 40–43*

**c. 1445 BC · Second year — Ordained.** Moses washes, clothes and anoints them with their father. For seven days they stay at the door of the tabernacle. *Leviticus 8:1–36*

**c. 1445 BC · The eighth day — The first service.** They help Aaron offer the first sacrifices. The glory of the LORD appears, and fire comes out from the LORD and consumes the offering. *Leviticus 9:1, 8–24*

**c. 1445 BC · Soon after — Strange fire.** Each takes his censer, puts fire and incense in it, and offers strange fire. Fire from the LORD devours them. Two of Aaron's cousins carry them out of the camp in their coats. *Leviticus 10:1–5*

### The world around them

Priests burned incense on a golden altar in the tabernacle each morning and evening (Exodus 30:7–8). A censer was a small pan for carrying burning coals. On the Day of Atonement, the high priest took coals "from off the altar before the LORD" (Leviticus 16:12). Many think this rule explains what went wrong: Nadab and Abihu used fire from somewhere else. In the ancient world, temples were often run by priestly families. Israel's priesthood was also a family office, which is why the death of two heirs was so shocking.

## Key activities

*Scripture does not say exactly what made the fire "strange." Suggestions include fire not taken from the altar, incense offered at the wrong time or place, or acting without being told. Because God warns priests against wine right afterward (Leviticus 10:8–11), some think they were drunk. The text does not settle it.*

1. **Went up the mountain.** They were among the few invited to come near on Sinai. *Exodus 24:1, 9*
2. **Saw God and ate before him.** "They saw God, and did eat and drink." *Exodus 24:10–11*
3. **Were set apart as priests.** They were clothed, anointed and ordained with their father. *Exodus 28:1; Leviticus 8:13, 30*
4. **Served at the first sacrifices.** They brought the blood to Aaron at the altar. *Leviticus 9:9, 12, 18*
5. **Offered strange fire.** Each took his own censer and offered fire "which he commanded them not." *Leviticus 10:1*
6. **Died before the LORD.** "There went out fire from the LORD, and devoured them." *Leviticus 10:2*

*Tradition.* Some Jewish teachers suggested that Nadab and Abihu were proud, or eager to lead before their time. These are later guesses, not statements of Scripture.

### Words and places

- **Censer.** A pan or firepan for carrying hot coals and burning incense (Leviticus 10:1; 16:12).
- **Incense.** A sweet-smelling mixture burned before God, often a picture of prayer (Exodus 30:34–38; Psalm 141:2).
- **Strange fire.** Fire that was "unauthorized" or not the kind God had commanded (Leviticus 10:1).
- **Sanctified.** Treated as holy, or set apart (Leviticus 10:3).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Nadab and Abihu.*

### Old Testament

- **Exodus 6:23; 28:1.** Their birth and calling as priests
- **Exodus 24:1, 9–11.** They see God on the mountain
- **Leviticus 10:1–7.** Their death and Aaron's silent grief
- **Leviticus 16:1–2.** Atonement rules "after the death of the two sons of Aaron"
- **Numbers 3:2–4; 1 Chronicles 24:1–2.** Childless; Eleazar and Ithamar carry on
- **Echo: 2 Samuel 6:6–7.** Uzzah dies for touching the ark
- **Echo: 2 Chronicles 26:16–21.** Uzziah struck for burning incense

### New Testament

- Nadab and Abihu are not named in the New Testament.
- **Echoes.** Ananias and Sapphira die for lying to the Holy Spirit (Acts 5:1–11). "Our God is a consuming fire" (Hebrews 12:28–29). Through Christ believers may now "come boldly unto the throne of grace" (Hebrews 4:16).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What did Nadab and Abihu experience in Exodus 24:9–11, and what did they do in Leviticus 10:1?
2. **Understand:** Why do you think God judged them so severely, and so quickly?
3. **Apply:** Nadab and Abihu had been given great closeness to God. Where might you have grown careless with worship or prayer?
4. **Apply:** Aaron "held his peace" in his grief. How can your group support someone facing a loss they cannot explain?
