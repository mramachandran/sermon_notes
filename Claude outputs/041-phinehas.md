# Phinehas

*The zealous priest who stopped a plague and was given a covenant of peace.*

Phinehas, son of Eleazar and grandson of Aaron, stepped onto the stage at one of the darkest moments of the wilderness years. On the plains of Moab many Israelites joined the women of Moab and Midian in immorality and in worship of Baal-peor, and a plague broke out. While the people wept at the tabernacle door, an Israelite leader brought a Midianite woman into his tent. Phinehas killed them both, and the plague stopped. God gave him "my covenant of peace." Later he served as a peacemaker between the tribes and as high priest.

## The life of Phinehas

*Dates are approximate (BC) and follow the early Exodus date, with Israel on the plains of Moab c. 1406 BC. On the late date, move them about 185 years later. Joshua 22 and Judges 20 are not dated.*

**Family — Born to Eleazar.** Eleazar, Aaron's son, marries one of the daughters of Putiel, and she bears Phinehas. He grows up in the priestly family. *Exodus 6:25*

**c. 1406 BC · Plains of Moab — Stops the plague.** Israel joins in the worship of Baal-peor, and a plague spreads. When Zimri brings Cozbi, a Midianite, into his tent, Phinehas kills them both with a javelin. The plague stops after 24,000 have died. *Numbers 25:1–9, 14–15*

**c. 1406 BC · Plains of Moab — Given a covenant.** God says Phinehas has turned away his wrath. He gives him a covenant of peace and a lasting priesthood for his descendants. *Numbers 25:10–13*

**c. 1406 BC · Midian — Goes to war.** Moses sends Phinehas with the army against Midian, carrying the holy instruments and the trumpets. *Numbers 31:1–8*

**After the conquest — Prevents a civil war.** The eastern tribes build a large altar by the Jordan. Phinehas leads a delegation, listens to their explanation, and brings back a report of peace. *Joshua 22:10–34*

**Days of the judges — Serves before the ark.** In the war against Benjamin, Phinehas stands before the ark at Bethel (the KJV reads "the house of God") and asks the LORD whether Israel should fight. *Judges 20:26–28*

### The world around them

Moab lay east of the Dead Sea, beside the plains where Israel camped, and Midianite clans were allied with Moab at this time (Numbers 22:4, 7). Baal was a Canaanite storm and fertility god, worshipped under local names such as "Baal of Peor." Many scholars think this worship included sexual rites, which would explain how immorality and idolatry came together in Numbers 25. Later Jewish writers made Phinehas a model of zeal for God's law (1 Maccabees 2:26, 54). That book is in the Apocrypha, which Catholic and Orthodox Christians include in their Bibles but Protestants do not.

## Key activities

*Many readers today find Numbers 25 troubling. The text presents Phinehas's act as God's judgment in a unique crisis, and later Scripture praises it. Christians differ on how such passages should shape life under the new covenant, where believers are told not to take vengeance (Romans 12:19).*

1. **Acted when others stood still.** While the congregation wept, he rose up and acted. *Numbers 25:6–7*
2. **Turned away God's wrath.** God said he had been "zealous for my sake" and had "made an atonement for the children of Israel." *Numbers 25:11–13*
3. **Received a covenant of peace.** God promised him and his descendants an everlasting priesthood. *Numbers 25:12–13*
4. **Went out with the army.** He carried the holy instruments and trumpets in the war against Midian. *Numbers 31:6*
5. **Listened before judging.** He heard the eastern tribes explain their altar and said, "This day we perceive that the LORD is among us." *Joshua 22:30–31*
6. **Gave his father a resting place.** Eleazar was buried in a hill that belonged to Phinehas in Ephraim. *Joshua 24:33*
7. **Led in worship.** He served before the ark and sought God's guidance for Israel. *Judges 20:27–28*

### Words and places

- **Baal-peor.** The Baal worshipped at Mount Peor in Moab (Numbers 25:3, 18).
- **Javelin.** A spear, the weapon Phinehas used (Numbers 25:7).
- **Covenant of peace.** God's promise of a lasting priesthood to Phinehas's family (Numbers 25:12; Malachi 2:4–5).
- **Shiloh.** The town where the tabernacle stood after the conquest, and where Phinehas's delegation set out (Joshua 18:1; 22:12).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Phinehas.*

### Old Testament

- **Exodus 6:25.** His birth in Aaron's family
- **Numbers 25:1–18; 31:6.** Peor, the covenant of peace, the war with Midian
- **Joshua 22:10–34.** He settles the dispute over the altar by the Jordan
- **Judges 20:27–28.** Priest before the ark
- **1 Chronicles 6:4, 50; Ezra 7:1–5.** In the high priestly line to Zadok and Ezra
- **1 Chronicles 9:20.** "The LORD was with him"
- **Psalm 106:30–31.** "Counted unto him for righteousness"

### New Testament

- Phinehas is not named in the New Testament.
- **Echo: John 2:17.** "The zeal of thine house hath eaten me up"
- **Echo: 1 Corinthians 10:8.** Paul probably recalls Peor
- **Echo: Ephesians 2:14–17.** Jesus the one who makes peace
