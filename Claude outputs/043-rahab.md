# Rahab

*The Canaanite woman of Jericho who hid Israel's spies and was saved by faith.*

Rahab lived in Jericho, the first city Israel faced across the Jordan. Scripture calls her a harlot, and her house was built into the city wall. When Joshua sent two spies into the city, she hid them on her roof and sent the king's men the wrong way. She had heard what the LORD had done for Israel, and she confessed that he is God in heaven above and in earth beneath. She asked for mercy for her family, and the spies gave her a sign: a scarlet cord in her window. When Jericho fell, she and her household were spared. The New Testament names her among the heroes of faith and in the family line of Jesus.

## The life of Rahab

*Dates follow the early date of the Exodus. On the late date, Jericho falls c. 1220 BC. Scripture gives no age for Rahab.*

**c. 1406 BC · In Jericho — Hears the news.** Word reaches Jericho of the Red Sea and of Israel's victory over Sihon and Og. The people's hearts melt with fear. *Joshua 2:9–11*

**c. 1406 BC · On her roof — Hides the spies.** Two men sent by Joshua lodge at her house. She hides them under stalks of flax and tells the king's men they have already gone. *Joshua 2:1–7*

**c. 1406 BC · At the window — Makes a pact.** She confesses faith in the LORD and asks for her family's lives. She lets the spies down by a cord, and they tell her to tie a scarlet line in the window. *Joshua 2:8–21*

**c. 1406 BC · When Jericho falls — Saved.** Joshua orders that she be spared. The two spies bring out Rahab, her parents, her brothers and all she has. *Joshua 6:17, 22–23*

**Afterward — Lives in Israel.** She and her family are settled outside the camp at first. The writer adds that she "dwelleth in Israel even unto this day." *Joshua 6:23, 25*

### The world around them

Jericho sat near the Jordan River, just north of the Dead Sea, beside a strong spring. It is one of the oldest cities known, and it guarded the road into the hill country. Some homes in ancient walled towns were built into or against the wall, which fits Rahab's house (Joshua 2:15). Flax was a crop used for linen, and it was often laid out on flat roofs to dry. Archaeologists debate what the ruins of Jericho show about the time of Joshua, and their conclusions depend partly on which date for the Exodus they follow.

## Key activities

*Some ancient writers, including Josephus, called Rahab an innkeeper. The Hebrew word and the New Testament both call her a harlot. Christians also differ on how to judge her lie to the king's men: some see it as wrong but forgiven, others as a lesser wrong to protect life.*

1. **Welcomed the spies.** She took them into her house and hid them on the roof. *Joshua 2:1–6*
2. **Misled the king's men.** She sent the searchers toward the Jordan while the spies were still hidden. *Joshua 2:3–7*
3. **Confessed the LORD.** She said, "for the LORD your God, he is God in heaven above, and in earth beneath." *Joshua 2:11*
4. **Asked for mercy.** She asked for a "true token" that her father's house would be spared. *Joshua 2:12–14*
5. **Helped them escape.** She let them down through the window and told them to hide in the mountain three days. *Joshua 2:15–16, 22*
6. **Kept the sign.** She tied the scarlet line in the window and gathered her family inside. *Joshua 2:18–21; 6:22–23*

*Tradition.* Some Jewish writings in the Talmud say Rahab later married Joshua. Scripture does not say this.

### Words and places

- **Scarlet line.** The red cord in her window, the sign that her house would be spared (Joshua 2:18, 21).
- **Accursed.** Jericho was "devoted" to the LORD, set apart for destruction, except for Rahab and her house (Joshua 6:17).
- **Flax.** A plant grown for linen. She hid the spies under its stalks (Joshua 2:6).
- **Rahab in the Psalms.** A different word, a poetic name for Egypt or a sea monster (Psalm 87:4; 89:10).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Rahab.*

### Old Testament

- **Joshua 2:1–24.** She hides the spies and makes a pact with them
- **Joshua 6:17, 22–25.** She and her household are spared
- **Echo: Exodus 12:13, 22–23.** Blood on the doorposts marks the saved houses
- **Echo: Ruth 1:16.** Another foreign woman chooses Israel's God

### New Testament

- **Matthew 1:5.** "Salmon begat Booz of Rachab"; most take her as Boaz's mother, though the time gap is debated
- **Hebrews 11:31.** Named among the heroes of faith
- **James 2:25.** "Justified by works, when she had received the messengers"
- **Echo: Matthew 21:31–32.** Harlots enter the kingdom before the leaders
- **Tradition.** Clement of Rome saw the scarlet cord as a picture of Christ's blood
