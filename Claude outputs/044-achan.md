# Achan

*The soldier whose hidden theft at Jericho brought defeat on all Israel.*

Achan was a man of Judah who fought at Jericho. God had said everything in the city was devoted to him, and nothing was to be kept. Achan saw a fine robe, silver and a bar of gold, wanted them, and buried them under his tent. No one knew. But when Israel attacked the small town of Ai, the army ran and thirty-six men died. By lot the guilt was traced to one man. Achan confessed, and he died in the valley of Achor, "trouble." His story shows how one person's secret sin can harm a whole community.

## The life of Achan

*Dates follow the early date of the Exodus. On the late date, these events fall c. 1220 BC. Scripture gives no age for Achan. All of his story comes from a few days.*

**c. 1406 BC · At Jericho — Takes the devoted things.** Israel is told that Jericho and all in it are devoted to the LORD. Achan takes some of the spoil for himself, and God's anger burns against Israel. *Joshua 6:17–19; 7:1*

**c. 1406 BC · At Ai — Israel is routed.** Spies advise sending only two or three thousand men. About three thousand go up, flee before the men of Ai, and lose thirty-six men. *Joshua 7:2–5*

**c. 1406 BC · The next morning — Taken by lot.** Joshua brings Israel near by tribes. Judah is taken, then the Zarhites, then Zabdi's household, and finally Achan. *Joshua 7:10–18*

**c. 1406 BC · Before Joshua — Confesses.** Achan admits what he saw, coveted, took and hid. Messengers run to his tent and find the goods buried there. *Joshua 7:19–23*

**c. 1406 BC · In the valley of Achor — Dies.** Achan is stoned, and a great heap of stones is raised over him. Then the LORD turns from his anger. *Joshua 7:24–26*

### The world around them

In the conquest, some cities were placed under the ban, in Hebrew *cherem*. The word means something set apart wholly to God, often for destruction, and never for private gain. Jericho was the first city taken in the land, and many readers see it as a kind of firstfruits given to God: its metals went into the LORD's treasury (Joshua 6:19). A shekel was a weight, not a coin, and was probably a little under half an ounce. The Hebrew calls Achan's robe "a garment of Shinar," the region of Babylon, so it was probably a costly imported robe.

## Key activities

*Joshua 7:24–25 says Achan's sons and daughters were taken to the valley with him, and "all Israel stoned him." Readers differ on whether the family was put to death with him, and if so whether they shared in hiding the goods. Deuteronomy 24:16 forbids punishing children for a parent's sin.*

1. **Fought at Jericho.** He was among the men who marched and saw the walls fall. *Joshua 6:1–21*
2. **Coveted and took.** In his own words: "I saw … then I coveted them, and took them." *Joshua 7:21*
3. **Hid the goods.** He buried them in the ground inside his tent, with the silver underneath. *Joshua 7:21–22*
4. **Stayed silent.** He said nothing while Israel was routed at Ai and Joshua lay on his face before the ark. *Joshua 7:5–9*
5. **Was found out.** God pointed him out step by step through the lot. *Joshua 7:14–18*
6. **Confessed.** Joshua called him "My son" and urged him to give glory to God and tell the truth. He did. *Joshua 7:19–21*

### Words and places

- **Accursed thing.** Something devoted to God and off limits to people (Joshua 6:18; 7:1).
- **Achor.** A valley near Jericho. The name means "trouble," a play on Achan's name (Joshua 7:26).
- **Wedge of gold.** A bar or tongue-shaped piece of gold weighing fifty shekels (Joshua 7:21).
- **Lot.** A way of seeking God's decision, probably by drawing or casting marked objects (Joshua 7:14; Proverbs 16:33).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Achan.*

### Old Testament

- **Joshua 7:1–26.** The main account
- **Joshua 22:20.** "That man perished not alone in his iniquity"
- **1 Chronicles 2:7.** "Achar, the troubler of Israel"
- **Hosea 2:15; Isaiah 65:10.** Achor becomes a "door of hope"
- **Echo: Genesis 3:6.** Eve also saw, desired and took
- **Echo: 1 Samuel 15:9–23.** Saul also kept what was devoted

### New Testament

- Achan is not named in the New Testament.
- **Echo: Acts 5:1–11.** Ananias and Sapphira keep back part and lie
- **Echo: James 1:14–15.** Sin traced from desire to action
- **Echo: 1 Corinthians 5:6.** "A little leaven leaveneth the whole lump"

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** List the four verbs in Achan's confession (Joshua 7:21). What does each step show?
2. **Understand:** Why did God treat Achan's private sin as the sin of all Israel (Joshua 7:1, 11)?
3. **Apply:** Achan buried what he took under his own tent. What might people "bury" today and hope no one finds?
4. **Apply:** Hosea turns the valley of trouble into a door of hope. Where do you need to hear that confession can lead to a fresh start?
