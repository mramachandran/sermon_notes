# Deborah

*The prophetess and judge who called Israel to battle and sang of the LORD's victory.*

Deborah is the only judge in the book of Judges who is also called a prophet, and the only woman among them. She held court under a palm tree in the hill country of Ephraim, and the people came to her to settle their disputes. For twenty years Jabin king of Canaan had crushed Israel with nine hundred iron chariots. God gave Deborah a message for Barak: gather an army at Mount Tabor, and the LORD would deliver Sisera, Jabin's general, into his hand. Barak would not go without her, so she went. God routed the chariots at the river Kishon, and Deborah and Barak sang one of the oldest songs in the Bible. The land had rest forty years.

## The life of Deborah

*Judges gives no calendar dates. On the early chronology the judges span c. 1375–1050 BC, but any single date for Deborah is rough. The entries follow the order of Judges 4–5.*

**Twenty years — Jabin oppresses Israel.** After Ehud dies, Israel does evil again. Jabin king of Canaan, reigning in Hazor, and his captain Sisera oppress Israel mightily with nine hundred chariots of iron. *Judges 4:1–3*

**Under the palm tree — Judges Israel.** Deborah, a prophetess and the wife of Lapidoth, judges Israel. She sits under a palm tree in mount Ephraim, and the people come up to her for judgment. *Judges 4:4–5*

**A word from God — Summons Barak.** She calls Barak from Kedesh-naphtali. God commands him to take ten thousand men of Naphtali and Zebulun to Mount Tabor, and promises to give Sisera into his hand. *Judges 4:6–7*

**At Kedesh — Goes with Barak.** Barak will not go unless she goes too. She agrees, but warns that the honour will go to a woman. Ten thousand men follow him. *Judges 4:8–10*

**At Mount Tabor — Gives the signal.** Sisera gathers his chariots by the river Kishon. Deborah tells Barak, "Up; for this is the day." Barak leads his men down the mountain. *Judges 4:12–14*

**By the Kishon — The LORD routs Sisera.** The LORD throws Sisera's army into confusion. Sisera flees on foot and is killed by Jael in her tent. *Judges 4:15–22*

**That day — Sings the song.** Deborah and Barak sing of God's victory. They praise the tribes that came, rebuke the tribes that stayed home, and bless Jael. *Judges 5:1–31*

**Forty years — The land rests.** Israel's hand grows stronger against Jabin until he is destroyed, and the land has rest forty years. *Judges 4:23–24; 5:31*

## Key activities

*Judges 4 tells the battle as a story and Judges 5 tells it as a poem. The poem adds details, such as rain from the clouds and a river that swept the enemy away. Many readers think a storm bogged down the chariots (Judges 5:4, 20–21). Read the two side by side.*

1. **Judged the people.** She settled Israel's disputes under the palm tree that bore her name. *Judges 4:4–5*
2. **Spoke for God.** As a prophetess she gave Barak God's command and promise. *Judges 4:6–7*
3. **Went with Barak.** When he said, "If thou wilt go with me, then I will go," she replied, "I will surely go with thee." *Judges 4:8–9*
4. **Named the day.** She told Barak, "is not the LORD gone out before thee?" *Judges 4:14*
5. **Saw God fight.** The LORD "discomfited Sisera, and all his chariots," so that Sisera left his chariot and fled on foot. *Judges 4:15*
6. **Sang praise first to God.** Her song begins, "Praise ye the LORD for the avenging of Israel." *Judges 5:1–5*
7. **Remembered hard times.** She recalled when the highways were empty and villages abandoned, until she "arose a mother in Israel." *Judges 5:6–8*
8. **Honoured the willing.** She praised the leaders and people who "offered themselves willingly." *Judges 5:2, 9, 14–15, 18*
9. **Rebuked the absent.** Reuben, Gilead, Dan and Asher stayed home, and Meroz was cursed for not coming "to the help of the LORD." *Judges 5:15–17, 23*
10. **Blessed Jael.** She sang, "Blessed above women shall Jael the wife of Heber the Kenite be." *Judges 5:24–27*
11. **Imagined the enemy's mother.** The song ends with Sisera's mother at the window, waiting for a son who will not come home. *Judges 5:28–30*
12. **Closed with a prayer.** "So let all thine enemies perish, O LORD: but let them that love him be as the sun when he goeth forth in his might." *Judges 5:31*

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Deborah.*

### Old Testament

- **Judges 4:1–24.** The battle against Sisera, told as a story.
- **Judges 5:1–31.** The Song of Deborah, which many scholars count among the oldest poems in the Bible.
- **Psalm 83:9–10.** The psalmist asks God to do to Israel's enemies as he did "to Sisera, as to Jabin, at the brook of Kison."
- **Joshua 11:1–11.** An earlier Jabin, king of Hazor, was defeated by Joshua. Some think Jabin was a royal title used by more than one king. Others think the two accounts are different tellings of related events.
- **Genesis 35:8.** A different Deborah, Rebekah's nurse, was buried under an oak below Bethel.
- **Echoes.** Other women called prophetesses include Miriam, who also sang after a victory (Exodus 15:20–21), and Huldah (2 Kings 22:14). Hannah's song praises God for reversing the fortunes of the strong and the weak (1 Samuel 2:1–10).

### New Testament

- Deborah is not named in the New Testament. Barak, who fought with her, is named in Hebrews 11:32.
- **Echoes.** Anna the prophetess served God in the temple (Luke 2:36–38). Mary's song celebrates God who "hath put down the mighty from their seats" (Luke 1:46–55). Philip's four daughters prophesied (Acts 21:9).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What roles did Deborah fill for Israel (Judges 4:4–6; 5:7)?
2. **Understand:** Her song praises God first and people second. Why is that order important?
3. **Apply:** Deborah honoured those who "offered themselves willingly." Where could you volunteer yourself for God's work this month?
4. **Apply:** Reuben had "great searchings of heart" but stayed home (Judges 5:16). What good intention do you need to turn into action?
