# Gideon

*The fearful farmer whom God called a mighty man of valour and used to rout Midian with three hundred men.*

Gideon was threshing wheat in a winepress, hiding his harvest from Midianite raiders, when the angel of the LORD greeted him as a "mighty man of valour." For seven years Midian and its allies had swarmed over Israel like locusts. Gideon felt weak and asked many questions. God answered with signs and patience. Gideon tore down his father's altar to Baal, gathered an army, and then watched God cut it from thirty-two thousand men to three hundred, so that Israel could not boast. With trumpets, jars and torches they put the enemy to flight. Gideon refused to be king, but a golden ephod he made became a snare. His life shows both what God can do through the weak and how easily success can lead people astray.

## The life of Gideon

*Judges gives no calendar dates. On the early chronology the judges span c. 1375–1050 BC, but any single date for Gideon is rough. The entries follow the order of Judges 6–8.*

**Seven years — Midian oppresses Israel.** Israel does evil again. Midianites, Amalekites and the children of the east invade each harvest, destroying crops and livestock. Israel hides in dens and caves and cries to the LORD. *Judges 6:1–6*

**At Ophrah — Called by God.** The angel of the LORD finds Gideon threshing wheat by the winepress. Gideon protests that he is the least in his father's house. Fire consumes his offering, and he builds an altar. *Judges 6:11–24*

**That night — Tears down Baal's altar.** With ten servants he breaks down his father's altar to Baal and cuts down the grove. The townsmen want him killed, but Joash defends him. *Judges 6:25–32*

**In the valley of Jezreel — Gathers an army.** The Spirit of the LORD comes on Gideon. He blows a trumpet and summons Manasseh, Asher, Zebulun and Naphtali. He asks for signs with a fleece, and God gives them. *Judges 6:33–40*

**At the well of Harod — Three hundred chosen.** God reduces the army from thirty-two thousand to ten thousand, and then to three hundred men who lapped the water. *Judges 7:1–8*

**At night — Midian is routed.** After overhearing a Midianite's dream, Gideon divides his men into three companies. They blow trumpets, break jars and shout, and the enemy turns on itself. *Judges 7:9–22*

**Across the Jordan — Pursues the kings.** "Faint, yet pursuing," he captures Zebah and Zalmunna. He punishes Succoth and Penuel for refusing help and kills the two kings. *Judges 7:23–8:21*

**Forty years — Rest, ephod and death.** He refuses the crown but makes an ephod that becomes a snare. The land is quiet forty years, and he dies "in a good old age." *Judges 8:22–32*

## Key activities

*Christians read the fleece in different ways. Some see it as weak faith that God kindly answered. Others see it as a sincere request for assurance. Scripture records it without comment, since God had already given Gideon his promise (Judges 6:14–16).*

1. **Hid his harvest.** He threshed wheat in a winepress to keep it from the Midianites. *Judges 6:11*
2. **Questioned God.** He asked, "if the LORD be with us, why then is all this befallen us?" *Judges 6:13*
3. **Confessed his weakness.** "My family is poor in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house." *Judges 6:15*
4. **Built an altar.** He named it Jehovah-shalom, "the LORD is peace." *Judges 6:22–24*
5. **Obeyed by night.** Afraid of his family and the town, he tore down Baal's altar under cover of darkness. *Judges 6:25–27*
6. **Earned a new name.** His father said, "if he be a god, let him plead for himself," and called him Jerubbaal. *Judges 6:31–32*
7. **Sought signs.** He asked for dew on the fleece and then on the ground, and God did both. *Judges 6:36–40*
8. **Trusted God with fewer men.** God said too many soldiers would let Israel say, "Mine own hand hath saved me." *Judges 7:2–8*
9. **Led a night attack.** His men cried, "The sword of the LORD, and of Gideon." *Judges 7:16–22*
10. **Calmed Ephraim.** He answered their anger with humility, and "their anger was abated toward him." *Judges 8:1–3*
11. **Refused the crown.** "I will not rule over you, neither shall my son rule over you: the LORD shall rule over you." *Judges 8:22–23*
12. **Made a golden ephod.** He set it up in Ophrah, and "all Israel went thither a whoring after it." *Judges 8:24–27*

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Gideon.*

### Old Testament

- **Judges 6–8.** This is the main account.
- **Judges 8:33–9:57.** After his death Israel turns to Baal-berith. His son Abimelech kills his brothers, and Jotham, the youngest, escapes.
- **1 Samuel 12:11.** Samuel names "Jerubbaal" among the deliverers God sent.
- **2 Samuel 11:21.** He is called "Jerubbesheth," replacing Baal with a Hebrew word for shame.
- **Psalm 83:9–12.** The psalmist asks God to make Israel's enemies like Oreb, Zeeb, Zebah and Zalmunna.
- **Isaiah 9:4** and **Isaiah 10:26.** Isaiah recalls "the day of Midian" and the slaughter at the rock of Oreb as pictures of God's saving power.

### New Testament

- **Hebrews 11:32–34.** "Gedeon" is named first among the judges who "out of weakness were made strong."
- **Echoes.** "My strength is made perfect in weakness" (2 Corinthians 12:9). God chose "the weak things of the world to confound the things which are mighty" (1 Corinthians 1:27). Some readers link the jars broken to reveal the light with Paul's words, "we have this treasure in earthen vessels" (2 Corinthians 4:7).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** How did God reduce Gideon's army, and what reason did he give (Judges 7:2–7)?
2. **Understand:** God called Gideon a "mighty man of valour" while he was hiding. What does that show about how God sees people?
3. **Apply:** Gideon's first act of obedience was at home, at his father's altar. What needs to change close to home before you serve more widely?
4. **Apply:** Gideon's ephod turned a victory into a snare. What good gift or success could pull your heart away from God?
