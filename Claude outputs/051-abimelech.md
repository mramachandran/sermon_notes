# Abimelech

*Gideon's son who killed his brothers to become king, and died under a millstone dropped by a woman.*

Abimelech was a son of Gideon, the judge who had refused to be king. His mother was a concubine from Shechem. After Gideon died, Abimelech persuaded the leaders of Shechem to back him. He hired a band of reckless men and killed seventy of his brothers on one stone. Only the youngest, Jotham, escaped. Shechem made Abimelech king, and he ruled for three years, long before Saul. From a mountaintop Jotham told a fable about trees that chose a thorn bush for their king, and his warning came true. Abimelech and Shechem turned on each other, a city burned, and Abimelech died when a woman dropped a millstone on his head. Judges tells his story as a clear case of God repaying evil.

## The life of Abimelech

*Judges gives no calendar dates for Abimelech. The judges' period runs roughly c. 1375–1050 BC on the early date of the Exodus. The order below follows Judges 8–9.*

**In Gideon's lifetime — Born in Shechem.** Gideon has seventy sons by many wives. A concubine who lives in Shechem bears him another son, and he names the boy Abimelech. Gideon had earlier refused to rule, saying, "the LORD shall rule over you." *Judges 8:22–23, 30–31*

**After Gideon's death — Wins over Shechem.** Israel turns to Baal-berith. Abimelech asks his mother's family to put a question to Shechem: seventy rulers, or one who is "your bone and your flesh"? They side with him and give him seventy pieces of silver. *Judges 8:33–9:4*

**At Ophrah — Kills his brothers.** With hired men he goes to his father's house and kills his brothers, the sons of Gideon, "upon one stone." Jotham, the youngest, hides and survives. Now no rival son of Gideon stands in his way. *Judges 9:4–5*

**At Shechem — Made king.** The men of Shechem and the house of Millo make Abimelech king by the pillar in Shechem. From Mount Gerizim, Jotham tells his fable of the trees and the bramble, then flees. *Judges 9:6–21*

**Three years on — Shechem turns.** God sends an evil spirit between Abimelech and Shechem. Its men rob travelers from ambush, and Gaal son of Ebed boasts against Abimelech. Zebul, the city's ruler, secretly warns Abimelech. *Judges 9:22–33*

**The next days — Destroys Shechem.** Abimelech defeats Gaal, takes the city, kills its people and sows it with salt. About a thousand men and women die when he burns the stronghold of the house of the god Berith. *Judges 9:34–49*

**At Thebez — Dies.** He attacks a strong tower and goes near its door to burn it. A woman drops a piece of millstone on his head. He orders his armor-bearer to kill him, so no one will say a woman slew him. *Judges 9:50–55*

### The world around them

Shechem lay in the hill country of Ephraim, in a pass between Mount Ebal and Mount Gerizim. It was an old and important city. Abraham camped there (Genesis 12:6), Joshua renewed the covenant there (Joshua 24:1, 25), and Joseph's bones were buried there (Joshua 24:32). By Abimelech's day its people worshipped Baal-berith, "lord of the covenant." Israel had no king, and the judges were leaders God raised up in times of crisis. Abimelech tried to turn that pattern into a family throne. Thebez was probably a town near Shechem, but its exact site is uncertain.

## Key activities

*Judges says Abimelech killed "threescore and ten" sons of Gideon, yet Jotham escaped, so many readers take seventy as a round figure. Judges never calls Abimelech a judge; his story works as a warning.*

1. **Played on family ties.** He asked Shechem's leaders to choose one ruler from their own kin instead of seventy. *Judges 9:1–3*
2. **Paid with temple silver.** He hired "vain and light persons" with seventy pieces of silver from the house of Baal-berith. *Judges 9:4*
3. **Murdered his brothers.** He killed Gideon's sons on one stone at Ophrah, and only Jotham escaped by hiding. *Judges 9:5*
4. **Took the title of king.** Shechem crowned him, and he reigned over Israel three years. *Judges 9:6, 22*
5. **Crushed a revolt.** He set ambushes against Gaal's forces and drove them back, then took Shechem the next day. *Judges 9:34–45*
6. **Burned the tower of Shechem.** He cut boughs on Mount Zalmon, piled them against the stronghold and set it on fire. *Judges 9:46–49*
7. **Died by his own order.** Struck by a millstone at Thebez, he had his young armor-bearer thrust him through. *Judges 9:50–54*

### Words and places

- **Jerubbaal.** Gideon's other name, "let Baal plead," given after he pulled down Baal's altar (Judges 6:32).
- **Baal-berith.** "Lord of the covenant," the god worshipped at Shechem (Judges 8:33; 9:4).
- **Mount Gerizim.** The mountain above Shechem where Jotham spoke his fable (Judges 9:7; Deuteronomy 11:29).
- **Millstone.** A heavy round stone for grinding grain. An upper millstone could be carried by hand (Judges 9:53).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Abimelech.*

### Old Testament

- **Judges 8:29–9:57.** This is the main account.
- **Judges 10:1.** Tola arises "after Abimelech" to defend Israel.
- **2 Samuel 11:21.** Joab expects David to recall how a woman on the wall killed Abimelech at Thebez. There Gideon is called Jerubbesheth.
- **Echoes.** A similar fable of a thistle and a cedar appears in 2 Kings 14:9. "He that rolleth a stone, it will return upon him" (Proverbs 26:27).

### New Testament

- Abimelech is not named in the New Testament.
- **Echoes.** "All they that take the sword shall perish with the sword" (Matthew 26:52). "Whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap" (Galatians 6:7). Jesus teaches that the one who would be great must serve (Mark 10:42–45), the opposite of Abimelech's grab for power.

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What reasons did Abimelech give the men of Shechem for choosing him (Judges 9:1–3)?
2. **Understand:** In Jotham's fable the useful trees would not leave their work to rule, but the bramble would. What was he saying about Abimelech?
3. **Apply:** Abimelech used family loyalty to get what he wanted. Where are you tempted to use people to get ahead?
4. **Apply:** Judges says God repaid both Abimelech and Shechem. Is there a wrong you need to leave in God's hands this week instead of paying it back yourself?
