# Samson

*The Nazarite strongman whose great gifts were matched by great weakness, and who died defeating the Philistines.*

Samson's birth was announced by the angel of the LORD to a childless woman of the tribe of Dan. He was to be a Nazarite from the womb, and he would "begin to deliver Israel out of the hand of the Philistines." The Spirit of the LORD gave him astonishing strength. He tore a lion apart, killed a thousand men with a jawbone and carried off a city gate. Yet he was ruled by his appetites and drawn again and again to Philistine women. Delilah finally drew out the secret of his strength, and he was blinded and set to grind grain in a prison. In his death he prayed once more and brought down the house of the god Dagon. He judged Israel twenty years.

## The life of Samson

*Judges gives no calendar dates for Samson. His twenty years fall within forty years of Philistine rule. Many scholars think his work overlapped with the days of Eli and Samuel, but the text does not say.*

**Before his birth — Announced by an angel.** The angel of the LORD tells Manoah's barren wife she will bear a son. He will be a Nazarite from the womb, and she must drink no wine. When Manoah offers a sacrifice, the angel ascends in the altar flame. *Judges 13:2–23*

**Childhood — Blessed and stirred.** Samson is born and grows, and the LORD blesses him. The Spirit of the LORD begins to move him at times in the camp of Dan, between Zorah and Eshtaol, near his family's home. *Judges 13:24–25*

**Young man — A wife from Timnath.** He demands a Philistine wife against his parents' wishes. On the way he tears apart a young lion, and later he eats honey from its carcase. At the wedding feast he puts forth a riddle. *Judges 14:1–14*

**The wedding week — The riddle betrayed.** His bride weeps and coaxes out the answer, then tells her people. In anger Samson kills thirty Philistines at Ashkelon to pay the wager. His wife is given to his companion. *Judges 14:15–20*

**Wheat harvest — Foxes and fire.** He ties three hundred foxes tail to tail with firebrands and burns the Philistines' grain, vineyards and olives. They burn his wife and her father, and he strikes back "hip and thigh." *Judges 15:1–8*

**At Lehi — The jawbone.** The men of Judah bind him and hand him over. The Spirit comes upon him, the ropes fall away, and he kills a thousand men with a fresh jawbone of an ass. God gives him water in his thirst. *Judges 15:9–20*

**Later — Gaza and Delilah.** He carries off the gates of Gaza. Then he loves Delilah, who is paid to learn his secret. When his hair is shaved, the LORD departs from him, and the Philistines blind him and set him to grind in prison. *Judges 16:1–21*

**His death — The house of Dagon.** At a feast to Dagon, the blind Samson prays for strength once more. He pulls down the two middle pillars, killing the lords and about three thousand people on the roof. His family buries him. *Judges 16:22–31*

## Key activities

*A Nazarite (Numbers 6:1–21) took no wine or grapes, let no razor touch his head, and kept away from dead bodies. Samson's vow was lifelong and set by God before his birth. Readers differ on how far he broke it before his hair was cut.*

1. **Set apart from birth.** He was to be a Nazarite to God from the womb, with no razor on his head. *Judges 13:5*
2. **Chose by sight.** He insisted on a Philistine wife because "she pleaseth me well." Judges adds that the LORD was seeking an occasion against the Philistines. *Judges 14:1–4*
3. **Tore a lion apart.** The Spirit came mightily upon him, and he rent a young lion with his bare hands, telling no one. *Judges 14:5–6*
4. **Took honey from the carcase.** He scooped honey from the lion's body and shared it with his parents without saying where it came from. *Judges 14:8–9*
5. **Told a riddle.** "Out of the eater came forth meat, and out of the strong came forth sweetness." *Judges 14:12–18*
6. **Paid his debt in blood.** He killed thirty men at Ashkelon and gave their garments to those who had answered the riddle. *Judges 14:19*
7. **Burned the harvest.** He used three hundred foxes and firebrands to burn Philistine fields, vineyards and olive groves. *Judges 15:3–5*
8. **Let his own people bind him.** He allowed three thousand men of Judah to tie him up and hand him over. *Judges 15:9–13*
9. **Struck with a jawbone.** He killed a thousand Philistines at Lehi, then cried to the LORD in his thirst. *Judges 15:14–19*
10. **Carried off Gaza's gates.** He lifted the city gate, posts and bar, and carried them to the top of a hill before Hebron. *Judges 16:1–3*
11. **Gave away his secret.** Worn down by Delilah, he "told her all his heart," and she had his head shaved. *Judges 16:15–19*
12. **Prayed and died.** Blind and captive, he asked God to remember him, then brought down Dagon's house. *Judges 16:28–30*

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Samson.*

### Old Testament

- **Judges 13–16.** This is the main account.
- **Numbers 6:1–21.** This is the law of the Nazarite, which shaped his life.
- **Echoes.** Hannah vows that no razor will come on her son's head (1 Samuel 1:11). Other barren women receive sons by God's promise, such as Sarah (Genesis 18:9–14) and Rebekah (Genesis 25:21).

### New Testament

- **Hebrews 11:32–34.** Samson is named among those who "out of weakness were made strong."
- **Echoes.** An angel announces the birth of John the Baptist, who "shall drink neither wine nor strong drink" (Luke 1:15). Many Christians also see an echo of Jesus, who won his greatest victory through his death (Colossians 2:15).
- **Tradition.** Some Jewish interpreters read "Bedan" in Samuel's list of deliverers as Samson, "a son of Dan" (1 Samuel 12:11). The Greek Septuagint reads "Barak" there.

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What instructions did the angel give Samson's mother (Judges 13:3–5)?
2. **Understand:** Samson's strength came from the Spirit, not his hair alone (Judges 14:6; 16:20). Why does it matter that "the LORD was departed from him"?
3. **Apply:** Samson often did what was right in his own eyes. Where do your feelings tend to overrule wisdom?
4. **Apply:** God answered Samson's last prayer after his great failure. What would it look like for you to turn back to God this week after a failure?
