# Naomi

*The widow who went out full, came home empty, and lived to hold her grandson in her arms.*

Naomi was a woman of Bethlehem in Judah. During a famine she left home with her husband Elimelech and their two sons. In Moab her husband died, her sons married Moabite women, and then both sons died too. Naomi returned home a grieving widow and told the women of Bethlehem to call her Mara, "bitter." Yet she did not come back alone. Her daughter-in-law Ruth refused to leave her. As Ruth gleaned in the fields of Boaz, Naomi saw God's kindness again, and she guided Ruth toward marriage with this kinsman. The book ends with Naomi holding Ruth's son Obed, the grandfather of King David. Her story shows grief spoken honestly and hope slowly restored.

## The life of Naomi

*Ruth is set "in the days when the judges ruled" and gives no calendar date. Boaz is David's great-grandfather, which some take to point to the later judges, though the family list may skip generations.*

**In a famine — Leaves Bethlehem.** A famine strikes the land. Elimelech takes Naomi and their sons Mahlon and Chilion from Bethlehem-judah to live for a time in the country of Moab, across the Jordan and the Dead Sea. *Ruth 1:1–2*

**About ten years in Moab — Loses her family.** Elimelech dies. The sons marry Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth. After about ten years both sons die, and Naomi is left without husband or children in a foreign land, with two widowed daughters-in-law. *Ruth 1:3–5*

**On the road — Turns for home.** Hearing that the LORD has given his people bread, she sets out for Judah. She urges her daughters-in-law to go back to their mothers' homes. Orpah kisses her and leaves, but Ruth clings to her. *Ruth 1:6–18*

**Barley harvest — Asks to be called Mara.** All Bethlehem is stirred at her return. She tells the women she went out full, and the LORD has brought her home empty. They arrive at the beginning of barley harvest. *Ruth 1:19–22*

**That evening — Sees God's kindness.** Ruth comes home from Boaz's field with an ephah of barley. Naomi blesses the LORD, who "hath not left off his kindness," and explains that Boaz is a near kinsman. *Ruth 2:17–23*

**End of harvest — Plans for Ruth's rest.** Naomi sends Ruth to the threshing floor to ask Boaz, their kinsman, to take her under his care. When Ruth returns, Naomi tells her to wait and see how the matter will fall. *Ruth 3:1–5, 16–18*

**Later — Holds her grandson.** Boaz redeems Elimelech's land and marries Ruth. When Obed is born, the women bless the LORD, and Naomi lays the child in her bosom and becomes his nurse. Her empty house is full again. *Ruth 4:9–17*

### The world around them

Moab lay east of the Dead Sea on a high plateau. Moab and Israel were related through Lot (Genesis 19:36–37), but they were often enemies; in the judges' days Eglon king of Moab had ruled over Israel for eighteen years (Judges 3:12–14). A widow in Israel had little protection. Without a husband or sons, Naomi had no one to work her family's land or provide for her. God's law made room for such people by letting the poor glean at harvest (Leviticus 19:9–10).

## Key activities

*Ruth 1:1 says "Bethlehem-judah" to set this Bethlehem apart from another in Zebulun (Joshua 19:15). Some readers see the move to Moab as a lapse of faith. The book itself does not judge it.*

1. **Moved her family to survive.** She went with Elimelech to Moab when famine struck. *Ruth 1:1–2*
2. **Blessed her daughters-in-law.** She prayed that the LORD would deal kindly with them and give them rest in new homes. *Ruth 1:8–9*
3. **Spoke her grief honestly.** She said "the hand of the LORD is gone out against me," and asked to be called Mara. *Ruth 1:13, 20–21*
4. **Came home.** She returned to Bethlehem because the LORD had visited his people with bread. *Ruth 1:6, 22*
5. **Recognized God's hand.** When she heard of Boaz, she praised the LORD's kindness "to the living and to the dead." *Ruth 2:19–20*
6. **Sought Ruth's future.** She planned for Ruth to find rest and a home with Boaz. *Ruth 3:1–4*
7. **Waited wisely.** "Sit still, my daughter, until thou know how the matter will fall." *Ruth 3:18*
8. **Nursed Obed.** She cared for Ruth's son, and the neighbor women said, "There is a son born to Naomi." *Ruth 4:16–17*

### Words and places

- **Moab.** The land east of the Dead Sea, home of Lot's descendants (Genesis 19:37; Ruth 1:1).
- **Mara.** Hebrew for "bitter," the name Naomi asked to be called (Ruth 1:20).
- **Kinsman.** A close relative with the duty to help family in need, including buying back land (Ruth 2:20; Leviticus 25:25).
- **Ephrathites.** People of Ephratah, an old name for the Bethlehem area (Ruth 1:2; Micah 5:2).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Naomi.*

### Old Testament

- **Ruth 1–4.** Naomi appears throughout the book.
- **Echoes.** Job also refused to hide his pain: "I will complain in the bitterness of my soul" (Job 7:11). "Weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning" (Psalm 30:5).

### New Testament

- Naomi is not named in the New Testament.
- **Echoes.** Her grandson Obed appears in Jesus' family line (Matthew 1:5; Luke 3:32). Jesus meets a grieving widow at Nain and gives her son back to her (Luke 7:11–15). "All things work together for good to them that love God" (Romans 8:28).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What did Naomi say to the women of Bethlehem when she came home (Ruth 1:20–21)?
2. **Understand:** Naomi said God had afflicted her, yet she still prayed for and blessed others. What does that show about honest faith?
3. **Apply:** Naomi said she came home "empty," but Ruth was beside her. Who has God placed beside you in a hard season?
4. **Apply:** Naomi helped plan Ruth's future. Whom could you encourage or help toward a good future this week?
