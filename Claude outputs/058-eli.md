# Eli

*The aging priest of Shiloh who trained Samuel but would not restrain his own sons.*

Eli was the priest at Shiloh, where the tabernacle and the ark of God stood in the last days of the judges. He judged Israel for forty years. Eli blessed a weeping woman named Hannah, and later he raised her son Samuel in the house of the LORD. He also taught the boy how to answer when God called. But his own sons, Hophni and Phinehas, were corrupt priests who treated the LORD's offerings with contempt, and Eli only scolded them. God warned him twice that judgment would fall on his house. In one day the ark was captured, both sons were killed, and Eli fell and died. His life shows both humble faith and the cost of failing to act.

## The life of Eli

*Scripture gives no calendar dates for Eli. He died at 98 after judging Israel forty years. Samuel was born c. 1100 BC (a rough date), and Eli died some years later, while Samuel was still young.*

**At Shiloh — Blesses Hannah.** Eli sits by a post of the temple of the LORD. He mistakes Hannah's silent prayer for drunkenness, then hears her story and blesses her: "the God of Israel grant thee thy petition." *1 Samuel 1:9–18*

**Some years later — Receives Samuel.** Hannah brings her weaned son to Shiloh and gives him to the LORD. The child ministers to the LORD before Eli, and each year Eli blesses Elkanah and Hannah when they come up to sacrifice. *1 Samuel 1:24–2:11, 19–21*

**Very old — Rebukes his sons.** Eli hears of his sons' greed with the sacrifices and their sin with the women who served at the tabernacle door. He warns them that no one can plead for a man who sins against the LORD, but they will not listen. *1 Samuel 2:12–17, 22–25*

**A warning — The man of God.** A prophet tells Eli that he honors his sons above God. Both sons will die on one day, and God will raise up a faithful priest who will do all that is in God's heart. *1 Samuel 2:27–36*

**One night — Samuel's call.** When the LORD calls Samuel three times, Eli perceives that it is God. He tells the boy to answer, "Speak, LORD; for thy servant heareth." In the morning he accepts God's word of judgment. *1 Samuel 3:1–18*

**Age 98 — Dies at the news.** Israel loses to the Philistines, the ark is taken, and Hophni and Phinehas are killed. When Eli hears that the ark is taken, he falls backward from his seat, breaks his neck and dies. *1 Samuel 4:1–18*

### The world around them

Shiloh lay in the hill country of Ephraim, north of Bethel. Joshua had set up the tabernacle there (Joshua 18:1), and Israelites came each year to worship and sacrifice (1 Samuel 1:3). By Eli's time it had doorposts and doors, and it is called "the temple of the LORD" (1 Samuel 1:9; 3:15). Priests were to receive set portions of the offerings (Leviticus 7:31–34), but the fat belonged to the LORD and had to be burned first (Leviticus 3:16). Eli's sons took what they wanted.

## Key activities

*Scripture does not state Eli's ancestry. Many trace it to Aaron's son Ithamar, because a later priest in his line is counted among Ithamar's sons (1 Chronicles 24:3). The judgment on his house was fulfilled when Solomon removed Abiathar (1 Kings 2:27).*

1. **Served at Shiloh.** He sat at the temple of the LORD as priest and judged Israel forty years. *1 Samuel 1:9; 4:18*
2. **Misjudged, then blessed.** He first thought Hannah was drunk, but he listened and sent her away in peace. *1 Samuel 1:12–17*
3. **Raised Samuel.** The boy ministered to the LORD under Eli's care. *1 Samuel 2:11, 18; 3:1*
4. **Rebuked too little.** He asked his sons, "Why do ye such things?" but did not stop them. *1 Samuel 2:23–25; 3:13*
5. **Honored his sons above God.** The man of God charged him with making himself fat on the best of Israel's offerings. *1 Samuel 2:29*
6. **Taught Samuel to listen.** He helped the boy recognize God's voice and respond. *1 Samuel 3:8–10*
7. **Accepted God's judgment.** He insisted that Samuel tell him everything, then said, "It is the LORD." *1 Samuel 3:17–18*
8. **Trembled for the ark.** He waited by the wayside, "for his heart trembled for the ark of God." *1 Samuel 4:13*

### Words and places

- **Shiloh.** The town in Ephraim where the tabernacle stood (Joshua 18:1; 1 Samuel 1:3).
- **Sons of Belial.** A Hebrew phrase for worthless, wicked men (1 Samuel 2:12).
- **Fleshhook.** A three-pronged fork used to lift meat from the pot (1 Samuel 2:13–14).
- **Ichabod.** Hebrew for "no glory." Eli's daughter-in-law gave her son this name, saying, "The glory is departed from Israel" (1 Samuel 4:21).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Eli.*

### Old Testament

- **1 Samuel 1–4.** This is the main account.
- **1 Samuel 14:3.** Eli's great-grandson Ahiah serves as priest in Saul's day.
- **1 Kings 2:27.** Solomon removes Abiathar, fulfilling "the word of the LORD, which he spake concerning the house of Eli in Shiloh."
- **Psalm 78:60–64** and **Jeremiah 7:12–14.** God forsook the tabernacle at Shiloh, and "their priests fell by the sword."

### New Testament

- Eli is not named in the New Testament. Heli in Luke 3:23 is a different man, and Jesus' cry "Eli, Eli" (Matthew 27:46) means "My God."
- **Echoes.** "Whom the Lord loveth he chasteneth" (Hebrews 12:6). A church leader must be one who rules his own house well (1 Timothy 3:4–5).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What did Eli tell Samuel to say when the LORD called again (1 Samuel 3:9)?
2. **Understand:** Eli spoke to his sons but "restrained them not" (1 Samuel 3:13). What is the difference between rebuking and restraining?
3. **Apply:** Eli helped a young person learn to hear God. Whom could you encourage in faith this month?
4. **Apply:** Is there a wrong you have put up with because confronting it feels hard? What is one step you could take?
