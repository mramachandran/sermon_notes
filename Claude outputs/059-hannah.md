# Hannah

*The childless woman who poured out her soul to God and gave her son Samuel back to him.*

Hannah was one of the two wives of Elkanah, a man from the hill country of Ephraim. Her rival, Peninnah, had children, but Hannah had none. Every year at Shiloh, Peninnah provoked her until she wept and would not eat. One year Hannah went to the house of the LORD and prayed in deep distress. She vowed that if God gave her a son, she would give him to the LORD for all his life. The priest Eli thought she was drunk, then blessed her. God remembered Hannah, and she bore Samuel. When he was weaned she brought him to Shiloh and left him there. Then she sang a song of praise that echoes all the way to Mary's song in Luke.

## The life of Hannah

*Dates are rough. The house chronology places Samuel's birth c. 1100 BC, near the end of the judges' period. First Samuel gives no ages for Hannah.*

**Year by year — Provoked at Shiloh.** Elkanah takes his family to Shiloh each year to worship and sacrifice. He gives Hannah a worthy portion because he loves her. But Peninnah provokes her, because the LORD has shut up her womb. *1 Samuel 1:1–7*

**One year at Shiloh — Comforted, still grieving.** Hannah weeps and will not eat. Elkanah asks, "am not I better to thee than ten sons?" After the meal at Shiloh she rises and goes to the house of the LORD to pray. *1 Samuel 1:7–9*

**At the temple — Prays and vows.** In bitterness of soul she prays and weeps sore. She vows that if God gives her a son, she will give him to the LORD all the days of his life, and no razor will come upon his head. *1 Samuel 1:10–11*

**At the temple — Blessed by Eli.** Eli sees her lips moving without sound and thinks she is drunk. She explains that she has poured out her soul before the LORD. He blesses her, and her face is no longer sad. *1 Samuel 1:12–18*

**c. 1100 BC · At Ramah — Samuel is born.** The LORD remembers Hannah. She bears a son and names him Samuel, "Because I have asked him of the LORD." She stays at home with him until he is weaned. *1 Samuel 1:19–23*

**After weaning — Gives Samuel to the LORD.** She brings him to Shiloh with three bullocks, flour and a bottle of wine. She tells Eli, "For this child I prayed," and lends him to the LORD for as long as he lives. *1 Samuel 1:24–28*

**That day — Sings her song.** Hannah prays a song of praise. She rejoices in God's salvation. God humbles the proud and raises the poor, guards his saints, and will give strength to his king and exalt his anointed. *1 Samuel 2:1–10*

**Each year after — More children.** She makes Samuel a little coat and brings it at the yearly sacrifice. Eli blesses her and Elkanah, and the LORD visits Hannah. She bears three sons and two daughters, while Samuel grows before the LORD. *1 Samuel 2:18–21*

## Key activities

*Hannah speaks of God exalting "the horn of his anointed" (1 Samuel 2:10) before Israel has any king. Some read this as looking ahead to Israel's kings, others as pointing beyond them to the Messiah; many see both.*

1. **Worshipped faithfully.** She went up to Shiloh year after year, even though the trip brought her pain. *1 Samuel 1:3–7*
2. **Endured provocation.** Peninnah "provoked her sore," yet Scripture records no reply from Hannah. *1 Samuel 1:6–7*
3. **Poured out her soul.** She brought her grief to God in prayer and tears. *1 Samuel 1:10, 15*
4. **Made a vow.** She promised to give her son to the LORD for life. *1 Samuel 1:11*
5. **Answered respectfully.** When Eli accused her, she explained herself gently and did not argue. *1 Samuel 1:14–16*
6. **Left her burden with God.** After Eli's blessing she ate, "and her countenance was no more sad." *1 Samuel 1:18*
7. **Named her son.** She called him Samuel as a reminder that she had asked him of the LORD. *1 Samuel 1:20*
8. **Kept her vow.** She weaned Samuel, then brought him to Shiloh and left him in the LORD's service. *1 Samuel 1:24–28*
9. **Sang God's praise.** "There is none holy as the LORD: for there is none beside thee." *1 Samuel 2:1–2*
10. **Cared from a distance.** Each year she made Samuel a little coat and brought it to him. *1 Samuel 2:19*

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Hannah.*

### Old Testament

- **1 Samuel 1:1–2:21.** This is the main account.
- **Echoes.** Other barren women become mothers by God's help: Sarah (Genesis 21:1–2), Rebekah (Genesis 25:21), Rachel (Genesis 30:22–24) and Samson's mother (Judges 13:2–3). God "maketh the barren woman to keep house, and to be a joyful mother of children" (Psalm 113:9). Psalm 113:7–8 repeats Hannah's words about lifting the poor from the dust (1 Samuel 2:8).

### New Testament

- Hannah is not named in the New Testament.
- **Echoes.** Mary's song follows the pattern of Hannah's: God "hath put down the mighty from their seats, and exalted them of low degree" (Luke 1:46–55). Elizabeth, long childless, also bears a son promised by God (Luke 1:7, 13). The prophetess Anna bears the Greek form of Hannah's name (Luke 2:36). "Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you" (1 Peter 5:7).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** How did Hannah describe what she was doing when Eli thought she was drunk (1 Samuel 1:15–16)?
2. **Understand:** Hannah asked God for a son, then gave him back. What does that show about how she saw her gift?
3. **Apply:** Hannah took her deepest pain to God in honest prayer. What would it look like for you to pour out your soul to God this week?
4. **Apply:** Is there something God has given you, such as a child, a skill or your time, that you need to offer back to him?
