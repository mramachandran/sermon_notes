# Saul

*Israel's first king, who began small in his own eyes and ended at war with God's word.*

Saul was a tall, handsome young man from the small tribe of Benjamin. He went out looking for his father's lost donkeys and came home anointed as king. Israel had asked Samuel for "a king to judge us like all the nations" (1 Samuel 8:5), and Saul looked the part. He started well. He rescued a besieged town, united the tribes and fought the Philistines all his days. But again and again he put his own plans above God's clear commands. He offered a sacrifice he was told to wait for. He spared what he was told to destroy. Then jealousy of David ate away at him. His story is a sober study in how a gifted leader can lose his way one small disobedience at a time.

## The life of Saul

*Dates are approximate (BC). The number of years in 1 Samuel 13:1 is missing or unclear in the Hebrew text, so Saul's age at his crowning is unknown. Acts 13:21 gives his reign as forty years.*

**c. 1050 BC · Young man — Anointed in secret.** Sent to find his father's lost donkeys, Saul goes to the seer Samuel. Samuel pours oil on his head and tells him the LORD has anointed him captain over his inheritance. *1 Samuel 9:1–10:16*

**c. 1050 BC · Start of reign — Chosen by lot.** At Mizpeh, Samuel casts lots before the tribes, and the lot falls on Saul. He is found hiding "among the stuff," and the people shout, "God save the king." *1 Samuel 10:17–27*

**Early reign — Rescues Jabesh-gilead.** Nahash the Ammonite threatens to put out the right eye of every man in Jabesh-gilead. The Spirit of God comes on Saul, he rallies Israel, and the town is saved. *1 Samuel 11:1–15*

**Early reign — Offers the sacrifice himself.** Facing a huge Philistine army at Michmash, Saul waits seven days for Samuel, then offers the burnt offering himself. Samuel arrives and says his kingdom will not continue. *1 Samuel 13:5–14*

**Later — Spares Agag.** Told to destroy Amalek completely, Saul spares King Agag and the best animals. Samuel tells him that because he rejected the word of the LORD, the LORD has rejected him as king. *1 Samuel 15:1–35*

**Middle years — David arrives.** The Spirit of the LORD leaves Saul, and a troubling spirit torments him. Young David plays the harp to calm him, then kills Goliath. Soon Saul hears the women singing David's praises. *1 Samuel 16:14–18:9*

**Last years — Hunts David.** Saul throws his spear at David, orders the priests of Nob killed, and chases David through the wilderness. Twice David spares Saul's life, and twice Saul admits he has been wrong. *1 Samuel 18:10–26:25*

**c. 1010 BC · End of reign — Endor and Gilboa.** With God silent, Saul consults a medium at Endor. The next day the Philistines defeat Israel on Mount Gilboa. His sons die, and the wounded Saul falls on his own sword. *1 Samuel 28:3–25; 31:1–13*

## Key activities

*1 Samuel 31 says Saul fell on his own sword. In 2 Samuel 1 an Amalekite claims he finished Saul off. Many readers think the Amalekite lied, hoping for a reward from David.*

1. **Obeyed the call, reluctantly.** He was humble at first, saying Benjamin was the smallest tribe, and he hid when the lot fell on him. *1 Samuel 9:21; 10:21–22*
2. **Prophesied with the prophets.** God gave him "another heart," and he prophesied, so people asked, "Is Saul also among the prophets?" *1 Samuel 10:9–12*
3. **Saved Jabesh-gilead.** He cut up a yoke of oxen to summon the tribes, led a dawn attack on the Ammonites, and spared those who had mocked him. *1 Samuel 11:5–13*
4. **Ran ahead of God.** He offered the burnt offering at Gilgal instead of waiting for Samuel, and blamed the pressure of the moment. *1 Samuel 13:8–14*
5. **Made a rash oath.** He forbade his army to eat until evening. The tired troops sinned, and he nearly put Jonathan to death. *1 Samuel 14:24–45*
6. **Fought Israel's enemies.** He fought Moab, Ammon, Edom, Zobah and the Philistines, and there was hard war with the Philistines all his days. *1 Samuel 14:47–52*
7. **Spared what God condemned.** He kept Agag and the best of the flocks, set up a monument for himself at Carmel, and claimed he had obeyed. *1 Samuel 15:7–21*
8. **Fed his jealousy.** When the women sang that David had slain ten thousands, Saul "eyed David from that day and forward." He threw his spear at him more than once. *1 Samuel 18:6–11; 19:9–10*
9. **Killed the priests of Nob.** He had Doeg the Edomite put to death eighty-five priests who had helped David, and struck their town as well. *1 Samuel 22:9–19*
10. **Admitted his wrong, briefly.** After David spared him, Saul said, "I have played the fool, and have erred exceedingly," yet the chase went on. *1 Samuel 24:16–22; 26:21*
11. **Sought a medium.** He had banned mediums, but in his fear he went to one at Endor. There he heard that he and his sons would die. *1 Samuel 28:3–19*
12. **Died on Gilboa.** Wounded by archers, he fell on his sword. The men of Jabesh-gilead risked their lives to bury him. *1 Samuel 31:1–13*

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Saul.*

### Old Testament

- **1 Samuel 8–31.** This is the main account, from the people's request for a king to Saul's death.
- **2 Samuel 1:17–27.** David's lament for Saul and Jonathan: "how are the mighty fallen!"
- **2 Samuel 21:1–14.** A famine is traced to Saul's killing of the Gibeonites, and David buries the bones of Saul and Jonathan.
- **1 Samuel 28:11–19.** At Endor the woman sees a figure the text calls Samuel. Christians differ on whether Samuel truly appeared by God's permission or whether it was a deception.
- **1 Chronicles 10:1–14.** Chronicles says Saul died for his unfaithfulness and for consulting a medium instead of the LORD.
- **Echoes.** Deuteronomy 17:14–20 sets out what Israel's king should be. Psalm 18, David's song, is headed "in the day that the LORD delivered him from the hand of all his enemies, and from the hand of Saul."

### New Testament

- **Acts 13:21–22.** Paul recalls that God gave Israel "Saul the son of Cis, a man of the tribe of Benjamin, by the space of forty years," then removed him and raised up David.
- **Echoes.** Paul, also named Saul, came from the same tribe of Benjamin (Acts 13:9; Philippians 3:5). Jesus warns that calling him "Lord" is not the same as doing what he says (Luke 6:46).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What reasons did Saul give for offering the sacrifice and for sparing Agag (1 Samuel 13:11–12; 15:15, 20–21)?
2. **Understand:** Samuel said "to obey is better than sacrifice." Why might religious activity be easier than obedience?
3. **Apply:** Saul's jealousy began with a song about someone else's success. Whose success is hard for you to celebrate right now?
4. **Apply:** Saul often acted under pressure instead of waiting. Where are you tempted to rush ahead this week, and what would waiting on God look like?
