# David

*Shepherd, giant-killer, king and psalm-writer, a man after God's own heart who fell hard and turned back to God.*

David was the youngest son of Jesse of Bethlehem, a shepherd boy whom no one expected to be chosen. God sent Samuel to anoint him king while Saul still sat on the throne. David killed Goliath, served in Saul's court, and then spent years on the run from Saul's jealousy. He became king first over Judah and then over all Israel, made Jerusalem his capital, and brought the ark of God there. God promised him a house and a throne that would last for ever. David was also a man of great failures, including adultery and murder, and his family suffered for them. Yet he confessed his sin honestly and threw himself on God's mercy. Many of the psalms come from his pen, and the Messiah would be called "the son of David."

## The life of David

*Dates are approximate (BC). David was 30 when he became king and reigned 40 years (2 Samuel 5:4), which places his birth c. 1040 BC. The order of some events in 2 Samuel is thematic rather than strictly in time.*

**Youth — Anointed in Bethlehem.** Samuel passes over Jesse's older sons and anoints the youngest, who is out keeping the sheep. The Spirit of the LORD comes on David from that day. He is soon called to play the harp for Saul. *1 Samuel 16:1–23*

**Youth — Faces Goliath.** Bringing food to his brothers at the valley of Elah, David hears Goliath's challenge. He goes out with a sling and five smooth stones in the name of the LORD and kills the giant. *1 Samuel 17:1–58*

**Saul's last years — A hunted man.** Saul's jealousy drives David from court. He hides in caves and wildernesses, gathers four hundred men at Adullam, spares Saul's life twice, and finally lives among the Philistines at Ziklag. *1 Samuel 18–30*

**c. 1010 BC · Age 30 — King in Hebron.** After Saul and Jonathan die on Gilboa, David mourns them. The men of Judah anoint him king in Hebron, while Saul's son Ish-bosheth rules the northern tribes. *2 Samuel 1:1–2:11; 5:4–5*

**c. 1003 BC · Age 37 — King in Jerusalem.** All the tribes anoint David king. He captures the Jebusite stronghold of Zion, makes Jerusalem his capital, and brings up the ark of God with great joy. *2 Samuel 5:1–10; 6:1–19*

**Middle of his reign — Sin and repentance.** David commits adultery with Bathsheba and arranges her husband Uriah's death. Nathan the prophet confronts him, and David confesses. Their first child dies, and Solomon is born later. *2 Samuel 11:1–12:25*

**Later years — Absalom's revolt.** Trouble grows in David's family until his son Absalom seizes the throne. David flees Jerusalem weeping. His army wins, but Absalom is killed, and David mourns him bitterly. *2 Samuel 13:1–19:8*

**c. 970 BC · Age 70 — Solomon crowned.** When his son Adonijah tries to take the throne, David has Solomon anointed king. He charges Solomon to keep God's ways, then dies and is buried in the city of David. *1 Kings 1:1–2:11*

## Key activities

*The Hebrew titles of 73 psalms include "of David." Some Christians read this as authorship in every case, and others as "for" or "in the style of" David. The New Testament names David as the speaker of several psalms (Mark 12:36; Acts 2:25; 4:25).*

1. **Guarded the flock.** As a shepherd he fought off a lion and a bear, and he trusted God to deliver him from Goliath too. *1 Samuel 17:34–37*
2. **Defeated Goliath.** He came against the giant "in the name of the LORD of hosts," so that all the earth might know there is a God in Israel. *1 Samuel 17:45–47*
3. **Made a covenant with Jonathan.** Saul's son became his closest friend and protector. *1 Samuel 18:1–4; 20:1–42*
4. **Spared the LORD's anointed.** Twice he had Saul at his mercy and refused to harm him. *1 Samuel 24:1–22; 26:1–25*
5. **Strengthened himself in God.** When Ziklag was burned and his own men talked of stoning him, "David encouraged himself in the LORD his God." *1 Samuel 30:1–20*
6. **Took Jerusalem and brought the ark.** He made Zion his capital and "danced before the LORD with all his might" as the ark came in. *2 Samuel 5:6–10; 6:12–15*
7. **Received God's covenant.** He wanted to build God a house. God promised instead to build David's house, a kingdom established for ever. *2 Samuel 7:1–29*
8. **Showed kindness to Saul's family.** He brought Jonathan's lame son Mephibosheth to eat at the king's table. *2 Samuel 9:1–13*
9. **Fell and repented.** He took Bathsheba and had Uriah killed. When confronted, he said, "I have sinned against the LORD," and prayed, "Create in me a clean heart, O God." *2 Samuel 11–12; Psalm 51:10*
10. **Grieved for a rebel son.** He wept, "O my son Absalom, my son, my son Absalom!" *2 Samuel 18:33*
11. **Bought a place for the altar.** After his census brought a plague, he refused to offer God what "doth cost me nothing." The spot became the temple site. *2 Samuel 24:1–25; 2 Chronicles 3:1*
12. **Prepared for the temple.** Not allowed to build it himself, he gathered materials and gave Solomon the plans. *1 Chronicles 22:1–19; 28:1–29:9*

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names David.*

### Old Testament

- **1 Samuel 16 – 1 Kings 2** and **1 Chronicles 11–29.** These are the two main accounts of his life and reign.
- **Psalms.** Many psalm titles tie a psalm to a moment in his life, such as Psalm 3 (fleeing Absalom) and Psalm 51 (after Nathan's visit). He is called "the sweet psalmist of Israel" (2 Samuel 23:1).
- **1 Kings 11:4; 15:5.** Later kings are measured against David, who did right "save only in the matter of Uriah the Hittite."
- **Isaiah 11:1; Jeremiah 23:5; Ezekiel 34:23–24.** The prophets promise a king from "the stem of Jesse," a righteous Branch raised up for David, and a shepherd called "my servant David."

### New Testament

- **Matthew 1:1.** "The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham."
- **Luke 1:32** and **Luke 2:4, 11.** Jesus will receive "the throne of his father David," and he is born in the city of David.
- **Mark 10:47** and **Matthew 21:9.** The blind and the crowds call Jesus "son of David."
- **Matthew 22:41–45.** Jesus asks how the Messiah can be David's son when David calls him Lord (Psalm 110:1).
- **Acts 2:29–36; 13:22, 36.** Peter and Paul preach that David died and was buried, but the one he spoke of rose again. God called David "a man after mine own heart."
- **Revelation 22:16.** Jesus says, "I am the root and the offspring of David."

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What did God look at when he chose David, and what did everyone else look at (1 Samuel 16:6–13)?
2. **Understand:** David sinned terribly, yet Scripture calls him a man after God's own heart. What does that tell you about what God values?
3. **Apply:** David wrote honest prayers in both joy and failure. What would an honest prayer from you sound like this week?
4. **Apply:** David twice refused to take a shortcut to the throne. Where are you tempted to grab something before God's time?
