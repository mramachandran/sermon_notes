# Michal

*Saul's daughter, who loved David, saved his life, and later despised him.*

Michal was the younger daughter of King Saul. Scripture says plainly that she loved David, and Saul used her love as a trap, hoping David would die earning the right to marry her. When her father's men came to kill David, Michal let him down through a window and bought him time with a trick. But while David was a fugitive, Saul gave her to another man. Years later David demanded her back as part of a political deal, and her second husband followed her weeping. The last scene is the saddest. When David danced before the ark, Michal looked down from a window and despised him. Her life shows how love can be bruised by the politics and pain around it, and how bitterness can close a heart to joy.

## The life of Michal

*Dates are approximate and rough (BC). Her story runs from Saul's court to the early years of David's reign in Jerusalem.*

**Saul's court — Loves David.** "Michal Saul's daughter loved David," and Saul is pleased. He asks as a bride-price proof that David has killed a hundred Philistines, hoping David will die. David brings double, and they marry. *1 Samuel 18:20–28*

**One night — Saves David's life.** Saul sends men to watch David's house and kill him in the morning. Michal lets David down through a window, puts an image in his bed with goats' hair at its head, and says he is sick. *1 Samuel 19:11–17*

**While David is a fugitive — Given to another man.** With David on the run in the wilderness, Saul gives Michal as wife to another man, Phalti son of Laish, of Gallim. Scripture does not record what Michal thought of it. *1 Samuel 25:44*

**David at Hebron — Taken back.** When Abner offers to bring Israel over to David, David demands Michal's return. Ish-bosheth takes her from Phaltiel, who follows weeping as far as Bahurim until Abner sends him home. *2 Samuel 3:12–16*

**In Jerusalem — Despises David.** As the ark comes into the city, Michal sees David dancing and despises him. She mocks him when he comes home, and David answers her sharply. *2 Samuel 6:16, 20–23*

### The world around them

In the ancient Near East a king's daughter was often a political gift. Marriage to her tied a family to the throne. Saul offered Merab and then Michal to David partly as a snare (1 Samuel 18:17–21). A bride-price, paid by the groom to the bride's father, was normal, though Saul's demand was not. David's claim to Michal later mattered because she linked him to Saul's house as the tribes chose a king. Her "image" was probably a household idol, called teraphim in Hebrew, like the ones Rachel stole from Laban (Genesis 31:19).

## Key activities

*2 Samuel 21:8 in the KJV mentions "the five sons of Michal the daughter of Saul" brought up for Adriel. But 1 Samuel 18:19 says Merab married Adriel, and 2 Samuel 6:23 says Michal had no child. A few Hebrew manuscripts and some Greek copies read Merab in 21:8, and most modern translations follow them. Others explain "Michal" as the aunt who raised them.*

1. **Loved David.** Scripture says twice that Michal loved David, and Saul saw it. *1 Samuel 18:20, 28*
2. **Chose her husband over her father.** She warned David, "If thou save not thy life to night, to morrow thou shalt be slain." *1 Samuel 19:11*
3. **Deceived Saul's men.** She helped David escape through a window and used an image in the bed to delay the soldiers. *1 Samuel 19:12–16*
4. **Lied to protect herself.** When Saul confronted her, she claimed David had threatened to kill her. *1 Samuel 19:17*
5. **Was passed between men.** She was given to Phalti and then taken back to David, with no word of her own recorded. *1 Samuel 25:44; 2 Samuel 3:13–16*
6. **Despised worship.** She scorned David's dancing: "How glorious was the king of Israel to day." David replied, "I will yet be more vile than thus." *2 Samuel 6:20–22*

### Words and places

- **Dowry.** The bride-price a groom paid to the bride's father (1 Samuel 18:25).
- **Image.** A household idol, in Hebrew teraphim (1 Samuel 19:13).
- **Gallim.** Phaltiel's home town, probably near Gibeah in Benjamin (1 Samuel 25:44).
- **Bahurim.** A village near Jerusalem, where Phaltiel turned back (2 Samuel 3:16).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Michal.*

### Old Testament

- **1 Samuel 14:49.** She is named as Saul's younger daughter.
- **1 Samuel 18:20–28; 19:11–17; 25:44.** Her marriage to David, her rescue of him, and her marriage to Phalti.
- **2 Samuel 3:13–16.** She is returned to David.
- **2 Samuel 6:16–23** and **1 Chronicles 15:29.** She despises David's dancing before the ark.
- **2 Samuel 21:8.** The KJV names her here, though most scholars read Merab (see the note with the key activities).
- **Echoes.** Rahab also let men down through a window to save them (Joshua 2:15). "Praise him with the timbrel and dance" (Psalm 150:4).

### New Testament

- Michal is not named in the New Testament.
- **Echoes.** A woman is criticized for pouring costly ointment on Jesus, and he defends her (Mark 14:3–9; John 12:1–8). The elder brother refuses to join the celebration for his returning brother (Luke 15:25–32).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What did Michal do for David in 1 Samuel 19, and what did she say to him in 2 Samuel 6?
2. **Understand:** What might have happened in Michal's heart between those two scenes?
3. **Apply:** Michal watched worship from a window instead of joining it. What keeps you at a distance from wholehearted worship?
4. **Apply:** Is there an old hurt that is making you critical of someone else's joy? What step toward healing could you take?
