# The Queen of Sheba

*A foreign queen who traveled far to test Solomon's wisdom, and went home praising Israel's God.*

The Queen of Sheba ruled a wealthy land far to the south of Israel. She heard of Solomon's fame "concerning the name of the LORD," and came to test him with hard questions. She arrived in Jerusalem with a great caravan of camels carrying spices, gold and precious stones. Solomon answered everything she asked. When she saw his wisdom, his house, his servants and his worship, she was overwhelmed. She confessed that "the half was not told me," and she blessed the LORD, Solomon's God. Scripture does not give her name and says nothing of her after she went home. Yet Jesus held her up as an example. She traveled a long way to hear the wisdom of Solomon, and "a greater than Solomon" had now come.

## The life of the Queen of Sheba

*Scripture gives no dates or ages for the queen. Her visit came sometime during Solomon's reign (c. 970–931 BC), after his fame had spread widely.*

**In Sheba — Hears of Solomon.** News reaches her of Solomon's fame "concerning the name of the LORD." She decides to test him for herself with hard questions. *1 Kings 10:1; 2 Chronicles 9:1*

**The journey — Arrives in Jerusalem.** She comes with a very great train of camels bearing spices, gold and precious stones. She talks with Solomon about all that is in her heart. *1 Kings 10:2*

**At the court — Tests and sees.** Solomon answers all her questions; nothing is hidden from him. She sees his wisdom, his house, his food, his servants and his offerings, and "there was no more spirit in her." *1 Kings 10:3–5*

**Before leaving — Blesses Israel's God.** She says the reports were true and "the half was not told me." She blesses the LORD for setting Solomon on Israel's throne and gives rich gifts. *1 Kings 10:6–10*

**Going home — Receives all she desires.** Solomon gives her all she desires and asks for, beyond the gifts he gives her from his royal bounty. Then she turns and goes back to her own country with her servants. *1 Kings 10:13; 2 Chronicles 9:12*

### The world around them

Most scholars place Sheba in southwest Arabia, in what is now Yemen, home of the Sabaeans. It was a rich trading kingdom that grew frankincense and myrrh and sold them north along caravan routes. The journey to Jerusalem was well over a thousand miles by camel. Some scholars think her visit was partly a trade mission, since Solomon's ships and trading posts touched the same routes (1 Kings 9:26–28; 10:15). Others connect Sheba with lands across the Red Sea in East Africa.

## Key activities

*1 Kings 10 and 2 Chronicles 9 tell the same story in almost the same words. Compare them side by side.*

1. **Sought the truth for herself.** She did not rest on reports but came to see and hear. *1 Kings 10:1, 6–7*
2. **Asked hard questions.** She came "to prove him with hard questions," and spoke with him of all that was in her heart. *1 Kings 10:1–3*
3. **Observed carefully.** She took in his wisdom, his house, his table, his servants and his worship at the house of the LORD. *1 Kings 10:4–5*
4. **Admitted she was wrong.** "I believed not the words, until I came, and mine eyes had seen it." *1 Kings 10:7*
5. **Praised God.** "Blessed be the LORD thy God, which delighted in thee, to set thee on the throne of Israel." *1 Kings 10:9*
6. **Gave generously.** She gave a hundred and twenty talents of gold and more spices than ever came again. *1 Kings 10:10*

*Tradition.* Ethiopian tradition calls her Makeda and says she bore Solomon a son, Menelik, founder of Ethiopia's royal line. Arabic tradition calls her Bilqis. The Bible gives her no name and records no marriage or child.

### Words and places

- **Sheba.** A land in the far south, known for gold, spices and incense (Psalm 72:10, 15; Jeremiah 6:20).
- **Hard questions.** Riddles or difficult sayings, a common test of wisdom in the ancient world (1 Kings 10:1).
- **Talent.** A large weight of about 75 pounds (about 34 kilograms). Her gift was roughly 9,000 pounds (about 4,000 kilograms) of gold (1 Kings 10:10).
- **Queen of the south.** Jesus' name for her (Matthew 12:42).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names the queen.*

### Old Testament

- **1 Kings 10:1–13** and **2 Chronicles 9:1–12.** These are the two accounts of her visit.
- **Psalm 72:10, 15.** "The kings of Sheba and Seba shall offer gifts," and gold of Sheba is given to the king.
- **Isaiah 60:6.** "All they from Sheba shall come: they shall bring gold and incense."
- **Echoes.** Other people from outside Israel who come to honor Israel's God include Ruth and Naaman (Ruth 1:16; 2 Kings 5:15).

### New Testament

- **Matthew 12:42** and **Luke 11:31.** "The queen of the south shall rise up in the judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: for she came from the uttermost parts of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon; and, behold, a greater than Solomon is here." This is Matthew's wording; Luke's is almost the same.
- **Echoes.** Wise men from the east bring gold, frankincense and myrrh to the child Jesus (Matthew 2:11). An Ethiopian official travels to Jerusalem to worship and hears the gospel on the way home (Acts 8:27–39).
