# Rehoboam

*Solomon's son, who took the wrong advice and lost ten tribes in a single day.*

Rehoboam was the son of Solomon and the first king of Judah after the kingdom divided. He came to the throne at forty-one, heir to the richest court Israel had ever known. The northern tribes came to him with one request: lighten the heavy load his father had laid on them. He listened to the young men he had grown up with instead of his father's older advisers, and he answered harshly. Ten tribes walked away and made Jeroboam I their king. Rehoboam spent the rest of his seventeen-year reign fortifying a smaller kingdom, drifting from God, and paying tribute to an Egyptian invader. Chronicles adds that he humbled himself when judgment came, and God eased his hand.

## The life of Rehoboam

*Dates are approximate (BC) and follow the house chronology for the divided kingdom. Years are counted from the start of his reign.*

**c. 931 BC · Age 41 — Goes to Shechem.** After Solomon's death, all Israel gathers at Shechem to make Rehoboam king. Jeroboam, back from Egypt, speaks for the tribes: ease the heavy yoke, and we will serve you. *1 Kings 11:43–12:5*

**c. 931 BC · Three days later — Rejects the elders.** The old men who served Solomon urge him to serve the people. His young friends urge him to be harsher still. He takes their advice and threatens to chastise the tribes with scorpions. *1 Kings 12:6–15*

**c. 931 BC · Soon after — The kingdom splits.** The northern tribes reject David's house. They stone Adoram, his taskmaster, and Rehoboam flees to Jerusalem by chariot. He musters 180,000 men for war, but Shemaiah tells him to stand down, and he obeys. *1 Kings 12:16–24*

**c. 931–928 BC · Years 1–3 — Strengthens Judah.** He fortifies cities across Judah and Benjamin. Priests and Levites from the north move south, and for three years the kingdom walks in the way of David and Solomon. *2 Chronicles 11:5–17*

**c. 926 BC · 5th year — Shishak invades.** Once secure, Rehoboam and Judah forsake God's law. Shishak of Egypt takes the fortified cities and strips the temple and palace, including Solomon's gold shields. The leaders humble themselves, and God spares Jerusalem. *1 Kings 14:25–28; 2 Chronicles 12:1–12*

**c. 913 BC · Age 58 — Dies.** After seventeen years marked by constant war with Jeroboam, he is buried in the city of David. His son Abijah reigns in his place. *1 Kings 14:29–31; 2 Chronicles 12:13–16*

### The world around them

The tribes had never been fully one. David first reigned over Judah alone at Hebron, and the northern cry "to your tents, O Israel" had been heard once before, in Sheba's revolt (2 Samuel 20:1). Solomon's building projects relied on forced labour and heavy taxes (1 Kings 5:13–14; 12:4). Shishak is usually identified with Shoshenq I, founder of a new Egyptian dynasty. A victory relief he carved at the temple of Karnak lists many towns in the land that he claimed to have taken.

## Key activities

*Kings tells the story of the split and Judah's sin. Chronicles adds his building, his large family, and his repentance when Shishak came.*

1. **Went to Shechem to be crowned.** He went north to Shechem, the old meeting place of the tribes, where all Israel had gathered to make him king. *1 Kings 12:1*
2. **Asked for time and advice.** He sent the people away for three days and consulted both the old men and the young men. *1 Kings 12:5–11*
3. **Answered roughly.** He told the people, "my father made your yoke heavy, and I will add to your yoke." Ten tribes rebelled that day. *1 Kings 12:12–16*
4. **Obeyed the word to stand down.** When Shemaiah said, "this thing is from me," Rehoboam and his army went home instead of fighting. *1 Kings 12:21–24; 2 Chronicles 11:1–4*
5. **Built a line of fortresses.** He fortified Bethlehem, Lachish, Hebron and other towns, and stocked them with food, oil, wine, shields and spears. *2 Chronicles 11:5–12*
6. **Welcomed exiles from the north.** Priests, Levites and faithful people who rejected Jeroboam's calves came to Jerusalem to worship. *2 Chronicles 11:13–17*
7. **Built a large household.** He had eighteen wives and sixty concubines. He loved Maachah, Absalom's daughter, most and chose her son Abijah as heir. *2 Chronicles 11:18–23*
8. **Forsook the law.** Once strong, he and all Israel with him forsook God's law. Judah built high places and images on every high hill. *1 Kings 14:22–24; 2 Chronicles 12:1*
9. **Humbled himself.** When Shemaiah announced judgment through Shishak, the king and princes said, "The LORD is righteous." God granted them some deliverance. *2 Chronicles 12:5–7, 12*
10. **Replaced gold with brass.** He made brass shields to replace the gold ones Shishak had carried off. *1 Kings 14:26–28*

### Words and places

- **Shechem.** An old city in the hill country of Ephraim, where Joshua renewed the covenant (Joshua 24:1; 1 Kings 12:1).
- **Scorpions.** Probably a harsher kind of whip, perhaps with barbs or spikes. The exact meaning is uncertain (1 Kings 12:11).
- **Adoram.** The officer over the forced labour, probably the same man as Adoniram under Solomon (1 Kings 4:6; 12:18).
- **High places.** Hilltop shrines, often linked with idols, that Judah's kings were repeatedly judged for keeping (1 Kings 14:23).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Rehoboam.*

### Old Testament

- **1 Kings 11:29–39.** Ahijah foretells that ten tribes will be torn from Solomon's son, while one tribe stays for David's sake.
- **1 Kings 12:1–24; 14:21–31** and **2 Chronicles 10–12.** These are the two main accounts of his reign.
- **1 Kings 12:15.** The narrator says "the cause was from the LORD," fulfilling Ahijah's word.
- **1 Kings 15:6.** There was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam all their days.
- **2 Chronicles 13:7.** His son Abijah later says Rehoboam was "young and tenderhearted" when worthless men gathered against him.
- **Echoes.** "A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger" (Proverbs 15:1). Solomon wondered whether his heir would be "a wise man or a fool" (Ecclesiastes 2:18–19).

### New Testament

- **Matthew 1:7.** "Solomon begat Roboam; and Roboam begat Abia." He stands in the royal line of Jesus.
- **Echoes.** Jesus teaches that whoever would be great must be the servant of all (Mark 10:42–44). The elders' advice to Rehoboam was much the same (1 Kings 12:7).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What did the older men advise, and what did the young men advise (1 Kings 12:6–11)?
2. **Understand:** The text says the split was Rehoboam's choice and also "from the LORD" (1 Kings 12:15, 24). How can both be true?
3. **Apply:** Whose advice do you tend to seek when you face a hard decision? Do they ever tell you what you would rather not hear?
4. **Apply:** Rehoboam drifted from God once he felt strong (2 Chronicles 12:1). Where does success make you less dependent on God, and what is one habit that would keep you close?
