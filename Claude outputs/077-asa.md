# Asa

*A reforming king who trusted God against a vast army, then leaned on men in his later years.*

Asa was the third king of Judah after the kingdom divided, the great-grandson of Solomon. He reigned for forty-one years, one of the longest reigns in Judah. He began well. He tore down idols, removed his own grandmother from her royal position because of the idol she had made, and led Judah to renew its covenant with God. When a huge army from Cush marched against him, he prayed a short, honest prayer and saw God give the victory. But late in his reign, facing a smaller threat from Israel, he paid a pagan king for help instead. When a prophet rebuked him, Asa threw him in prison. His life asks whether faith that starts strong will also finish strong.

## The life of Asa

*Dates are approximate (BC) and follow the house chronology. Years are counted from the start of his reign. Chronicles' "thirty-sixth year" (2 Chronicles 16:1) is a known puzzle; see the next page.*

**c. 911 BC · Year 1 — Becomes king.** After his father Abijah's short reign, Asa takes the throne of Judah. He does what is right in God's eyes, as David his father did. *1 Kings 15:8–11; 2 Chronicles 14:1–2*

**c. 911–901 BC · Years 1–10 — Reform and rest.** He removes foreign altars, images and groves, and commands Judah to seek God. The land is quiet for ten years, and he builds fortified cities. *2 Chronicles 14:1–7*

**Before his 15th year — Victory over Zerah.** Zerah the Ethiopian comes against Judah with a huge army. Asa cries to God at Mareshah, and Judah routs the invaders and carries off great spoil. *2 Chronicles 14:9–15*

**c. 896 BC · 15th year — Covenant renewed.** Stirred by the prophet Azariah, Asa removes more idols and gathers Judah and many from the north. They swear with shouting to seek God with all their heart. *2 Chronicles 15:1–15*

**Later in his reign — Buys help from Syria.** When Baasha of Israel fortifies Ramah on Judah's border, Asa sends temple silver and gold to Ben-hadad of Syria. Ben-hadad attacks the north, and Baasha withdraws. *1 Kings 15:16–22; 2 Chronicles 16:1–6*

**Soon after — Rebuked by Hanani.** The seer Hanani says he trusted Syria instead of God. Asa is angry, puts the seer in prison, and oppresses some of the people. *2 Chronicles 16:7–10*

**c. 872–870 BC · Years 39–41 — Disease and death.** His feet become severely diseased, yet he seeks physicians rather than God. He dies in his forty-first year and is buried with great honour. *1 Kings 15:23–24; 2 Chronicles 16:12–14*

### The world around them

Asa's small kingdom sat between powerful neighbours. To the north, Israel and Judah fought over the border land of Benjamin, only a few miles from Jerusalem. Ramah controlled the main road north, which is why Baasha fortified it. To the northeast, the Aramean kingdom of Syria, ruled from Damascus, was rising. To the south lay Egypt. The "Ethiopians" of Chronicles are the people of Cush, the land south of Egypt. Who Zerah was, and exactly where his army came from, is debated.

## Key activities

*2 Chronicles 16:1 dates Baasha's move to Asa's 36th year, but 1 Kings 16:8 says Baasha died in Asa's 26th year. Some scholars count the 36 years from the division of the kingdom, and others suspect a copying error.*

1. **Cleared away idols.** He took away foreign altars, high places, images and groves, and told Judah to seek the God of their fathers. *2 Chronicles 14:2–5*
2. **Built in peace.** He used the quiet years to fortify cities with walls, towers, gates and bars. *2 Chronicles 14:6–7*
3. **Prayed before battle.** Facing Zerah's army, he prayed, "help us, O LORD our God; for we rest on thee." *2 Chronicles 14:11*
4. **Listened to a prophet.** Azariah told him, "The LORD is with you, while ye be with him." Asa took courage and acted. *2 Chronicles 15:1–8*
5. **Removed his grandmother.** He deposed Maachah as queen mother because of her idol in a grove. He cut it down and burned it by the brook Kidron. *1 Kings 15:13; 2 Chronicles 15:16*
6. **Led a covenant renewal.** Judah and settlers from Ephraim, Manasseh and Simeon gathered in Jerusalem, sacrificed, and swore to seek God. *2 Chronicles 15:9–15*
7. **Bribed a foreign king.** He sent treasure from the temple and palace to Ben-hadad to break Syria's treaty with Baasha. *1 Kings 15:18–20; 2 Chronicles 16:2–4*
8. **Imprisoned the messenger.** When Hanani rebuked him, Asa raged and put him in the prison house. *2 Chronicles 16:7–10*
9. **Sought physicians, not God.** In his final illness he looked only to doctors for help. *2 Chronicles 16:12*

### Words and places

- **Queen mother.** A powerful royal office in Judah, often held by the king's mother or grandmother (1 Kings 15:13).
- **Grove.** The KJV word for an Asherah, a wooden pole or image of a Canaanite goddess (1 Kings 15:13).
- **Mareshah.** A town in the lowlands of Judah, southwest of Jerusalem (2 Chronicles 14:9–10).
- **Ramah.** A town about five miles north of Jerusalem, on the road into Judah (1 Kings 15:17).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Asa.*

### Old Testament

- **1 Kings 15:8–24** and **2 Chronicles 14–16.** These are the two accounts of his reign. Chronicles gives much more detail.
- **1 Kings 15:14.** Although the high places remained, "Asa's heart was perfect with the LORD all his days."
- **1 Kings 22:43** and **2 Chronicles 20:32.** His son Jehoshaphat walks in the ways of Asa his father.
- **Jeremiah 41:9.** The pit Asa dug when fortifying against Baasha is still known in Jeremiah's day.
- **Echoes.** "Some trust in chariots, and some in horses: but we will remember the name of the LORD our God" (Psalm 20:7). "Cursed be the man that trusteth in man" (Jeremiah 17:5).

### New Testament

- **Matthew 1:7–8.** "Abia begat Asa," and "Asa begat Josaphat." He stands in the royal line of Jesus.
- **Echoes.** "He which hath begun a good work in you will perform it" (Philippians 1:6). Near the end, Paul could say, "I have finished my course" (2 Timothy 4:7).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** Compare Asa's prayer in 2 Chronicles 14:11 with his actions in 2 Chronicles 16:1–4. What changed?
2. **Understand:** Hanani says God looks for people "whose heart is perfect toward him" (2 Chronicles 16:9). What does that mean, since Asa was far from sinless?
3. **Apply:** Asa trusted God in a crisis but not in a smaller, more manageable problem. Where do you rely on your own resources because the problem seems small?
4. **Apply:** Asa reacted angrily to correction. Who has permission to tell you hard truths, and how do you usually respond?
