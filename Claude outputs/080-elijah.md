# Elijah

*The prophet of fire who stood alone against Baal, then met God in a still small voice.*

Elijah burst onto the scene in the days of Ahab and Jezebel, when Baal worship had become the religion of Israel's court. He came from Gilead, east of the Jordan, and Scripture tells us nothing of his family. He announced a drought, was fed by ravens and a foreign widow, and called down fire on Mount Carmel. Then, worn out and afraid, he asked God to let him die. God fed him, met him at Horeb, and sent him back to work. Scripture records no death for Elijah. He was taken up to heaven in a whirlwind. Centuries later the prophets promised he would return. The New Testament sees that promise in John the Baptist, and shows Elijah himself speaking with Jesus on the mountain of transfiguration.

## The life of Elijah

*Dates are approximate (BC) and follow the house chronology. Kings does not date most of these events, so they appear in the order the text tells them.*

**c. 860s BC — Announces the drought.** Elijah appears before Ahab and declares, in the name of the living God, that there will be no dew or rain except at his word. Then God sends him into hiding. *1 Kings 17:1*

**During the drought — Cherith and Zarephath.** Ravens bring him bread and meat by the brook Cherith until it dries up. Then a widow in Zarephath shares her last meal, her flour and oil never run out, and her son is raised. *1 Kings 17:2–24*

**Third year — Mount Carmel.** Elijah challenges 450 prophets of Baal. They cry out all day with no answer. He soaks his altar with water and prays, and fire falls from the LORD. The people cry, "The LORD, he is the God." *1 Kings 18:1–40*

**The same day — Rain.** On Carmel's top he prays while his servant looks out to sea seven times, until a cloud like a man's hand appears. A great rain follows, and Elijah runs ahead of Ahab's chariot all the way to Jezreel. *1 Kings 18:41–46*

**Soon after — Flight to Horeb.** Jezebel's threat sends him into the wilderness, where he asks to die. An angel feeds him twice. He travels forty days to Horeb, where God speaks in a still small voice and gives him new work. *1 Kings 19:1–18*

**On the way back — Calls Elisha.** He finds Elisha ploughing with twelve yoke of oxen and throws his mantle over him. Elisha leaves everything to follow and serve him. *1 Kings 19:19–21*

**Later in Ahab's reign — Confronts Ahab.** After Naboth's murder, God sends Elijah to meet Ahab in the stolen vineyard with a word of judgment on him and on Jezebel. *1 Kings 21:17–29*

**c. 852–850 BC — Final acts.** He rebukes King Ahaziah for consulting Baal-zebub, and fire falls on soldiers sent to seize him. Then, crossing the Jordan with Elisha, he is taken up by a whirlwind into heaven. *2 Kings 1:1–2:12*

## Key activities

*Elijah's story has two sides: bold public victories and private exhaustion. Both are told honestly. 2 Chronicles 21:12–15 records a letter from Elijah to Jehoram of Judah; how this fits the timing of his departure is debated.*

1. **Stood before a king.** He spoke God's word to Ahab plainly: "As the LORD God of Israel liveth, before whom I stand." *1 Kings 17:1*
2. **Lived by daily provision.** He drank from a brook and ate what ravens brought, then lived on a widow's handful of flour. *1 Kings 17:2–16*
3. **Prayed for a dead boy.** He carried the widow's son upstairs, stretched himself on him three times, and cried to God. The child revived. *1 Kings 17:17–24*
4. **Challenged a divided people.** On Carmel he asked, "How long halt ye between two opinions?" *1 Kings 18:21*
5. **Mocked Baal's silence.** At noon he taunted the prophets of Baal: perhaps their god was talking, travelling or asleep. *1 Kings 18:26–29*
6. **Rebuilt the LORD's altar.** He repaired the broken altar with twelve stones, one for each tribe, and prayed a short prayer. *1 Kings 18:30–38*
7. **Prayed for rain.** He bowed down with his face between his knees and sent his servant to look seven times. *1 Kings 18:42–44*
8. **Asked to die.** Under a juniper tree he said, "It is enough; now, O LORD, take away my life." *1 Kings 19:4*
9. **Heard the still small voice.** At Horeb God was not in the wind, the earthquake or the fire, but in "a still small voice." *1 Kings 19:11–13*
10. **Passed on the mantle.** He called Elisha and later asked what he could do for him before he was taken away. *1 Kings 19:19–21; 2 Kings 2:9–10*
11. **Confronted injustice.** He told Ahab that dogs would lick his blood where they had licked Naboth's. *1 Kings 21:17–24*
12. **Was taken up.** A chariot of fire and horses of fire parted him from Elisha, and he went up by a whirlwind into heaven. *2 Kings 2:11*

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Elijah.*

### Old Testament

- **1 Kings 17:1 – 2 Kings 2:12.** This is the main account of his ministry.
- **2 Chronicles 21:12–15.** A letter from Elijah warns King Jehoram of Judah.
- **Malachi 4:5–6.** "Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the coming of the great and dreadful day of the LORD."
- **Echoes.** Like Moses, Elijah spends forty days and forty nights with God's provision and meets God at Horeb, the mountain of God (1 Kings 19:8; Exodus 24:18; 34:28). Elisha later repeats several of his miracles (2 Kings 2:13–14; 4:18–37).

### New Testament

- **Luke 1:17** and **Matthew 11:14; 17:10–13.** John the Baptist comes "in the spirit and power of Elias." Jesus says John is the Elijah who was to come.
- **John 1:21.** Asked, "Art thou Elias?" John himself answers, "I am not."
- **Matthew 17:1–4.** Elijah appears with Moses at the transfiguration, talking with Jesus.
- **Luke 4:25–26.** Jesus points to Elijah's visit to a foreign widow.
- **Romans 11:2–5.** Paul cites the seven thousand who had not bowed to Baal.
- **James 5:17–18.** "Elias was a man subject to like passions as we are," yet his prayers shut and opened the heavens.
- **Matthew 27:46–49.** Bystanders at the cross think Jesus is calling for Elijah.

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What did God give Elijah under the juniper tree before he gave him any new instructions (1 Kings 19:5–8)?
2. **Understand:** Why do you think God spoke in a still small voice rather than in wind, earthquake or fire (1 Kings 19:11–13)?
3. **Apply:** Elijah crashed right after his greatest victory. When have you felt low after a high point? What helps you recover?
4. **Apply:** Elijah thought he was the only faithful one left, but there were seven thousand (1 Kings 19:14, 18). Who could you reach out to this week so neither of you feels alone?
