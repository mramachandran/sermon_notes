# The widow of Zarephath

*A poor foreign widow who shared her last meal and saw God keep her alive.*

The widow of Zarephath lived in the country of Sidon, the homeland of Queen Jezebel and a centre of Baal worship. During the great drought of Ahab's reign she was down to her last handful of flour. She was gathering sticks to cook one final meal for herself and her son. Then Elijah, a stranger and an Israelite, asked her to feed him first. She did, and her flour and oil lasted until the rain came. Later, when her son died, Elijah prayed and the boy lived again. Scripture never tells us her name. Yet Jesus chose her story to show that God's mercy reaches beyond Israel, and his hometown was furious.

## The life of the widow of Zarephath

*Dates are approximate. The drought falls in Ahab's reign (c. 874–853 BC), probably in the 860s BC. Kings gives no exact year.*

**During the drought — God's command.** When the brook Cherith dries up, God sends Elijah to Zarephath. He says he has "commanded a widow woman there to sustain thee." She does not yet know it. *1 Kings 17:7–9*

**At the city gate — Meets Elijah.** She is gathering sticks when a stranger asks for water and a piece of bread. She has only a handful of flour and a little oil, and she expects to eat it with her son and then die. *1 Kings 17:10–12*

**That day — Bakes for the prophet first.** Elijah tells her not to fear. She should make him a small cake first, trusting God's promise that her flour and oil will last until the rain. She does as he says. *1 Kings 17:13–15*

**Many days — The jar does not run out.** Through the long drought the flour and oil keep lasting. They feed her, her household and Elijah, "according to the word of the LORD, which he spake by Elijah." *1 Kings 17:15–16*

**Later — Her son dies.** Her son falls sick until there is no breath left in him. In her grief she asks whether the prophet has come to call her sin to remembrance and slay her son. *1 Kings 17:17–18*

**The same day — Her son is raised.** Elijah carries the boy to his upstairs room, stretches himself on him three times, and cries to God. The child revives, and she confesses that Elijah is a man of God. *1 Kings 17:19–24*

### The world around them

Zarephath stood on the Mediterranean coast between Tyre and Sidon, in Phoenicia. This was Jezebel's homeland and the heart of Baal worship. Baal was believed to send rain, so a drought there was a direct challenge to his claims. Widows in the ancient world often had no land and no one to provide for them. With a child to feed and no harvest, a widow in a famine was among the most helpless people in society.

## Key activities

*She was a Gentile living in Baal's own country. Yet she speaks of "the LORD thy God" (1 Kings 17:12) and acts on the word of his prophet.*

1. **Helped a stranger.** When Elijah asked for water, she went to fetch it, though water was scarce. *1 Kings 17:10–11*
2. **Told the truth about her need.** She did not pretend. She had "not a cake, but an handful of meal in a barrel, and a little oil in a cruse." *1 Kings 17:12*
3. **Gave first.** She made Elijah's cake before her own, trusting a promise she could not yet see. *1 Kings 17:13–15*
4. **Opened her home.** Elijah stayed in an upstairs room of her house for many days. *1 Kings 17:15, 19*
5. **Brought her grief to the prophet.** When her son died, she poured out her grief and fear honestly to Elijah. *1 Kings 17:17–18*
6. **Confessed her faith.** Receiving her son alive, she declared that the word of the LORD in Elijah's mouth was truth. *1 Kings 17:23–24*

*Tradition.* Some Jewish traditions identify her son as the prophet Jonah son of Amittai. Scripture does not say so.

### Words and places

- **Zarephath.** A Phoenician town between Tyre and Sidon, called Sarepta in the New Testament (1 Kings 17:9; Luke 4:26).
- **Barrel and cruse.** A large storage jar for flour and a small jug for oil (1 Kings 17:12).
- **Loft.** An upper room built on the roof of a house (1 Kings 17:19).
- **Man of God.** A common Old Testament title for a prophet (1 Kings 17:18, 24).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names the widow.*

### Old Testament

- **1 Kings 17:8–24.** This is the only account of her life.
- **Obadiah 1:20.** Zarephath appears as a northern boundary in a promise about Israel's return.
- **Echoes.** Elisha later multiplies a widow's oil and raises a mother's son (2 Kings 4:1–7, 18–37). God "doth execute the judgment of the fatherless and widow, and loveth the stranger" (Deuteronomy 10:18).

### New Testament

- **Luke 4:25–26.** Jesus says there were many widows in Israel during the drought, "But unto none of them was Elias sent, save unto Sarepta, a city of Sidon, unto a woman that was a widow."
- **Luke 4:28–29.** The synagogue in Nazareth is filled with anger and tries to throw Jesus off a cliff.
- **Echoes.** Jesus raises the only son of a widow at Nain (Luke 7:11–17). He praises a poor widow who gave "all her living" (Mark 12:41–44). "Women received their dead raised to life again" (Hebrews 11:35).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What exactly did Elijah ask the widow to do, and what did he promise (1 Kings 17:13–14)?
2. **Understand:** Jesus used her story in his hometown sermon (Luke 4:25–29). Why would a foreign widow be such a pointed example?
3. **Apply:** She gave from almost nothing. What is one thing, however small, that you could give this week before you feel you have enough?
4. **Apply:** In her grief she blamed herself and the prophet (1 Kings 17:18). How do you tend to react when trouble comes even after you have obeyed God?
