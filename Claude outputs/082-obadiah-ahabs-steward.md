# Obadiah, Ahab's steward

*A quiet believer in a wicked court who hid a hundred prophets under the queen's nose.*

Obadiah ran the royal household of King Ahab. He held one of the highest offices in Israel, in a court where Jezebel was killing the LORD's prophets. Yet Scripture says he "feared the LORD greatly." While the queen hunted the prophets, Obadiah hid a hundred of them in caves and kept them alive with bread and water through a famine. He appears in only one chapter. His meeting with Elijah on the road is honest and very human: he is glad, afraid and a little defensive all at once. Obadiah is a picture of faith lived out under pressure. He did not resign in protest, but he did not bow to Baal either.

## The life of Obadiah, Ahab's steward

*Dates are approximate. The drought falls in Ahab's reign (c. 874–853 BC), probably in the 860s BC. Kings gives no exact year.*

**Ahab's reign — Serves at court.** Obadiah is governor over King Ahab's house, a senior royal official. Yet Scripture says he feared the LORD greatly and had done so since he was young. *1 Kings 18:3, 12*

**When Jezebel strikes — Hides a hundred prophets.** As Jezebel cuts off the prophets of the LORD, Obadiah takes a hundred of them. He hides them by fifty in a cave and feeds them with bread and water. *1 Kings 18:4, 13*

**Third year of drought — Searches for grass.** The famine in Samaria is severe. Ahab and Obadiah divide the land between them and go looking for springs and grass to keep the horses and mules alive. *1 Kings 18:1–6*

**On the road — Meets Elijah.** Elijah suddenly stands before him. Obadiah recognizes him, falls on his face, and is told to go and tell Ahab, "Behold, Elijah is here." *1 Kings 18:7–8*

**The same hour — Pleads his fear.** Obadiah fears that the Spirit will carry Elijah away and Ahab will kill the messenger. He reminds Elijah how he saved the prophets. Elijah swears to appear before Ahab that day. *1 Kings 18:9–15*

**That day — Brings Ahab.** Obadiah goes to Ahab and tells him, and Ahab goes to meet Elijah. The meeting leads straight to the contest on Mount Carmel. *1 Kings 18:16–20*

### The world around them

The man "over the house" was the palace steward, one of the highest officials in the kingdom. Solomon had one (1 Kings 4:6), and so did later kings of Judah (Isaiah 22:15). Such a man managed the king's household, lands and supplies, which may explain how Obadiah could feed a hundred people in a famine. The limestone hills of the region are full of caves. Horses and mules mattered because chariots were the heart of a king's army.

## Key activities

*Scripture never says whether Ahab or Jezebel knew of Obadiah's faith. The text simply sets his fear of God beside his high office and lets both stand.*

1. **Feared God in a hostile court.** He served a king who worshipped Baal, yet he kept faith with the LORD. *1 Kings 18:3*
2. **Hid a hundred prophets.** At great personal risk, he hid them in caves while Jezebel was killing the LORD's prophets. *1 Kings 18:4*
3. **Fed them through a famine.** He kept them supplied with bread and water when food and water were scarce. *1 Kings 18:4, 13*
4. **Did his job faithfully.** He went out on the king's errand to search for grass and water. *1 Kings 18:5–6*
5. **Honoured the prophet.** When he met Elijah, he fell on his face and called him "my lord Elijah." *1 Kings 18:7*
6. **Spoke his fear honestly.** He told Elijah plainly that the errand could cost him his life. *1 Kings 18:9–14*
7. **Carried the message.** Once Elijah promised to appear, Obadiah went and told Ahab. *1 Kings 18:15–16*

*Tradition.* The Talmud identifies him with the prophet who wrote the book of Obadiah, but most scholars think they were different men. The historian Josephus says the prophet's widow in 2 Kings 4:1 was Obadiah's wife. Scripture does not name her husband.

### Words and places

- **Governor of the house.** The royal steward, who ran the palace and the king's estates (1 Kings 4:6; 18:3).
- **Obadiah.** A common name. About a dozen different men in the Old Testament are called Obadiah (1 Chronicles 3:21; Ezra 8:9).
- **Carried by the Spirit.** Some people believed God's Spirit might suddenly carry Elijah elsewhere (1 Kings 18:12; 2 Kings 2:16).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names this Obadiah.*

### Old Testament

- **1 Kings 18:1–16.** This is the only account of his life.
- **1 Kings 19:18.** God tells Elijah that seven thousand in Israel have not bowed to Baal. Elijah felt alone, but Obadiah and the prophets he hid show that he never was.
- **Echoes.** Joseph ran Pharaoh's house (Genesis 41:40). Rahab hid the spies at great risk (Joshua 2:1–6). Daniel served pagan kings without bowing to their gods (Daniel 6).

### New Testament

- Obadiah is not named in the New Testament.
- **Echoes.** Hebrews speaks of God's people wandering "in dens and caves of the earth" (Hebrews 11:38). Paul sends greetings from believers "of Caesar's household" (Philippians 4:22). Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus were leaders among their people who quietly honoured Jesus (Luke 23:50–51; John 19:38–39).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** How did Obadiah describe himself and his past to Elijah (1 Kings 18:12–13)?
2. **Understand:** Obadiah served Ahab and also protected God's prophets. Was he compromising or being wise? What in the text helps you decide?
3. **Apply:** Obadiah worked inside a system run by people who opposed God. Where do you live or work under leaders who do not share your faith? What does faithfulness look like there?
4. **Apply:** Obadiah's courage was quiet and practical: bread and water, day after day. Who needs quiet, practical help from you this week?
