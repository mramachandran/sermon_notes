# Elisha

*Elijah's successor, a prophet of practical mercy who saw the unseen armies of God.*

Elisha was a farmer's son ploughing a field when Elijah threw a prophet's mantle over him. He left home, served Elijah, and asked for a double portion of his spirit. For more than fifty years he was God's prophet in the northern kingdom, through the reigns of several kings. Where Elijah's ministry was marked by fire and confrontation, Elisha's was often marked by help for ordinary people. He purified a spring, filled a widow's jars with oil, raised a boy to life, fed a crowd, healed a Syrian general, and made an iron axe head float. He also anointed kings and advised armies. Even after his death, a dead man who touched his bones came back to life.

## The life of Elisha

*Dates are approximate (BC) and follow the house chronology. Many of Elisha's stories in 2 Kings 4–8 are not dated, and they may not be told in time order.*

**c. 850s BC — Called from the plough.** Elijah finds him ploughing with twelve yoke of oxen and throws his mantle over him. Elisha kills the oxen, feeds the people, and follows Elijah as his servant. *1 Kings 19:16, 19–21*

**c. 850 BC — Receives the mantle.** Elisha refuses to leave Elijah's side. He asks for a double portion of his spirit, sees him taken up, and parts the Jordan with Elijah's fallen mantle. *2 Kings 2:1–15*

**Early ministry — Signs at Jericho and Bethel.** He heals Jericho's bad spring with salt. On the road to Bethel, youths mock him, he curses them in God's name, and two bears maul forty-two of them. *2 Kings 2:19–25*

**Probably under Joram — Mercy for ordinary people.** He multiplies a widow's oil, gives the Shunammite a son and later raises him, makes poisoned stew safe, and feeds a hundred men with twenty loaves. *2 Kings 4:1–44*

**Probably under Joram — Naaman healed.** A Syrian general with leprosy is sent to wash seven times in the Jordan. He is healed and confesses Israel's God. Gehazi's greed brings leprosy on himself. *2 Kings 5:1–27*

**Syrian wars — Dothan and Samaria.** Syria's army surrounds Dothan, but Elisha's servant sees the mountain full of horses and chariots of fire. Later, during a terrible siege of Samaria, Elisha promises food by the next day. *2 Kings 6:8–7:20*

**c. 842–841 BC — Hazael and Jehu.** In Damascus he weeps as he tells Hazael he will be king and will do great harm to Israel. Then he sends a young prophet to anoint Jehu king of Israel. *2 Kings 8:7–15; 9:1–10*

**c. 795 BC — Death and burial.** Dying, he has King Joash shoot arrows, promising victories over Syria. After his burial, a dead man thrown into his tomb touches his bones and revives. *2 Kings 13:14–21*

## Key activities

*Scholars debate whether "little children" in 2 Kings 2:23 were children or young men, since the Hebrew words can cover both. The account itself is brief and hard, and readers differ on how to understand it.*

1. **Left everything to follow.** He burned his ploughing equipment, cooked the oxen, and went after Elijah. *1 Kings 19:19–21*
2. **Asked for a double portion.** When Elijah offered him a request, he said, "let a double portion of thy spirit be upon me." *2 Kings 2:9–10*
3. **Took up the mantle.** He struck the Jordan and asked, "Where is the LORD God of Elijah?" The waters parted. *2 Kings 2:13–14*
4. **Healed bad water.** He threw salt into Jericho's spring, and the water became wholesome. *2 Kings 2:19–22*
5. **Filled a widow's jars.** A prophet's widow faced losing her sons to a creditor. Her little oil filled every jar she could borrow. *2 Kings 4:1–7*
6. **Raised a dead boy.** He prayed and stretched himself on the Shunammite's son, and the child sneezed seven times and opened his eyes. *2 Kings 4:32–37*
7. **Fed a hungry crowd.** He had twenty barley loaves set before a hundred men, and they ate and had some left. *2 Kings 4:42–44*
8. **Refused payment.** He would take no gift from Naaman, and he confronted Gehazi for secretly taking one. *2 Kings 5:15–27*
9. **Made iron float.** When a borrowed axe head fell into the Jordan, he threw in a stick and the iron swam. *2 Kings 6:1–7*
10. **Opened his servant's eyes.** He prayed, "LORD, I pray thee, open his eyes, that he may see," and the mountain was full of chariots of fire. *2 Kings 6:15–17*
11. **Fed his enemies.** He led blinded Syrian raiders into Samaria, then told the king to feed them and send them home. *2 Kings 6:18–23*
12. **Wept over Israel's future.** He fixed his gaze on Hazael and wept, knowing the evil Hazael would do. *2 Kings 8:11–13*

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Elisha.*

### Old Testament

- **1 Kings 19:16–21** and **2 Kings 2:1–13:21.** These are the main accounts of his life.
- **1 Kings 19:16.** God tells Elijah to anoint Elisha "to be prophet in thy room."
- **2 Kings 8:4–5.** The king asks Gehazi to tell "all the great things that Elisha hath done."
- **2 Kings 13:14.** King Joash weeps over him with the words once spoken of Elijah: "O my father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof."
- **Echoes.** Like Joshua after Moses, Elisha crosses the Jordan on dry ground as a leader takes up his work (Joshua 3:14–17). Psalm 34:7 speaks of the angel of the LORD camping round those who fear him.

### New Testament

- **Luke 4:27.** "And many lepers were in Israel in the time of Eliseus the prophet; and none of them was cleansed, saving Naaman the Syrian."
- **Echoes.** Jesus feeds thousands with a few barley loaves, with food left over (John 6:9–13). He raises a widow's son at Nain, near Shunem (Luke 7:11–17). "If thine enemy hunger, feed him" (Romans 12:20).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What did Elisha's servant see before and after Elisha prayed (2 Kings 6:15–17)?
2. **Understand:** Many of Elisha's miracles meet small, everyday needs, such as a debt, a pot of stew or a borrowed axe. What does that suggest about God's care?
3. **Apply:** Elisha left his oxen and his family's farm to follow God's call. What might God be asking you to leave behind, or to take up?
4. **Apply:** Elisha fed the soldiers who came to capture him. Who is one person you could treat with unexpected kindness this week?
