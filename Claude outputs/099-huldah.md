# Huldah

*The prophetess the king's men consulted when the Book of the Law was found.*

Huldah lived in Jerusalem in the days of King Josiah. She was married to Shallum, the keeper of the royal wardrobe, and Scripture calls her "the prophetess." In 622 BC, workers repairing the temple found the Book of the Law. When it was read to Josiah, he tore his clothes in grief and sent his highest officials to ask the LORD what it meant. They went to Huldah. Her answer was clear and brave. Judgment would come on Jerusalem, just as the Book said. But because the king's heart was tender, the disaster would not come in his lifetime. Her words moved Josiah to lead the nation in renewing its covenant with God.

## The life of Huldah

*Dates are approximate (BC) and agree with the Josiah chapter. All we know of Huldah comes from one scene, told twice.*

**Before c. 622 BC · In Jerusalem — A prophetess at home.** Huldah lives in Jerusalem's second quarter with her husband Shallum, who keeps the wardrobe, probably the royal or temple robes. *2 Kings 22:14; 2 Chronicles 34:22*

**c. 622 BC · Josiah's 18th year — The Book is found.** Hilkiah the high priest finds the Book of the Law in the temple. Shaphan reads it to the king, who tears his clothes. *2 Kings 22:8–13*

**c. 622 BC · The same year — The king's men come.** Josiah sends Hilkiah, Shaphan, Ahikam, Achbor and Asahiah to inquire of the LORD. They go to Huldah. *2 Kings 22:12–14; 2 Chronicles 34:20–22*

**c. 622 BC · Her answer — Judgment and mercy.** She says the Book's curses will fall on Jerusalem, because the people have forsaken God. But Josiah will be gathered to his grave in peace. *2 Kings 22:15–20; 2 Chronicles 34:23–28*

**c. 622 BC · Soon after — The covenant renewed.** The king gathers the people, reads the Book to them, and makes a covenant to follow the LORD. *2 Kings 23:1–3*

**c. 609 and 586 BC · Years later — Her words come true.** Josiah dies before the disaster, and Jerusalem falls to Babylon about 36 years after her prophecy. *2 Kings 23:29–30; 25:8–10*

### The world around them

Josiah's grandfather Manasseh had filled Judah with idols for decades, and it seems God's Law had been neglected and even lost. The Book that was found is often identified with Deuteronomy, or part of it, because Josiah's reforms follow its commands closely. Jeremiah and Zephaniah were prophesying in these same years (Jeremiah 1:2; Zephaniah 1:1). Scripture does not say why the officials went to Huldah rather than to them.

## Key activities

*Huldah said Josiah would go to his grave "in peace," yet he died in battle (2 Kings 23:29). Most readers take her words to mean he would die before the disaster fell, which he did.*

1. **Was sought out.** The king's highest officials came to her for the word of the LORD. *2 Kings 22:14*
2. **Spoke with authority.** She began, "Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Tell the man that sent you to me." *2 Kings 22:15*
3. **Confirmed the Book.** She said all the words of the Book the king had read would come true. *2 Kings 22:16–17*
4. **Did not soften judgment.** She named the reason plainly: the people had forsaken God and burned incense to other gods. *2 Kings 22:17*
5. **Spoke mercy to the king.** Because Josiah had humbled himself and wept, God had heard him. *2 Kings 22:18–20*
6. **Set reform in motion.** Her message led straight to the covenant renewal and the cleansing of the land. *2 Kings 23:1–25*

*Tradition.* Two gates on the south side of the Temple Mount were later called the Huldah Gates. Some Jewish tradition connects the name with her, and some suggests she was related to Jeremiah. Scripture says neither.

### Words and places

- **Prophetess.** A woman who speaks God's word. Others include Miriam, Deborah and Anna (Exodus 15:20; Judges 4:4; Luke 2:36).
- **The college.** KJV wording for the "second quarter" of Jerusalem, probably a newer district (2 Kings 22:14).
- **Keeper of the wardrobe.** An official in charge of robes, either the king's or the priests' (2 Kings 22:14).
- **Ahikam.** Son of Shaphan and one of the king's messengers. He later protected Jeremiah (Jeremiah 26:24).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Huldah.*

### Old Testament

- **2 Kings 22:14–20** and **2 Chronicles 34:22–28.** The two accounts of her prophecy.
- **2 Kings 23:1–3.** The covenant renewal that followed.
- **Echoes.** Miriam and Deborah were earlier prophetesses (Exodus 15:20; Judges 4:4). Isaiah's wife is called "the prophetess" (Isaiah 8:3). God promised to pour out his Spirit so that "your sons and your daughters shall prophesy" (Joel 2:28).

### New Testament

- Huldah is not named in the New Testament.
- **Echoes.** Anna the prophetess speaks of the child Jesus in the temple (Luke 2:36–38). Peter quotes Joel's promise at Pentecost (Acts 2:16–18). Philip the evangelist had four daughters who prophesied (Acts 21:9).
