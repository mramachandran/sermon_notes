# Jeremiah

*The weeping prophet who warned Jerusalem for forty years and promised a new covenant.*

Jeremiah was a young man from a priestly family in Anathoth when God called him in King Josiah's thirteenth year. For about forty years, through Judah's last kings, he warned that Babylon would destroy Jerusalem unless the people turned back to God. Few listened. He was beaten, put in the stocks, thrown into a muddy cistern and accused of treason. He wept and argued honestly with God. Yet he bought a field during the siege as a sign of hope, and foretold a new covenant written on the heart. He saw Jerusalem fall and ended his days in Egypt.

## The life of Jeremiah

*Dates are approximate (BC). The book is not in time order, so entries gather events from different chapters.*

**c. 627 BC · 13th year of Josiah — Called as a youth.** He says he is only a child. God touches his mouth and sets him over nations. *Jeremiah 1:1–10*

**c. 609 BC · Start of Jehoiakim's reign — The temple sermon.** He warns that the temple will not save lawbreakers. Priests demand his death, but Ahikam protects him. *Jeremiah 7:1–15; 26:1–24*

**c. 605–604 BC · Jehoiakim's 4th–5th years — The scroll burned.** Baruch reads Jeremiah's words in the temple. The king cuts up the scroll and burns it, and Jeremiah dictates it again. *Jeremiah 36:1–32*

**c. 594 BC · 4th year of Zedekiah — The yoke.** Jeremiah wears a yoke and tells the nations to serve Babylon. Hananiah breaks it and dies that year. *Jeremiah 27:2–12; 28:1–17*

**c. 588 BC · During the siege — Prison and the cistern.** Arrested as a deserter, he is lowered into a muddy cistern, and Ebed-melech pulls him out. *Jeremiah 37:11–38:13*

**c. 587 BC · 10th year of Zedekiah — Buys a field.** From prison he buys his cousin's field at Anathoth as a sign that fields will again be bought. *Jeremiah 32:1–15*

**c. 586 BC · Jerusalem falls — Set free.** The Babylonians release him, and he stays with Gedaliah at Mizpah. *Jeremiah 39:1–14; 40:1–6*

**After c. 586 BC · In Egypt — Carried away.** After Gedaliah is murdered, the survivors flee to Egypt and take Jeremiah and Baruch with them. *Jeremiah 41:1–43:7; 44:1*

## Key activities

*Jeremiah is often called "the weeping prophet." Tradition names him as the author of Lamentations, though the book itself does not name its author.*

1. **Accepted a hard call.** He felt too young, but God said, "Behold, I have put my words in thy mouth." *Jeremiah 1:6–9*
2. **Preached at the temple gate.** He told Judah to amend their ways, and warned that the temple had become "a den of robbers." *Jeremiah 7:1–11*
3. **Acted out God's message.** He used a linen girdle, a potter's clay, a broken bottle and a wooden yoke as signs. *Jeremiah 13:1–11; 18:1–6; 19:1–11; 27:2*
4. **Suffered for the truth.** Pashur the priest had him beaten and put in the stocks. *Jeremiah 20:1–2*
5. **Poured out his heart.** He complained bitterly to God, yet said the word was like "a burning fire shut up in my bones." *Jeremiah 20:7–18*
6. **Wrote to the exiles.** He told them to build houses, plant gardens and seek the peace of Babylon, for they would return after seventy years. *Jeremiah 29:4–14*
7. **Promised a new covenant.** God would write his law in their hearts and remember their sin no more. *Jeremiah 31:31–34*
8. **Bought a field in a war zone.** He paid for land the Babylonians were about to take, trusting that nothing was too hard for God. *Jeremiah 32:6–27*
9. **Spoke plainly to a weak king.** He told Zedekiah in secret that surrender to Babylon would save the city. *Jeremiah 38:14–23*
10. **Stayed with those left behind.** Offered a place in Babylon, he chose to stay with those left in the land. *Jeremiah 40:1–6*
11. **Warned against fleeing to Egypt.** He told the survivors to remain in Judah, but they would not listen. *Jeremiah 42:7–43:4*

## Across Scripture

*Direct references first. Echoes are study links, not places that name Jeremiah.*

### Old Testament

- **Jeremiah 1–52.** His book of prophecies, laments and history
- **2 Chronicles 35:25.** He wrote a lament for Josiah
- **2 Chronicles 36:12, 21–22; Ezra 1:1.** Zedekiah ignored him; the return fulfilled his word
- **Daniel 9:2.** Daniel reads his prophecy of seventy years
- **Echo: Ezekiel 1:1–3.** The same message to the exiles in Babylon

### New Testament

- **Matthew 2:17–18.** Rachel weeping (Jeremiah 31:15) and Bethlehem's children
- **Matthew 16:14.** Some thought Jesus was "Jeremias"
- **Matthew 21:13.** "A den of thieves," echoing Jeremiah 7:11
- **Luke 22:20; Hebrews 8:8–12.** The new covenant; Hebrews quotes Jeremiah 31:31–34
- **Tradition.** Later writers say he was stoned in Egypt; Scripture is silent

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What excuse did Jeremiah give, and how did God answer it (Jeremiah 1:6–9)?
2. **Understand:** Why do you think God had Jeremiah buy a field just before Jerusalem fell (Jeremiah 32:6–15)?
3. **Apply:** Jeremiah told God exactly how he felt. What would it look like for you to be that honest with God in prayer?
4. **Apply:** The exiles were told to seek the good of the city where they lived. What is one way you could do that where you live?
