# Habakkuk

*The prophet who argued with God and learned to rejoice when everything failed.*

Habakkuk is different from most prophets. Instead of carrying God's word to the people, he brings the people's questions to God. He lived in Judah shortly before the Babylonian invasions, and he was troubled by the violence and injustice he saw around him. Why did God not act? God's answer shocked him: he was raising up the Chaldeans, the Babylonians, to judge Judah. That raised a harder question. How could a holy God use a nation more wicked than Judah? Habakkuk climbed his watchtower to wait. God told him that "the just shall live by his faith." His book ends with a song of trust that has comforted believers in hard times ever since.

## The life of Habakkuk

*Dates are approximate (BC). Habakkuk gives no date or king. Most scholars place him around the time Babylon defeated Egypt at Carchemish in 605 BC (Jeremiah 46:2). His book is a conversation, so the entries follow its order.*

**c. 605 BC · Before the invasion — "How long?"** Habakkuk cries out that he sees violence, strife and injustice, and God does not seem to hear or save. *Habakkuk 1:1–4*

**c. 605 BC · God's first answer — The Chaldeans are coming.** God says he is raising up the Chaldeans, "that bitter and hasty nation," and Judah will not believe it even when told. *Habakkuk 1:5–11*

**c. 605 BC · His second complaint — Why use the wicked?** He asks how a God of purer eyes than to behold evil can let a more wicked nation swallow a more righteous one. *Habakkuk 1:12–17*

**On the watchtower — Waiting for God.** He decides to stand on his watch and see what God will say. God tells him to write the vision plainly and wait for it, for it will surely come. *Habakkuk 2:1–4*

**After the vision — A prayer and a song.** He prays, "in wrath remember mercy," recalls God's mighty deeds, and resolves to rejoice in God even if every crop fails. *Habakkuk 3:1–19*

### The world around them

For more than a century Assyria had dominated the region. But Babylon rebelled, and in 612 BC Nineveh fell to the Babylonians and their allies. In 605 BC Nebuchadnezzar defeated Egypt at Carchemish, and soon Judah was under Babylon's control (2 Kings 24:1). Inside Judah, King Jehoiakim was undoing his father Josiah's reforms (Jeremiah 22:13–17). Habakkuk's complaint about injustice probably reflects these years, though he names no king.

## Key activities

*Habakkuk 2:4 has been read in several ways. The Hebrew word for "faith" can also mean "faithfulness." Paul quotes the verse to show that the righteous live by trusting God.*

1. **Asked God hard questions.** "O LORD, how long shall I cry, and thou wilt not hear!" *Habakkuk 1:2–4*
2. **Received a shocking answer.** God said he would do a work that Judah would not believe: send the Babylonians. *Habakkuk 1:5–6*
3. **Pressed his case.** He appealed to God's holiness: "Thou art of purer eyes than to behold evil." *Habakkuk 1:12–13*
4. **Waited on the watchtower.** "I will stand upon my watch, and set me upon the tower, and will watch to see what he will say unto me." *Habakkuk 2:1*
5. **Wrote the vision plainly.** God told him to write it on tablets so that anyone could read it and run. *Habakkuk 2:2–3*
6. **Heard the heart of the answer.** The proud will fall, but "the just shall live by his faith." *Habakkuk 2:4*
7. **Announced five woes.** He pronounced woe on greed, violence, cruelty and idolatry. "The earth shall be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD." *Habakkuk 2:6–20*
8. **Sang his trust.** Though the fig tree fail and the fields yield no food, he would rejoice in the God of his salvation. *Habakkuk 3:17–19*

*Tradition.* The book of Bel and the Dragon, an addition to Daniel found in the Apocrypha, says an angel carried Habakkuk to Babylon to feed Daniel in the lions' den. It is not part of the Hebrew Bible.

### Words and places

- **Burden.** A heavy message from God, often of judgment (Habakkuk 1:1).
- **Chaldeans.** The people who ruled Babylon in this period (Habakkuk 1:6).
- **Shigionoth.** A term in the heading of his prayer, probably musical. Its meaning is uncertain (Habakkuk 3:1).
- **Selah.** A word found in Habakkuk 3 and in the Psalms, probably a musical pause (Habakkuk 3:3, 9, 13).

### Read it yourself

- **Day 1.** Habakkuk 1:1–11. What was Habakkuk's complaint, and how did God answer?
- **Day 2.** Habakkuk 1:12–2:4. What did Habakkuk decide to do with his questions?
- **Day 3.** Habakkuk 3:16–19. How can someone rejoice when everything is failing?

## Across Scripture

*Direct references first. Echoes are study links, not places that name Habakkuk.*

### Old Testament

- **Habakkuk 1–3.** The book of his complaints, God's answers and his prayer.
- **Echoes.** Jeremiah also asked why the wicked prosper (Jeremiah 12:1). Job and many psalmists bring honest complaints to God (Job 23:3–4; Psalm 13:1). "Be still, and know that I am God" (Psalm 46:10).

### New Testament

- **Romans 1:17** and **Galatians 3:11.** Paul quotes Habakkuk 2:4, "The just shall live by faith," at the heart of his teaching on the gospel.
- **Hebrews 10:37–38.** Hebrews quotes Habakkuk 2:3–4 to encourage believers to wait patiently for Christ.
- **Acts 13:40–41.** Paul warns his hearers with Habakkuk 1:5: "Behold, ye despisers, and wonder, and perish."
- **Echoes.** Paul and Silas sing in prison at midnight (Acts 16:25). Paul learned to be content in every circumstance (Philippians 4:11–13).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What two questions did Habakkuk ask God (Habakkuk 1:2–4, 12–13)?
2. **Understand:** Why do you think God answered Habakkuk with a call to faith instead of a full explanation?
3. **Apply:** What question would you most like to bring to God right now? What would it look like to wait on the watchtower with it?
4. **Apply:** Finish Habakkuk's sentence for your own life: "Although ... yet I will rejoice in the LORD." What would you write?
