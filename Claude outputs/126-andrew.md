# Andrew

*The brother who kept bringing people to Jesus.*

Andrew was a fisherman from Bethsaida and the brother of Simon Peter. He first followed John the Baptist, and he was one of the first two men to follow Jesus. His first act as a disciple was to find his brother and say, "We have found the Messias." That pattern marks nearly every scene where John's Gospel names him. He brings Peter to Jesus. He brings a boy with five loaves to Jesus. He brings a question from some Greeks to Jesus. Andrew never preaches a sermon in Scripture, and he often stands in his famous brother's shadow. Yet his quiet habit of introducing others to Christ has made him a model for ordinary believers.

## The life of Andrew

*Dates are approximate (AD). Jesus' ministry is dated c. AD 27/28–30 in this study. Scripture does not give Andrew's age.*

**c. AD 27–28 · Beyond Jordan — Follows Jesus.** Andrew hears John the Baptist call Jesus "the Lamb of God." He and another disciple follow Jesus and spend the rest of the day with him. *John 1:35–40*

**c. AD 27–28 · Beyond Jordan — Brings his brother.** He finds Simon and tells him they have found the Messiah. Then he brings Simon to Jesus, who names him Cephas. *John 1:41–42*

**c. AD 28 · Sea of Galilee — Called to fish for men.** Jesus sees Simon and Andrew casting a net into the sea and calls them to follow him. They leave their nets at once. *Mark 1:16–18*

**c. AD 29 · Near the Sea of Galilee — Finds the loaves.** Before Jesus feeds the five thousand, Andrew points out a boy with five barley loaves and two small fishes, though he wonders what good they are. *John 6:5–13*

**c. AD 30 · Jerusalem — Brings the Greeks.** At the last Passover some Greeks ask Philip to see Jesus. Philip tells Andrew, and together they tell Jesus. *John 12:20–22*

### The world around them

Bethsaida was a fishing town at the north end of the Sea of Galilee, near where the Jordan River flows in. The name probably means "house of fishing." Its exact site is still debated by archaeologists. Fishing was hard, skilled work done mostly at night, with nets cast from the shore or dragged between boats. Andrew had also spent time with John the Baptist in the wilderness by the Jordan. This suggests he was a man already looking for God's promised Messiah before he met Jesus.

## Key activities

*Andrew is named only about a dozen times in the New Testament, most often in John's Gospel. The other disciple with him in John 1:35–40 is not named; many think it was John the apostle.*

1. **Followed the Lamb of God.** When his teacher pointed to Jesus, Andrew followed and asked where he was staying. *John 1:37–39*
2. **Brought his brother.** He went first to Simon with the news that the Messiah had come. *John 1:40–42*
3. **Left his nets.** He answered Jesus' call to become a fisher of men. *Mark 1:16–18; Matthew 4:18–20*
4. **Was chosen as an apostle.** He is named among the Twelve in every list. *Matthew 10:2; Mark 3:18; Luke 6:14; Acts 1:13*
5. **Asked about the future.** With Peter, James and John he asked Jesus privately on the Mount of Olives when the temple would fall. *Mark 13:3–4*
6. **Offered what little there was.** He brought a boy's lunch to Jesus, asking, "But what are they among so many?" *John 6:8–9*
7. **Opened the way for Greeks.** He helped carry the request of some Greek visitors to Jesus. *John 12:20–22*

### Words and places

- **Messias.** The Greek form of the Hebrew word Messiah, meaning "anointed." The Greek equivalent is Christ (John 1:41).
- **Bethsaida.** Andrew's hometown, also the home of Peter and Philip (John 1:44).
- **Lamb of God.** John the Baptist's title for Jesus, pointing to sacrifice (John 1:29, 36).
- **Barley loaves.** Barley was cheaper than wheat and was the common bread of the poor (John 6:9).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references first. Echoes are study links, not places that name Andrew.*

### Old Testament

- Andrew is not named in the Old Testament.
- **Echo: 2 Samuel 7:12–16.** The anointed king Andrew hoped for as "the Messias"
- **Echo: 2 Kings 4:42–44.** Elisha feeds a hundred with twenty barley loaves

### New Testament

- **John 1:35–42.** Andrew follows Jesus and brings Simon
- **Mark 1:16–18, 29; Matthew 4:18–20.** Called with Peter; Jesus visits their home
- **Mark 13:3.** With the inner three on the Mount of Olives
- **John 6:8–9; 12:22.** He brings the loaves and the Greeks
- **Acts 1:13.** In the upper room; his last mention in Scripture
- **Tradition.** Preached by the Black Sea and Greece, crucified at Patras; the X-shaped cross is much later

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** Who did Andrew bring to Jesus in John 1, John 6 and John 12?
2. **Understand:** Andrew wondered what five loaves could do "among so many." What does Jesus' answer teach about small offerings?
3. **Apply:** Andrew started with his own brother. Who in your family or circle could you invite to "come and see"?
4. **Apply:** What small gift, skill or hour could you hand to Jesus this week, even if it seems too little?
