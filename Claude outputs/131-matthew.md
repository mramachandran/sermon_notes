# Matthew

*The tax collector who left his table to follow Jesus and threw a party for sinners.*

Matthew, also called Levi, collected taxes by the Sea of Galilee, probably at Capernaum. To most Jews of his day, that made him a collaborator and a cheat. Jesus walked past his tax booth and said, "Follow me." Matthew left it all and followed. Then he threw a great feast so his fellow tax collectors could meet Jesus too. When the Pharisees objected, Jesus answered that the sick, not the healthy, need a doctor. No Gospel records a word Matthew spoke, yet the church has long linked his name with the first Gospel.

## The life of Matthew

*Dates are approximate (AD). Jesus' ministry is dated c. AD 27/28–30 in this study. Scripture does not give Matthew's age.*

**c. AD 28 · Capernaum — Called from the tax booth.** Walking by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus sees Levi the son of Alphaeus sitting at the receipt of custom. At the words "Follow me," he leaves everything and follows. *Mark 2:13–14; Luke 5:27–28*

**c. AD 28 · His own house — Hosts a great feast.** Levi makes a great feast for Jesus, and many tax collectors and sinners sit down to eat with him and his disciples. *Luke 5:29; Matthew 9:10*

**c. AD 28 · His own house — Hears Jesus defend him.** The Pharisees ask why Jesus eats with publicans and sinners. Jesus answers that he came to call sinners to repentance. *Matthew 9:11–13*

**c. AD 28 · On a mountain — Chosen as an apostle.** Jesus names the Twelve. In Matthew's Gospel the list calls him "Matthew the publican," keeping his old label. *Matthew 10:2–4; Luke 6:13–16*

**c. AD 30 · Jerusalem — Waits in the upper room.** After the ascension Matthew is with the other apostles and the women, praying and waiting for the Spirit. *Acts 1:13–14*

### The world around them

Capernaum stood on the north shore of the Sea of Galilee, near a trade road and the border between the lands of Herod Antipas and his brother Philip. Matthew probably collected tolls on goods for Herod Antipas. Tax collection was usually farmed out. A collector paid for the right and made his living from whatever he could charge above the official rate. Many grew rich by cheating (Luke 3:12–13; 19:8). Religious Jews saw them as traitors who worked for foreign powers and as unclean because of their dealings with Gentiles.

## Key activities

*Mark and Luke call him Levi, while Matthew's Gospel calls him Matthew. Most readers take these as two names for one man, as with Simon and Peter.*

1. **Sat at the receipt of custom.** He worked in a trade that made him an outsider among his people. *Matthew 9:9*
2. **Left everything.** Luke says he "left all, rose up, and followed him." *Luke 5:27–28*
3. **Opened his home.** He made a great feast so other tax collectors could meet Jesus. *Luke 5:29*
4. **Saw Jesus defend sinners.** Jesus answered the critics, "They that be whole need not a physician, but they that are sick." *Matthew 9:12*
5. **Heard mercy taught.** Jesus sent the critics to learn what Hosea meant: "I will have mercy, and not sacrifice." *Matthew 9:13*
6. **Joined the Twelve.** He was listed among the apostles, still named as a publican. *Matthew 10:3*

### Words and places

- **Publican.** A tax or toll collector; in the Gospels the word is often paired with "sinners" (Matthew 9:10–11).
- **Receipt of custom.** The tax booth or toll office where duties were paid (Matthew 9:9).
- **Capernaum.** A lakeside town that became the base of Jesus' ministry in Galilee (Matthew 4:13; Mark 2:1).
- **Pharisees.** A strict religious party devoted to keeping the Law and the traditions of the elders (Matthew 9:11).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Matthew.*

### Old Testament

- **Hosea 6:6.** Mercy, not sacrifice; quoted by Jesus at Matthew's feast (Matthew 9:13)
- **Echo: Matthew 1:22; 2:15; 4:14.** His Gospel's deep interest in fulfilled prophecy

### New Testament

- **Matthew 9:9–13; Mark 2:14–17; Luke 5:27–32.** His call and feast
- **Matthew 10:3; Mark 3:18; Luke 6:15; Acts 1:13.** Listed among the Twelve
- **Echo: Luke 19:1–10; 18:9–14.** Zacchaeus, and the publican who went home justified
- **Tradition.** Papias (in Eusebius) credits Matthew with this Gospel; many scholars debate the link

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What did Matthew do right after he left his tax booth (Luke 5:28–29)?
2. **Understand:** Why did the Pharisees object to Jesus eating with publicans, and what did Jesus mean by his answer (Matthew 9:11–13)?
3. **Apply:** Matthew's first act as a disciple was to throw a party for his old friends. Who could you invite to meet Jesus over a meal?
4. **Apply:** Is there anyone you have written off as too far gone? How might you show them mercy this week?
