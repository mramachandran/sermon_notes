# The lesser-known apostles

*Faithful names on the list: James son of Alphaeus, Judas (Thaddaeus), Simon the Zealot, and Matthias.*

Every list of the Twelve ends with a few names we know almost nothing about. James the son of Alphaeus is never quoted. Judas, "not Iscariot," asks Jesus one question at the last supper. Simon is remembered only by his label, "the Canaanite" or "Zelotes," which probably marks him as zealous for Israel's cause. After the resurrection Matthias is chosen to take the place of Judas Iscariot. These men were sent out with the same authority as Peter and John. Their place on the list reminds us that most of Christ's servants work without fame.

## The lives of the lesser-known apostles

*Dates are approximate (AD). Jesus' ministry is dated c. AD 27/28–30 in this study, and the crucifixion c. AD 30. Scripture gives no ages.*

**c. AD 28 · On a mountain — Chosen as apostles.** After a night of prayer, Jesus chooses twelve from among his disciples. James son of Alphaeus, Judas and Simon are named among them. *Luke 6:12–16*

**c. AD 29 · Galilee — Sent out two by two.** Jesus gives the Twelve power over unclean spirits and sends them to preach repentance, heal the sick, and anoint with oil. *Mark 6:7–13; Matthew 10:1–15*

**c. AD 30 · The last supper — Judas asks a question.** Judas, "not Iscariot," asks why Jesus will show himself to the disciples and not to the world. Jesus speaks of love, obedience and his dwelling with them. *John 14:21–23*

**c. AD 30 · Jerusalem — Wait in the upper room.** After the ascension James, Judas and Simon pray with the other apostles, the women, and Jesus' mother and brothers. *Acts 1:13–14*

**c. AD 30 · Jerusalem — Matthias is chosen.** Peter calls for a witness of the resurrection to replace Judas Iscariot. After prayer the lot falls on Matthias, who is numbered with the eleven apostles. *Acts 1:15–26*

### The world around them

Jewish Palestine in Jesus' day was restless under Roman rule. Some Jews looked for a Messiah who would drive out the Romans by force. In AD 6 Judas the Galilean led a revolt against a Roman census (Acts 5:37). Josephus later names the Zealots as a party in the war with Rome that began in AD 66. Whether that organized party existed in Jesus' day is debated. Simon's title may mean he belonged to such a group, or simply that he was zealous for God's law.

## Key activities

*The names in the lists differ slightly. Most readers take Thaddaeus, Lebbaeus and "Judas of James" as one man known by more than one name. Luke's Greek says only "Judas of James," so he may be James's son rather than his brother; the KJV supplies "brother."*

1. **Were chosen by Jesus.** Each was named an apostle after Jesus prayed all night. *Luke 6:12–16*
2. **Shared the mission.** They were sent with power to heal and to preach the kingdom of God. *Luke 9:1–6*
3. **Asked about Jesus' self-revelation.** Judas asked, "Lord, how is it that thou wilt manifest thyself unto us, and not unto the world?" *John 14:22*
4. **Held on after the cross.** They stayed together and continued in prayer with one accord. *Acts 1:13–14*
5. **Stepped into an empty place.** Matthias, who had been with Jesus since John's baptism, became a witness of the resurrection with the eleven. *Acts 1:21–26*
6. **Received the Spirit.** All the apostles were together on the day of Pentecost, and Peter stood up "with the eleven." *Acts 2:1–4, 14*

### Words and places

- **Zelotes.** A Greek word for "zealot," a person on fire for a cause (Luke 6:15).
- **Canaanite.** In Matthew 10:4 this probably comes from an Aramaic word for "zealous," not from the land of Canaan.
- **Alphaeus.** The father of James; Levi's father has the same name, but Scripture does not say they were brothers (Mark 2:14; 3:18).
- **Casting lots.** A way of seeking God's choice, used before the Spirit was given at Pentecost (Acts 1:26).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names these apostles.*

### Old Testament

- **Psalm 69:25; 109:8.** Applied by Peter to Judas's empty place (Acts 1:20)
- **Proverbs 16:33.** The lot is cast, but the LORD decides
- **Echo: Genesis 49:28.** Twelve apostles, like Israel's twelve tribes
- **Echo: Numbers 25:11–13.** Phinehas, zealous for his God, like Simon's title

### New Testament

- **Matthew 10:2–4; Mark 3:16–19; Luke 6:14–16; Acts 1:13.** The four lists
- **John 14:22; Acts 1:23–26.** Judas asks his question; Matthias is chosen
- **Matthew 19:28; Revelation 21:14.** Twelve thrones and twelve foundations
- **Tradition.** Simon and Jude are said to have died in Persia

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** According to Mark 3:14, what were the Twelve chosen for?
2. **Understand:** What did the church look for in Judas's replacement (Acts 1:21–22)?
3. **Apply:** These apostles served with little recognition. Where are you serving unnoticed, and how can you keep at it?
4. **Apply:** A zealot and a tax collector served together. Who very different from you could you work with or pray for?
