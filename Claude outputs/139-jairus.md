# Jairus

*A synagogue ruler who fell at Jesus' feet for his dying daughter and learned to "only believe."*

Jairus was "one of the rulers of the synagogue" in a town by the Sea of Galilee. He was a respected man, but when his twelve-year-old daughter lay dying, he pushed through the crowd and fell at Jesus' feet. Jesus went with him. Then came a delay, as Jesus stopped to heal a woman who touched his clothes. While they waited, word came that the girl had died. Jesus turned to Jairus and said, "Be not afraid, only believe." At the house he took the girl by the hand and raised her to life. Matthew, Mark and Luke all tell the story. It is a story about a desperate parent, an unwelcome delay, and trust in Jesus when all hope seems gone.

## The life of Jairus

*The date is approximate (AD) and follows the house chronology, with the crucifixion c. AD 30. Matthew tells the story more briefly. There, the ruler says at the start that his daughter "is even now dead" (Matthew 9:18).*

**c. AD 28–29 · By the sea — Falls at Jesus' feet.** When Jesus crosses back over the lake, a crowd gathers. Jairus comes, falls at his feet, and begs him to come and lay hands on his little daughter, who "lieth at the point of death." *Mark 5:21–23*

**On the way — A delay.** A woman who has suffered bleeding for twelve years touches Jesus' garment and is healed. Jesus stops, asks who touched him, and speaks with her while Jairus waits. *Mark 5:24–34*

**On the way — Bad news.** Messengers arrive from Jairus's house: "Thy daughter is dead: why troublest thou the Master any further?" Jesus tells Jairus not to be afraid, but only to believe. *Mark 5:35–36*

**At the house — Laughed to scorn.** Jesus lets only Peter, James and John follow him. He tells the loud mourners that the child "is not dead, but sleepeth," and they laugh at him. *Mark 5:37–40*

**At the house — Talitha cumi.** With the parents and the three disciples, Jesus takes the girl's hand and tells her to arise. She gets up and walks. He orders them to tell no one and to give her something to eat. *Mark 5:40–43*

### The world around them

A synagogue was a local meeting place for prayer, the reading of the Law and the Prophets, and teaching (Luke 4:16–21). The ruler of the synagogue was a respected leader who arranged the meetings and chose who would read and speak (Acts 13:15). Capernaum, Jesus' base in Galilee, had a synagogue where he taught and healed (Mark 1:21–28). Mourning in that culture was loud and public. Families often brought in flute players and wailing mourners, which explains the crowd Jesus found already gathered (Matthew 9:23).

## Key activities

*Mark and Luke tell Jairus's story wrapped around the healing of the woman with the issue of blood. Both stories involve the number twelve, and Jesus calls the woman "Daughter" (Mark 5:34). Many readers see the link as deliberate.*

1. **Humbled himself.** A synagogue ruler fell at the feet of a travelling teacher in front of the crowd. *Mark 5:22*
2. **Pleaded for his daughter.** He begged Jesus earnestly to come and lay his hands on her, that she might live. *Mark 5:23*
3. **Waited.** He stood by while Jesus stopped to find and speak with the woman who had touched him. *Mark 5:30–34*
4. **Kept believing.** When the news of death came, he heard Jesus' word, "Be not afraid, only believe," and went on with him. *Mark 5:35–37*
5. **Witnessed the miracle.** He and his wife watched Jesus raise their daughter, and they were "astonished with a great astonishment." *Mark 5:40–42*
6. **Was told to keep quiet.** Jesus charged them that no one should know it, and told them to feed her. *Mark 5:43*

### Words and places

- **Ruler of the synagogue.** A leader in charge of the synagogue's meetings (Mark 5:22; Acts 13:15).
- **Talitha cumi.** Aramaic words that Mark translates, "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise" (Mark 5:41).
- **Issue of blood.** Long-lasting bleeding, which under the law made a woman ceremonially unclean (Mark 5:25; Leviticus 15:25–27).
- **Minstrels.** The KJV word for the musicians at a funeral (Matthew 9:23).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Jairus.*

### Old Testament

- Jairus is not named in the Old Testament.
- **Echoes.** Elijah raises the son of the widow of Zarephath (1 Kings 17:17–24). Elisha raises the son of the Shunammite woman (2 Kings 4:18–37). In both stories the prophet goes into a private room with the child.

### New Testament

- **Mark 5:21–43.** The fullest account, with the Aramaic words Jesus spoke.
- **Matthew 9:18–26.** A shorter account that calls him "a certain ruler."
- **Luke 8:40–56.** Luke names him and says she was his only daughter.
- **Echoes.** Jesus raises a widow's son at Nain (Luke 7:11–15) and Lazarus of Bethany (John 11:43–44). Peter later says "Tabitha, arise" to Dorcas (Acts 9:40), words close to Jesus' own.
