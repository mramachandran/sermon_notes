# Zacchaeus

*A rich tax collector who climbed a tree to see Jesus and came down a changed man.*

Zacchaeus was the chief tax collector in Jericho, and he was rich. People despised tax collectors as cheats who worked for Rome, and Zacchaeus was at the top of that trade. When Jesus passed through Jericho on his way to Jerusalem, Zacchaeus was too short to see over the crowd, so he ran ahead and climbed a sycomore tree. Jesus stopped, called him by name, and invited himself to his house. The crowd grumbled, but Zacchaeus stood up and promised to give half his goods to the poor and repay fourfold anyone he had cheated. Jesus said salvation had come to his house.

## The life of Zacchaeus

*The date is approximate (AD) and follows the house chronology, with the crucifixion c. AD 30. Luke sets the story on Jesus' last journey up to Jerusalem (Luke 19:11, 28).*

**c. AD 30 · Jericho — Tries to see Jesus.** Jesus passes through Jericho. Zacchaeus wants to see who Jesus is but cannot because of the crowd, for he is "little of stature." He runs ahead and climbs a sycomore tree. *Luke 19:1–4*

**The same day — Called by name.** At the tree Jesus looks up: "Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house." Zacchaeus hurries down and receives him joyfully. *Luke 19:5–6*

**The same day — The crowd murmurs.** Everyone who sees it complains that Jesus has gone "to be guest with a man that is a sinner." To them, a chief tax collector is beyond the pale. *Luke 19:7*

**At his house — A changed man.** Zacchaeus stands and promises half his goods to the poor and fourfold repayment to anyone he has cheated. Jesus says, "This day is salvation come to this house." *Luke 19:8–10*

### The world around them

Jericho lay in the Jordan valley, roughly fifteen miles northeast of Jerusalem. It was a rich oasis known for its palm trees and balsam, and it sat on the pilgrim road up to Jerusalem. The Romans usually sold the right to collect certain taxes and tolls to local contractors, who could add their own profit. A "chief" collector like Zacchaeus probably oversaw others. Tax collectors were despised as greedy collaborators with a foreign power, and they were often lumped together with "sinners" (Luke 15:1).

## Key activities

*Zacchaeus's words in Luke 19:8 can be read two ways. Most take them as a promise of change from that day. Some read them as a description of what he already did, defending himself against the crowd. Either way, Jesus declares that salvation has come.*

1. **Sought Jesus.** He wanted to see who Jesus was, and he did not let the crowd stop him. *Luke 19:3*
2. **Climbed a tree.** He ran ahead and climbed a sycomore, setting aside his dignity to catch a glimpse. *Luke 19:4*
3. **Welcomed Jesus joyfully.** He came down at once and received Jesus into his home. *Luke 19:6*
4. **Gave to the poor.** He pledged half of his goods to the poor. *Luke 19:8*
5. **Promised to repay fourfold.** He offered to restore four times over anything taken by false accusation, far more than the law usually required. *Luke 19:8; Numbers 5:7*
6. **Heard a new name.** Jesus called him "a son of Abraham," a true member of God's people. *Luke 19:9*

*Tradition.* Later Christian writings known as the Clementine literature say Peter made Zacchaeus the leader of the church at Caesarea. This is tradition, not Scripture.

### Words and places

- **Publican.** A tax or toll collector working for Rome or its local rulers (Luke 19:2; 3:12–13).
- **Sycomore.** A sturdy fig tree with low, spreading branches, not the English sycamore (Luke 19:4; Amos 7:14).
- **Fourfold.** Four times over, the law's penalty for a stolen sheep (Luke 19:8; Exodus 22:1).
- **Son of Abraham.** A true member of God's covenant people (Luke 19:9; 13:16).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Zacchaeus.*

### Old Testament

- **Exodus 22:1; 2 Samuel 12:6.** Fourfold repayment, the measure Zacchaeus chose
- **Leviticus 6:1–5; Numbers 5:6–7.** The usual repayment: the full amount plus a fifth
- **Echo: Ezekiel 34:16.** "I will seek that which was lost"

### New Testament

- **Luke 19:1–10.** The only account
- **Luke 3:12–13.** John tells tax collectors to exact no more than appointed
- **Luke 5:27–32; 15:1–2.** Jesus eats with tax collectors and sinners
- **Echo: Matthew 9:9.** Matthew, another tax collector, follows Jesus
- **Echo: Luke 15.** The lost sheep, coin and son are found

## For discussion

3. **Apply:** Zacchaeus made things right with what he had wrongly taken. Is there something you need to set right with someone this week?
4. **Apply:** Who in your community is treated like Zacchaeus, and how could you welcome them?
