# Philip the evangelist

*The table-server who carried the gospel to Samaria, to an African official, and down the coast.*

Philip was one of the seven chosen to care for the widows in the Jerusalem church. When persecution broke out after Stephen's death, he went to Samaria, a place most Jews avoided, and preached Christ with great results. Then an angel sent him to a desert road, where he met an Ethiopian official reading Isaiah. Philip pointed him to Jesus and baptized him, then preached town to town up to Caesarea. Twenty years later he was still there, "Philip the evangelist," with four daughters who prophesied.

## The life of Philip the evangelist

*Dates are approximate (AD). Acts gives no years. The mission to Samaria follows Stephen's death, c. AD 33–35, and Paul's visit to Caesarea is c. AD 57.*

**Early church · Jerusalem — Chosen as one of the seven.** When Greek-speaking widows are neglected, the church chooses seven men full of the Spirit and wisdom. Philip is named second, after Stephen. The apostles pray and lay hands on them. *Acts 6:1–6*

**c. AD 33–35 · Samaria — Preaches Christ.** Persecution scatters the believers. Philip goes down to the city of Samaria and preaches Christ. Crowds listen, unclean spirits are cast out, the lame are healed, and "there was great joy in that city." *Acts 8:1–8*

**Samaria — Baptizes many, including Simon.** Men and women believe and are baptized. Even Simon, a well-known sorcerer, believes and follows Philip. Peter and John come from Jerusalem, and the Samaritans receive the Holy Ghost. *Acts 8:9–17*

**The Gaza road — Meets the Ethiopian.** An angel sends Philip south to a desert road. He finds a treasurer of the queen of Ethiopia reading Isaiah, explains the passage, preaches Jesus, and baptizes him in roadside water. *Acts 8:26–38*

**Azotus to Caesarea — Preaches along the coast.** The Spirit catches Philip away. He is found at Azotus and preaches in all the cities along the way until he comes to Caesarea. *Acts 8:39–40*

**c. AD 57 · Caesarea — Hosts Paul.** At the end of his third journey, Paul and his companions stay many days in Philip's house. The prophet Agabus comes and warns Paul about what awaits him in Jerusalem. *Acts 21:8–14*

### The world around them

Samaritans lived in the hill country between Judea and Galilee. They worshipped the God of Israel but accepted only the five books of Moses and had their own holy mountain, Gerizim. Jews and Samaritans had been bitter rivals for centuries (John 4:9, 20). The Ethiopian official served the Candace, a title for the queen of the kingdom of Meroë in what is now Sudan, south of Egypt. Caesarea was a large port city built by Herod the Great and the seat of the Roman governor.

## Key activities

*The KJV includes Acts 8:37, the Ethiopian's confession of faith. Many of the oldest Greek manuscripts do not have this verse, so some modern translations put it in a footnote.*

1. **Served the widows.** He was trusted to oversee the daily care of the church's needy widows. *Acts 6:3–5*
2. **Crossed a cultural line.** He took the gospel to Samaria, and "there was great joy in that city." *Acts 8:5–8*
3. **Did signs.** Unclean spirits came out, and many who were palsied and lame were healed. *Acts 8:6–7, 13*
4. **Obeyed without delay.** Told to go to a desert road, "he arose and went." *Acts 8:26–27*
5. **Ran to the chariot.** The Spirit said, "Go near, and join thyself to this chariot," and Philip ran to it. *Acts 8:29–30*
6. **Explained Scripture.** He asked, "Understandest thou what thou readest?" and preached Jesus from Isaiah 53. *Acts 8:30–35*
7. **Baptized the Ethiopian.** The official went on his way rejoicing. *Acts 8:36–39*
8. **Kept preaching.** He preached in every city from Azotus to Caesarea, and years later was still known as the evangelist. *Acts 8:40; 21:8*

### Words and places

- **Evangelist.** One who announces good news, especially the gospel of Jesus (Acts 21:8; Ephesians 4:11).
- **Eunuch.** A man who had been castrated, often trusted with royal service. The word could also mean a court official (Acts 8:27).
- **Candace.** Not a personal name but the title of the queen mother who ruled in Ethiopia, or Meroë (Acts 8:27).
- **Azotus.** The Greek name for Ashdod, an old Philistine city on the coast (Acts 8:40).

*Direct references first. Echoes are study links, not places that name Philip.*

- **Isaiah 53:7–8.** The passage the Ethiopian was reading (Acts 8:32–35)
- **Echo: Isaiah 56:3–5.** Faithful eunuchs promised a better name
- **Echo: Psalm 68:31.** "Ethiopia shall soon stretch out her hands unto God"

### New Testament

- **Acts 6:5.** Listed second among the seven
- **Acts 8:4–40.** Samaria and the Ethiopian
- **Acts 21:8–9.** "Philip the evangelist," with four prophesying daughters
- **Echo: Acts 1:8.** Judaea, Samaria, the uttermost part: Philip reaches each
- **Tradition.** Often confused with Philip the apostle; later life in Asia Minor uncertain
