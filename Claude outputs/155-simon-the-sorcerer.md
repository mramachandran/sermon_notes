# Simon the sorcerer

*The magician of Samaria who tried to buy the power of the Holy Ghost.*

Simon was the most famous man in Samaria. For a long time he had amazed the people with sorcery, and they called him "the great power of God." Then Philip came preaching Christ, with real signs and healings. The crowds turned to Jesus, and Simon himself believed and was baptized. He followed Philip everywhere, amazed at the miracles. But when Peter and John arrived and the Holy Ghost was given through the laying on of their hands, Simon offered them money for the same power. Peter's rebuke was sharp: God's gift cannot be bought. Simon asked the apostles to pray for him, and there the story ends. His name lives on in the word "simony," the buying or selling of church offices and spiritual things.

## The life of Simon the sorcerer

*Dates are approximate (AD). The events follow the scattering after Stephen's death, c. AD 33–35. Acts does not tell us what became of Simon.*

**Before Philip comes · Samaria — Amazes the people.** For a long time Simon uses sorcery and bewitches the people of Samaria. He claims to be "some great one," and everyone from the least to the greatest listens to him. *Acts 8:9–11*

**c. AD 33–35 · Samaria — Believes and is baptized.** Philip preaches the kingdom of God and the name of Jesus Christ. Men and women believe and are baptized, and Simon himself believes too. He stays close to Philip, amazed at the signs. *Acts 8:12–13*

**Samaria — Watches the apostles.** The church in Jerusalem sends Peter and John. They pray for the Samaritans and lay hands on them, and the believers receive the Holy Ghost. Simon sees it happen. *Acts 8:14–18*

**Samaria — Offers money.** Simon offers the apostles money, saying, "Give me also this power, that on whomsoever I lay hands, he may receive the Holy Ghost." *Acts 8:18–19*

**Samaria — Rebuked and asks for prayer.** Peter tells him his heart is not right before God and calls him to repent. Simon answers, "Pray ye to the Lord for me, that none of these things which ye have spoken come upon me." *Acts 8:20–24*

### The world around them

Magic and sorcery were common across the Roman world. People used charms, spells and curses to seek healing, love, success or protection. Acts meets such practices again in Cyprus with the sorcerer Elymas and in Ephesus, where new believers burned their books of magic (Acts 13:6–12; 19:18–19). The law of Moses had long forbidden sorcery and fortune-telling in Israel (Deuteronomy 18:10–12). Samaria, once the capital of the northern kingdom, was by now home to the Samaritans. Acts does not say which Samaritan city Philip visited.

## Key activities

*Christians differ on whether Simon's faith was real. Some think he was a true but immature believer who sinned badly. Others think his faith was only on the surface, because Peter says his heart was "not right in the sight of God." Acts leaves the question open.*

1. **Practised sorcery.** He "used sorcery, and bewitched the people of Samaria, giving out that himself was some great one." *Acts 8:9*
2. **Believed and was baptized.** "Then Simon himself believed also: and when he was baptized, he continued with Philip." *Acts 8:13*
3. **Tried to buy spiritual power.** Seeing the Holy Ghost given, he offered the apostles money. *Acts 8:18–19*
4. **Heard a hard rebuke.** Peter said, "Thou hast neither part nor lot in this matter: for thy heart is not right in the sight of God." *Acts 8:20–21*
5. **Was called to repent.** Peter urged him to repent and pray, for he was "in the gall of bitterness, and in the bond of iniquity." *Acts 8:22–23*
6. **Asked for prayer.** He asked the apostles to pray for him. Acts does not say what happened next. *Acts 8:24*

*Tradition.* Early Christian writers such as Justin Martyr and Irenaeus described Simon Magus as the father of false teachings. Later legends tell of a contest between Simon and Peter in Rome. None of this is in Scripture.

### Words and places

- **Sorcery.** The use of magic arts to control people or events (Acts 8:9, 11).
- **Bewitched.** Here it means amazed or astonished the people (Acts 8:9).
- **Gall of bitterness.** A picture of a heart poisoned with bitterness (Acts 8:23; compare Deuteronomy 29:18).
- **Simony.** A later word, named after Simon, for buying or selling church offices or spiritual things.

## Across Scripture

*Direct references first. Echoes are study links, not places that name Simon.*

### Old Testament

- **Deuteronomy 18:10–12.** The law forbids witchcraft and enchantments
- **Deuteronomy 29:18.** "Gall and wormwood," behind Peter's phrase
- **Echo: 2 Kings 5:20–27.** Gehazi tries to profit from God's healing
- **Echo: Isaiah 55:1.** Buy "without money and without price"

### New Testament

- **Acts 8:9–24.** The only account of Simon
- **Echo: Matthew 10:8.** "Freely ye have received, freely give"
- **Echo: Acts 13:6–12.** Paul confronts the sorcerer Elymas
- **Tradition.** Later writers tied him to Gnosticism; these stories grew over time
