# Cornelius

*The Roman officer whose household became the first Gentile home filled with the Holy Ghost.*

Cornelius was a Roman centurion stationed at Caesarea. He was not a Jew, but he feared God, prayed constantly, and gave generously to the poor. One afternoon an angel told him to send for Peter in Joppa. Meanwhile God was preparing Peter with a vision of unclean animals and a voice saying, "What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common." When Peter arrived, Cornelius had gathered his relatives and friends. As Peter preached Jesus, the Holy Ghost fell on them all. It was a turning point: God welcomes every nation on the same terms, through faith in Christ.

## The life of Cornelius

*Acts gives no year. The story comes after Paul's conversion and before Herod Agrippa I's death in AD 44, so it probably happened in the late AD 30s. The timeline follows the days as Acts counts them.*

**Before the vision · Caesarea — A devout soldier.** Cornelius is a centurion who fears God with all his household. He gives much alms to the people and prays to God continually. *Acts 10:1–2*

**Day 1 · The ninth hour — An angel appears.** About three in the afternoon, an angel tells Cornelius that his prayers and alms have come up before God. He is to send to Joppa for Simon Peter. He sends two servants and a soldier. *Acts 10:3–8*

**Day 2 · Joppa — Peter's vision.** At noon on the rooftop, Peter sees a sheet full of animals, clean and unclean, and hears, "What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common." Cornelius's messengers arrive at the gate. *Acts 10:9–23*

**Day 4 · Caesarea — Peter arrives.** Cornelius has gathered his relatives and close friends. He falls at Peter's feet, but Peter raises him up, saying, "I myself also am a man." Cornelius explains why he sent for him. *Acts 10:24–33*

**Day 4 · Caesarea — The Spirit falls.** While Peter is still preaching about Jesus, the Holy Ghost falls on all who hear. The Jewish believers are astonished. Peter orders them to be baptized in the name of the Lord. *Acts 10:34–48*

**Soon after · Jerusalem — Peter explains.** Some believers criticise Peter for eating with Gentiles. He tells the whole story, and they fall silent and glorify God, saying God has granted the Gentiles "repentance unto life." *Acts 11:1–18*

### The world around them

Caesarea was a port on the Mediterranean coast, built by Herod the Great and named after Caesar Augustus. It was the headquarters of the Roman governor of Judea, so soldiers were stationed there. A centurion commanded about a hundred men. A "band," or cohort, held several hundred. Jews called Gentiles like Cornelius "God-fearers" when they worshipped Israel's God without becoming full converts. Most Jews would not share a meal with Gentiles, which is why Peter's visit caused such a stir.

## Key activities

*Some scholars point out that the Italian cohort is known from inscriptions at a slightly later date. Others note that auxiliary units moved often, so this does not create a real difficulty.*

1. **Feared God with his household.** He led his whole house in the worship of the God of Israel. *Acts 10:2*
2. **Gave and prayed.** He "gave much alms to the people, and prayed to God alway." *Acts 10:2*
3. **Obeyed at once.** As soon as the angel left, he sent men to Joppa. *Acts 10:7–8*
4. **Gathered others to hear.** He called together his relatives and near friends to wait for Peter. *Acts 10:24*
5. **Listened with an open heart.** "Now therefore are we all here present before God, to hear all things that are commanded thee of God." *Acts 10:33*
6. **Received the Holy Ghost.** "While Peter yet spake these words, the Holy Ghost fell on all them which heard the word." *Acts 10:44*
7. **Was baptized.** He and his household were baptized in the name of the Lord and asked Peter to stay on for some days. *Acts 10:47–48*

### Words and places

- **Centurion.** A Roman officer in charge of about a hundred soldiers (Acts 10:1).
- **Alms.** Gifts of money or food to the poor (Acts 10:2, 4).
- **Common or unclean.** Food or people that the law of Moses treated as ritually impure (Acts 10:14–15).
- **Joppa.** A port about thirty miles south of Caesarea, modern Jaffa (Acts 10:5).
