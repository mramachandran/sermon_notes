# Apollos

*The eloquent teacher from Alexandria who was willing to learn, and who "watered" what Paul had planted.*

Apollos was a Jewish believer from Alexandria in Egypt, a gifted speaker who knew the Scriptures deeply. He arrived in Ephesus teaching boldly and accurately about Jesus, though he knew only the baptism of John. Priscilla and Aquila took him aside and explained the way of God more fully, and he accepted their teaching. He then went to Corinth, where he strengthened the believers and argued powerfully from Scripture that Jesus was the Christ. His gifts made him so popular that some Corinthians began to say, "I of Apollos." Paul answered that he and Apollos were only servants: one planted, one watered, but God gave the growth. Apollos is a picture of great gifts held with humility.

## The life of Apollos

*Dates are approximate (AD), during Paul's second and third journeys. Nothing is known of Apollos before Acts 18 or after Titus 3.*

**Early life · Alexandria — Trained in Scripture.** Born a Jew in Alexandria, Apollos becomes an eloquent speaker and "mighty in the scriptures." He is instructed in the way of the Lord. *Acts 18:24–25*

**c. AD 52 · Ephesus — Bold but incomplete.** "Fervent in the spirit," he teaches accurately about Jesus in the synagogue, though he knows only the baptism of John. *Acts 18:25–26*

**c. AD 52 · Ephesus — Taught more perfectly.** Priscilla and Aquila hear him, take him aside, and explain the way of God more fully. *Acts 18:26*

**c. AD 52–53 · Corinth — Waters the church.** With a letter of welcome from the believers in Ephesus, he crosses to Achaia. He greatly helps the believers and publicly shows from the Scriptures "that Jesus was Christ." *Acts 18:27–28; 19:1*

**c. AD 53–55 · Ephesus — His name used for a faction.** Some in Corinth claim, "I of Apollos." Paul, writing from Ephesus, calls Apollos a fellow servant. Apollos, apparently near Paul, is not willing to return to Corinth yet. *1 Corinthians 1:12; 3:4–9; 16:12*

**Later · Probably Crete — Still traveling.** Paul asks Titus to help "Zenas the lawyer and Apollos" on their journey and see that they lack nothing. *Titus 3:13*

### The world around them

Alexandria in Egypt was one of the greatest cities of the Roman world, famous for its library and its scholars. It had a very large Jewish community. The Greek translation of the Old Testament known as the Septuagint was begun there in the third century BC, and the Jewish thinker Philo taught there in Apollos's lifetime. Corinth prized skilled public speaking. That may help explain why some believers there rallied behind the eloquent Apollos (1 Corinthians 1:12; 2:1–4).

## Key activities

*Acts does not explain how Apollos could teach about Jesus while knowing only John's baptism. Compare the disciples Paul met at Ephesus soon after (Acts 19:1–7).*

1. **Mastered the Scriptures.** He was "mighty in the scriptures," well trained in the Old Testament. *Acts 18:24*
2. **Taught boldly.** He "spake and taught diligently the things of the Lord" and spoke out in the synagogue. *Acts 18:25–26*
3. **Received correction.** He accepted fuller teaching from Priscilla and Aquila. *Acts 18:26*
4. **Strengthened believers.** In Achaia he "helped them much which had believed through grace." *Acts 18:27*
5. **Argued in public.** He "mightily convinced the Jews, and that publickly," from the Scriptures. *Acts 18:28*
6. **Built on Paul's work.** Paul describes their work as one task: he planted, Apollos watered, and God made it grow. *1 Corinthians 3:5–9*
7. **Stayed away from Corinth for a time.** Paul urged him to go, but he was not willing then. Many think he wished to avoid feeding the divisions; the text does not say. *1 Corinthians 16:12*
8. **Kept serving.** He was still traveling in ministry in Paul's later years. *Titus 3:13*

### Words and places

- **Alexandria.** A great port city and center of learning in Egypt (Acts 18:24).
- **Baptism of John.** John the Baptist's baptism of repentance, preparing people for the Messiah (Acts 18:25; 19:4).
- **Achaia.** The Roman province of southern Greece, with Corinth as its capital (Acts 18:27).
- **Watered.** Paul's picture of Apollos building up a church Paul had planted (1 Corinthians 3:6).

### New Testament

- **Acts 18:24–28.** Apollos in Ephesus and Achaia
- **Acts 19:1.** Paul returns "while Apollos was at Corinth"
- **1 Corinthians 1:12; 3:4–6, 22.** Believers take sides; both are servants
- **1 Corinthians 4:6; 16:12.** Not "puffed up"; he declines to come for now
- **Titus 3:13.** Sent on his way well supplied
- **Tradition.** Luther suggested he wrote Hebrews; its author is unnamed and debated
