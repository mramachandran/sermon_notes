# Titus

*The Greek co-worker Paul trusted with hard places: a test case in Jerusalem, a peacemaker in Corinth, and a builder in Crete.*

Titus was a Gentile believer and one of Paul's most trusted partners. Acts never mentions him, so everything we know comes from Paul's letters. As a young man he went with Paul to Jerusalem, where the church leaders did not require him to be circumcised. That made him a living test of the gospel's welcome to Gentiles. Later Paul sent him to the troubled church at Corinth, and his return with good news lifted Paul out of deep distress. He then organized the collection for poor believers in Jerusalem. Finally Paul left him in Crete to appoint elders, and wrote him the letter that bears his name. Titus is a picture of a reliable servant sent into difficult places.

## The life of Titus

*Dates are approximate (AD). Galatians 2:1 may describe the famine visit (Acts 11:30) or the council (Acts 15), so the first date could be c. AD 46–49.*

**c. AD 46–49 · Jerusalem — A test case.** Paul takes Titus, a Greek, to Jerusalem with Barnabas. The leaders there do not compel him to be circumcised. Paul treats this as a defense of the gospel's freedom for Gentiles. *Galatians 2:1–5*

**c. AD 55–56 · Troas — Missed by Paul.** Anxious about Corinth, Paul finds an open door to preach at Troas. Yet he has "no rest in my spirit" because Titus has not arrived, so he moves on to Macedonia. *2 Corinthians 2:12–13*

**c. AD 55–56 · Macedonia — Brings good news.** Titus reaches Paul with news of the Corinthians' sorrow, repentance and renewed love for Paul. Paul is comforted, and Titus himself is refreshed by them. *2 Corinthians 7:5–16*

**c. AD 56 · Corinth — Completes the collection.** Paul sends Titus back to finish gathering the gift for the poor believers in Jerusalem. Titus goes eagerly, "of his own accord." *2 Corinthians 8:6, 16–23*

**Later · Crete — Setting things in order.** Paul leaves Titus in Crete to "set in order the things that are wanting, and ordain elders in every city." *Titus 1:5*

**Later · Nicopolis — Called to Paul.** Paul asks Titus to join him at Nicopolis, where Paul plans to spend the winter. *Titus 3:12*

**c. AD 64–67 · Dalmatia — Sent on.** In his final letter Paul notes that Titus has gone to Dalmatia. *2 Timothy 4:10*

### The world around them

Crete is a large island south of Greece. Cretans had a poor reputation in the ancient world, and Paul quotes one of their own prophets: "The Cretians are alway liars, evil beasts, slow bellies" (Titus 1:12). The line is usually credited to Epimenides, a Cretan poet of about 600 BC. Cretans were present at Pentecost (Acts 2:11). Paul sailed past Crete as a prisoner (Acts 27:7–13), but Acts records no mission there. Many who hold Paul's authorship think he visited after a release from his first Roman imprisonment.

## Key activities

*Acts never names Titus, and no one knows why. Everything we know about him comes from Paul's letters.*

1. **Stood as a test case.** As an uncircumcised Greek believer, he went to Jerusalem and was accepted. *Galatians 2:1–3*
2. **Handled a painful situation.** After a sorrowful letter from Paul, he went to Corinth and found the church repentant. *2 Corinthians 7:8–13*
3. **Comforted Paul.** God "comforted us by the coming of Titus," Paul wrote. *2 Corinthians 7:6–7*
4. **Loved the Corinthians.** His affection for them grew as he remembered how they received him. *2 Corinthians 7:15*
5. **Led the collection.** He began the gift for Jerusalem's poor and was sent back to finish it. *2 Corinthians 8:6*
6. **Handled money with integrity.** He traveled with trusted brothers so the gift was above suspicion. "Did Titus make a gain of you?" Paul asked. *2 Corinthians 8:16–23; 12:18*
7. **Organized the churches of Crete.** He appointed elders who met clear standards. *Titus 1:5–9*
8. **Taught and modeled sound teaching.** He was to teach every age group and to be "a pattern of good works." *Titus 2:1–8*
9. **Went where he was sent.** He was last seen heading to Dalmatia. *2 Timothy 4:10*

### Words and places

- **Crete.** A large island in the Mediterranean, south of Greece (Titus 1:5; Acts 27:7).
- **Elders.** Church leaders, also called bishops or overseers in this letter (Titus 1:5–7).
- **Nicopolis.** "City of victory," probably the town of that name in western Greece (Titus 3:12).
- **Dalmatia.** A Roman region on the eastern shore of the Adriatic Sea, in modern Croatia (2 Timothy 4:10).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references first. Echoes are study links; Titus is not named in the Old Testament.*

### Old Testament

- **Echo: Exodus 18:21.** Jethro: share the work with "able men"
- **Echo: 1 Samuel 12:3.** Samuel: "whose ox have I taken?"

### New Testament

- **Galatians 2:1–3.** A Greek not compelled to be circumcised
- **2 Corinthians 2:12–13; 7:5–16.** Paul's relief when Titus arrives
- **2 Corinthians 8:6, 16–23; 12:18.** "My partner and fellowhelper"
- **Titus 1:4–5.** Left in Crete; Paul's authorship of the letter is debated
- **Titus 3:12–13; 2 Timothy 4:10.** Called to Nicopolis; gone to Dalmatia
- **Tradition.** Eusebius: first bishop in Crete; later said to die there old
