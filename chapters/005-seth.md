# Seth

*The son given "instead of Abel," through whom the line of promise continued.*

Seth was the third son of Adam and Eve named in Scripture. He was born after Cain killed Abel, when the family's future seemed broken. Eve saw him as a gift from God, "another seed instead of Abel." Genesis says little about what Seth did. But his line matters: in the days of his son Enos, "then began men to call upon the name of the LORD." Through Seth the genealogy runs to Noah, then to Abraham, David and, in Luke's Gospel, to Jesus. Seth's story is a quiet reminder that God keeps his promises through ordinary faithful families.

## The life of Seth

*Ages come from Genesis 5 in the Hebrew text. The Greek Septuagint gives different numbers.*

**Adam's 130th year — Born.** Adam fathers a son "in his own likeness, after his image" and names him Seth. Eve says God has appointed him "instead of Abel, whom Cain slew." *Genesis 4:25; 5:3*

**Age 105 — Father of Enos.** Seth fathers Enos. In his days people begin to call on the name of the LORD. *Genesis 4:26; 5:6*

**The next 807 years — Raises a family.** Seth lives on and fathers sons and daughters. *Genesis 5:7*

**Age 912 — Dies.** "All the days of Seth were nine hundred and twelve years: and he died." *Genesis 5:8*

### The world around them

Genesis 5 is a genealogy, a family list. Ancient peoples kept such lists to show who belonged to whom and to trace inheritance and promises. Genesis uses them to follow one line through history, from Adam to Noah and later to Abraham. The ages in Genesis 5 are very long. Many Christians take them as literal years. Others see a style of honoring early generations that also appears in other ancient lists, such as the Sumerian King List. Either way, the repeated words "and he died" show that death now reigned, as God had warned.

## Key activities

*Genesis 6:2 speaks of "the sons of God" marrying "the daughters of men." One view says these were Seth's godly descendants marrying Cain's line. Others see them as angels or as powerful rulers. Genesis does not say.*

1. **Was received as God's gift.** Eve named him as the seed God appointed after Abel's death. *Genesis 4:25*
2. **Bore Adam's likeness.** He was born in Adam's likeness and image, echoing how Adam was made in God's likeness. *Genesis 5:1–3*
3. **Fathered Enos.** He named his son Enos, whose name means "man." *Genesis 4:26*
4. **Saw worship begin.** In his son's days, people began to call on the name of the LORD. *Genesis 4:26*
5. **Carried the line to Noah.** Genesis 5 traces the family from Seth through Enoch and Methuselah to Noah. *Genesis 5:6–32*

### Words and places

- **Genealogy.** A family list. The KJV often calls it "generations" (Genesis 5:1).
- **Begat.** KJV wording for "fathered" (Genesis 5:3).
- **Enos.** Seth's son. His name means "man" (Genesis 4:26).
- **Call upon the name of the LORD.** To worship and pray to God by name (Genesis 4:26; 12:8).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Seth.*

### Old Testament

- **Genesis 4:25–26** and **5:3–8.** These are the main passages about Seth.
- **Genesis 5:9–32.** His descendants run from Enos to Noah.
- **1 Chronicles 1:1.** The genealogy begins "Adam, Sheth, Enosh."
- **Echoes.** Calling on the name of the LORD becomes a mark of true worship. Abraham builds altars and calls on the name of the LORD (Genesis 12:8).

### New Testament

- **Luke 3:38.** Luke traces Jesus back "which was the son of Enos, which was the son of Seth, which was the son of Adam."
- **Echoes.** "Whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved" (Romans 10:13, quoting Joel 2:32).
