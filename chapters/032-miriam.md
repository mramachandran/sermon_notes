# Miriam

*The prophetess who led Israel's women in song beside the sea.*

Miriam was the sister of Moses and Aaron and the first woman in the Bible called a prophetess. Most readers take her to be the unnamed sister who watched over baby Moses on the Nile and boldly offered to find a nurse for him. After the crossing of the sea, she took a timbrel and led the women of Israel in dancing and praise. Micah later names her with her brothers as one of the three leaders God sent to Israel. But Miriam also spoke against Moses, and God struck her with leprosy for seven days. She died at Kadesh before Israel entered the land. Her life joins courage, worship and leadership with a sobering warning about envy.

## The life of Miriam

*Dates are approximate (BC) and follow the early Exodus date, c. 1446 BC. On the late date, move them about 185 years later. Exodus 2 does not name Moses' sister, but Miriam is the only sister Scripture gives him.*

**c. 1526 BC — Watches over her brother.** She stands at a distance to see what will happen to the baby in the basket. When Pharaoh's daughter finds him, she offers to call a Hebrew nurse and brings their mother. *Exodus 2:4–8*

**c. 1446 BC — Leads the song.** After Israel crosses the sea, "Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron" takes a timbrel. All the women follow her with timbrels and dances as she sings. *Exodus 15:20–21*

**c. 1445 BC · Hazeroth — Speaks against Moses.** She and Aaron criticize Moses because of his Cushite wife and ask whether God speaks only through him. God calls the three to the tabernacle and rebukes her and Aaron. *Numbers 11:35; 12:1–9*

**c. 1445 BC · Hazeroth — Struck and healed.** She becomes leprous, "white as snow." Moses cries out, "Heal her now, O God." She is shut outside the camp for seven days, and the people wait for her. *Numbers 12:10–16*

**c. 1406 BC · Probably the fortieth year — Dies at Kadesh.** Israel comes to the wilderness of Zin in the first month. Miriam dies at Kadesh and is buried there. *Numbers 20:1*

### The world around them

In ancient Israel, women often celebrated victories with drums and dancing. Jephthah's daughter met him with timbrels, and women sang of Saul and David after Goliath fell (Judges 11:34; 1 Samuel 18:6–7). Many scholars think the Song of the Sea is one of the oldest poems in the Bible. The Old Testament names other prophetesses, such as Deborah and Huldah (Judges 4:4; 2 Kings 22:14). The "Cushite woman" of Numbers 12 (the KJV says "Ethiopian woman") is debated. Some think she was Zipporah, since Cush may have included a region near Midian. Others think Moses had married a second wife from Cush, south of Egypt.

## Key activities

*The year of Miriam's death is not stated. Numbers 20:1 names only "the first month," but it comes just before Aaron's death in the fortieth year, so most scholars place it in that year.*

1. **Kept watch.** As a girl she watched over her baby brother in the reeds. *Exodus 2:4*
2. **Spoke up at the right moment.** She asked Pharaoh's daughter, "Shall I go and call to thee a nurse of the Hebrew women?" *Exodus 2:7–8*
3. **Led worship.** She led the women in praise with timbrels and dancing after the sea crossing. *Exodus 15:20–21*
4. **Served as a leader.** God said he sent "Moses, Aaron, and Miriam" before his people. *Micah 6:4*
5. **Spoke against Moses.** She and Aaron questioned his marriage and his unique standing. She is named first, and she alone is punished. *Numbers 12:1–10*
6. **Was restored.** After seven days outside the camp she was brought back in, and Israel did not move on until she returned. *Numbers 12:14–15*

*Tradition.* Jewish tradition tells of "Miriam's well," a spring that followed Israel in the wilderness because of her merit. It links Numbers 20:1–2, where the water fails right after her death. Scripture does not say this.

### Words and places

- **Prophetess.** A woman who spoke God's word. Miriam is the first so named (Exodus 15:20).
- **Timbrel.** A hand drum or tambourine used in celebration (Exodus 15:20; Psalm 150:4).
- **Leprosy.** In the Bible, a term for several skin diseases, not only modern leprosy (Leviticus 13; Numbers 12:10).
- **Kadesh.** An oasis in the wilderness of Zin, south of Canaan, and Israel's main camp for many years (Numbers 13:26; 20:1).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Miriam.*

### Old Testament

- **Exodus 2:4–8.** The unnamed sister, usually taken to be Miriam
- **Exodus 15:20–21.** She leads the women in the song of victory
- **Numbers 12:1–15.** She speaks against Moses and is struck with leprosy
- **Numbers 20:1; 26:59.** Her death at Kadesh; her place in the family
- **Deuteronomy 24:9.** "Remember what the LORD thy God did unto Miriam"
- **Micah 6:4.** Sent with Moses and Aaron to lead Israel
- **1 Chronicles 4:17.** A different Miriam, of Judah

### New Testament

- Not named; "Mary" is the Greek form of her name.
- **Echo: Luke 1:46–55.** Mary sings of God's triumph over the proud
- **Echo: Revelation 15:2–3.** The redeemed sing "the song of Moses"
- **Echo: James 3:5–10.** A warning about the tongue
