# Balaam

*The hired prophet who came to curse Israel and could only bless.*

Balaam son of Beor was a famous seer from Pethor, near the Euphrates River. When Israel camped on the plains of Moab, Balak king of Moab offered him rich rewards to curse them. God told Balaam not to go, then allowed him to go but only to speak God's word. On the way, his donkey saw the angel of the LORD blocking the road and spoke to him. Three times Balak built altars, and three times Balaam blessed Israel instead. His fourth oracle foretold "a Star out of Jacob." Yet the New Testament remembers Balaam as a man who loved money. He later advised Moab to lead Israel into sin at Peor, and he died in battle.

## The life of Balaam

*Dates are approximate (BC) and follow the early Exodus date, with Israel on the plains of Moab c. 1406 BC. On the late date, move them about 185 years later. The order of events after Numbers 24 comes from Numbers 31.*

**c. 1406 BC · At Pethor — Summoned by Balak.** Moab is afraid of Israel. Balak sends elders with "the rewards of divination" to hire Balaam. God tells him, "Thou shalt not curse the people: for they are blessed." *Numbers 22:1–14*

**c. 1406 BC · At Pethor — Sent the second time.** Balak sends greater princes and promises great honor. Balaam says he cannot go beyond the LORD's word, yet asks again. God lets him go but only to speak what he is told. *Numbers 22:15–21*

**c. 1406 BC · On the road — The donkey and the angel.** The angel of the LORD blocks the road three times. The donkey turns aside and is beaten, then speaks. Balaam's eyes are opened, and he sees the angel with a drawn sword. *Numbers 22:22–35*

**c. 1406 BC · Hills of Moab — Blesses Israel.** From three high places Balak offers sacrifices, and three times Balaam blesses Israel. A fourth oracle foretells a star and sceptre from Israel. *Numbers 22:36–24:25*

**c. 1406 BC · Peor — Counsels sin.** Israel falls into immorality and idol worship with the women of Moab and Midian. Moses later says this happened "through the counsel of Balaam." *Numbers 25:1–3; 31:16*

**c. 1406 BC · Midian — Killed in battle.** Israel wars against Midian. Balaam son of Beor is killed with the sword along with the five kings of Midian. *Numbers 31:8; Joshua 13:22*

### The world around them

Kings in the ancient Near East often hired seers to read omens and to bless or curse their enemies before battle. Balaam was known for this work: Balak said, "he whom thou blessest is blessed, and he whom thou cursest is cursed" (Numbers 22:6). In 1967 archaeologists at Deir Alla in Jordan found a plaster inscription from about 800 BC. It tells of a vision of "Balaam son of Beor," a seer of the gods. It shows that his name was remembered in that region for centuries.

## Key activities

*Christians and Jews have long debated Balaam. Was he a true prophet of the LORD who went wrong, or a pagan diviner whom God used against his will? Readers also ask why God allowed him to go (Numbers 22:20) and then was angry that he went (Numbers 22:22).*

1. **Sought God's word.** He asked the LORD and at first refused to go. *Numbers 22:8–13*
2. **Kept the door open for reward.** He said he could not go beyond God's word "if Balak would give me his house full of silver and gold," yet he asked God a second time. *Numbers 22:18–19*
3. **Was rebuked by a donkey.** "The LORD opened the mouth of the ass," and Balaam finally saw the angel. *Numbers 22:28–31*
4. **Blessed instead of cursing.** He said, "How shall I curse, whom God hath not cursed?" *Numbers 23:7–10*
5. **Spoke of God's faithfulness.** He declared that God does not lie or change his mind. *Numbers 23:18–24*
6. **Foretold a coming ruler.** "There shall come a Star out of Jacob, and a Sceptre shall rise out of Israel." *Numbers 24:15–19*
7. **Taught Balak to trap Israel.** His counsel led Israel into sin at Baal-peor. *Numbers 31:16; Revelation 2:14*

### Words and places

- **Divination.** Seeking hidden knowledge through omens and signs, forbidden to Israel (Numbers 22:7; Deuteronomy 18:10–12).
- **Pethor.** Balaam's hometown near the Euphrates, several hundred miles from Moab (Numbers 22:5).
- **High places.** Hilltop sites for altars and sacrifice (Numbers 22:41; 23:14).
- **Baal-peor.** A god of Moab worshipped at Peor, where Israel sinned (Numbers 25:3).

### Read it yourself

- **Day 1.** Numbers 22:1–35. What did Balaam want, and what did God want?
- **Day 2.** Numbers 23:1–24. What do Balaam's blessings reveal about God?
- **Day 3.** 2 Peter 2:12–16. How does Peter sum up Balaam's life?

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Balaam.*

### Old Testament

- **Numbers 22–24.** The main account
- **Numbers 31:8, 16.** His death and his role at Peor
- **Deuteronomy 23:4–5.** God "turned the curse into a blessing"
- **Joshua 13:22; 24:9–10.** A soothsayer God would not listen to
- **Nehemiah 13:2; Micah 6:5.** Balak's plot and Balaam's answer recalled

### New Testament

- **2 Peter 2:15–16.** He "loved the wages of unrighteousness"
- **Jude 11.** "The error of Balaam for reward"
- **Revelation 2:14.** He "taught Balac to cast a stumblingblock"
- **Echo: Revelation 22:16.** Many see Numbers 24:17's star fulfilled in Jesus

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What did Balaam's donkey see that Balaam could not (Numbers 22:22–31)?
2. **Understand:** Balaam spoke true words yet is remembered as a warning. What does that teach about gifts and character?
3. **Apply:** Is there a decision where you keep hoping God will change his mind?
4. **Apply:** Where might money or approval be pulling you away from what is right?
