# Ehud

*The left-handed Benjamite who brought a hidden dagger and a message from God to the king of Moab.*

Ehud was the second judge of Israel. After Othniel died, Israel again did evil, and God strengthened Eglon king of Moab against them for eighteen years. When the people cried out, God raised up Ehud, a left-handed Benjamite. Sent with Israel's tribute to Eglon, he strapped a two-edged dagger to his right thigh. Alone with the king, he said, "I have a message from God unto thee," and struck him down. Then he escaped, rallied Israel, and won a victory that brought eighty years of rest.

## The life of Ehud

*Judges gives no calendar dates. On the early chronology the judges span c. 1375–1050 BC. Ehud follows Othniel, but any date for him is rough.*

**Eighteen years — Moab rules Israel.** Israel does evil again. Eglon of Moab, joined by Ammon and Amalek, defeats Israel and takes the city of palm trees. *Judges 3:12–14*

**When Israel cries — Raised up.** God raises up Ehud, who is chosen to bring Israel's present to Eglon. He makes a dagger a cubit long and hides it on his right thigh. *Judges 3:15–18*

**In the summer parlour — Strikes Eglon.** Ehud returns alone from the quarries by Gilgal. Claiming a secret errand and a message from God, he kills the king and locks the doors behind him. *Judges 3:19–23*

**While the servants wait — Escapes.** The servants think the king is resting and wait until they are ashamed. By then Ehud has passed the quarries and fled to Seirath. *Judges 3:24–26*

**At the fords of Jordan — Wins the battle.** He blows a trumpet in mount Ephraim and leads Israel down. They seize the fords and kill about ten thousand Moabites. *Judges 3:27–29*

**Eighty years — Rest.** Moab is subdued, and the land has rest for eighty years. *Judges 3:30*

### The world around them

Moab lay east of the Dead Sea. Its people were descended from Lot (Genesis 19:37), and Moab had tried to curse Israel through Balaam (Numbers 22–24). "The city of palm trees" usually means Jericho (Deuteronomy 34:3), so Eglon had crossed the Jordan and planted himself in Benjamin's territory. Conquered peoples paid tribute, a regular "present" of goods or money, to the ruling king. Most men were right-handed and drew a blade from the left side, so a dagger on the right thigh was probably less likely to be noticed.

## Key activities

*This is one of the most vivid and even darkly humorous stories in Scripture. Readers differ on whether Ehud's deception should be seen as approved, or simply reported. The text says God raised him up, but it does not comment on each of his methods.*

1. **Answered God's call.** He was the deliverer God raised up when Israel cried out. *Judges 3:15*
2. **Made a weapon.** He made a two-edged dagger "of a cubit length" and girded it under his clothes. *Judges 3:16*
3. **Delivered the tribute.** He presented Israel's gift to Eglon, "a very fat man." *Judges 3:17–18*
4. **Went back alone.** At the quarries by Gilgal he turned back and asked to see the king privately. *Judges 3:19*
5. **Struck the king.** He said, "I have a message from God unto thee," and thrust the dagger into Eglon's belly. *Judges 3:20–22*
6. **Rallied Israel.** He told them, "Follow after me: for the LORD hath delivered your enemies the Moabites into your hand." *Judges 3:27–28*
7. **Held the fords.** Israel seized the Jordan crossings, and not a Moabite escaped. *Judges 3:28–29*

### Words and places

- **Lefthanded.** Literally "restricted in his right hand." Benjamin, meaning "son of the right hand," produced many skilled left-handed fighters (Judges 20:16; 1 Chronicles 12:2).
- **Cubit.** The length from elbow to fingertip. The word used for Ehud's cubit is unusual and may mean a shorter measure (Judges 3:16).
- **Quarries.** The KJV margin reads "graven images." The Hebrew can mean carved stones or idols near Gilgal (Judges 3:19, 26).
- **Summer parlour.** A cool upper room on the roof (Judges 3:20).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Ehud.*

### Old Testament

- **Judges 3:12–30.** The main account
- **Judges 3:31.** Shamgar follows him, killing six hundred Philistines
- **Judges 4:1.** Israel again did evil "when Ehud was dead"
- **1 Chronicles 7:10; 8:6.** Benjamites named Ehud; unclear if either is the judge
- **Echo: 1 Samuel 9:1–2; 14:1–14.** Saul and Jonathan, later Benjamite fighters
- **Echo: Judges 6:15.** God chooses the overlooked, like Gideon

### New Testament

- Ehud is not named in the New Testament.
- **Echo: Hebrews 4:12.** God's word "sharper than any twoedged sword"
- **Echo: 1 Corinthians 1:27.** God chooses "the weak things of the world"
