# Ruth

*The foreign widow whose loyalty placed her in the family line of King David.*

Ruth was a Moabite, an outsider to Israel, who lost her husband and had every reason to go home. Instead she bound herself to her bitter, grieving mother-in-law and to Naomi's God. In the fields of Bethlehem her quiet faithfulness met the kindness of Boaz, a kinsman who redeemed the family's land and married her. Her son became the grandfather of David.

## The life of Ruth

*The book gives places and seasons rather than exact years. The date is approximate.*

**c. 1100 BC · Days of the judges — Famine drives a family to Moab.** Elimelech of Bethlehem moves to Moab with his wife Naomi and their sons Mahlon and Chilion. *Ruth 1:1–2*

**In Moab · About 10 years — Marriage and loss.** Ruth marries Mahlon; Orpah marries Chilion. Elimelech dies, then both sons, leaving three widows. *Ruth 1:3–5; 4:10*

**On the road — The great choice.** Naomi urges both daughters-in-law to go home. Orpah kisses her and returns; Ruth clings to Naomi and vows to stay. *Ruth 1:6–18*

**Bethlehem · Barley harvest — Arrival.** The town stirs at their return. Naomi asks to be called Mara, "bitter," for the Almighty has dealt bitterly with her. *Ruth 1:19–22*

**Boaz's field · Barley and wheat harvests — Gleaning.** Ruth gleans behind the reapers. Boaz, a relative of Elimelech, protects her and lets extra grain fall for her. *Ruth 2:1–23*

**Threshing floor · End of harvest — A request at midnight.** On Naomi's advice Ruth lies at Boaz's feet and asks him to spread his covering over her as near kinsman. *Ruth 3:1–18*

**City gate · The next day — Redeemed.** A nearer kinsman declines. Boaz buys Elimelech's land before the elders and takes Ruth as his wife. *Ruth 4:1–12*

**Bethlehem · Later — A son for Naomi.** Ruth bears Obed. The women bless Naomi; Obed becomes the father of Jesse, the father of David. *Ruth 4:13–22*

## Key activities

*What Ruth did, in the order the book tells it.*

1. **Married into an Israelite family.** As a Moabite she joined a family from Bethlehem living in her country. *Ruth 1:4; 4:10*
2. **Stayed when she could have left.** Orpah went back to her people; Ruth clung to Naomi. *Ruth 1:14*
3. **Chose Naomi's God.** She vowed to share Naomi's people, her God, and even her grave. *Ruth 1:16–17*
4. **Took the initiative to provide.** She asked to glean in the fields so the two widows could eat. *Ruth 2:2*
5. **Worked from morning to evening.** The overseer noticed she had barely rested since dawn. *Ruth 2:7, 17*
6. **Answered kindness with humility.** She bowed before Boaz and asked why a foreigner had found favour. *Ruth 2:10, 13*
7. **Shared what she was given.** She brought home her grain and saved food from her meal for Naomi. *Ruth 2:14, 18*
8. **Trusted Naomi's counsel.** "All that thou sayest unto me I will do." *Ruth 3:5*
9. **Asked Boaz to redeem.** She appealed to his role as near kinsman, and he called her a virtuous woman. *Ruth 3:9–11*
10. **Became a mother in David's line.** Her son Obed was the grandfather of Israel's greatest king. *Ruth 4:13, 17*

## Across Scripture

*Direct references first. Echoes are study links, not places that name Ruth.*

### Old Testament

- **Ruth 1–4.** The whole of her story
- **Genesis 19:36–37.** Where the Moabites came from
- **Deuteronomy 23:3.** Moabites barred from Israel's assembly
- **Leviticus 19:9–10.** The gleaning law that fed her
- **Deut 25:5–10; Lev 25:25.** Kinsman duties and redeeming land
- **1 Chronicles 2:11–15.** Boaz to David; Ruth is not named

### New Testament

- **Matthew 1:5.** One of four women named in Jesus' line
- **Luke 3:32.** Boaz and Obed appear; Ruth does not
- **Echo: Ephesians 2:12–19.** Outsiders brought near and made fellow citizens
- **Echo: Galatians 4:4–5.** Christ as the one who redeems

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What reasons did Naomi give for sending her daughters-in-law home (Ruth 1:8–13)?
2. **Understand:** Why do you think Ruth stayed when Orpah, just as loving, went back?
3. **Apply:** Ruth's faith showed up as hard work and loyalty. Where could yours show up that way this week?
4. **Apply:** Who is an "outsider" in your church or neighbourhood you could welcome as Boaz welcomed Ruth?
