# Joab

*David's fierce and loyal general, who won his battles and settled his scores with the sword.*

Joab was David's nephew and commander of his army for most of his reign. He was brave, shrewd and fiercely loyal to David's kingdom. He captured Jerusalem's stronghold, led Israel against the Ammonites and Syrians, and told David hard truths. But Joab also did as he pleased. He murdered two rival commanders, Abner and Amasa, arranged Uriah's death at David's order, and killed Absalom against David's command. In the end he backed the wrong son for the throne, and Solomon had him executed. Joab shows that usefulness is not the same as obedience to God.

## The life of Joab

*Dates are approximate (BC). Joab served through David's whole reign, c. 1010–970 BC, and was killed early in Solomon's reign.*

**c. 1010 BC · David at Hebron — Battle at Gibeon.** Joab leads David's men against Abner and Saul's forces at the pool of Gibeon. His swift brother Asahel chases Abner, and Abner kills him. *2 Samuel 2:12–32*

**David at Hebron — Murders Abner.** Abner comes to make peace with David. Joab calls him aside in the gate of Hebron and kills him to avenge Asahel. David mourns Abner publicly. *2 Samuel 3:22–39*

**c. 1003 BC — First up at Jerusalem.** David promises that whoever first strikes the Jebusites will be chief. Joab goes up first and becomes commander of the army. *1 Chronicles 11:4–6; 2 Samuel 8:16*

**Ammonite wars — Rabbah and Uriah.** Joab besieges Rabbah. At David's written order, he sets Uriah where the fighting is fiercest, and Uriah is killed. Then he calls David to come and take the city. *2 Samuel 11:1–25; 12:26–29*

**Absalom's revolt — Kills Absalom.** Joab arranges Absalom's return from exile. Later, when Absalom rebels and is caught in an oak, Joab kills him despite David's order to deal gently with him. *2 Samuel 14:1–33; 18:1–17*

**After the revolt — Kills Amasa.** David makes Amasa commander in Joab's place. Joab greets Amasa as a brother and stabs him, then ends Sheba's revolt at Abel. *2 Samuel 19:13; 20:4–22*

**c. 970 BC — Backs Adonijah and dies.** Joab supports Adonijah's bid for the throne. When Solomon is crowned, Joab flees to the altar, and Benaiah kills him there at Solomon's command. *1 Kings 1:7; 2:28–34*

### The world around them

Kings in the ancient Near East depended on their army commanders, who could make or break a throne. Blood revenge was also part of that world: a close relative could see it as his duty to avenge a killing. The law gave cities of refuge to limit it (Numbers 35:9–28). Grasping the horns of the altar was a plea for mercy. But the law said a deliberate murderer could be taken even from God's altar (Exodus 21:14).

## Key activities

*David's last charge to Solomon about Joab named the killings of Abner and Amasa: he "shed the blood of war in peace" (1 Kings 2:5–6). Joab's victories in battle could not cover what he did in peacetime.*

1. **Took Jerusalem's stronghold.** He led the attack on the Jebusites and became chief. *1 Chronicles 11:6*
2. **Avenged his brother.** He killed Abner "for the blood of Asahel his brother," leaving David to say, "these men the sons of Zeruiah be too hard for me." *2 Samuel 3:27, 39*
3. **Fought with faith and courage.** Facing Ammonites and Syrians on two sides, he divided the army with Abishai and trusted the outcome to the LORD. *2 Samuel 10:9–14*
4. **Carried out David's evil order.** He placed Uriah in the hottest battle and let him die. *2 Samuel 11:14–17*
5. **Worked to bring Absalom home.** He sent a wise woman from Tekoa to persuade David with a story. *2 Samuel 14:1–23*
6. **Killed the king's son.** He thrust three darts into Absalom as he hung in the oak. *2 Samuel 18:9–15*
7. **Rebuked a grieving king.** He told David his mourning was shaming the men who had saved his life. *2 Samuel 19:1–8*
8. **Warned against the census.** He asked why David wanted to number the people, but David overruled him. *2 Samuel 24:1–4*

### Words and places

- **Zeruiah.** David's sister and Joab's mother. Her three sons are usually called by her name (1 Chronicles 2:16).
- **Rabbah.** The Ammonite capital, modern Amman in Jordan (2 Samuel 11:1).
- **Horns of the altar.** Corner projections on the altar. Holding them was a plea for mercy (1 Kings 2:28).
- **Abel of Beth-maachah.** A northern town where a wise woman ended Sheba's revolt (2 Samuel 20:14–22).

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** List the men Joab killed with his own hand (2 Samuel 3:27; 18:14; 20:10). What reason lay behind each?
2. **Understand:** Joab was loyal to David's kingdom but often disobeyed David. What is the difference between being useful and being faithful?
3. **Apply:** Joab carried out an order he knew was wrong. Where might you be asked to go along with something wrong at work or at home?
4. **Apply:** Joab settled his grudges himself. What grudge do you need to hand over to God this week?
