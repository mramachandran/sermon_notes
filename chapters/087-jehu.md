# Jehu

*The furious charioteer who wiped out Ahab's house and Baal worship, but never gave God his whole heart.*

Jehu was an army commander when a young prophet sent by Elisha anointed him king, to carry out God's judgment on the house of Ahab. He acted with frightening speed. In days he killed King Joram of Israel and King Ahaziah of Judah, had Jezebel thrown from a window, and saw to the deaths of Ahab's seventy sons. Then he trapped Baal's worshippers in one temple and destroyed them. God commended him. Yet Jehu kept Jeroboam's golden calves, and nearly a century later Hosea announced judgment for the bloodshed at Jezreel.

## The life of Jehu

*Dates are approximate (BC) and follow the house chronology. 2 Kings 9–10 appears to cover only a few days or weeks.*

**Years before — Named by God.** At Horeb, God tells Elijah to anoint "Jehu the son of Nimshi" king over Israel. The task passes to Elisha's circle years later. *1 Kings 19:15–17*

**c. 841 BC — Anointed at Ramoth-gilead.** A young prophet sent by Elisha takes Jehu into an inner room and pours oil on his head. He charges him to strike down the house of Ahab. The captains proclaim him king. *2 Kings 9:1–13*

**Soon after — Kills two kings.** Jehu drives furiously to Jezreel. He shoots Joram of Israel through the heart and has his body thrown into Naboth's field. Ahaziah of Judah is wounded as he flees and dies at Megiddo. *2 Kings 9:14–29*

**That day — Jezebel's death.** At Jezreel, Jezebel mocks him from a window. Jehu calls out, "Who is on my side? who?" Her servants throw her down, and Elijah's word about the dogs is fulfilled. *2 Kings 9:30–37*

**The following days — Ends Ahab's line.** He writes to the leaders in Samaria, and they send him the heads of Ahab's seventy sons. He also kills Ahab's officials, Ahaziah's relatives and all who remain of Ahab's house. *2 Kings 10:1–17*

**In Samaria — Destroys Baal worship.** Pretending to be a greater servant of Baal than Ahab, he gathers Baal's worshippers into their temple and has them killed. The temple is torn down. *2 Kings 10:18–28*

**c. 841–814 BC — Reigns 28 years.** He keeps Jeroboam's golden calves at Bethel and Dan. God begins to cut Israel short, and Hazael of Syria takes all the land east of the Jordan. *2 Kings 10:29–36*

### The world around them

Assyria was rising in the east. A black stone obelisk of the Assyrian king Shalmaneser III, now in the British Museum, shows a man bowing low, with tribute being brought. The inscription names "Jehu son of Omri." Most scholars take this as the biblical Jehu, or his envoy, in about 841 BC. The Assyrians named Israel after its best-known royal house, though Jehu had destroyed it. At the same time Hazael, the new king of Syria, was pressing hard on Israel's eastern lands.

## Key activities

*God commends Jehu for carrying out his word against Ahab's house (2 Kings 10:30), yet Hosea later condemns "the blood of Jezreel" (Hosea 1:4). Readers differ on how to hold these together. Many think Jehu did what God commanded but went beyond it, or did it for his own ends.*

1. **Accepted the call.** When his fellow officers asked why the prophet came, he told them, and they made him king on the spot. *2 Kings 9:11–13*
2. **Drove furiously.** The watchman knew him from a distance: "the driving is like the driving of Jehu the son of Nimshi; for he driveth furiously." *2 Kings 9:20*
3. **Fulfilled Elijah's word.** He had Joram's body cast into Naboth's field, recalling God's judgment for Naboth's blood. *2 Kings 9:24–26*
4. **Judged Jezebel.** He called for allies at the window, and she was thrown down. *2 Kings 9:32–37*
5. **Wiped out Ahab's house.** He left none of Ahab's family, great men or priests in Jezreel. *2 Kings 10:6–11*
6. **Boasted of his zeal.** He told Jehonadab, "Come with me, and see my zeal for the LORD." *2 Kings 10:15–16*
7. **Destroyed Baal worship.** He tricked the worshippers of Baal, killed them, and turned Baal's temple into a latrine. *2 Kings 10:18–28*
8. **Kept the calves.** He did not turn from the golden calves at Bethel and Dan. *2 Kings 10:29, 31*

### Words and places

- **Jezreel.** Ahab's royal city in the north, where Naboth's vineyard lay (1 Kings 21:1; 2 Kings 9:30).
- **Ramoth-gilead.** A fortified town east of the Jordan, often fought over with Syria (2 Kings 9:1).
- **Jehonadab son of Rechab.** Founder of the Rechabites, a family known for strict living (2 Kings 10:15; Jeremiah 35:6).
- **Jehu son of Hanani.** A different man: a prophet who rebuked Jehoshaphat (2 Chronicles 19:2).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Jehu.*

### Old Testament

- **1 Kings 19:16–17.** God names Jehu as the one who will judge
- **2 Kings 9–10.** The main account of his rise and reign
- **2 Chronicles 22:7–9.** God brought Ahaziah of Judah to Jehu
- **2 Kings 10:30; 15:12.** Four generations on the throne, promised and kept
- **Hosea 1:4.** God will avenge "the blood of Jezreel" on Jehu's house
- **Echo: Numbers 25:10–13.** Phinehas praised for zeal against idolatry

### New Testament

- Jehu is not named in the New Testament.
- **Echo: Romans 10:2.** Some have "a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge"
- **Echo: Matthew 15:8.** Honouring God with lips, the heart far away

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What did Jehu remove from Israel, and what did he leave in place (2 Kings 10:28–31)?
2. **Understand:** How can Jehu be commended by God (2 Kings 10:30) and still be condemned in Hosea 1:4?
3. **Apply:** Jehu said, "see my zeal for the LORD." When is your zeal for God really zeal for being seen?
4. **Apply:** Jehu cleared out Baal but kept the calves. What is one "calf" you have kept, a habit or loyalty you have never given up to God?
