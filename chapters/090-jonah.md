# Jonah

*The runaway prophet who learned that God's mercy is wider than his own.*

Jonah was a prophet of the northern kingdom in the days of King Jeroboam II. At home he brought good news: God would restore Israel's borders. But when God sent him to Nineveh, a great city of Assyria, Israel's fierce enemy, Jonah ran the other way. A storm, a great fish and a second call brought him to Nineveh at last. He preached, and the whole city repented. Instead of rejoicing, Jonah was angry, because he had known all along that God was gracious. The book ends with God's question to him, still unanswered. Jesus pointed to Jonah as a sign of his own death and resurrection, and to Nineveh as a people who repented when they heard.

## The life of Jonah

*Dates are approximate (BC). The book of Jonah does not say when he went to Nineveh. Christians differ on how to read it: most have read it as history, while some read it as a teaching story built around a real prophet.*

**c. 790–760 BC · Reign of Jeroboam II — A word of hope.** Jonah foretells that Israel's borders will be restored from the entering of Hamath to the Dead Sea. Jeroboam II wins them back, as the LORD had spoken. *2 Kings 14:23–27*

**Date uncertain — Called and runs.** God tells Jonah to go and cry against Nineveh. He goes down to Joppa, pays the fare, and boards a ship for Tarshish, fleeing "from the presence of the LORD." *Jonah 1:1–3*

**At sea — The storm.** A great wind threatens the ship while Jonah sleeps below. The lot falls on him. He tells the sailors to throw him overboard, and the sea grows calm. *Jonah 1:4–16*

**Three days and three nights — In the great fish.** The LORD prepares a great fish to swallow Jonah. From inside it he prays, and the fish casts him out on dry land. *Jonah 1:17–2:10*

**At Nineveh — The second call.** The word of the LORD comes a second time. Jonah walks into the great city and cries, "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown." *Jonah 3:1–4*

**At Nineveh — The city repents.** The people believe God, fast and put on sackcloth. The king sits in ashes and orders everyone to turn from violence. God relents and spares them. *Jonah 3:5–10*

**East of the city — Anger and the gourd.** Jonah is angry and asks to die. God grows a plant to shade him, then sends a worm to kill it, and asks whether Jonah does well to be angry. *Jonah 4:1–11*

## Key activities

*The Hebrew says "a great fish," not a whale. The King James New Testament says "whale's belly" (Matthew 12:40), translating a Greek word for a large sea creature. Tarshish was probably far to the west, perhaps in Spain.*

1. **Prophesied good news.** He spoke the word that Israel's lost borders would return, even under a king who did evil. *2 Kings 14:25–27*
2. **Ran from God.** Told to go east to Nineveh, he went west toward Tarshish. *Jonah 1:1–3*
3. **Slept through the storm.** While the pagan sailors prayed to their gods, he lay fast asleep in the ship. *Jonah 1:5–6*
4. **Confessed his faith.** He told the sailors, "I fear the LORD, the God of heaven, which hath made the sea and the dry land." *Jonah 1:8–10*
5. **Gave himself up.** He told them to throw him into the sea. They did, the storm stopped, and the sailors feared the LORD and made vows. *Jonah 1:11–16*
6. **Prayed from the deep.** Inside the fish he gave thanks and declared, "Salvation is of the LORD." *Jonah 2:1–9*
7. **Obeyed the second time.** He went to Nineveh and delivered a short, blunt warning. *Jonah 3:1–4*
8. **Saw a city repent.** From the king down, Nineveh turned from its evil ways, and God spared it. *Jonah 3:5–10*
9. **Complained about mercy.** He said he had fled because he knew God was "gracious ... and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness." *Jonah 4:1–3*
10. **Sulked outside the city.** He built a shelter and waited to see what would happen to Nineveh. *Jonah 4:5*
11. **Was questioned by God.** God used a plant, a worm and a hot east wind to show Jonah his own heart. *Jonah 4:6–11*

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Jonah.*

### Old Testament

- **2 Kings 14:23–27.** His only mention outside his book: home and prophecy
- **Jonah 1–4.** The full account of his mission to Nineveh
- **Jonah 4:2; Exodus 34:6; Joel 2:13.** Jonah quotes God's classic self-description
- **Nahum 1:1; 3:1–7.** Nineveh's fall foretold; it fell in 612 BC
- **Echo: 1 Kings 19:3–4.** Elijah also ran and asked to die

### New Testament

- **Matthew 12:39–41; Luke 11:29–32.** "The sign of the prophet Jonas"; "a greater than Jonas is here"
- **Matthew 16:4.** No sign but the sign of Jonas
- **Echo: Mark 4:35–41.** Jesus sleeps in a storm and calms the sea
- **Echo: Acts 10:5–35.** At Joppa, Peter is sent to Gentiles
- **Tradition.** A tomb of Jonah at Nebi Yunus, Mosul (tradition only)

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What reason did Jonah give for running away (Jonah 4:2)?
2. **Understand:** Why do you think the book ends with God's question instead of Jonah's answer?
3. **Apply:** Is there a person or group you would secretly rather God did not bless? What would it take to pray for them this week?
4. **Apply:** Jonah obeyed the second time. Where is God giving you a second chance to do something you avoided?
