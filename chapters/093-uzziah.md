# Uzziah

*A strong and gifted king whose pride carried him into the temple and out again a leper.*

Uzziah, also called Azariah, became king of Judah at sixteen and reigned fifty-two years, one of the longest reigns in Judah's history. For most of it he did what was right. He sought God, and God made him prosper. He won wars, rebuilt Elath, fortified Jerusalem, dug wells, and loved farming. But success went to his head. He walked into the temple to burn incense, a task God had given only to the priests, and he was struck with leprosy on the spot. He spent his last years shut away while his son ruled for him. The year he died, Isaiah saw the true King on his throne.

## The life of Uzziah

*Dates are approximate (BC). Many scholars think Uzziah reigned alongside his father Amaziah at first, and that Jotham took over the daily rule after Uzziah became a leper. Scripture does not date the incense incident.*

**c. 792 BC · Age 16 — Made king.** The people of Judah make Uzziah king in place of his father Amaziah. *2 Kings 14:21; 2 Chronicles 26:1, 3*

**Early reign — Rebuilds Elath.** He builds Elath on the Red Sea and restores it to Judah. *2 Kings 14:22; 2 Chronicles 26:2*

**In the days of Zechariah — Seeks God and prospers.** He seeks God while Zechariah, a man with "understanding in the visions of God," guides him. As long as he seeks the LORD, God makes him prosper. *2 Chronicles 26:4–5*

**His strong years — Wars and building.** He defeats the Philistines and Arabians, receives gifts from the Ammonites, builds towers, digs wells, and equips a large army with new machines. *2 Chronicles 26:6–15*

**c. 750 BC · Probably — Struck with leprosy.** Proud and strong, he enters the temple to burn incense. The priests resist him. As he rages, leprosy breaks out on his forehead. *2 Chronicles 26:16–20*

**c. 750–740 BC · Last years — Lives apart.** He lives in a separate house until his death, while his son Jotham rules the palace and judges the people. *2 Kings 15:5; 2 Chronicles 26:21*

**c. 740 BC · Age 68 — Dies.** He is buried in a field near the royal tombs, "for they said, He is a leper." That year Isaiah sees the Lord on his throne. *2 Chronicles 26:22–23; Isaiah 6:1*

### The world around them

During Uzziah's reign Judah and Israel were both strong, and Assyria was not yet pressing on them. Jeroboam II ruled the north in these same years. Amos, Hosea and Isaiah all began to prophesy while Uzziah was king (Amos 1:1; Hosea 1:1; Isaiah 1:1). A great earthquake struck in his reign and was remembered for centuries (Zechariah 14:5).

## Key activities

*The Zechariah who guided Uzziah is not the later prophet Zechariah. Only priests descended from Aaron could burn incense in the holy place (Exodus 30:7–8; Numbers 16:40).*

1. **Sought God.** He did right in the LORD's sight and sought God in the days of Zechariah. *2 Chronicles 26:4–5*
2. **Won back territory.** He fought the Philistines, broke down the walls of Gath, Jabneh and Ashdod, and built cities of his own. *2 Chronicles 26:6–8*
3. **Built and farmed.** He built towers in Jerusalem and the desert, dug many wells, and kept herds and vineyards, "for he loved husbandry." *2 Chronicles 26:9–10*
4. **Armed Judah.** He organized a trained army and made machines "invented by cunning men" to shoot arrows and great stones from the towers. *2 Chronicles 26:11–15*
5. **Grew proud.** Once he was strong, his heart was lifted up. *2 Chronicles 26:15–16*
6. **Took the priest's role.** He went into the temple to burn incense. Azariah and eighty priests told him it belonged only to the priests. *2 Chronicles 26:16–18*
7. **Raged and was struck.** He grew angry with the censer in his hand, and leprosy rose on his forehead. He hurried out, for the LORD had struck him. *2 Chronicles 26:19–20*
8. **Ended his days apart.** He lived as a leper, cut off from the house of the LORD, until he died. *2 Chronicles 26:21*

*Tradition.* A stone plaque from about the time of Jesus, found in Jerusalem in the 1930s, says in Aramaic that the bones of Uzziah king of Judah were brought there and must not be opened.

### Words and places

- **Azariah.** Uzziah's other name, used mostly in Kings (2 Kings 15:1–7). A priest named Azariah also confronted him (2 Chronicles 26:17).
- **Elath.** A port on the Gulf of Aqaba, on the Red Sea (2 Kings 14:22).
- **Husbandry.** Farming (2 Chronicles 26:10).
- **Several house.** A separate house, where he lived apart as a leper (2 Kings 15:5).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references first. Echoes are study links, not places that name Uzziah.*

### Old Testament

- **2 Kings 14:21–22; 15:1–7.** The short account, where he is called Azariah
- **2 Chronicles 26.** The full account, including his pride and leprosy
- **2 Chronicles 26:22.** Isaiah wrote his acts; that record is lost
- **Isaiah 1:1; 6:1.** Isaiah dates his ministry and vision by Uzziah
- **Amos 1:1; Hosea 1:1; Zechariah 14:5.** Other prophets date events by him
- **Echo: 1 Samuel 13:8–14.** Saul offered a sacrifice not his to offer
- **Echo: Numbers 16:1–40.** Korah's men took censers and died

### New Testament

- **Matthew 1:8–9.** "Ozias" in the royal line of Jesus
- **Echo: James 4:6.** "God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble"
- **Echo: Hebrews 7:1–3; 8:1.** Jesus, King and true high priest

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** How long did Uzziah prosper, according to 2 Chronicles 26:5?
2. **Understand:** Why do you think success made Uzziah proud instead of grateful?
3. **Apply:** Uzziah's trouble started when he was strong. Where are you most at risk of pride?
4. **Apply:** Who is a Zechariah for you, helping you seek God? How could you stay close to them?
