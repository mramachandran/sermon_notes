# Micah

*A country prophet who named the sins of the powerful and pointed to a ruler from Bethlehem.*

Micah came from Moresheth, a small town in the lowlands of Judah, far from the palaces of Jerusalem. He prophesied in the days of Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, at the same time as Isaiah. He saw what the powerful were doing to ordinary families: seizing fields and houses, bribing judges, and hiring prophets to say what they wanted to hear. Micah warned that Samaria would fall and that even Jerusalem would be ploughed like a field. A century later, his words were remembered at Jeremiah's trial, because King Hezekiah had listened and repented. Micah also foretold a ruler from little Bethlehem, and he summed up God's will in one of the Bible's best-known sentences: do justly, love mercy, walk humbly with God.

## The life of Micah

*Dates are approximate (BC). Micah does not date his individual messages, so the order below is partly a best guess.*

**Before his call · At Moresheth — A man from the lowlands.** Micah grows up in a farming town southwest of Jerusalem, close to the border with the Philistines. *Micah 1:1, 14*

**c. 735 BC · Days of Jotham — The word comes.** The word of the LORD comes to Micah concerning Samaria and Jerusalem. *Micah 1:1*

**Before 722 BC · Warning to the north — Samaria will fall.** He announces that Samaria will become a heap in the field, and that the same judgment is coming to the gate of Jerusalem. *Micah 1:2–9*

**c. 715–701 BC · Days of Hezekiah — Zion will be ploughed.** He warns that Jerusalem will be ploughed like a field for its leaders' sins. Hezekiah fears the LORD and prays, and the disaster is held back. *Micah 3:9–12; Jeremiah 26:18–19*

**Undated — A ruler from Bethlehem.** He foretells that a ruler in Israel will come from Bethlehem Ephratah, "whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting." *Micah 5:2–5*

**c. 609 BC · About a century later — Quoted to save Jeremiah.** When priests and prophets demand Jeremiah's death, some elders recall Micah's words and Hezekiah's response. *Jeremiah 26:1, 16–19, 24*

### The world around them

The lowland towns Micah lists in chapter 1 lay on the road armies used to reach Jerusalem. Lachish, one of Judah's strongest cities, stood nearby (Micah 1:13). In 701 BC the Assyrian king Sennacherib swept through this region. His own records claim he captured forty-six of Judah's walled cities, and carvings from his palace show the siege of Lachish. For Micah's neighbours, his warnings were close to home.

## Key activities

*Micah 4:1–3 closely matches Isaiah 2:2–4. Scholars differ on whether one prophet borrowed from the other or both used an older saying. Micah is also a different man from Micaiah, who spoke to King Ahab (1 Kings 22).*

1. **Lamented his people's ruin.** He said he would wail and go stripped and barefoot because Judah's wound was incurable. *Micah 1:8–9*
2. **Exposed land grabs.** He condemned those who lie awake planning evil and at morning light seize fields and houses. *Micah 2:1–2*
3. **Confronted leaders.** He rebuked rulers who judged for bribes, priests who taught for hire, and prophets who divined for money. *Micah 3:1–11*
4. **Spoke with God's power.** Unlike the false prophets, he said he was full of power by the Spirit of the LORD, to declare Israel's sin. *Micah 3:5–8*
5. **Promised peace.** He foresaw a day when nations would "beat their swords into plowshares" and everyone would sit under his own vine and fig tree. *Micah 4:1–4*
6. **Pointed to Bethlehem.** He foretold a ruler from the small town of Bethlehem who would shepherd God's people. *Micah 5:2–4*
7. **Summed up God's will.** Not endless sacrifices, but justice, mercy and humble walking with God. *Micah 6:6–8*
8. **Waited for God.** "Therefore I will look unto the LORD; I will wait for the God of my salvation." *Micah 7:7*
9. **Praised God's mercy.** "Who is a God like unto thee, that pardoneth iniquity?" He will cast all their sins "into the depths of the sea." *Micah 7:18–19*

### Words and places

- **Morasthite.** A man from Moresheth (Micah 1:1; Jeremiah 26:18).
- **Bethlehem Ephratah.** Bethlehem in Judah, David's home town (Micah 5:2; Ruth 4:11).
- **High places.** Local shrines, often used for idol worship (Micah 1:5).
- **Remnant.** The survivors God keeps for himself after judgment (Micah 2:12; 5:7–8).

### Read it yourself

- **Day 1.** Micah 3:1–12. What was wrong with Judah's leaders?
- **Day 2.** Micah 5:2–5. What kind of ruler does Micah describe?
- **Day 3.** Micah 6:6–8 and 7:18–20. What does God want, and what is God like?

## Across Scripture

*Direct references first. Echoes are study links, not places that name Micah.*

### Old Testament

- **Micah 1–7.** The book of his prophecies
- **Jeremiah 26:17–19.** Elders quote Micah 3:12 and help save Jeremiah's life
- **Echo: Isaiah 2:2–4.** The same vision of peace, in the same years
- **Echo: Amos 5:24.** Justice to "run down as waters"

### New Testament

- **Matthew 2:5–6.** Micah 5:2 tells Herod where the Christ would be born
- **John 7:42.** The Christ comes "out of the town of Bethlehem"
- **Matthew 10:35–36.** Jesus uses Micah 7:6 on divided households
- **Echo: Matthew 23:23.** "Judgment, mercy, and faith," the weightier matters
- **Echo: Hebrews 8:12.** God remembers our sins no more

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What three things does God require in Micah 6:8?
2. **Understand:** Why do you think Micah links worship so closely with how people treat their neighbours?
3. **Apply:** Which of the three, justice, mercy or humility, is hardest for you right now? What would one step look like?
4. **Apply:** Micah's words changed a king. Whose words has God used to change your direction, and whom could you encourage this week?
