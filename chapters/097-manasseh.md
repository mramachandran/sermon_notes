# Manasseh

*Judah's most wicked king, who was humbled in chains and found that God still hears.*

Manasseh was the son of good King Hezekiah, but he undid almost everything his father had done. He became king at twelve and reigned fifty-five years, longer than any other king of Judah. He set up altars to Baal and the stars inside the temple, practised sorcery, made his children pass through the fire, and filled Jerusalem with innocent blood. Kings says his sins sealed Judah's fate. Yet Chronicles adds a surprising turn. Taken to Babylon in chains by the Assyrians, Manasseh humbled himself and prayed, and God brought him home. He spent his last years trying to repair the damage.

## The life of Manasseh

*Dates are approximate (BC). Many scholars think Manasseh reigned with his father from c. 697 BC and alone from c. 686 BC. Scripture does not say when he was taken to Babylon.*

**c. 697 BC · Age 12 — Becomes king.** Manasseh begins his reign of fifty-five years in Jerusalem. *2 Kings 21:1; 2 Chronicles 33:1*

**c. 686 BC · Sole king — Undoes his father's reforms.** He rebuilds the high places, raises altars to Baal, makes an Asherah pole, and worships the stars, even in the temple courts. *2 Kings 21:2–7; 2 Chronicles 33:2–7*

**During his reign — Deaf to the prophets.** The LORD warns through his prophets that he will wipe Jerusalem "as a man wipeth a dish." Manasseh and his people will not listen. *2 Kings 21:10–15; 2 Chronicles 33:10*

**During his reign — Innocent blood.** He sheds so much innocent blood that Jerusalem is filled with it from one end to the other. *2 Kings 21:16*

**Date unknown — Captive and humbled.** The captains of the king of Assyria bind him with fetters and carry him to Babylon. In distress he humbles himself and prays, and God restores him to Jerusalem. *2 Chronicles 33:11–13*

**After his return — Repairs the damage.** He removes the foreign gods from the temple, restores the LORD's altar, and commands Judah to serve the LORD. He also strengthens Jerusalem's walls. *2 Chronicles 33:14–17*

**c. 642 BC · Age 67 — Dies.** He is buried in the garden of his own house, and his son Amon reigns. *2 Kings 21:18; 2 Chronicles 33:20*

### The world around them

In Manasseh's day Assyria was at the height of its power. Assyrian records list "Manasseh king of Judah" among the kings who sent tribute and materials to the Assyrian kings Esarhaddon and Ashurbanipal. Babylon was then under Assyrian rule, so an Assyrian army taking a captive there makes sense. Some scholars link his captivity to a Babylonian revolt against Assyria in 652–648 BC, but this is only a suggestion.

## Key activities

*Kings does not mention Manasseh's repentance. It stresses that his sins were so great that judgment on Judah could not be turned back (2 Kings 23:26; 24:3–4). Chronicles adds his captivity and prayer. The two accounts stand side by side.*

1. **Rebuilt what his father tore down.** He restored the high places Hezekiah had destroyed. *2 Kings 21:3*
2. **Defiled the temple.** He built altars for the host of heaven in both temple courts and set a carved idol in the house of God. *2 Kings 21:4–7; 2 Chronicles 33:4–7*
3. **Gave his children to the fire.** He made his children pass through the fire in the valley of the son of Hinnom and turned to witchcraft and mediums. *2 Kings 21:6; 2 Chronicles 33:6*
4. **Led Judah astray.** He led the people to do more evil than the nations God had driven out. *2 Kings 21:9; 2 Chronicles 33:9*
5. **Shed innocent blood.** He "shed innocent blood very much, till he had filled Jerusalem from one end to another." *2 Kings 21:16*
6. **Humbled himself in chains.** In Babylon he "besought the LORD his God, and humbled himself greatly." *2 Chronicles 33:11–12*
7. **Knew the LORD was God.** God heard him and brought him home, and he learned who God really was. *2 Chronicles 33:13*
8. **Tried to undo the harm.** He removed the idols, restored true worship, and built up Jerusalem's defences. The people still sacrificed at the high places, though now to the LORD. *2 Chronicles 33:14–17*

*Tradition.* A short "Prayer of Manasseh" appears in the Apocrypha and in some Orthodox Bibles. It was written much later and is not part of the Hebrew Bible. Jewish and early Christian tradition also says Manasseh had Isaiah killed.

### Words and places

- **Host of heaven.** The sun, moon and stars, worshipped as gods (2 Kings 21:3, 5).
- **Valley of the son of Hinnom.** A valley south of Jerusalem where children were sacrificed. Its name later gave the word Gehenna (2 Chronicles 33:6).
- **Grove.** KJV word for an Asherah, a wooden pole or image for a Canaanite goddess (2 Kings 21:3, 7).
- **Familiar spirit.** A medium who claimed to speak with the dead (2 Kings 21:6).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references first. Echoes are study links, not places that name Manasseh.*

### Old Testament

- **2 Kings 21:1–18; 2 Chronicles 33:1–20.** The two accounts of his reign
- **2 Kings 23:26; 24:3–4.** Judah's fall traced to his sins and innocent blood
- **Jeremiah 15:4.** Exile "because of Manasseh the son of Hezekiah"
- **2 Chronicles 33:18–19.** His prayer recorded among the kings and seers
- **Echo: 1 Kings 21:27–29.** Ahab humbled himself, and judgment was delayed
- **Echo: Isaiah 1:18.** Sins as scarlet made "white as snow"

### New Testament

- **Matthew 1:10.** "Manasses" in the royal line of Jesus
- **Echo: Luke 15:17–24.** The prodigal comes to himself and goes home
- **Echo: 1 Timothy 1:15–16.** The chief of sinners, shown mercy as a pattern

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What happened to Manasseh in Babylon, and what did he do there (2 Chronicles 33:11–13)?
2. **Understand:** Manasseh was forgiven, yet the harm he did lived on after him. How do both of those things fit together?
3. **Apply:** Is there anyone you think is too far gone for God? How does Manasseh's story challenge that?
4. **Apply:** Manasseh tried to repair what he had broken. Is there something you need to put right, and what is the first step?
