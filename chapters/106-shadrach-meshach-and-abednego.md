# Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego

*Three young exiles who would not bow, and who found God with them in the fire.*

Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah were young men of Judah taken to Babylon with Daniel and renamed Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego. King Nebuchadnezzar set up a huge golden image and ordered everyone to bow. The three refused, with one of Scripture's bravest statements of faith: God is able to save us, "but if not," we still will not bow. Thrown into a blazing furnace, they walked in the fire with a fourth figure and came out without even the smell of fire.

## The life of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego

*Dates are approximate (BC). Daniel 3 gives no date for the golden image. It is not certain how long after Daniel 2 it took place.*

**c. 605 BC · As youths — Taken and renamed.** Nebuchadnezzar takes young nobles from Jerusalem to train for his court. Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah are among them, along with their friend Daniel. The chief official gives them Babylonian names: Shadrach, Meshach and Abed-nego. *Daniel 1:1–7*

**c. 605–602 BC · Three years of training — Faithful at the table.** With Daniel they ask for vegetables and water instead of the king's food. After ten days they look healthier than the others. At the end, the king finds them wiser than all his advisers. *Daniel 1:8–20*

**c. 603 BC · Nebuchadnezzar's 2nd year — Praying for mercy.** When the king orders all wise men killed, Daniel asks his three friends to pray. God reveals the dream. At Daniel's request, the king sets them over the affairs of the province of Babylon. *Daniel 2:17–18, 49*

**Date not given — They will not bow.** Accused by certain Chaldeans, the three refuse to worship the golden image. They tell the king that God is able to save them, "but if not," they still will not serve his gods. *Daniel 3:1–18*

**The same day — Through the fire.** Bound and thrown into a furnace so hot it kills the soldiers, they walk free with a fourth figure. They come out unharmed, and the king praises their God and promotes them. *Daniel 3:19–30*

### The world around them

Babylon took the brightest young captives and trained them for three years in "the learning and the tongue of the Chaldeans" (Daniel 1:4). That probably meant the old cuneiform writing and the sciences of the day, as well as the omens and dream books. Giving them new names that honoured Babylon's gods was part of making them Babylonian. Bowing to an image was a test of loyalty to the king as much as a religious act. Babylon was built of fired brick, so large kilns were common, and many think the "burning fiery furnace" was one of these.

## Key activities

*Nebuchadnezzar says the fourth figure in the fire was "like the Son of God" (Daniel 3:25, KJV). The Aramaic can also be read "a son of the gods," and the king later calls him God's "angel" (Daniel 3:28). Many Christians have seen here an appearance of Christ before his birth. Others see an angel sent by God.*

1. **Stayed faithful at the king's table.** With Daniel they asked to eat vegetables and drink water, and God blessed them with health and wisdom. *Daniel 1:11–17*
2. **Served well in Babylon.** They stood before the king, and later they managed the affairs of the province of Babylon. *Daniel 1:19–20; 2:49*
3. **Prayed in a crisis.** They sought mercy from the God of heaven when every wise man faced death. *Daniel 2:17–18*
4. **Refused to bow.** When the music played and the crowds fell down, they stayed on their feet. *Daniel 3:8–12*
5. **Answered the king calmly.** They said they did not need to defend themselves. God was able to deliver them, but even if he did not, they would not worship the image. *Daniel 3:16–18*
6. **Walked in the fire.** Bound and thrown into the furnace, they were seen walking loose with a fourth figure, unharmed. *Daniel 3:19–25*
7. **Came out unharmed.** Not a hair was singed, and "the smell of fire" had not passed on them. The king blessed their God. *Daniel 3:26–30*

### Words and places

- **Sackbut and psaltery.** KJV names for instruments in the king's band. In English a sackbut was an early trombone, but the Aramaic words probably mean harps or lyres (Daniel 3:5).
- **Hosen.** Old English for trousers or leggings. The men were thrown in fully clothed (Daniel 3:21).
- **Chaldeans.** Here, a group of the king's wise men who accused the Jews (Daniel 3:8).
- **Dura.** The plain in the province of Babylon where the image stood (Daniel 3:1).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references first. Echoes are study links, not places that name the three friends.*

### Old Testament

- **Daniel 1:6–7, 11, 19.** Named among the youths trained with Daniel
- **Daniel 2:17–18, 49.** They pray with Daniel and are promoted
- **Daniel 3:1–30.** The golden image and the furnace
- **Echo: Exodus 20:4–5.** God's law forbids bowing to any image
- **Echo: Isaiah 43:2.** "When thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned"

### New Testament

- The three friends are not named in the New Testament.
- **Hebrews 11:34.** Those who "quenched the violence of fire," often taken as them
- **Echo: Acts 5:29.** "We ought to obey God rather than men"
- **Echo: 1 Peter 4:12.** Do not be surprised at "the fiery trial"
- **Tradition.** The Greek "Song of the Three," in the Apocrypha, not the Hebrew text

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What exactly did the three friends say to the king in Daniel 3:16–18?
2. **Understand:** Why do you think "but if not" matters so much? What does it show about their faith?
3. **Apply:** Where do you feel pressure to "bow" to something at work, at school or online? What would standing firm look like?
4. **Apply:** The three faced the fire together. Who could you stand beside this week when their faith is being tested?
