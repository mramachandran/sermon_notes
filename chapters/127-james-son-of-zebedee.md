# James, son of Zebedee

*A "son of thunder" who asked for glory and was given a cup of suffering.*

James was a Galilean fisherman, the son of Zebedee and brother of John. With Peter and John he formed Jesus' inner circle, seeing a dead girl raised and Jesus transfigured. Jesus nicknamed the brothers "the sons of thunder." They once wanted to call down fire on a Samaritan village, and they asked for the best seats in Christ's kingdom. Jesus told them they would drink his cup, and for James that came true first. He is the only one of the Twelve whose martyrdom the New Testament records, killed by Herod Agrippa I in AD 44.

## The life of James, son of Zebedee

*Dates are approximate (AD). Jesus' ministry is dated c. AD 27/28–30 in this study. The date of James's death is firm, because Herod Agrippa I died in AD 44.*

**c. AD 28 · Sea of Galilee — Called from the boat.** Jesus finds James and John mending nets with their father Zebedee. They leave their father in the ship with the hired servants and follow Jesus. *Mark 1:19–20*

**c. AD 28 · On a mountain — Chosen and named.** Jesus appoints the Twelve and gives James and his brother the name Boanerges, "the sons of thunder." *Mark 3:13–17*

**c. AD 29 · On a high mountain — Sees the glory.** James, Peter and John watch Jesus transfigured, his clothes shining, as Moses and Elijah talk with him. *Mark 9:2–8*

**c. AD 30 · Road to Jerusalem — Asks for a throne.** James and John ask to sit at Jesus' right and left hand in his glory. Jesus asks if they can drink his cup, and they say they can. *Mark 10:35–40*

**AD 44 · Jerusalem — Killed by Herod.** King Herod Agrippa I turns against the church and has James put to death with the sword, the first of the Twelve to die for Christ. *Acts 12:1–2*

### The world around them

Zebedee's family seems to have been fairly well off. They owned at least one boat, hired workers, and worked in partnership with Simon and Andrew. Fishing on the Sea of Galilee fed a busy trade in fresh and salted fish. The "Herod" of Acts 12 is Herod Agrippa I, a grandson of Herod the Great. Rome made him king over Judea in AD 41. He courted popular favor, and he died suddenly at Caesarea in AD 44, an event recorded both in Acts 12:20–23 and by the historian Josephus.

## Key activities

*Matthew says the mother of James and John made the request for seats of glory, while Mark has the brothers ask. Both agree that Jesus answered the brothers directly.*

1. **Left his father and his nets.** He walked away from the family business to follow Jesus. *Mark 1:19–20; Matthew 4:21–22*
2. **Saw a dead girl raised.** Jesus let only Peter, James and John go in with Jairus's family. *Mark 5:37–43*
3. **Saw the Transfiguration.** He was one of three witnesses on the mountain. *Mark 9:2–8*
4. **Wanted fire from heaven.** When a Samaritan village refused Jesus, James and John asked if they should call down fire "even as Elias did." Jesus rebuked them. *Luke 9:51–56*
5. **Asked for glory.** He and John asked for the places of honor, and Jesus answered, "Ye know not what ye ask." *Mark 10:35–45; Matthew 20:20–28*
6. **Asked about the end.** With Peter, John and Andrew he asked Jesus privately about the temple's fall. *Mark 13:3–4*
7. **Was called to watch in Gethsemane.** Jesus took the three aside as he began to be sore amazed and very heavy, but they fell asleep. *Mark 14:32–41*
8. **Drank the cup.** He became the first apostle to die as a martyr. *Acts 12:1–2*

### Words and places

- **Zebedee.** James's father, a fisherman who stayed with the boat when his sons left (Mark 1:20).
- **Boanerges.** An Aramaic nickname Jesus gave to James and John, meaning "sons of thunder" (Mark 3:17).
- **The cup.** A picture of the suffering God gives someone to bear (Mark 10:38; 14:36).
- **Herod the king.** Herod Agrippa I, king of Judea AD 41–44 (Acts 12:1, 20–23).

## Group notes

*Tradition.* An early story in Eusebius says the man who brought James to trial was moved by his witness, confessed Christ, and died with him. A medieval tradition says James preached in Spain and that his bones lie at Santiago de Compostela. Scripture says nothing of either.

## Across Scripture

*Direct references first. Tradition, not Scripture: his accuser confessed Christ and died with him (Eusebius); a medieval story links him to Spain and Santiago de Compostela.*

### Old Testament

- James is not named in the Old Testament.
- **Echo: 2 Kings 1:9–12.** Elijah's fire, which the brothers wanted to copy
- **Echo: Isaiah 51:17.** "The cup" of God's fury, which Jesus drinks himself

### New Testament

- **Matthew 10:2; Mark 3:17; Acts 1:13.** Among the first names of the Twelve
- **Mark 5:37; 9:2; 14:33.** One of the three closest to Jesus
- **Mark 10:35–45.** "To give his life a ransom for many"
- **John 21:2.** "The sons of Zebedee" meet the risen Jesus
- **Acts 12:1–2.** His death, told in one verse
