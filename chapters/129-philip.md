# Philip

*The practical disciple who asked to see the Father and was shown the Son.*

Philip came from Bethsaida, the hometown of Peter and Andrew. Jesus found him and said simply, "Follow me." Philip at once went to find his friend Nathanael and told him they had found the one Moses and the prophets wrote about. When Nathanael scoffed, Philip did not argue. He said, "Come and see." John's Gospel shows Philip as a careful, practical man. He counts the cost of bread for a crowd. He passes a request from some Greeks on to Andrew. At the last supper he asks Jesus to show them the Father. Jesus' answer to Philip is one of the clearest claims in Scripture: "he that hath seen me hath seen the Father."

## The life of Philip

*Dates are approximate (AD). Jesus' ministry is dated c. AD 27/28–30 in this study. Only John's Gospel tells stories about Philip; the other Gospels and Acts only list him.*

**c. AD 27–28 · Toward Galilee — Called by Jesus.** The day after Andrew and Peter meet Jesus, Jesus goes to Galilee, finds Philip, and says to him, "Follow me." *John 1:43–44*

**c. AD 27–28 · Galilee — Brings Nathanael.** Philip tells Nathanael they have found Jesus of Nazareth, the one Moses and the prophets wrote of. When Nathanael doubts, Philip says, "Come and see." *John 1:45–46*

**c. AD 29 · Near the Sea of Galilee — Tested by a question.** Facing a hungry crowd, Jesus asks Philip where to buy bread. Philip reckons that two hundred pennyworth would not be enough for each to have a little. *John 6:5–7*

**c. AD 30 · Jerusalem — Approached by Greeks.** Some Greeks at the Passover come to Philip with a request: "Sir, we would see Jesus." He tells Andrew, and they tell Jesus. *John 12:20–22*

**c. AD 30 · The last supper — Asks to see the Father.** Philip says, "Lord, shew us the Father, and it sufficeth us." Jesus answers that whoever has seen him has seen the Father. *John 14:8–11*

### The world around them

Philip is a Greek name, and it was common in the region. Herod Philip, a son of Herod the Great, ruled the lands east and north of the Sea of Galilee, and Bethsaida lay near the border of his territory. Galilee had many Greek-speaking towns, and many Galileans probably spoke some Greek as well as Aramaic. This may be why the Greeks in John 12 came to Philip first, though John does not say so. Philip and Andrew are the only two of the Twelve known to us only by Greek names.

## Key activities

*John notes that Jesus asked Philip about bread "to prove him: for he himself knew what he would do" (John 6:6). The question was a test of faith, not a request for advice.*

1. **Followed at once.** He answered Jesus' simple call to follow. *John 1:43*
2. **Shared the news.** He told Nathanael that Jesus fulfilled the Law and the Prophets. *John 1:45*
3. **Invited a doubter.** Instead of arguing, he said to Nathanael, "Come and see." *John 1:46*
4. **Was chosen as an apostle.** He is named among the Twelve in every list. *Matthew 10:3; Mark 3:18; Luke 6:14; Acts 1:13*
5. **Counted the cost.** He calculated that even two hundred pennyworth of bread would not feed the crowd. *John 6:5–7*
6. **Passed on a request.** He brought the Greeks' wish to see Jesus through Andrew to Jesus. *John 12:20–22*
7. **Asked an honest question.** He asked to see the Father and heard Jesus say that to see him is to see the Father. *John 14:8–11*

### Words and places

- **Moses and the prophets.** A way of speaking of the whole Hebrew Scriptures (John 1:45; Luke 24:27).
- **Pennyworth.** The KJV "penny" translates the Roman denarius, about a day's pay for a laborer (John 6:7; Matthew 20:2).
- **Greeks.** Probably non-Jews who worshipped Israel's God and came up for the feast (John 12:20).
- **Bethsaida.** A fishing town at the north end of the Sea of Galilee (John 1:44).

### Read it yourself

- **Day 1.** John 1:43–51. How did Philip answer Nathanael's doubt?
- **Day 2.** John 6:1–14. What did Philip see, and what did he miss?
- **Day 3.** John 14:1–14. What does Jesus reveal about himself in answer to Philip?

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Philip.*

### Old Testament

- **Deuteronomy 18:15.** The prophet like Moses, perhaps in Philip's mind (John 1:45)
- **Echo: Exodus 33:18.** Moses asks to see God's glory; Philip asks to see the Father

### New Testament

- **John 1:43–46.** Called, and brings Nathanael
- **John 6:5–7; 12:21–22.** Tested about bread; brings the Greeks
- **John 14:8–9.** Asks to see the Father
- **Matthew 10:3; Mark 3:18; Luke 6:14; Acts 1:13.** Listed among the Twelve
- **Tradition.** Polycrates (in Eusebius) says he was buried at Hierapolis; perhaps confused with Philip the evangelist (Acts 21:8–9)

## For discussion

1. **Observe:** What three words did Philip say to his doubting friend (John 1:46)?
2. **Understand:** Why do you think Jesus tested Philip with a question he already knew the answer to (John 6:5–6)?
3. **Apply:** Philip counted what he had instead of trusting what Jesus could do. Where are you doing the math without Jesus?
4. **Apply:** Who in your life has honest doubts about Jesus, and how could you say "Come and see" to them this week?
