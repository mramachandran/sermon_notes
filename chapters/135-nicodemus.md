# Nicodemus

*The teacher of Israel who came to Jesus by night and stood by him in the end.*

Nicodemus was a Pharisee and "a ruler of the Jews," a respected teacher in Jerusalem. He appears only in John's Gospel, and only three times. First he comes to Jesus by night and hears that he must be "born again." Later he speaks up for Jesus in the council and is mocked for it. Last, he joins Joseph of Arimathea in burying Jesus with a costly load of spices. John never says when Nicodemus came to faith, but his three scenes trace a man moving slowly out of the shadows and into the open.

## The life of Nicodemus

*Dates are approximate (AD) and follow the house chronology, with the crucifixion c. AD 30. On the other main view (AD 33), add about three years.*

**c. AD 28–29 · Passover — Comes by night.** At Passover in Jerusalem many believe when they see Jesus' miracles. Nicodemus comes to Jesus by night and calls him "a teacher come from God," because no one could do such signs unless God were with him. *John 2:23–3:2*

**The same night — Hears of new birth.** Jesus tells him a man must be "born of water and of the Spirit." Nicodemus asks, "How can these things be?" Jesus speaks of the Son of man being lifted up, as Moses lifted up the serpent. *John 3:3–15*

**c. AD 29 · Feast of tabernacles — Speaks up in the council.** Officers sent to arrest Jesus return empty-handed. When the Pharisees scorn the crowd, Nicodemus asks whether the law judges a man before hearing him. They answer, "Art thou also of Galilee?" *John 7:45–52*

**c. AD 30 · The day of the crucifixion — Helps bury Jesus.** With Joseph of Arimathea he brings about a hundred pounds of myrrh and aloes. They wind Jesus' body in linen with the spices and lay it in a new tomb in a nearby garden. *John 19:38–42*

### The world around them

The Pharisees were a Jewish party devoted to careful keeping of the law of Moses and "the tradition of the elders" (Mark 7:3). Many ordinary people admired them. The Sanhedrin was the high council in Jerusalem. It was led by the high priest and included chief priests, elders and scribes, and under Roman rule it handled religious and some civil cases. Later Jewish writings say it had seventy-one members. A Pharisee who sat on this council, as Nicodemus probably did, was among the most respected teachers in the nation.

## Key activities

*John does not say why Nicodemus came by night. Some think he feared the other leaders, others that night was simply a quiet time for study. Many also notice John's theme of light and darkness (John 3:19–21).*

1. **Came to Jesus.** He sought Jesus out and honoured him as a teacher from God because of the signs he had done. *John 3:1–2*
2. **Asked honest questions.** He asked how a grown man could be born again, and then, "How can these things be?" *John 3:4, 9*
3. **Heard the heart of the gospel.** In this conversation he heard that God so loved the world that he gave his only begotten Son. *John 3:14–17*
4. **Stood up for fairness.** He reminded the council that the law does not judge a man before hearing him. *John 7:50–51*
5. **Bore the council's scorn.** The others mocked him as if he were a Galilean follower, and told him to search the Scriptures. *John 7:52*
6. **Gave a costly burial.** He brought about a hundred Roman pounds of myrrh and aloes, roughly seventy pounds by modern weight, a gift fit for a king. *John 19:39*
7. **Stepped into the open.** With Joseph of Arimathea he handled Jesus' body publicly on the day the council had condemned him. *John 19:38–40*

*Tradition.* A later writing called the Gospel of Nicodemus, also known as the Acts of Pilate, carries his name. It is not Scripture and was written centuries after John. Other stories say he was baptized and lost his place among the leaders, but the Bible does not tell us what became of him.

### Words and places

- **Pharisee.** A member of a Jewish party that stressed careful obedience to the law and to the traditions of the elders (Mark 7:3; Philippians 3:5).
- **Ruler of the Jews.** A leader of the nation, probably a member of the Sanhedrin (John 3:1; 7:50).
- **Born again.** The Greek word can also mean "born from above." Jesus explains it as being born of the Spirit (John 3:3–8).
- **Myrrh and aloes.** Fragrant spices used in burial to honour the dead (John 19:39–40).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Nicodemus.*

### Old Testament

- **Numbers 21:8–9.** The brass serpent, Jesus' picture for Nicodemus (John 3:14–15)
- **Echo: Ezekiel 36:25–27.** Clean water, a new heart and a new spirit
- **Echo: Deuteronomy 1:16–17.** Judges must hear a matter fairly

### New Testament

- **John 3:1–21.** The night talk; where Jesus' words end is debated
- **John 7:45–52.** He defends a fair hearing for Jesus
- **John 19:38–42.** He helps Joseph of Arimathea bury Jesus
- **Echo: John 12:42–43.** Rulers who believed but feared to confess
- **Echo: 1 Peter 1:23.** Born again by the word of God
