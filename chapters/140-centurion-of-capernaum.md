# The centurion of Capernaum

*A Gentile officer whose faith in Jesus' word made Jesus marvel.*

The centurion of Capernaum was a military officer, a Gentile living among Jews in Galilee. Unusually, the local Jewish elders spoke well of him: he loved their nation and had built their synagogue. When a servant he valued lay sick and close to death, he asked Jesus for help. Yet he did not think himself worthy to have Jesus under his roof. As a soldier he understood authority, and he believed Jesus only had to give the word. Jesus marvelled and said he had not found such great faith in Israel. The servant was healed that same hour, a foretaste of the nations coming to Christ.

## The life of the centurion of Capernaum

*The date is approximate (AD) and follows the house chronology, with the crucifixion c. AD 30. Both Gospels place the story soon after Jesus' great sermon, when he returns to Capernaum.*

**Before Jesus came — Builds a synagogue.** Stationed at Capernaum, the centurion comes to love the Jewish nation. He builds the town a synagogue, and the elders of the Jews speak warmly of him. *Luke 7:4–5*

**c. AD 28–29 · Capernaum — His servant is dying.** A servant who is dear to him lies at home, sick of the palsy and grievously tormented, ready to die. Hearing of Jesus, he sends elders of the Jews to ask Jesus to come and heal him. *Matthew 8:5–6; Luke 7:2–3*

**Near the house — Speak the word only.** As Jesus comes near, the centurion sends friends with a message. He is not worthy for Jesus to enter his house. Like a soldier under orders, a word from Jesus will be enough. *Luke 7:6–8; Matthew 8:8–9*

**The same hour — Healed from a distance.** Jesus marvels and says he has not found so great faith, no, not in Israel. He says, "as thou hast believed, so be it done unto thee," and the servant is healed. *Matthew 8:10–13; Luke 7:9–10*

### The world around them

A centurion commanded about a hundred soldiers and was the backbone of the Roman army. In Jesus' day Galilee was ruled by Herod Antipas, not by a Roman governor. So this officer may have served in Herod's forces, which were organized on Roman lines, and many scholars think so. Capernaum was a fishing town on the north shore of the Sea of Galilee, near a main road and a border. Jesus made it his home base (Matthew 4:13). The ruins of a later synagogue, built centuries afterwards, still stand there today.

## Key activities

*Matthew says the centurion came to Jesus himself. Luke says he sent Jewish elders and then friends. Many readers explain that people often spoke of someone doing what he did through messengers. Others see two ways of telling one story. John 4:46–54, about a nobleman's son, is usually treated as a different event.*

1. **Loved another nation.** Though a Gentile officer, he loved the Jewish people and built their synagogue. *Luke 7:4–5*
2. **Cared for his servant.** He treated a sick servant as someone dear to him, not as property to replace. *Luke 7:2; Matthew 8:6*
3. **Asked for help.** He sent respected Jewish elders to plead with Jesus on his behalf. *Luke 7:3–4*
4. **Confessed his unworthiness.** The elders said he was worthy, but he said, "I am not worthy." *Luke 7:4, 6–7; Matthew 8:8*
5. **Understood authority.** He said he was "a man under authority, having soldiers under me," and saw that Jesus' word had authority over sickness. *Matthew 8:9*
6. **Amazed Jesus.** Jesus marvelled at him, which the Gospels record only here and at the unbelief of Nazareth. *Matthew 8:10; Luke 7:9; Mark 6:6*

### Words and places

- **Centurion.** A Roman-style officer commanding about a hundred soldiers (Matthew 8:5; Acts 10:1).
- **Palsy.** The KJV word for paralysis (Matthew 8:6).
- **Under authority.** The centurion's picture of commands passing down a chain of rank (Matthew 8:9).
- **Capernaum.** A lakeside town in Galilee, Jesus' base for his ministry there (Matthew 4:13; Mark 1:21).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names the centurion.*

### Old Testament

- The centurion is not named in the Old Testament.
- **Echoes.** Naaman, a Gentile army commander, is healed at a word through Elisha, without the prophet touching him (2 Kings 5:9–14). "He sent his word, and healed them" (Psalm 107:20). Many nations will come up to the mountain of the Lord (Isaiah 2:2–3).

### New Testament

- **Matthew 8:5–13.** Jesus adds that many will come from the east and west and sit down with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob.
- **Luke 7:1–10.** Luke tells of the elders' and friends' messages.
- **Echoes.** Other centurions in the New Testament: the one at the cross who said, "Truly this man was the Son of God" (Mark 15:39); Cornelius, the first Gentile convert in Acts (Acts 10:1–48); and Julius, who treated Paul kindly (Acts 27:1–3, 43).
