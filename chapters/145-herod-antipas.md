# Herod Antipas

*The ruler of Galilee who beheaded John the Baptist and mocked Jesus.*

Herod Antipas, a son of Herod the Great, ruled Galilee and Perea for more than forty years, the region where Jesus grew up and did most of his ministry. The Gospels show a man pulled in two directions. He feared John the Baptist and liked to hear him, yet imprisoned him for condemning his marriage and beheaded him to keep a rash oath. He wanted to see Jesus do a miracle, yet when Jesus stood before him, Herod mocked him. Jesus called him "that fox." His life shows how curiosity about God can harden into contempt.

## The life of Herod Antipas

*Dates are approximate. His reign and exile come from Josephus. Gospel events follow the house chronology, with the crucifixion c. AD 30; on the other main view (AD 33), add about three years.*

**c. 4 BC · After his father's death — Becomes tetrarch.** When Herod the Great dies, Rome divides his kingdom among his sons. Antipas receives Galilee and Perea. Luke later dates John's ministry by him: "Herod being tetrarch of Galilee." *Luke 3:1*

**c. AD 28–29 · Galilee — Imprisons John.** John rebukes Herod for taking Herodias, his brother's wife, "and for all the evils which Herod had done." Herod shuts John up in prison. *Luke 3:19–20; Mark 6:17–18*

**c. AD 29 · His birthday — Beheads John.** At a birthday feast Herodias' daughter dances, and Herod swears to give her whatever she asks. Prompted by her mother, she asks for John's head. Herod, "exceeding sorry," sends an executioner. *Mark 6:21–28*

**c. AD 29 · Galilee — Troubled by Jesus.** Hearing of Jesus' works, Herod is perplexed. He says, "It is John, whom I beheaded: he is risen from the dead," and he wants to see Jesus. *Mark 6:14–16; Luke 9:7–9*

**c. AD 29–30 · On the road — Threatens Jesus.** Some Pharisees warn Jesus that Herod wants to kill him. Jesus replies, "Go ye, and tell that fox," and continues his work on the way to Jerusalem. *Luke 13:31–33*

**c. AD 30 · Crucifixion day — Mocks Jesus.** Pilate sends Jesus to Herod, who is in Jerusalem. Herod questions him at length, but Jesus answers nothing. Herod and his soldiers mock him, dress him in a gorgeous robe, and send him back. *Luke 23:6–12*

### The world around them

Galilee was a fertile, crowded region of farms, fishing villages and towns. Antipas rebuilt Sepphoris, a few miles from Nazareth, and founded Tiberias on the lake around AD 20, naming it after the emperor. He was not a king, though Mark uses that courtesy title (Mark 6:14). Josephus says he divorced the daughter of Aretas, king of the Nabateans, to marry Herodias. Aretas later defeated him in battle, and some Jews saw this as punishment for killing John. In AD 39 the emperor Caligula removed him and sent him into exile.

## Key activities

*Mark 6:17 calls Herodias's first husband Philip, while Josephus calls him Herod. Many think he had both names, and he is not the same as Philip the tetrarch of Luke 3:1. Herodias's daughter is not named in Scripture; Josephus names her Salome.*

1. **Ruled Galilee.** He governed the region where Jesus lived and ministered. *Luke 3:1; 23:6–7*
2. **Took his brother's wife.** He married Herodias, which John said was not lawful. *Mark 6:17–18*
3. **Listened to John.** He feared John as a just and holy man and heard him gladly, yet did not change. *Mark 6:20*
4. **Kept a foolish oath.** Rather than lose face before his guests, he kept a rash promise. *Mark 6:22–26*
5. **Killed John.** He had John beheaded in prison, and John's disciples buried the body. *Mark 6:27–29*
6. **Wanted to see Jesus.** He was curious and hoped to see a miracle. *Luke 9:9; 23:8*
7. **Mocked the silent Jesus.** When Jesus would not answer, Herod set him at nought and mocked him. *Luke 23:9–11*
8. **Made friends with Pilate.** That same day he and Pilate, once enemies, became friends. *Luke 23:12*

### Words and places

- **Tetrarch.** A ruler of part of a province. The word first meant "ruler of a fourth" (Luke 3:1; 9:7).
- **Herodians.** Supporters of the Herod family, who joined the Pharisees against Jesus (Mark 3:6; 12:13).
- **Leaven of Herod.** Jesus' warning about the corrupting influence of Herod's ways (Mark 8:15).
- **Beyond Jordan.** The region east of the river, part of it called Perea, also ruled by Antipas (Matthew 19:1).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Herod Antipas.*

### Old Testament

- **Leviticus 18:16; 20:21.** No taking a brother's wife, behind John's rebuke
- **Psalm 2:1–2.** Applied to Herod and Pilate (Acts 4:25–27)
- **Echo: 1 Kings 19:1–2; 21:17–24.** Ahab and Jezebel against the prophet Elijah
- **Echo: Esther 5:3.** A king offers "to the half of the kingdom" (Mark 6:23)

### New Testament

- **Matthew 14:1–12; Mark 6:14–29.** The death of John the Baptist
- **Luke 3:1, 19–20; 9:7–9; 13:31–33; 23:6–12.** Luke's fuller portrait
- **Luke 8:3; Acts 13:1.** Joanna, wife of his steward, and Manaen, raised with him
- **Acts 4:27.** Named among those gathered against Jesus
- **Mark 8:15.** "The leaven of Herod"
