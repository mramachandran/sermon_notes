# The thief on the cross

*A dying criminal who asked Jesus to remember him and heard, "To day shalt thou be with me in paradise."*

Two criminals were crucified with Jesus, one on each side. Matthew and Mark say both of them joined in mocking him. But Luke records that one of them spoke up. When the other railed at Jesus, he rebuked him: they were getting what they deserved, "but this man hath done nothing amiss." Then he turned to Jesus and asked, "Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom." Jesus promised that he would be with him in paradise that very day. We do not know his name, his crime, or his past. He could offer Jesus nothing but a request, and his story is one of Scripture's clearest pictures of grace.

## The life of the thief on the cross

*The date follows the house chronology, with the crucifixion c. AD 30; on the other main view, AD 33. Mark says Jesus was crucified at the third hour, and darkness fell from the sixth hour to the ninth (Mark 15:25, 33).*

**c. AD 30 · Friday morning — Led out with Jesus.** Two malefactors are led out with Jesus to be put to death. At the place called Calvary they are crucified, one on his right hand and the other on his left. *Luke 23:32–33*

**On the cross — Joins the mocking.** Matthew and Mark say that the thieves crucified with Jesus reviled him, joining the priests, the scribes and those passing by. *Matthew 27:44; Mark 15:32*

**Before the ninth hour — Rebukes the other.** One malefactor rails, "If thou be Christ, save thyself and us." The other rebukes him: they suffer justly, "but this man hath done nothing amiss." *Luke 23:39–41*

**Before the ninth hour — Asks to be remembered.** He says, "Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom." Jesus promises that he will be with him in paradise that very day. *Luke 23:42–43*

**Before sunset — Dies.** So that the bodies will not remain on the crosses on the sabbath, the soldiers break the legs of the two men crucified with Jesus. *John 19:31–32*

### The world around them

Crucifixion was Rome's cruellest punishment, used mainly for slaves, rebels and violent criminals. It was carried out in public, often beside roads, to shame the victim and warn others. A man could hang for hours or even days, dying of exhaustion and suffocation. Breaking the legs made breathing impossible and brought death quickly. The law said a body hanged on a tree must not remain there overnight (Deuteronomy 21:22–23), which is why the soldiers hurried before the sabbath began.

## Key activities

*Matthew and Mark say both thieves reviled Jesus, while Luke says one did and one did not. Some think both mocked at first and one later changed; others think Matthew and Mark speak in general terms. The comma in Luke 23:43 is also debated. Most read "To day shalt thou be with me," while a few read "I say unto thee to day" as a solemn formula.*

1. **Faced the truth.** He admitted, "we receive the due reward of our deeds." *Luke 23:41*
2. **Feared God.** He asked his fellow criminal, "Dost not thou fear God, seeing thou art in the same condemnation?" *Luke 23:40*
3. **Defended Jesus.** In the middle of the mockery, he said Jesus had done nothing wrong. *Luke 23:41*
4. **Called on Jesus as King.** He asked to be remembered when Jesus came into his kingdom. *Luke 23:42*
5. **Received a promise.** Jesus assured him that he would be with him in paradise that day. *Luke 23:43*

*Tradition.* The Gospel of Nicodemus, also called the Acts of Pilate, a writing from centuries later, names the repentant thief Dismas and the other Gestas. The Arabic Infancy Gospel calls them Titus and Dumachus. These names are tradition, not Scripture.

### Words and places

- **Malefactor.** An evildoer or criminal (Luke 23:32).
- **Paradise.** A word first meaning a garden or park, used for the place of blessing with God (Luke 23:43; 2 Corinthians 12:4; Revelation 2:7).
- **Railed.** Spoke insults (Luke 23:39).
- **Ninth hour.** About three in the afternoon, the hour when Jesus died (Mark 15:34, 37).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names the thief on the cross.*

### Old Testament

- **Isaiah 53:12.** "Numbered with the transgressors" (Luke 22:37; Mark 15:28 KJV)
- **Deuteronomy 21:22–23.** A body must not remain on a tree overnight
- **Echo: Genesis 40:14.** Joseph asks the butler, "think on me"

### New Testament

- **Luke 23:32–43.** The only account of his request and Jesus' promise
- **Matthew 27:38, 44; Mark 15:27–32.** Two thieves crucified with Jesus revile him
- **John 19:18, 31–33.** Jesus between the two; their legs broken
- **Echo: 2 Corinthians 12:4; Revelation 2:7.** Paradise mentioned again
- **Echo: Matthew 20:1–16.** Eleventh-hour workers receive full pay
