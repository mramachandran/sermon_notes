# Joseph of Arimathea

*A rich member of the council who went boldly to Pilate and laid Jesus in his own tomb.*

Joseph of Arimathea was a wealthy member of the Jewish council and a secret disciple of Jesus, afraid of the other leaders. He had not agreed with the council's plan to kill Jesus. On the afternoon Jesus died, with the sabbath hours away, Joseph stepped out of the shadows and boldly asked Pilate for the body. With Nicodemus he wrapped it in linen and spices and laid it in his own new rock tomb. All four Gospels tell of him, and his tomb became the place where the resurrection was first announced.

## The life of Joseph of Arimathea

*The date follows the house chronology, with the crucifixion c. AD 30; on the other main view, AD 33. Joseph appears only on the day of Jesus' death, though Luke tells us how he had voted before.*

**Before the crucifixion · In the council — Refuses to consent.** Joseph is "a good man, and a just," who waits for the kingdom of God. He has not agreed with the council's plan and action against Jesus. *Luke 23:50–51*

**c. AD 30 · Friday afternoon — Goes boldly to Pilate.** It is the day of preparation, the day before the sabbath. Joseph goes in to Pilate and asks for the body. Pilate checks with the centurion that Jesus is dead, then gives it to him. *Mark 15:42–45*

**c. AD 30 · Before sunset — Takes down the body.** Joseph buys fine linen and takes the body down. With Nicodemus, who brings myrrh and aloes, he winds it in linen with the spices, as the Jews bury their dead. *Mark 15:46; John 19:38–40*

**c. AD 30 · Before the sabbath — Lays Jesus in his tomb.** In a garden near the place of crucifixion, he lays Jesus in "his own new tomb, which he had hewn out in the rock." He rolls a great stone to the door and departs. *Matthew 27:59–60; John 19:41–42*

### The world around them

Wealthy families in Jerusalem were buried in tombs cut into the rock outside the city walls. A body was laid on a shelf or bench inside. Later, when only bones remained, they were often gathered into a stone box called an ossuary. A large stone was rolled across the low entrance. Jewish burial was quick, usually on the day of death, and the law required that a body hanged on a tree be buried before night (Deuteronomy 21:22–23). With the sabbath beginning at sunset, Joseph had only a few hours.

## Key activities

*Arimathaea's location is uncertain. Many identify it with Ramathaim-zophim, Samuel's hometown in the hill country of Ephraim (1 Samuel 1:1), but this is not certain.*

1. **Waited for the kingdom.** He was looking for the kingdom of God, like Simeon and Anna before him. *Mark 15:43; Luke 23:51*
2. **Dissented in the council.** He had not consented to the council's decision against Jesus. *Luke 23:51*
3. **Followed secretly.** For a time he kept his faith hidden, out of fear of the other leaders. *John 19:38*
4. **Went in boldly.** He risked his standing by asking Pilate for the body of a condemned man. *Mark 15:43*
5. **Gave his own tomb.** He laid Jesus in a new tomb where no one had yet been laid. *Matthew 27:60; Luke 23:53*
6. **Worked with Nicodemus.** Together they prepared the body with linen and spices. *John 19:39–40*

*Tradition.* Medieval legends say Joseph brought the cup of the Last Supper, the Holy Grail, to Britain and founded a church at Glastonbury in England. These stories appear more than a thousand years after the Gospels and are not Scripture.

### Words and places

- **Counsellor.** A member of the council, the Sanhedrin (Mark 15:43; Luke 23:50).
- **Preparation.** The day before the sabbath, when food and other needs were made ready (Mark 15:42).
- **Sepulchre.** A tomb (Matthew 27:60–61).
- **Arimathaea.** Joseph's home town, "a city of the Jews" (Luke 23:51).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Joseph of Arimathea.*

### Old Testament

- **Isaiah 53:9.** "With the rich in his death," seen in the rich man's tomb
- **Deuteronomy 21:22–23.** No body left on a tree overnight
- **Echo: Psalm 16:10.** No corruption for the Holy One (Acts 2:27)

### New Testament

- **Matthew 27:57–60; Mark 15:42–46; Luke 23:50–54; John 19:38–42.** The burial
- **Matthew 28:1–6.** The women find his tomb empty
- **Acts 13:29; 1 Corinthians 15:3–4.** Laid in a sepulchre; buried and rose again
- **Echo: John 12:42–43.** Rulers who believed but would not confess
