# Stephen

*The first Christian martyr, who died praying for those who killed him.*

Stephen first appears as one of seven men chosen to make sure the widows of the Jerusalem church were fed fairly. He was known as a man "full of faith and of the Holy Ghost." But he did far more than serve tables. He did wonders among the people and argued so wisely that his opponents could not answer him. Brought before the council on false charges, he gave the longest speech in Acts, retelling Israel's story from Abraham to Solomon. When he said he saw Jesus standing at God's right hand, the crowd stoned him. He died praying for his killers. A young man named Saul watched, and the church was scattered far beyond Jerusalem.

## The life of Stephen

*Dates are approximate (AD). Acts gives no year for Stephen's death. Most place it a few years after the resurrection, c. AD 33–35.*

**Early church · Jerusalem — Chosen as one of the seven.** Greek-speaking believers complain that their widows are overlooked in the daily food. The apostles ask the church to choose seven trusted men, and Stephen is named first. *Acts 6:1–6*

**Jerusalem — Wonders and wisdom.** Stephen, "full of faith and power," does great wonders among the people. Men from several synagogues argue with him, but they cannot resist the wisdom and Spirit with which he speaks. *Acts 6:8–10*

**Before the council — False charges.** His opponents stir up the people and bring false witnesses. They claim he speaks against the temple and the law. The council sees his face "as it had been the face of an angel." *Acts 6:11–15*

**Before the council — The great speech.** Stephen retells Israel's history: Abraham, Joseph, Moses, the tabernacle and the temple. He shows that Israel often rejected the ones God sent, and he charges his hearers with doing the same. *Acts 7:1–53*

**c. AD 33–35 · Outside the city — Stoned.** Stephen sees the heavens opened and Jesus standing at God's right hand. The crowd drags him out and stones him. He prays for them and "fell asleep." *Acts 7:54–60*

**After his death — Buried and mourned.** Devout men bury Stephen with great lamentation. A great persecution breaks out, and believers scatter, preaching the word wherever they go. *Acts 8:1–4; 11:19*

### The world around them

Jerusalem at this time held many Jews who had grown up elsewhere in the Roman world and spoke Greek. Acts calls them "Grecians." They had their own synagogues, such as the one Acts names for freedmen, Cyrenians, Alexandrians and people from Cilicia and Asia. Stephen's Greek name suggests he may have come from this background, though Acts does not say. The council, or Sanhedrin, was the highest Jewish court, led by the high priest. Under Roman rule its power to carry out executions was limited, so many scholars think Stephen's stoning was a mob action.

## Key activities

*Stephen's speech in Acts 7 sometimes differs in small details from Genesis, such as where Abraham's call came and the numbers in Jacob's family. Most readers see Stephen following the Greek Old Testament or summarising freely.*

1. **Served the widows.** He was chosen to oversee the daily distribution of food, so the apostles could give themselves to prayer and the word. *Acts 6:2–6*
2. **Did great wonders.** "Stephen, full of faith and power, did great wonders and miracles among the people." *Acts 6:8*
3. **Spoke with wisdom.** His opponents "were not able to resist the wisdom and the spirit by which he spake." *Acts 6:10*
4. **Retold Israel's story.** He traced God's work from Abraham to Solomon and taught that "the most High dwelleth not in temples made with hands." *Acts 7:2–50*
5. **Spoke hard truth.** He called his hearers "stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears." *Acts 7:51–53*
6. **Saw Jesus in glory.** "Behold, I see the heavens opened, and the Son of man standing on the right hand of God." *Acts 7:55–56*
7. **Forgave his killers.** He kneeled and cried, "Lord, lay not this sin to their charge." *Acts 7:60*

### Words and places

- **Grecians.** Greek-speaking Jews, many raised outside the land of Israel (Acts 6:1).
- **The seven.** Men "of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom" chosen to serve the church's widows (Acts 6:3).
- **Council.** The Sanhedrin, the Jewish ruling court in Jerusalem (Acts 6:12).
- **Martyr.** From a Greek word meaning "witness." Paul later calls Stephen "thy martyr" (Acts 22:20).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references first. Echoes are study links. "Protomartyr" is a title from later tradition.*

### Old Testament

- **Genesis 12 – Exodus 32; 1 Kings 6–8.** The history his speech retells
- **Amos 5:25–27; Isaiah 66:1–2.** Prophets he quotes (Acts 7:42–43, 49–50)
- **Echo: 2 Chronicles 24:20–22.** Zechariah stoned for speaking God's word

### New Testament

- **Acts 6:1–8:2.** The main account
- **Acts 8:1; 22:20.** Saul consented and kept the killers' clothes
- **Acts 11:19.** The scattering spreads the gospel to Antioch
- **Echo: Luke 23:34, 46.** Jesus' dying prayers, mirrored by Stephen's
- **Echo: Revelation 2:10.** The "crown of life," often linked with his name
