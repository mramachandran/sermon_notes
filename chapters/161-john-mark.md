# John Mark

*The young helper who turned back, got a second chance, and became "profitable ... for the ministry."*

John Mark grew up in Jerusalem, in a home where the first believers gathered to pray. He was a cousin of Barnabas, and he set out with Barnabas and Paul on their first missionary journey. Partway through, at Perga, he left them and went home. That choice later caused a sharp quarrel that split Paul and Barnabas. Barnabas gave Mark a second chance, and years later Paul himself called Mark a fellow worker and asked for him near the end of his life. Peter called him "Marcus my son." Early church tradition names him as the writer of the Gospel of Mark. His story is one of failure, patience and restoration.

## The life of John Mark

*Dates are approximate (AD) and follow Paul's journeys. The order of events after Acts 15 is pieced together from the letters.*

**c. AD 44 · Jerusalem — A praying household.** Freed from prison, Peter goes to "the house of Mary the mother of John, whose surname was Mark," where many are praying. *Acts 12:1–17*

**c. AD 44–46 · Antioch and Cyprus — Helper on the first journey.** Barnabas and Saul take Mark to Antioch, then on to Cyprus, "and they had also John to their minister." *Acts 12:25; 13:1–5*

**c. AD 46–47 · Perga — Turns back.** At Perga in Pamphylia, John leaves them and returns to Jerusalem. Luke does not say why. *Acts 13:13*

**c. AD 49 · Antioch — The sharp split.** Paul refuses to take Mark again. He and Barnabas part, and Barnabas sails with Mark to Cyprus. *Acts 15:36–39*

**c. AD 60–62 · With Paul in prison — A fellow worker.** Mark is with Paul again, one of the few Jewish co-workers who have been "a comfort unto me." *Colossians 4:10–11; Philemon 24*

**c. AD 60s · "Babylon" — With Peter.** Peter sends greetings from "Marcus my son," probably meaning a son in the faith. Many take "Babylon" as a code name for Rome. *1 Peter 5:13*

**c. AD 64–67 · Paul's last letter — Sent for.** Near the end of his life, Paul asks Timothy to bring Mark, "for he is profitable to me for the ministry." *2 Timothy 4:11*

### The world around them

John Mark grew up in the first years of the Jerusalem church, when believers met in homes like his mother's. Like many Jews of his day he had two names: a Hebrew name, John, and a Roman one, Mark. The first journey took him to Cyprus, Barnabas's home island (Acts 4:36), and then to Perga near the south coast of modern Turkey. From there the road climbed inland through the rugged Taurus Mountains toward Pisidian Antioch. Scripture does not say why Mark turned back, and every suggestion is a guess.

## Key activities

*Scripture never explains why John Mark left at Perga or how he and Paul were reconciled. It records only the break and, years later, Paul's warm words.*

1. **Grew up among praying believers.** His mother's house was a gathering place where the church prayed for Peter's release. *Acts 12:12*
2. **Went with Barnabas and Saul.** He traveled from Jerusalem to Antioch with the two leaders. *Acts 12:25*
3. **Served as an assistant.** On Cyprus he helped the missionaries in their work. *Acts 13:5*
4. **Left the mission.** At Perga he departed and went back to Jerusalem, a choice Paul still held against him later. *Acts 13:13; 15:38*
5. **Received a second chance.** Barnabas, his cousin, insisted on taking him again and took him to Cyprus when Paul would not. *Acts 15:37–39; Colossians 4:10*
6. **Worked alongside Paul again.** Mark became one of Paul's "fellowlabourers" during his imprisonment. *Colossians 4:10–11; Philemon 24*
7. **Proved useful in ministry.** Paul asked for Mark because he was "profitable" to him for the ministry. *2 Timothy 4:11*
8. **Stood by Peter.** Peter called him "Marcus my son." *1 Peter 5:13*

*Tradition.* Papias, an early second-century bishop quoted by the historian Eusebius, says Mark was Peter's interpreter and wrote down what Peter remembered of Jesus. The Gospel itself does not name its author. Later tradition also links Mark with the church in Alexandria.

### Words and places

- **Surname.** In the KJV, a second or added name, here Mark's Roman name alongside his Jewish name John (Acts 12:12).
- **Minister.** In this verse, an assistant who served the missionaries, not a church office (Acts 13:5).
- **Pamphylia.** A region on the south coast of modern Turkey; Perga was one of its main cities (Acts 13:13; 15:38).
- **Sister's son.** The KJV wording in Colossians 4:10. The Greek word usually means cousin, and most modern translations say so (Colossians 4:10).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references first. Echoes are study links; Mark is not named in the Old Testament.*

### Old Testament

- **Echo: Jonah 3:1.** The word came to Jonah "the second time"
- **Echo: Exodus 2:11–15; 3:10.** Moses flees, then is sent back years later

### New Testament

- **Acts 12:12, 25.** His mother's house; the move to Antioch
- **Acts 13:5, 13.** Helper on Cyprus; leaves at Perga
- **Acts 15:36–39.** The sharp contention between Paul and Barnabas
- **Colossians 4:10–11; Philemon 24.** With Paul again as a fellow worker
- **2 Timothy 4:11.** "Take Mark"; Paul's authorship of the letter is debated
- **1 Peter 5:13.** "Marcus my son" greets from "Babylon"
- **Tradition.** Some suggest he is the young man of Mark 14:51–52; unnamed there
