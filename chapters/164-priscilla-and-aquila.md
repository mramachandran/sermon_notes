# Priscilla and Aquila

*A tentmaking couple who opened their home to Paul, taught Apollos, and risked their lives for the gospel.*

Priscilla and Aquila were a Jewish couple who made tents for a living. Forced out of Rome by the emperor Claudius, they settled in Corinth, where Paul lived and worked with them. They later moved with him to Ephesus, where they quietly explained the way of God more fully to the gifted preacher Apollos. Wherever they lived, a church met in their house. Paul said they had risked their own necks for his life. They are never mentioned apart, and often Priscilla is named first. Their story shows how ordinary work, a shared home and a steady marriage can serve the gospel.

## The lives of Priscilla and Aquila

*Dates are approximate (AD) and follow Paul's journeys. The date of Claudius's order comes from Roman writers, not from Acts.*

**c. AD 49 · Rome — Forced out.** The emperor Claudius orders all Jews to leave Rome. Aquila and Priscilla move east to Corinth. Acts does not say whether they already believed in Jesus. *Acts 18:2*

**c. AD 50 · Corinth — Paul moves in.** Paul arrives from Athens and finds them. Because they share the same trade, he lives and works with them while reasoning in the synagogue every sabbath. He stays in Corinth eighteen months. *Acts 18:1–4, 11*

**c. AD 52 · Cenchrea to Ephesus — Sail with Paul.** When Paul leaves Corinth, Priscilla and Aquila sail with him as far as Ephesus. Paul travels on, and they stay behind. *Acts 18:18–19*

**c. AD 52 · Ephesus — Teach Apollos.** They hear the eloquent preacher Apollos speak boldly in the synagogue. They take him aside and explain "the way of God more perfectly." *Acts 18:24–26*

**c. AD 53–55 · Ephesus — A church in their house.** Writing from Ephesus, Paul sends greetings from Aquila and Priscilla "with the church that is in their house." *1 Corinthians 16:8, 19*

**c. AD 57 · Rome — Back home.** Paul greets them in Rome, praising them as helpers who risked their lives for him. Again a church meets in their house. *Romans 16:3–5*

**c. AD 64–67 · Probably Ephesus — Still remembered.** In his last letter Paul sends greetings to "Prisca and Aquila." Timothy, who receives the letter, seems to be in Ephesus. *2 Timothy 4:19*

### The world around them

The Roman historian Suetonius says Claudius expelled the Jews from Rome because of riots stirred up by "Chrestus." Many scholars think this refers to disputes about Christ in Rome's synagogues, and a later writer, Orosius, dates the order to AD 49. After Claudius died in AD 54, Jews were able to return. Corinth, where the couple resettled, was a busy trading city on the narrow neck of land between two seas, with ports on both sides. Tentmakers worked with cloth and leather, and their trade could move with them.

## Key activities

*The couple are always named together. In several verses Priscilla comes first, which was unusual at the time. Some think she was the more prominent in the church; others suggest she came from a higher social rank. Manuscripts differ on the order in Acts 18:26.*

1. **Started over.** Forced from Rome, they rebuilt their home and business in Corinth. *Acts 18:2–3*
2. **Took Paul in.** They gave him lodging and shared their work with him. *Acts 18:3*
3. **Moved for the gospel.** They went with Paul to Ephesus and stayed there. *Acts 18:18–19*
4. **Taught Apollos privately.** Rather than correcting him in public, "they took him unto them, and expounded unto him the way of God more perfectly." *Acts 18:26*
5. **Hosted churches.** Believers met in their home in Ephesus and in Rome. *1 Corinthians 16:19; Romans 16:5*
6. **Risked their lives for Paul.** They "laid down their own necks" for him. Scripture does not say when or where. *Romans 16:4*
7. **Earned the thanks of many churches.** Paul says all the churches of the Gentiles gave thanks for them. *Romans 16:4*
8. **Stayed faithful to the end.** Paul still sent them greetings in his final letter. *2 Timothy 4:19*

### Words and places

- **Tentmakers.** Craftsmen who made tents, and possibly other goods from leather or cloth (Acts 18:3).
- **Pontus.** A region on the southern shore of the Black Sea, in modern Turkey (Acts 18:2; 1 Peter 1:1).
- **Cenchrea.** Corinth's eastern port, where Paul set sail (Acts 18:18; Romans 16:1).
- **Church in their house.** The early believers met in homes; there were no church buildings yet (1 Corinthians 16:19).

## Across Scripture

*Direct references first. Echoes are study links; the couple is not named in the Old Testament.*

### Old Testament

- **Echo: Exodus 31:1–6.** Bezalel and skilled workers serve God with their hands
- **Echo: Ecclesiastes 4:9.** "Two are better than one"

### New Testament

- **Acts 18:1–3.** Tentmakers with Paul in Corinth
- **Acts 18:18–19, 26.** They sail to Ephesus and teach Apollos
- **1 Corinthians 16:19.** Greetings "with the church that is in their house"
- **Romans 16:3–5.** Helpers who risked their lives; a church in Rome
- **2 Timothy 4:19.** "Salute Prisca and Aquila"; Paul's authorship debated
- **Echo: 1 Thessalonians 2:9.** Paul works so as not to burden the church
