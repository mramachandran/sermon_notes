# Felix, Festus and Agrippa

*Three rulers who heard Paul: one trembled, one scoffed, one was "almost" persuaded.*

In custody at Caesarea, Paul stood before three powerful men. Felix, the governor, heard Paul speak of righteousness, self-control and judgment, and trembled. Yet he put off any decision, hoping for a bribe. His successor, Festus, proposed a trial in Jerusalem to please the Jewish leaders, so Paul appealed to Caesar. King Agrippa II heard Paul's story and answered, "Almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian." All three agreed Paul had done nothing deserving death, yet none is known to have believed.

## The lives of Felix, Festus and Agrippa

*Dates are approximate (AD). Felix governed Judea c. AD 52–59 and Festus c. AD 59–62. The exact year Festus arrived is debated.*

**c. AD 52 · Judea — Felix takes office.** Felix becomes governor of Judea. Paul later tells him he has been "of many years a judge unto this nation." *Acts 24:10*

**c. AD 57 · Caesarea — Paul sent to Felix.** After a plot to kill Paul in Jerusalem, the commander Claudius Lysias sends him under heavy guard to Governor Felix at Caesarea. *Acts 23:12–35*

**c. AD 57 · Caesarea — The hearing.** The lawyer Tertullus accuses Paul for the high priest. Paul answers calmly. Felix, who knows about "that way," puts off the case until Lysias comes. *Acts 24:1–23*

**c. AD 57–59 · Caesarea — Felix trembles.** With Drusilla, Felix hears Paul speak of "righteousness, temperance, and judgment to come" and trembles. He sends Paul away, but often calls for him, hoping for money. *Acts 24:24–26*

**c. AD 59 · Caesarea — Festus and the appeal.** After two years Festus replaces Felix, who leaves Paul bound. When Festus suggests a trial in Jerusalem, Paul appeals to Caesar. *Acts 24:27; 25:1–12*

**c. AD 59 · Caesarea — Before King Agrippa.** Agrippa and Bernice visit Festus and hear Paul "with great pomp." Paul tells his story; Festus calls him mad, and Agrippa is almost persuaded. *Acts 25:13–26:32*

**c. AD 59–60 · Caesarea — Sent to Rome.** Agrippa says Paul could have gone free if he had not appealed. Paul is sent by sea to Rome under a centurion named Julius. *Acts 26:32; 27:1*

### The world around them

Caesarea was the harbor city Herod the Great had built on the coast, and the Roman governors of Judea ruled from there. The emperor at this time, the "Caesar" of Paul's appeal, was Nero (AD 54–68). A Roman citizen could ask to be tried before the emperor's court. Felix was a former slave whose brother Pallas was powerful in Rome. The historian Tacitus said Felix used a king's power with a slave's mind. Agrippa II ruled lands north and east of Galilee and controlled the appointment of Jerusalem's high priests.

## Key activities

*Josephus, a first-century Jewish historian, tells much more about these rulers. He says Festus died in office. Before the next governor arrived, the high priest had James, brother of Jesus, put to death in AD 62.*

1. **Felix kept Paul under guard.** He ordered Paul "to be kept in Herod's judgment hall" until his accusers came. *Acts 23:35*
2. **Felix delayed the verdict.** He put the case off, saying he would decide when Lysias came down. *Acts 24:22–23*
3. **Felix trembled but waited.** "Go thy way for this time; when I have a convenient season, I will call for thee." *Acts 24:25*
4. **Felix hoped for a bribe.** He sent for Paul often, hoping to be paid to release him. *Acts 24:26*
5. **Felix left Paul bound.** "Willing to shew the Jews a pleasure," he left Paul in prison. *Acts 24:27*
6. **Festus acted quickly.** Three days after arriving he went up to Jerusalem, heard the charges, then held court in Caesarea. *Acts 25:1–6*
7. **Festus granted the appeal.** "Hast thou appealed unto Caesar? unto Caesar shalt thou go." *Acts 25:12*
8. **Festus sought Agrippa's help.** He admitted he had "no certain thing to write" to the emperor. *Acts 25:14–27*
9. **Festus dismissed the resurrection.** He called Paul mad: "much learning doth make thee mad." *Acts 25:19; 26:24*
10. **Agrippa gave Paul a hearing.** "Thou art permitted to speak for thyself." *Acts 26:1*
11. **Agrippa stopped short.** He admitted he was almost persuaded, then rose and ended the hearing. *Acts 26:27–31*
12. **All agreed Paul was innocent.** "This man doeth nothing worthy of death or of bonds." *Acts 26:31–32*

### Words and places

- **Governor.** The Roman official who ruled Judea for the emperor (Acts 23:24).
- **Appeal unto Caesar.** A Roman citizen's request to have his case heard at the emperor's court (Acts 25:11).
- **Caesarea.** The port city on the coast that served as Rome's seat of government in Judea (Acts 23:33).
- **That way.** An early name for the Christian faith (Acts 24:14, 22).
