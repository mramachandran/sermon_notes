Abel

The shepherd whose offering pleased God, the first to die, and one who "being dead yet speaketh."

Abel was the second son of Adam and Eve and the first shepherd in Scripture. He brought God the firstborn of his flock and their fat portions, and God accepted him and his gift. His brother Cain, angry and jealous, killed him in the field. Abel never speaks a single recorded word in Genesis. Yet the New Testament calls him righteous, lists him first among the heroes of faith, and says that through his faith he still speaks. His short life raises big questions about worship, faith and justice, and it points ahead to another innocent death whose blood "speaketh better things."

The life of Abel

Genesis gives no dates or ages for Abel. The order below follows the text.

  1. After Eden

    Born to Eve

    Eve bears Abel after his brother Cain.

    Genesis 4:2
  2. Grown

    Keeps sheep

    "Abel was a keeper of sheep, but Cain was a tiller of the ground."

    Genesis 4:2
  3. Later

    Brings an offering

    Abel brings the firstlings of his flock and their fat. "The LORD had respect unto Abel and to his offering."

    Genesis 4:4
  4. In the field

    Killed by Cain

    Cain talks with Abel, then rises up against him in the field and kills him.

    Genesis 4:8
  5. Afterward

    His blood cries out

    God tells Cain that the voice of his brother's blood cries to him from the ground.

    Genesis 4:10
  6. Later

    Remembered by Eve

    Eve names Seth as "another seed instead of Abel, whom Cain slew."

    Genesis 4:25

Key activities

Genesis does not explain why Abel's offering was accepted. Hebrews 11:4 says it was offered by faith. Some also note that he brought the firstborn and the best parts.

  1. 1

    Kept sheep

    He was the first shepherd named in the Bible.

    Genesis 4:2
  2. 2

    Brought the firstborn and the best

    He offered the firstlings of his flock and the fat of them.

    Genesis 4:4
  3. 3

    Offered in faith

    His gift was "a more excellent sacrifice," and God counted him righteous.

    Hebrews 11:4
  4. 4

    Was accepted by God

    The LORD had respect to Abel himself and to his offering.

    Genesis 4:4
  5. 5

    Died an innocent death

    He was killed by his own brother, the first death recorded in Scripture.

    Genesis 4:8
  6. 6

    Still speaks

    His blood cried out to God, and his faith still speaks to readers today.

    Genesis 4:10; Hebrews 11:4

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Abel.

Old Testament

  • Genesis 4:2–10The whole account of his life and death
  • Genesis 4:25Eve names Seth in Abel's place
  • EchoesFirstborn and fat belong to God (Exodus 13:12; Leviticus 3:16)

New Testament

  • Matthew 23:35; Luke 11:50–51"From the blood of Abel unto the blood of Zacharias"
  • Hebrews 11:4Abel heads the list of the faithful
  • Hebrews 12:24Jesus' blood "speaketh better things than that of Abel"
  • 1 John 3:12Cain's works evil, "and his brother's righteous"
  • Echo: John 10:11The Good Shepherd lays down his life

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Abel bring to God, and how did God respond (Genesis 4:4)?
  2. Understand: Hebrews says Abel "being dead yet speaketh." What does his life still say?
  3. Apply: Abel gave God the first and best. What would giving God your first and best look like this week?
  4. Apply: Abel was righteous and still suffered. How does his story help you when doing right does not seem to pay off?
