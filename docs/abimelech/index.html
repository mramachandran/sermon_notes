Abimelech

Gideon's son who killed his brothers to become king, and died under a millstone dropped by a woman.

Abimelech was a son of Gideon, the judge who had refused to be king, by a concubine from Shechem. After Gideon died, Abimelech won Shechem's backing, hired reckless men, and killed seventy of his brothers on one stone. Only the youngest, Jotham, escaped. Shechem made Abimelech king for three years. Jotham's fable of the trees and the bramble came true: Abimelech and Shechem turned on each other, and Abimelech died when a woman dropped a millstone on his head. Judges tells it as God repaying evil.

The life of Abimelech

Judges gives no calendar dates; on the early date of the Exodus the judges run c. 1375–1050 BC.

  1. In Gideon's lifetime

    Born in Shechem

    Gideon has seventy sons by many wives. A concubine in Shechem bears him another son, Abimelech.

    Judges 8:22–23, 30–31
  2. After Gideon's death

    Wins over Shechem

    Abimelech asks Shechem: seventy rulers, or one who is "your bone and your flesh"? They side with him and give him seventy pieces of silver.

    Judges 8:33–9:4
  3. At Ophrah

    Kills his brothers

    With hired men he kills his brothers "upon one stone." Jotham, the youngest, hides and survives.

    Judges 9:4–5
  4. At Shechem

    Made king

    Shechem makes Abimelech king. From Mount Gerizim, Jotham tells his fable of the trees and the bramble, then flees.

    Judges 9:6–21
  5. Three years on

    Shechem turns

    God sends an evil spirit between Abimelech and Shechem. Gaal son of Ebed boasts against him, and Zebul secretly warns Abimelech.

    Judges 9:22–33
  6. The next days

    Destroys Shechem

    Abimelech takes the city and sows it with salt. About a thousand die when he burns the stronghold of the god Berith.

    Judges 9:34–49
  7. At Thebez

    Dies

    A woman drops a piece of millstone on his head from a tower. He orders his armor-bearer to kill him, so no one will say a woman slew him.

    Judges 9:50–55

Key activities

Judges says Abimelech killed "threescore and ten" sons of Gideon, yet Jotham escaped, so many readers take seventy as a round figure. Judges never calls Abimelech a judge; his story works as a warning.

  1. 1

    Played on family ties

    He asked Shechem's leaders to choose one ruler from their own kin instead of seventy.

    Judges 9:1–3
  2. 2

    Paid with temple silver

    He hired "vain and light persons" with seventy pieces of silver from the house of Baal-berith.

    Judges 9:4
  3. 3

    Murdered his brothers

    He killed Gideon's sons on one stone at Ophrah, and only Jotham escaped by hiding.

    Judges 9:5
  4. 4

    Took the title of king

    Shechem crowned him, and he reigned over Israel three years.

    Judges 9:6, 22
  5. 5

    Crushed a revolt

    He set ambushes against Gaal's forces and drove them back, then took Shechem the next day.

    Judges 9:34–45
  6. 6

    Burned the tower of Shechem

    He cut boughs on Mount Zalmon, piled them against the stronghold and set it on fire.

    Judges 9:46–49
  7. 7

    Died by his own order

    Struck by a millstone at Thebez, he had his young armor-bearer thrust him through.

    Judges 9:50–54

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Abimelech.

Old Testament

  • Judges 8:29–9:57This is the main account.
  • Judges 10:1Tola arises "after Abimelech" to defend Israel.
  • 2 Samuel 11:21Joab expects David to recall how a woman on the wall killed Abimelech at Thebez. There Gideon is called Jerubbesheth.
  • EchoesA similar fable of a thistle and a cedar appears in 2 Kings 14:9. "He that rolleth a stone, it will return upon him" (Proverbs 26:27).

New Testament

  • Echoes"All they that take the sword shall perish with the sword" (Matthew 26:52). "Whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap" (Galatians 6:7). Jesus teaches that the one who would be great must serve (Mark 10:42–45), the opposite of Abimelech's grab for power.

For discussion

  1. Observe: What reasons did Abimelech give the men of Shechem for choosing him (Judges 9:1–3)?
  2. Understand: In Jotham's fable the useful trees would not leave their work to rule, but the bramble would. What was he saying about Abimelech?
  3. Apply: Abimelech used family loyalty to get what he wanted. Where are you tempted to use people to get ahead?
  4. Apply: Judges says God repaid both Abimelech and Shechem. Is there a wrong you need to leave in God's hands this week instead of paying it back yourself?

The world around them

Shechem lay in the hill country of Ephraim, in a pass between Mount Ebal and Mount Gerizim. It was an old and important city. Abraham camped there (Genesis 12:6), Joshua renewed the covenant there (Joshua 24:1, 25), and Joseph's bones were buried there (Joshua 24:32). By Abimelech's day its people worshipped Baal-berith, "lord of the covenant." Israel had no king, and the judges were leaders God raised up in times of crisis. Abimelech tried to turn that pattern into a family throne. Thebez was probably a town near Shechem, but its exact site is uncertain.

Words and places

  • Jerubbaal. Gideon's other name, "let Baal plead," given after he pulled down Baal's altar (Judges 6:32).
  • Baal-berith. "Lord of the covenant," the god worshipped at Shechem (Judges 8:33; 9:4).
  • Mount Gerizim. The mountain above Shechem where Jotham spoke his fable (Judges 9:7; Deuteronomy 11:29).
  • Millstone. A heavy round stone for grinding grain. An upper millstone could be carried by hand (Judges 9:53).
