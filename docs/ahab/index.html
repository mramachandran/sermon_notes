Ahab

A strong, able king who sold himself to evil, and whom God still met with warnings and mercy.

Ahab, son of Omri, was the seventh king of the northern kingdom of Israel. By worldly standards he was a success. He ruled from a splendid capital at Samaria, won battles against Syria, and made alliances by marriage with Phoenicia and Judah. But Scripture judges him by another standard. He married Jezebel, brought Baal worship into the heart of Israel, and let his wife murder an innocent man for a vineyard. Again and again God sent prophets to him. Ahab listened just enough to show he knew the truth, but never enough to change course.

The life of Ahab

Dates are approximate (BC). Kings does not date the middle events, so they follow the text's order.

  1. c. 874 BC

    King and Jezebel's husband

    Ahab succeeds Omri in Samaria. He marries Jezebel of the Zidonians and builds Baal a temple and altar.

    1 Kings 16:28–33
  2. c. 860s BC

    The drought

    Elijah announces no dew or rain but by his word. Ahab searches every nation for him.

    1 Kings 17:1; 18:1–16
  3. Third year of drought

    Mount Carmel

    Fire falls on Elijah's sacrifice, Baal's prophets are killed, and rain returns as Ahab rides to Jezreel.

    1 Kings 18:17–46
  4. Syrian wars

    Two victories

    Guided by a prophet, Ahab defeats Ben-hadad twice, but spares him under a treaty, and a prophet condemns it.

    1 Kings 20:1–43
  5. Later

    Naboth's vineyard

    Jezebel has Naboth stoned, and Ahab takes his vineyard. At Elijah's word of judgment, Ahab humbles himself.

    1 Kings 21:1–29
  6. c. 853 BC22nd year

    Dies at Ramoth-gilead

    With Jehoshaphat as ally and ignoring Micaiah, he fights in disguise. A random arrow kills him.

    1 Kings 22:1–40

Key activities

Outside the Bible, an inscription of the Assyrian king Shalmaneser III names "Ahab the Israelite" among the kings who fought him at Qarqar in 853 BC, with 2,000 chariots. Scripture does not mention this battle.

  1. 1

    Married for alliance

    He married Jezebel, a Phoenician princess, and built Baal a temple in Samaria.

    1 Kings 16:31–33
  2. 2

    Hunted for Elijah

    He made every nation swear they had not hidden the prophet.

    1 Kings 18:10
  3. 3

    Blamed the messenger

    "Art thou he that troubleth Israel?" Elijah said Ahab was the real troubler.

    1 Kings 18:17–18
  4. 4

    Reported to Jezebel

    After Carmel he told his wife all Elijah had done, and she vowed to kill the prophet.

    1 Kings 19:1–2
  5. 5

    Answered a boast

    "Let not him that girdeth on his harness boast himself as he that putteth it off."

    1 Kings 20:10–11
  6. 6

    Spared his enemy

    He called Ben-hadad "my brother" and let him go. A prophet said Ahab's life would go for his.

    1 Kings 20:31–43
  7. 7

    Sulked over a vineyard

    When Naboth would not sell, Ahab lay on his bed and would not eat.

    1 Kings 21:1–4
  8. 8

    Took what was not his

    Told Naboth was dead, he went at once to take the vineyard.

    1 Kings 21:15–16
  9. 9

    Humbled himself

    At Elijah's word he tore his clothes, fasted, and went softly. God delayed the disaster to his son's days.

    1 Kings 21:27–29
  10. 10

    Hated the true prophet

    He said of Micaiah, "I hate him; for he doth not prophesy good concerning me, but evil."

    1 Kings 22:8

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Ahab.

Old Testament

  • 1 Kings 16:28–22:40; 2 Chronicles 18The main account of his reign
  • 2 Kings 9–10Jehu judges Ahab's house, as Elijah foretold
  • 2 Kings 8:18, 27"The house of Ahab" leads Judah's kings astray
  • 2 Kings 21:3, 13Manasseh makes a grove "as did Ahab"
  • Micah 6:16"All the works of the house of Ahab"
  • Echo: Leviticus 25:23Family land not sold for ever, so Naboth refused

New Testament

  • Luke 4:25; James 5:17His reign's drought: "three years and six months"
  • Revelation 2:20Jezebel's name used for a false teacher

For discussion

  1. Observe: How did Ahab react when Naboth refused to sell his vineyard (1 Kings 21:1–4)?
  2. Understand: God delayed judgment when Ahab humbled himself (1 Kings 21:27–29). What does that show about God?
  3. Apply: Ahab hated Micaiah because he never said anything good about him (1 Kings 22:8). Whose honest words do you avoid, and why?
  4. Apply: Ahab let Jezebel do the dirty work, then took the vineyard. Is there a wrong you quietly benefit from?
