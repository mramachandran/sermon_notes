Andrew

The brother who kept bringing people to Jesus.

Andrew was a fisherman from Bethsaida and the brother of Simon Peter. He first followed John the Baptist, and he was one of the first two men to follow Jesus. His first act as a disciple was to find his brother and say, "We have found the Messias." That pattern marks nearly every scene where John's Gospel names him. He brings Peter to Jesus. He brings a boy with five loaves to Jesus. He brings a question from some Greeks to Jesus. Andrew never preaches a sermon in Scripture, and he often stands in his famous brother's shadow. Yet his quiet habit of introducing others to Christ has made him a model for ordinary believers.

The life of Andrew

Dates are approximate (AD). Jesus' ministry is dated c. AD 27/28–30 in this study. Scripture does not give Andrew's age.

  1. c. AD 27–28Beyond Jordan

    Follows Jesus

    Andrew hears John the Baptist call Jesus "the Lamb of God." He and another disciple follow Jesus and spend the rest of the day with him.

    John 1:35–40
  2. c. AD 27–28Beyond Jordan

    Brings his brother

    He finds Simon and tells him they have found the Messiah. Then he brings Simon to Jesus, who names him Cephas.

    John 1:41–42
  3. c. AD 28Sea of Galilee

    Called to fish for men

    Jesus sees Simon and Andrew casting a net into the sea and calls them to follow him. They leave their nets at once.

    Mark 1:16–18
  4. c. AD 29Near the Sea of Galilee

    Finds the loaves

    Before Jesus feeds the five thousand, Andrew points out a boy with five barley loaves and two small fishes, though he wonders what good they are.

    John 6:5–13
  5. c. AD 30Jerusalem

    Brings the Greeks

    At the last Passover some Greeks ask Philip to see Jesus. Philip tells Andrew, and together they tell Jesus.

    John 12:20–22

Key activities

Andrew is named only about a dozen times in the New Testament, most often in John's Gospel. The other disciple with him in John 1:35–40 is not named; many think it was John the apostle.

  1. 1

    Followed the Lamb of God

    When his teacher pointed to Jesus, Andrew followed and asked where he was staying.

    John 1:37–39
  2. 2

    Brought his brother

    He went first to Simon with the news that the Messiah had come.

    John 1:40–42
  3. 3

    Left his nets

    He answered Jesus' call to become a fisher of men.

    Mark 1:16–18; Matthew 4:18–20
  4. 4

    Was chosen as an apostle

    He is named among the Twelve in every list.

    Matthew 10:2; Mark 3:18; Luke 6:14; Acts 1:13
  5. 5

    Asked about the future

    With Peter, James and John he asked Jesus privately on the Mount of Olives when the temple would fall.

    Mark 13:3–4
  6. 6

    Offered what little there was

    He brought a boy's lunch to Jesus, asking, "But what are they among so many?"

    John 6:8–9
  7. 7

    Opened the way for Greeks

    He helped carry the request of some Greek visitors to Jesus.

    John 12:20–22

For discussion

  1. Observe: Who did Andrew bring to Jesus in John 1, John 6 and John 12?
  2. Understand: Andrew wondered what five loaves could do "among so many." What does Jesus' answer teach about small offerings?
  3. Apply: Andrew started with his own brother. Who in your family or circle could you invite to "come and see"?
  4. Apply: What small gift, skill or hour could you hand to Jesus this week, even if it seems too little?
