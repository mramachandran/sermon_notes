Apollos

The eloquent teacher from Alexandria who was willing to learn, and who "watered" what Paul had planted.

Apollos was a Jewish believer from Alexandria in Egypt, a gifted speaker who knew the Scriptures deeply. He arrived in Ephesus teaching boldly and accurately about Jesus, though he knew only the baptism of John. Priscilla and Aquila took him aside and explained the way of God more fully, and he accepted their teaching. He then went to Corinth, where he strengthened the believers and argued powerfully from Scripture that Jesus was the Christ. His gifts made him so popular that some Corinthians began to say, "I of Apollos." Paul answered that he and Apollos were only servants: one planted, one watered, but God gave the growth. Apollos is a picture of great gifts held with humility.

The life of Apollos

Dates are approximate (AD), during Paul's second and third journeys. Nothing is known of Apollos before Acts 18 or after Titus 3.

  1. Early lifeAlexandria

    Trained in Scripture

    Born a Jew in Alexandria, Apollos becomes an eloquent speaker and "mighty in the scriptures." He is instructed in the way of the Lord.

    Acts 18:24–25
  2. c. AD 52Ephesus

    Bold but incomplete

    "Fervent in the spirit," he teaches accurately about Jesus in the synagogue, though he knows only the baptism of John.

    Acts 18:25–26
  3. c. AD 52Ephesus

    Taught more perfectly

    Priscilla and Aquila hear him, take him aside, and explain the way of God more fully.

    Acts 18:26
  4. c. AD 52–53Corinth

    Waters the church

    With a letter of welcome from the believers in Ephesus, he crosses to Achaia. He greatly helps the believers and publicly shows from the Scriptures "that Jesus was Christ."

    Acts 18:27–28; 19:1
  5. c. AD 53–55Ephesus

    His name used for a faction

    Some in Corinth claim, "I of Apollos." Paul, writing from Ephesus, calls Apollos a fellow servant. Apollos, apparently near Paul, is not willing to return to Corinth yet.

    1 Corinthians 1:12; 3:4–9; 16:12
  6. LaterProbably Crete

    Still traveling

    Paul asks Titus to help "Zenas the lawyer and Apollos" on their journey and see that they lack nothing.

    Titus 3:13

Key activities

Acts does not explain how Apollos could teach about Jesus while knowing only John's baptism. Compare the disciples Paul met at Ephesus soon after (Acts 19:1–7).

  1. 1

    Mastered the Scriptures

    He was "mighty in the scriptures," well trained in the Old Testament.

    Acts 18:24
  2. 2

    Taught boldly

    He "spake and taught diligently the things of the Lord" and spoke out in the synagogue.

    Acts 18:25–26
  3. 3

    Received correction

    He accepted fuller teaching from Priscilla and Aquila.

    Acts 18:26
  4. 4

    Strengthened believers

    In Achaia he "helped them much which had believed through grace."

    Acts 18:27
  5. 5

    Argued in public

    He "mightily convinced the Jews, and that publickly," from the Scriptures.

    Acts 18:28
  6. 6

    Built on Paul's work

    Paul describes their work as one task: he planted, Apollos watered, and God made it grow.

    1 Corinthians 3:5–9
  7. 7

    Stayed away from Corinth for a time

    Paul urged him to go, but he was not willing then. Many think he wished to avoid feeding the divisions; the text does not say.

    1 Corinthians 16:12
  8. 8

    Kept serving

    He was still traveling in ministry in Paul's later years.

    Titus 3:13

For discussion

  1. Observe: What words does Luke use to describe Apollos in Acts 18:24–25?
  2. Understand: Why did Paul insist that "God gave the increase" (1 Corinthians 3:6–7)?
  3. Apply: Apollos accepted correction from two tentmakers. How do you respond when someone offers to teach you?
  4. Apply: Have you compared church leaders or picked favorites? How could you thank God for each?
