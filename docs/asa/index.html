Asa

A reforming king who trusted God against a vast army, then leaned on men in his later years.

Asa, Solomon's great-grandson, was the third king of Judah after the kingdom divided, and reigned forty-one years. He began well. He tore down idols, removed his own grandmother as queen mother because of her idol, and led Judah to renew its covenant with God. When a huge army from Cush marched against him, he prayed a short, honest prayer and God gave the victory. But later, facing a smaller threat from Israel, he paid a pagan king for help, and jailed the prophet who rebuked him. His life asks whether faith that starts strong will finish strong.

The life of Asa

Dates are approximate (BC) and follow the house chronology. Years are counted from the start of his reign. Chronicles' "thirty-sixth year" (2 Chronicles 16:1) is a known puzzle; see the next page.

  1. c. 911 BCYear 1

    Becomes king

    After his father Abijah's short reign, Asa takes the throne of Judah. He does what is right in God's eyes, as David his father did.

    1 Kings 15:8–11; 2 Chronicles 14:1–2
  2. c. 911–901 BCYears 1–10

    Reform and rest

    He removes foreign altars, images and groves, and commands Judah to seek God. The land is quiet for ten years, and he builds fortified cities.

    2 Chronicles 14:1–7
  3. Before his 15th year

    Victory over Zerah

    Zerah the Ethiopian comes against Judah with a huge army. Asa cries to God at Mareshah, and Judah routs the invaders and carries off great spoil.

    2 Chronicles 14:9–15
  4. c. 896 BC15th year

    Covenant renewed

    Stirred by the prophet Azariah, Asa removes more idols and gathers Judah and many from the north. They swear with shouting to seek God with all their heart.

    2 Chronicles 15:1–15
  5. Later in his reign

    Buys help from Syria

    When Baasha of Israel fortifies Ramah on Judah's border, Asa sends temple silver and gold to Ben-hadad of Syria. Ben-hadad attacks the north, and Baasha withdraws.

    1 Kings 15:16–22; 2 Chronicles 16:1–6
  6. Soon after

    Rebuked by Hanani

    The seer Hanani says he trusted Syria instead of God. Asa is angry, puts the seer in prison, and oppresses some of the people.

    2 Chronicles 16:7–10
  7. c. 872–870 BCYears 39–41

    Disease and death

    His feet become severely diseased, yet he seeks physicians rather than God. He dies in his forty-first year and is buried with great honour.

    1 Kings 15:23–24; 2 Chronicles 16:12–14

Key activities

2 Chronicles 16:1 dates Baasha's move to Asa's 36th year, but 1 Kings 16:8 says Baasha died in Asa's 26th year. Some scholars count the 36 years from the division of the kingdom, and others suspect a copying error.

  1. 1

    Cleared away idols

    He took away foreign altars, high places, images and groves, and told Judah to seek the God of their fathers.

    2 Chronicles 14:2–5
  2. 2

    Built in peace

    He used the quiet years to fortify cities with walls, towers, gates and bars.

    2 Chronicles 14:6–7
  3. 3

    Prayed before battle

    Facing Zerah's army, he prayed, "help us, O LORD our God; for we rest on thee."

    2 Chronicles 14:11
  4. 4

    Listened to a prophet

    Azariah told him, "The LORD is with you, while ye be with him." Asa took courage and acted.

    2 Chronicles 15:1–8
  5. 5

    Removed his grandmother

    He deposed Maachah as queen mother because of her idol in a grove. He cut it down and burned it by the brook Kidron.

    1 Kings 15:13; 2 Chronicles 15:16
  6. 6

    Led a covenant renewal

    Judah and settlers from Ephraim, Manasseh and Simeon gathered in Jerusalem, sacrificed, and swore to seek God.

    2 Chronicles 15:9–15
  7. 7

    Bribed a foreign king

    He sent treasure from the temple and palace to Ben-hadad to break Syria's treaty with Baasha.

    1 Kings 15:18–20; 2 Chronicles 16:2–4
  8. 8

    Imprisoned the messenger

    When Hanani rebuked him, Asa raged and put him in the prison house.

    2 Chronicles 16:7–10
  9. 9

    Sought physicians, not God

    In his final illness he looked only to doctors for help.

    2 Chronicles 16:12

For discussion

  1. Observe: Compare Asa's prayer in 2 Chronicles 14:11 with his actions in 2 Chronicles 16:1–4. What changed?
  2. Understand: Hanani says God looks for people "whose heart is perfect toward him" (2 Chronicles 16:9). What does that mean, since Asa was far from sinless?
  3. Apply: Asa trusted God in a crisis but not in a smaller problem. Where do you rely on yourself because a problem seems small?
  4. Apply: Asa reacted angrily to correction. Who has permission to tell you hard truths, and how do you usually respond?

The world around them

Asa's small kingdom sat between powerful neighbours. To the north, Israel and Judah fought over the border land of Benjamin, only a few miles from Jerusalem. Ramah controlled the main road north, which is why Baasha fortified it. To the northeast, the Aramean kingdom of Syria, ruled from Damascus, was rising. To the south lay Egypt. The "Ethiopians" of Chronicles are the people of Cush, the land south of Egypt. Who Zerah was, and exactly where his army came from, is debated.

Words and places

  • Queen mother. A powerful royal office in Judah, often held by the king's mother or grandmother (1 Kings 15:13).
  • Grove. The KJV word for an Asherah, a wooden pole or image of a Canaanite goddess (1 Kings 15:13).
  • Mareshah. A town in the lowlands of Judah, southwest of Jerusalem (2 Chronicles 14:9–10).
  • Ramah. A town about five miles north of Jerusalem, on the road into Judah (1 Kings 15:17).
