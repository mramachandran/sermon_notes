Barak

The hesitant commander who led ten thousand men down Mount Tabor and is remembered for his faith.

Barak came from Kedesh in Naphtali. When Jabin king of Canaan had oppressed Israel for twenty years, the prophetess Deborah brought him God's command: gather ten thousand men at Mount Tabor, and the LORD would give Sisera into his hand. Barak agreed, but only if Deborah went with him. She went, warning that the honour would go to a woman. At her word Barak charged down the mountain, and the LORD routed Sisera's army, though Sisera died at the hand of Jael. Hebrews lists Barak among the people of faith.

The life of Barak

Judges gives no calendar dates. On the early chronology the judges span c. 1375–1050 BC, but any single date for Barak is rough.

  1. At Kedesh-naphtali

    Called by Deborah

    Deborah sends for Barak and gives him God's command to take ten thousand men to Mount Tabor.

    Judges 4:6–7
  2. Before the battle

    Asks Deborah to come

    Barak says he will go only if she goes with him. She agrees, but says the LORD will sell Sisera into the hand of a woman.

    Judges 4:8–9
  3. At Kedesh

    Gathers the army

    Barak calls Zebulun and Naphtali to Kedesh, and ten thousand men go up with him and Deborah.

    Judges 4:10
  4. From Mount Tabor

    Charges down

    At Deborah's word he leads his men down the mountain. The LORD throws Sisera's army into confusion before Barak's sword.

    Judges 4:14–15
  5. To Harosheth

    Pursues the enemy

    Barak chases the chariots to Harosheth, and not a man of Sisera's army is left. He follows Sisera to Jael's tent and finds him dead.

    Judges 4:16, 22
  6. That day

    Sings with Deborah

    Barak and Deborah sing of God's victory together.

    Judges 5:1, 12

Key activities

Readers judge Barak's request in different ways. Some see weak faith, since Deborah said the honour would pass to a woman. Others see a wise wish to have God's prophet with him. Hebrews 11:32 names him among those who acted by faith.

For discussion

  1. Observe: What exact condition did Barak give Deborah, and what was her reply (Judges 4:8–9)?
  2. Understand: Barak did not get the honour of killing Sisera, yet Hebrews honours his faith. What does that say about how God measures faith?
  3. Apply: Barak wanted Deborah beside him. Whose presence or prayers help you obey God when you are afraid?
  4. Apply: Barak had to leave the safety of Mount Tabor. What "high ground" might God be asking you to leave this week?

The world around them

Chariots were the tanks of the ancient world. On open, dry ground they gave a huge advantage to Canaanite armies, which is why Israel struggled in the valleys (Judges 1:19). Mount Tabor rises alone from the plain in Galilee, a strong place to gather infantry. The river Kishon runs through the Jezreel Valley below. Deborah's song speaks of rain and of the Kishon sweeping the enemy away, and many readers think a sudden storm turned the plain to mud and trapped the chariots (Judges 5:4, 21).

Words and places

  • Kedesh-naphtali. Barak's home town in Naphtali, usually placed north of the Sea of Galilee (Judges 4:6).
  • Mount Tabor. A rounded mountain rising from the plain in lower Galilee (Judges 4:6, 12).
  • Harosheth of the Gentiles. Sisera's base, probably near the western end of the Jezreel Valley. Its exact site is uncertain (Judges 4:2, 16).
  • Discomfited. KJV word for "threw into panic" or "routed" (Judges 4:15).
