Barnabas

The "son of consolation" who saw the best in people and helped them grow.

His given name was Joses, a form of Joseph, but the apostles called him Barnabas, "the son of consolation." It fit him. He first appears selling land and giving the money to the church. When everyone in Jerusalem was afraid of Saul, Barnabas took him to the apostles and vouched for him. When Gentiles began to believe in Antioch, Barnabas was sent to check, and he rejoiced at what he saw. Then he went to Tarsus to find Saul and bring him into the work. Together they carried famine relief to Jerusalem and made the first great missionary journey. Later they parted over John Mark, and Barnabas chose to give the young man a second chance. Acts calls him "a good man, and full of the Holy Ghost and of faith."

The life of Barnabas

Dates are approximate (AD). Acts gives the order but few years; the Claudius famine is usually dated c. AD 44–48.

  1. c. AD 30–33Jerusalem

    Gives his land

    Joses, a Levite from Cyprus, sells land and lays the money at the apostles' feet. They call him Barnabas.

    Acts 4:36–37
  2. c. AD 36–38Jerusalem

    Vouches for Saul

    The disciples fear Saul, but Barnabas brings him to the apostles and tells how he preached boldly in Damascus.

    Acts 9:26–28; Galatians 1:18
  3. Early AD 40sAntioch

    Encourages new believers

    Sent to check on Greek believers, he sees God's grace, is glad, and urges them to cleave to the Lord.

    Acts 11:19–24
  4. Early AD 40sTarsus and Antioch

    Brings Saul

    He fetches Saul from Tarsus. They teach a whole year, and the disciples are first called Christians.

    Acts 11:25–26
  5. c. AD 44–46Jerusalem

    Carries famine relief

    He and Saul take Antioch's gift to Judea and return with John Mark.

    Acts 11:27–30; 12:25
  6. c. AD 46–49Galatia and Jerusalem

    Journey and council

    He preaches with Saul in Cyprus and Galatia, then defends the Gentiles at the Jerusalem council.

    Acts 13:1–15:35; Galatians 2:1–10
  7. c. AD 49Antioch

    Parts from Paul

    After a sharp disagreement over John Mark, Barnabas takes Mark and sails to Cyprus.

    Acts 15:36–39

Key activities

Galatians 2:11–13 tells of a time at Antioch when Barnabas was drawn away from eating with Gentile believers. Scholars debate whether this happened before or after the Jerusalem council.

  1. 1

    Gave generously

    "Having land, sold it, and brought the money, and laid it at the apostles' feet."

    Acts 4:37
  2. 2

    Stood up for Saul

    "But Barnabas took him, and brought him to the apostles."

    Acts 9:27
  3. 3

    Rejoiced at God's grace

    In Antioch he "had seen the grace of God, was glad, and exhorted them all."

    Acts 11:23
  4. 4

    Brought others into the work

    He went to Tarsus to find Saul and brought him to Antioch to teach.

    Acts 11:25–26
  5. 5

    Carried help to the poor

    He and Saul took the Antioch church's gift to the elders in Judea.

    Acts 11:29–30
  6. 6

    Answered God's call

    The Holy Ghost said, "Separate me Barnabas and Saul," and the church sent them out.

    Acts 13:1–4
  7. 7

    Preached in Cyprus and Galatia

    He shared in the first journey, including the meeting with the governor Sergius Paulus.

    Acts 13:4–12; 14:1–7
  8. 8

    Refused worship

    At Lystra the crowd called him Jupiter and Paul Mercurius. He tore his clothes and cried out that they were only men.

    Acts 14:11–15
  9. 9

    Defended the Gentiles

    At the council he and Paul told what God had done among the Gentiles.

    Acts 15:2, 12
  10. 10

    Stumbled at Antioch

    Paul writes that "Barnabas also was carried away with their dissimulation."

    Galatians 2:13
  11. 11

    Gave Mark a second chance

    He parted from Paul rather than leave Mark behind.

    Acts 15:37–39
  12. 12

    Worked for his own living

    Paul names him as one who, like himself, worked rather than rely on the churches' support.

    1 Corinthians 9:6
