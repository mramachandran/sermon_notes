Boaz

The generous landowner of Bethlehem who became Ruth's kinsman-redeemer.

Boaz was "a mighty man of wealth" in Bethlehem and a relative of Naomi's late husband, Elimelech. When the Moabite widow Ruth came to glean in his field, he protected her, fed her and quietly left extra grain for her. When she came to him at night on the threshing floor, he treated her with honor and promised to act. At the city gate he settled the matter before the elders, bought Elimelech's land and married Ruth. Their son Obed became King David's grandfather.

The life of Boaz

Ruth gives no calendar date. Matthew 1:5 names Boaz's mother as Rachab, usually taken to be Rahab of Jericho. If so, Boaz lived early in the judges' period, and the family list from Boaz to David may skip generations.

  1. Barley harvest

    Meets Ruth

    Boaz comes from Bethlehem and greets his reapers, "The LORD be with you." He asks about the young woman gleaning and learns she is the Moabitess who came back with Naomi.

    Ruth 2:1–7
  2. That day

    Shows her kindness

    He tells Ruth to stay in his field and orders the young men not to touch her. He invites her to eat with the reapers, and he tells them to let fall handfuls of grain on purpose for her.

    Ruth 2:8–16
  3. At the threshing floor

    Makes a promise

    Waking at midnight, he finds Ruth at his feet. He blesses her kindness and promises to act, but notes that a nearer kinsman comes first. He sends her home with six measures of barley.

    Ruth 3:7–15
  4. At the gate

    Redeems the land

    Before ten elders he offers the nearer kinsman Elimelech's land. When the man learns he must also marry Ruth, he declines and draws off his shoe. Boaz buys the land and takes Ruth as his wife.

    Ruth 4:1–10
  5. Afterward

    Father of Obed

    The elders bless him, praying that his house will be like the house of Pharez. Ruth bears a son, Obed, who becomes the father of Jesse and the grandfather of David.

    Ruth 4:11–17

Key activities

A "kinsman" (Hebrew go'el, often called a kinsman-redeemer) was a close relative who could buy back family land or rescue a relative in need (Leviticus 25:25). Ruth 4 also draws on the custom of raising up a dead man's name through his widow (Deuteronomy 25:5–10), though Boaz was not a brother-in-law.

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Boaz.

Old Testament

  • Ruth 2–4This is the main account.
  • Ruth 4:18–22and 1 Chronicles 2:11–12. Boaz is listed between Salmon (Salma) and Obed.
  • 1 Kings 7:21and 2 Chronicles 3:17. One of the bronze pillars at Solomon's temple was named Boaz. Scripture does not say whether it was named after him.
  • EchoesThe laws of gleaning (Leviticus 19:9–10) and of redeeming land (Leviticus 25:25) come to life in his field and at his gate.

New Testament

  • Matthew 1:5"Salmon begat Booz of Rachab; and Booz begat Obed of Ruth."
  • Luke 3:32Booz appears in Luke's genealogy of Jesus.
  • EchoesMany Christians see Boaz as a picture of Christ the Redeemer, "who gave himself for us, that he might redeem us from all iniquity" (Titus 2:14).

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Boaz tell his young men to do for Ruth (Ruth 2:8–9, 15–16)?
  2. Understand: Boaz could have married Ruth quietly. Why do you think he gave the nearer kinsman the first chance?
  3. Apply: Boaz went beyond what the law required. Where could you be generous beyond what is expected this week?
  4. Apply: Boaz greeted his workers with a blessing. How do you speak to the people who work for you or with you?
