Caiaphas

The high priest who judged it expedient that one man should die for the people.

Caiaphas was the high priest in Jerusalem through the whole of Jesus' ministry. He was the son-in-law of Annas, an earlier high priest, and he held the office for about eighteen years under Roman rule. After Jesus raised Lazarus, Caiaphas told the council that it was better for one man to die than for the whole nation to perish. John says he spoke more truly than he knew. Caiaphas presided at Jesus' night trial, put him under oath, tore his clothes at his answer, and sent him to Pontius Pilate. Weeks later he sat in judgment on Peter and John. He is a picture of religious power protecting itself, and of God's purpose working even through its opponents.

The life of Caiaphas

Dates are approximate (AD). His years in office come from Josephus. Gospel events follow the house chronology, with the crucifixion c. AD 30; on the other main view (AD 33), add about three years.

  1. c. AD 28–29In office

    High priest when John preaches

    Luke dates John the Baptist's call to a time when "Annas and Caiaphas" were high priests. Caiaphas holds the office, while his father-in-law Annas keeps great influence.

    Luke 3:1–2
  2. c. AD 30After Lazarus

    Counsels Jesus' death

    After Lazarus is raised, the council fears that the Romans will take away "both our place and nation." Caiaphas says it is expedient that one man die for the people.

    John 11:47–53
  3. c. AD 30Before Passover

    Plots at his palace

    The chief priests, scribes and elders gather at the palace of the high priest, Caiaphas. They agree to take Jesus by subtlety and kill him, but not on the feast day.

    Matthew 26:3–5
  4. c. AD 30Night

    Tries Jesus

    Jesus is sent bound from Annas to Caiaphas. False witnesses disagree. Caiaphas puts Jesus under oath, hears his answer, tears his clothes and declares it blasphemy.

    John 18:24; Matthew 26:57–66
  5. c. AD 30Morning

    Sends Jesus to Pilate

    In the morning the council binds Jesus and leads him from Caiaphas to the Roman governor's hall of judgment.

    Matthew 27:1–2; John 18:28
  6. c. AD 30Weeks later

    Questions the apostles

    With Annas and others, he questions Peter and John about healing a lame man. The council forbids them to speak or teach in Jesus' name.

    Acts 4:5–21

Key activities

Luke 3:2 and Acts 4:6 speak of Annas as high priest alongside Caiaphas. Annas had held the office earlier (c. AD 6–15, according to Josephus) and kept the title and much influence. Readers differ on exactly how the two shared authority.

  1. 1

    Held the high priesthood

    He was "the high priest that same year," the year of Jesus' death.

    John 11:49; 18:13
  2. 2

    Spoke an unknowing prophecy

    John says he spoke "not of himself," but prophesied that Jesus would die for the nation and gather God's scattered children.

    John 11:49–52
  3. 3

    Plotted in secret

    He hosted the leaders who planned to arrest Jesus quietly.

    Matthew 26:3–4
  4. 4

    Put Jesus under oath

    "I adjure thee by the living God, that thou tell us whether thou be the Christ, the Son of God."

    Matthew 26:63–64
  5. 5

    Tore his clothes

    He rent his clothes, said Jesus had spoken blasphemy, and the council judged him worthy of death.

    Matthew 26:65–66
  6. 6

    Handed Jesus to Rome

    He sent Jesus on to Pilate, since the council could not lawfully put anyone to death.

    John 18:28–31
  7. 7

    Opposed the apostles

    He sat with the council that threatened Peter and John.

    Acts 4:6, 18–21

For discussion

  1. Observe: What reason did the council give for wanting to stop Jesus (John 11:47–48)?
  2. Understand: John says Caiaphas prophesied without knowing it (John 11:51). What does that show about God's purposes?
  3. Apply: Caiaphas chose what seemed safe for his position over what was true. Where are you tempted to do the same?
  4. Apply: Jesus stayed silent before false witnesses (Matthew 26:62–63). How could you respond with restraint to an unfair criticism this week?

The world around them

The high priest led the temple and presided over the Sanhedrin, the Jewish council. Under Rome, high priests were appointed and removed by the governor. Josephus says the prefect Valerius Gratus appointed Caiaphas about AD 18, and Vitellius, governor of Syria, removed him about AD 36/37. His long term suggests he worked closely with Pontius Pilate. In 1990 a family tomb was found in Jerusalem with an ossuary, or bone box, inscribed "Joseph son of Caiaphas." Many scholars think it belonged to his family, though this is debated.

Words and places

  • High priest. The chief priest of Israel, who led the temple and, once a year, entered the most holy place (Leviticus 16; Hebrews 9:7).
  • Council. The Sanhedrin, the ruling body of Jewish leaders in Jerusalem (Matthew 26:59; Acts 4:15).
  • Expedient. Useful or to one's advantage (John 11:50; 18:14).
  • Blasphemy. Speaking against God, or claiming what belongs to God alone. The law punished it with death (Matthew 26:65; Leviticus 24:16).
