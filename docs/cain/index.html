Cain

The first son and the first murderer, warned by God that sin was crouching at his door.

Cain was the firstborn of Adam and Eve and the first person born in the Bible. He worked the ground like his father. When God accepted his brother Abel's offering but not his own, Cain grew angry. God warned him that sin was waiting to master him, but Cain killed Abel in the field. Even then, God questioned him, judged him, and protected him from being killed in turn. Cain went out from God's presence, built a city, and fathered a line known for both skill and violence. His story shows how quickly sin grew after Eden, and how God speaks to us before we fall.

The life of Cain

Genesis gives no dates or ages for Cain. The order below follows the text.

  1. After Eden

    Born to Eve

    Eve bears her first son and says, "I have gotten a man from the LORD."

    Genesis 4:1
  2. Grown

    Works the ground

    Abel keeps sheep, "but Cain was a tiller of the ground."

    Genesis 4:2
  3. Later

    Brings an offering

    Cain brings fruit of the ground to the LORD. God respects Abel's offering but not Cain's, and Cain's face falls in anger.

    Genesis 4:3–5
  4. Then

    Warned by God

    God asks why he is angry and warns that sin is lying at the door, but that he must rule over it.

    Genesis 4:6–7
  5. In the field

    Kills Abel

    Cain talks with his brother, and in the field he rises up against Abel and kills him.

    Genesis 4:8
  6. Afterward

    Judged and marked

    God asks, "Where is Abel thy brother?" Cain says, "Am I my brother's keeper?" God curses him to be a wanderer. When Cain fears being killed, God sets a mark on him for protection.

    Genesis 4:9–15
  7. East of Eden

    Settles in Nod

    Cain goes out from the presence of the LORD to the land of Nod. He fathers Enoch and builds a city named after him.

    Genesis 4:16–17

Key activities

Genesis does not say why God rejected Cain's offering. Some point to the kind of gift; Hebrews 11:4 points to faith. Genesis also does not name Cain's wife. A common view is that she was one of Adam's daughters (Genesis 5:4). Scripture does not say what the mark was.

  1. 1

    Worked the ground

    He took up the work given to Adam after the fall.

    Genesis 3:23; 4:2
  2. 2

    Brought an offering

    He brought "of the fruit of the ground" to the LORD, but it was not accepted.

    Genesis 4:3–5
  3. 3

    Grew angry

    He was very angry, and his face fell.

    Genesis 4:5
  4. 4

    Ignored God's warning

    God urged him to do well and to master sin before it mastered him.

    Genesis 4:6–7
  5. 5

    Killed his brother

    He attacked Abel in the field, the first murder in Scripture.

    Genesis 4:8
  6. 6

    Denied and dodged

    He lied ("I know not") and asked, "Am I my brother's keeper?"

    Genesis 4:9
  7. 7

    Cried out under judgment

    He said, "My punishment is greater than I can bear," and feared for his life.

    Genesis 4:13–14
  8. 8

    Built a city

    He fathered Enoch and named a city after him.

    Genesis 4:17

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Cain.

Old Testament

  • Genesis 4:1–16The whole account of Cain
  • Genesis 4:17–24His line, down to boastful, violent Lamech
  • Genesis 4:25Seth given "instead of Abel, whom Cain slew"
  • Echo: Genesis 9:6Shedding blood forbidden; humans bear God's image

New Testament

  • Hebrews 11:4Abel's "more excellent sacrifice than Cain"
  • 1 John 3:12"Not as Cain, who was of that wicked one"
  • Jude 11False teachers "have gone in the way of Cain"
  • Echo: Matthew 5:21–22Anger in the heart linked to murder

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did God say to Cain before he killed Abel (Genesis 4:6–7)?
  2. Understand: Why do you think God protected Cain with a mark?
  3. Apply: Sin waits at the door. What "doors" in your life need watching?
  4. Apply: "Am I my brother's keeper?" Who has God given you to care for this week?
