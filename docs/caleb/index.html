Caleb

The spy who "wholly followed the LORD" and at eighty-five asked for a mountain.

Caleb was one of twelve men Moses sent to explore Canaan. Ten came back afraid of giants and walled cities. Only Caleb and Joshua urged the people to trust God and go in. The people wanted to stone them, and God sentenced that whole generation to die in the wilderness. But he made an exception for Caleb, because he "hath followed me fully." Forty-five years later, at eighty-five, Caleb was still strong. He asked for the hill country of Hebron, where the giants lived, and he took it. His story is about faith that does not fade with age, and about a man who waited decades for a promise and never gave up on it.

The life of Caleb

Dates are approximate (BC) and follow the early Exodus date, c. 1446 BC. On the late date, move them about 185 years later. His ages come from Joshua 14:7–10.

  1. c. 1445 BCAge 40

    Sent as a spy

    Moses chooses one leader from each tribe to search the land of Canaan. Caleb represents Judah. For forty days the spies explore the land, including Hebron.

    Numbers 13:1–25; Joshua 14:7
  2. c. 1445 BCAge 40

    Calls for courage

    Ten spies say the people are too strong. Caleb quiets the crowd: "Let us go up at once, and possess it; for we are well able to overcome it."

    Numbers 13:26–33
  3. c. 1445 BCAge 40

    Spared from the sentence

    The people weep and want new leaders. Caleb and Joshua tear their clothes and plead with them. God declares that of all the adults only these two will enter the land.

    Numbers 14:1–38
  4. c. 1406 BCAge 79

    Chosen to divide the land

    On the plains of Moab, God names Caleb as Judah's leader for dividing the land of Canaan among the tribes.

    Numbers 26:65; 34:16–19
  5. c. 1400 BCAge 85

    Asks for the mountain

    At Gilgal he reminds Joshua of Moses' promise and asks for the hill country where the giants live. Joshua blesses him and gives him Hebron.

    Joshua 14:6–15
  6. c. 1400 BC or later

    Takes Hebron

    He drives out the three sons of Anak and offers his daughter Achsah to whoever takes Kirjath-sepher. Othniel wins her, and Caleb gives her springs of water.

    Joshua 15:13–19

Key activities

Caleb is called "the Kenezite." Kenaz is also an Edomite clan name (Genesis 36:11, 42), so some think Caleb's family was not Israelite by birth but joined the tribe of Judah. Others think Kenaz was simply an ancestor in Judah. The text does not say.

  1. 1

    Saw the same land differently

    He saw the same giants as the others but trusted God's promise.

    Numbers 13:30; 14:6–9
  2. 2

    Stood against the crowd

    He and Joshua told the people, "the LORD is with us: fear them not," even as the people talked of stoning them.

    Numbers 14:9–10
  3. 3

    Followed the LORD fully

    God himself gave Caleb this testimony, and Moses and Joshua repeated it.

    Numbers 14:24; Deuteronomy 1:36; Joshua 14:8–9, 14
  4. 4

    Waited forty-five years

    He wandered with a faithless generation for a sin that was not his, then fought for the land.

    Joshua 14:10
  5. 5

    Asked for the hardest place

    "Now therefore give me this mountain," he said, where the Anakim lived.

    Joshua 14:12
  6. 6

    Won Hebron

    He drove out Sheshai, Ahiman and Talmai, the three sons of Anak.

    Joshua 15:13–14; Judges 1:20
  7. 7

    Blessed his daughter

    When Achsah asked for springs, he gave her "the upper springs, and the nether springs."

    Joshua 15:18–19; Judges 1:12–15

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Caleb.

  • Numbers 13–14The spying of the land and God's verdict
  • Numbers 32:11–12; Deuteronomy 1:34–36Only Caleb and Joshua will see the land
  • Numbers 34:19Judah's leader for dividing the land
  • Joshua 14:6–15; 15:13–19; 21:11–12He receives Hebron's fields
  • Judges 1:10–15, 20Hebron taken; Achsah's springs
  • 1 Samuel 25:3; 1 Chronicles 4:15Nabal of Caleb's house; Caleb's sons

New Testament

  • Hebrews 3:16–4:2That generation could not enter "because of unbelief"
  • 1 Corinthians 10:1–11Wilderness events written "for our admonition"
  • Echo: 2 Timothy 4:7"I have kept the faith"

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Caleb see in Canaan, and what did he say about it (Numbers 13:30; 14:6–9)?
  2. Understand: What do you think it means that Caleb had "another spirit" and "wholly followed the LORD"?
  3. Apply: Caleb asked for the hardest place at 85. What challenge might God be giving you now?
  4. Apply: Where do you need to speak up for faith when the crowd around you is afraid?
