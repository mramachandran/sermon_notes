Cyrus

The Persian king whom God called "my shepherd," who sent the exiles home to rebuild the temple.

Cyrus founded the Persian Empire. In 539 BC his forces took Babylon, and within a year he decreed that any Jew who wished could go home and rebuild the house of the Lord. He even sent back the vessels Nebuchadnezzar had taken. Cyrus was not an Israelite and did not follow Israel's God alone. Yet Isaiah names him God's "shepherd" and "anointed," called "though thou hast not known me." Cyrus shows that God can move the heart of any ruler to carry out his promises.

The life of Cyrus

Dates approximate (BC). Traditionally Isaiah son of Amoz named Cyrus about 150 years ahead; many scholars date Isaiah 40–55 to a later prophet in the exile, in the 540s BC.

  1. Before his reign

    Called by name

    Through the prophet, God names Cyrus as his shepherd and anointed, who will subdue nations and order Jerusalem and the temple rebuilt.

    Isaiah 44:28–45:7
  2. c. 559 BCBecomes king

    Rise of Persia

    Cyrus becomes king of Anshan. Within twenty years he conquers the Medes, Lydia and much of the east, "to subdue nations before him."

    Isaiah 45:1–3
  3. c. 539 BCBabylon falls

    The Medes and Persians take Babylon

    On the night of Belshazzar's feast, Babylon falls to the Medes and Persians, as the writing on the wall foretold. Daniel continues to serve in the new government and prospers "in the reign of Cyrus the Persian."

    Daniel 5:28–31; 6:28
  4. c. 538 BC1st year over Babylon

    The decree

    God stirs his spirit, and Cyrus proclaims in writing across his kingdom that the Jews may return and build the house of God. He orders the costs paid from the royal treasury.

    Ezra 1:1–4; 6:3–5; 2 Chronicles 36:22–23
  5. c. 538/537 BCThe journey home

    The vessels return

    Cyrus has his treasurer, Mithredath, count out 5,400 gold and silver vessels from Babylon's temple. They are handed to Sheshbazzar, prince of Judah, who carries them home with the returning exiles.

    Ezra 1:7–11; 5:14–15
  6. c. 536 BC3rd year

    Daniel's last vision

    Daniel's final vision, beside the Tigris River, is dated by Cyrus's reign. Meanwhile, enemies in Judah hire counsellors to slow the temple work throughout his reign. Cyrus dies c. 530 BC.

    Daniel 10:1, 4; Ezra 4:4–5

Key activities

Did Cyrus believe in Israel's God? In Ezra 1:2 he says "the LORD God of heaven" gave him his kingdoms. On the Cyrus Cylinder he gives Marduk the credit. Many think he honoured each people's god in its own terms. Isaiah says God used him "though thou hast not known me."

  1. 1

    Was called before he knew God

    God named him, held his right hand, and "girded" him for his work.

    Isaiah 45:1–5
  2. 2

    Conquered Babylon

    His rise ended the empire that had destroyed Jerusalem, as Daniel had told Belshazzar.

    Daniel 5:28–31
  3. 3

    Proclaimed the return

    He invited any of God's people to "go up to Jerusalem" and build the house of the Lord God of Israel.

    Ezra 1:1–4
  4. 4

    Sent back the temple vessels

    He returned the gold and silver vessels Nebuchadnezzar had taken.

    Ezra 1:7–11
  5. 5

    Paid for the rebuilding

    His decree set the temple's size and ordered the expenses paid from the king's house. He also allowed cedar to be brought from Lebanon.

    Ezra 3:7; 6:3–5
  6. 6

    Kept Daniel in service

    Daniel continued "unto the first year of king Cyrus" and prospered in his reign.

    Daniel 1:21; 6:28
  7. 7

    Left a lasting decree

    Years later, when the temple work was challenged, the record of his decree was found and protected the builders.

    Ezra 5:13–17; 6:1–5

For discussion

  1. Observe: What does God say he will do for Cyrus in Isaiah 45:1–4, and why?
  2. Understand: Why do you think God chose a foreign king, rather than an Israelite, to send his people home?
  3. Apply: Is there a leader or authority in your life you find hard to trust? How might God be at work even there?
  4. Apply: Cyrus's decree opened a door, but each family still had to choose to go. What open door is God setting before you right now?
