David

Shepherd, giant-killer, king and psalm-writer, a man after God's own heart who fell hard and turned back to God.

David was the youngest son of Jesse of Bethlehem, a shepherd boy no one expected to be chosen. Samuel anointed him while Saul still reigned. David killed Goliath, then spent years on the run from Saul's jealousy. He became king over all Israel, made Jerusalem his capital, and received God's promise of a throne for ever. He also fell into adultery and murder, and his family suffered for it. Yet he confessed honestly and threw himself on God's mercy. The Messiah would be called "the son of David."

The life of David

Dates are approximate. He was 30 when he became king and reigned 40 years (2 Samuel 5:4).

  1. Youth

    Anointed in Bethlehem

    Samuel passes over Jesse's older sons and anoints the youngest. The Spirit of the LORD comes on David.

    1 Samuel 16:1–23
  2. Youth

    Faces Goliath

    David hears Goliath's challenge and kills him with a sling in the name of the LORD.

    1 Samuel 17:1–58
  3. Saul's last years

    A hunted man

    Saul's jealousy drives David from court. He hides in the wilderness, spares Saul twice, and settles at Ziklag.

    1 Samuel 18–30
  4. c. 1010 BCAge 30

    King in Hebron

    After Saul dies, the men of Judah anoint David king in Hebron.

    2 Samuel 1:1–2:11; 5:4–5
  5. c. 1003 BCAge 37

    King in Jerusalem

    All the tribes anoint David. He takes Zion, makes Jerusalem his capital, and brings up the ark.

    2 Samuel 5:1–10; 6:1–19
  6. Middle of his reign

    Sin and repentance

    David takes Bathsheba and arranges Uriah's death. Nathan confronts him, and David confesses.

    2 Samuel 11:1–12:25
  7. Later years

    Absalom's revolt

    Absalom seizes the throne and David flees. Absalom is killed, and David mourns him bitterly.

    2 Samuel 13:1–19:8
  8. c. 970 BCAge 70

    Solomon crowned

    When Adonijah grasps at the throne, David has Solomon anointed, charges him, and dies.

    1 Kings 1:1–2:11

Key activities

73 psalm titles include "of David"; some read authorship in every case, others "for" or "in the style of" David. The New Testament names him as speaker of several (Mark 12:36; Acts 2:25).

  1. 1

    Guarded the flock

    He fought off a lion and a bear, and trusted God against Goliath too.

    1 Samuel 17:34–37
  2. 2

    Defeated Goliath

    He came "in the name of the LORD of hosts."

    1 Samuel 17:45–47
  3. 3

    Made a covenant with Jonathan

    Saul's son became his closest friend and protector.

    1 Samuel 18:1–4; 20:1–42
  4. 4

    Spared the LORD's anointed

    Twice he had Saul at his mercy and refused to harm him.

    1 Samuel 24:1–22; 26:1–25
  5. 5

    Strengthened himself in God

    At burned Ziklag, "David encouraged himself in the LORD his God."

    1 Samuel 30:1–20
  6. 6

    Took Jerusalem and brought the ark

    He made Zion his capital and "danced before the LORD with all his might" as the ark came in.

    2 Samuel 5:6–10; 6:12–15
  7. 7

    Received God's covenant

    God promised to build David's house, a kingdom for ever.

    2 Samuel 7:1–29
  8. 8

    Showed kindness to Saul's family

    He brought Jonathan's lame son Mephibosheth to eat at the king's table.

    2 Samuel 9:1–13
  9. 9

    Fell and repented

    Confronted, he said, "I have sinned against the LORD."

    2 Samuel 11–12; Psalm 51:10
  10. 10

    Grieved for a rebel son

    He wept, "O my son Absalom, my son, my son Absalom!"

    2 Samuel 18:33
  11. 11

    Bought a place for the altar

    He refused to offer what "doth cost me nothing"; it became the temple site.

    2 Samuel 24:1–25; 2 Chronicles 3:1
  12. 12

    Prepared for the temple

    Not allowed to build it himself, he gathered materials and gave Solomon the plans.

    1 Chronicles 22:1–19; 28:1–29:9

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did God look at when he chose David, and what did everyone else look at (1 Samuel 16:6–13)?
  2. Understand: David sinned terribly, yet was a man after God's own heart. What does that say about what God values?
  3. Apply: David wrote honest prayers in both joy and failure. What would an honest prayer from you sound like this week?
  4. Apply: David twice refused to take a shortcut to the throne. Where are you tempted to grab something before God's time?
