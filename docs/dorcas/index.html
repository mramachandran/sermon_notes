Dorcas

The disciple of Joppa whose kindness was so missed that Peter was called to her deathbed.

Dorcas, also called Tabitha, lived in the port town of Joppa. Acts calls her "a certain disciple" and says she "was full of good works and almsdeeds." She made coats and garments for the town's widows, some of the poorest people of her day. When she died, the believers laid her in an upper room and sent for Peter from nearby Lydda. The widows met him in tears, holding up the clothes she had made. Peter knelt, prayed, and said, "Tabitha, arise." She opened her eyes and sat up, and many in Joppa believed. Her story shows how much quiet, practical love matters to God.

The life of Dorcas

Acts gives no year. The story comes after Saul's conversion and just before Peter's visit to Cornelius, so it probably happened in the late AD 30s.

  1. JoppaBefore her illness

    A disciple full of good works

    Tabitha, called Dorcas in Greek, is a believer in Joppa. She is known for her good works and her gifts to the poor. She makes coats and garments for the widows.

    Acts 9:36, 39
  2. JoppaIn those days

    Sickness and death

    Dorcas falls sick and dies. The believers wash her body, as was the custom, and lay her in an upper room instead of burying her at once.

    Acts 9:37
  3. Lydda, nearby

    A message for Peter

    Hearing that Peter is at Lydda, where he has just healed Aeneas, the disciples send two men. They beg him not to delay in coming to them.

    Acts 9:32–35, 38
  4. The upper room

    The widows weep

    Peter comes at once. The widows stand beside him weeping, showing him the coats and garments Dorcas made while she was with them.

    Acts 9:39
  5. The upper room

    Raised to life

    Peter sends them all out, kneels and prays. He says, "Tabitha, arise." She opens her eyes, sees Peter and sits up. He gives her his hand and presents her alive to the saints and widows.

    Acts 9:40–41
  6. JoppaAfterward

    Many believe

    The news spreads through all Joppa, and many believe in the Lord. Peter stays many days in the town with Simon, a tanner.

    Acts 9:42–43

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Dorcas.

Old Testament

  • EchoesElijah raised the widow of Zarephath's son in an upper room (1 Kings 17:17–24). Elisha shut the door and prayed before the Shunammite woman's son was raised (2 Kings 4:32–37). The good wife of Proverbs "stretcheth out her hand to the poor" (Proverbs 31:20).

New Testament

  • Acts 9:36–43This is the only account of Dorcas.
  • EchoesJesus put the mourners out and said to Jairus' daughter, "Talitha cumi," meaning "Damsel, I say unto thee, arise" (Mark 5:40–41). Peter follows the same pattern. Jesus said, "Naked, and ye clothed me" (Matthew 25:36). "Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction" (James 1:27).
  • TraditionA traditional burial place of Tabitha is shown in Jaffa today, but it cannot be confirmed.

The world around them

Joppa was the main seaport of Judea for centuries, the place where Jonah once boarded a ship for Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). Lydda lay about ten or twelve miles inland. In the ancient world, widows often had no income and depended on family or charity. The law of Moses told Israel to care for them (Deuteronomy 24:19–21), and the early church took this seriously (Acts 6:1; 1 Timothy 5:3). Handmade clothing was costly and took many hours to produce, so Dorcas's gifts were a real and practical help.

Tradition

In the 1800s, church sewing groups that made clothing for the poor were often called "Dorcas societies" in her honour. Some still meet today.

Words and places

  • Almsdeeds. Acts of charity, especially gifts to the poor (Acts 9:36).
  • Upper chamber. An upstairs room, often used for guests or gatherings (Acts 9:37, 39).
  • Joppa. A port on the Mediterranean coast, modern Jaffa, now part of Tel Aviv (Acts 9:36).
  • Lydda. A town inland from Joppa, where Peter healed Aeneas (Acts 9:32–35, 38).
