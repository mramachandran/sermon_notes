Enoch

The man who walked with God, "and he was not; for God took him."

Enoch lived in the generations between Adam and Noah, and Genesis gives him only a few verses. Yet those verses stand out. In a chapter that repeats "and he died" again and again, Enoch's life ends differently: he "walked with God: and he was not; for God took him." Hebrews says he was taken without seeing death because he pleased God. Jude adds that he was a prophet who warned of the Lord's coming judgment. Enoch was also the father of Methuselah and the great-grandfather of Noah. His short story invites every reader to ask what it means to walk with God, day by day, for a lifetime.

Key activities

Jude's words about Enoch closely match a passage in the book of 1 Enoch, a Jewish writing from the centuries before Christ. Most churches do not include 1 Enoch in the Bible, though the Ethiopian Orthodox Church does. Christians differ on how Jude used it.

  1. 1

    Walked with God

    Genesis says it twice. His life was marked by close, steady fellowship with God.

    Genesis 5:22, 24
  2. 2

    Raised a family

    He fathered Methuselah and other sons and daughters while walking with God.

    Genesis 5:21–22
  3. 3

    Pleased God by faith

    Hebrews says he had this testimony, that he pleased God.

    Hebrews 11:5–6
  4. 4

    Prophesied judgment

    He warned that the Lord would come "with ten thousands of his saints" to judge the ungodly.

    Jude 14–15
  5. 5

    Was taken without death

    God "translated" him so that he did not see death.

    Genesis 5:24; Hebrews 11:5

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Enoch.

Old Testament

  • Genesis 5:18–24This is the main account.
  • 1 Chronicles 1:3He is listed as "Henoch" in the line to Noah.
  • EchoesNoah also "walked with God" (Genesis 6:9). Elijah is taken up into heaven without dying (2 Kings 2:11). "Can two walk together, except they be agreed?" (Amos 3:3).

New Testament

  • Luke 3:37Enoch appears in the genealogy of Jesus.
  • Hebrews 11:5–6"By faith Enoch was translated that he should not see death." The next verse adds that without faith it is impossible to please God.
  • Jude 14–15Enoch, "the seventh from Adam," prophesied the Lord's coming in judgment.
  • TraditionSome early Christian writers suggested Enoch and Elijah are the two witnesses of Revelation 11:3–12. The text does not name them.

For discussion

  1. Observe: What phrase does Genesis repeat about Enoch (Genesis 5:22, 24)?
  2. Understand: Hebrews links Enoch's walk with faith (Hebrews 11:5–6). How are walking with God and trusting God connected?
  3. Apply: Enoch walked with God while raising a family. What does walking with God look like in your ordinary week?
  4. Apply: What is one habit that would help you walk more closely with God?

The world around them

Enoch lived in the long generations before the flood, when wickedness was spreading on the earth (Genesis 6:5). In Genesis 5 almost every life ends with "and he died," so Enoch's different ending stands out. Walking is a common Bible picture for a whole way of life. To walk with God meant living close to him day by day, as Noah later did (Genesis 6:9). The prophet Micah uses the same picture when he calls God's people "to walk humbly with thy God" (Micah 6:8).

Words and places

  • Translated. KJV wording for "moved" or "taken" from one place to another. Here it means taken to God without dying (Hebrews 11:5).
  • Walked with God. Lived in close, steady fellowship with God (Genesis 5:22).
  • Seventh from Adam. Enoch's place in the family line, counting Adam as the first (Jude 14).
  • Jared. Enoch's father (Genesis 5:18).

Read it yourself

  • Day 2. Hebrews 11:5–6. What pleases God, according to these verses?
