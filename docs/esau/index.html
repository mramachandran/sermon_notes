Esau

The firstborn twin who sold his birthright for a meal, lost his father's blessing, and still ran to embrace the brother who wronged him.

Esau was the elder twin son of Isaac and Rebekah, born red and hairy, with Jacob gripping his heel. He grew into a skilled hunter and his father's favorite. Coming in hungry one day, he traded his birthright for a bowl of red stew. Later Jacob took the blessing Isaac meant for Esau, and Esau swore to kill him. Yet twenty years later Esau ran to meet his brother and embraced him with tears. He became the father of the Edomites. Scripture remembers him as a warning against despising what is holy, but his story also shows a surprising grace.

The life of Esau

Isaac's ages come from Genesis 25:26 and 35:28; Esau's age at marriage from Genesis 26:34. On the traditional chronology the twins were born c. 2006 BC; many scholars place the patriarchs c. 2000–1700 BC.

  1. Isaac age 60

    Born first

    Esau comes out "red, all over like an hairy garment," and his twin follows, holding his heel.

    Genesis 25:24–26
  2. Grown

    A man of the field

    Esau becomes a skilled hunter. Isaac loves him for the game he brings home.

    Genesis 25:27–28
  3. Coming in hungry

    Sells his birthright

    Faint from the field, Esau asks for Jacob's red stew. Jacob demands the birthright, and Esau swears it away. "Thus Esau despised his birthright."

    Genesis 25:29–34
  4. Age 40

    Marries Hittite wives

    Esau marries Judith and Bashemath, and they become "a grief of mind" to his parents.

    Genesis 26:34–35
  5. Isaac old and blind

    Loses the blessing

    Esau returns from hunting to find that Jacob has taken his blessing. He cries, "Bless me, even me also, O my father."

    Genesis 27:30–40
  6. After the blessing

    Plots revenge

    Esau plans to kill Jacob after Isaac's death. Seeing that Canaanite wives displease his father, he also marries a daughter of Ishmael.

    Genesis 27:41; 28:6–9
  7. Twenty years later

    Embraces Jacob

    Esau comes from Seir with four hundred men. Instead of attacking, he runs to Jacob, kisses him, and they weep together.

    Genesis 32:3–6; 33:1–16
  8. Isaac age 180

    Buries his father

    Isaac dies at Mamre, and Esau and Jacob bury him together. Esau later settles in mount Seir.

    Genesis 35:27–29; 36:6–8

Key activities

Malachi 1:2–3 and Romans 9:13 say "Esau have I hated." Many read "hated" as a Hebrew way of saying "loved less" or "did not choose" (compare Genesis 29:30–31). Christians differ on whether Paul means the choice of individuals for salvation or of nations for a role in God's plan.

  1. 1

    Hunted

    He became "a cunning hunter, a man of the field," his father's favorite.

    Genesis 25:27–28
  2. 2

    Traded his birthright

    He sold it for "bread and pottage of lentiles," saying, "What profit shall this birthright do to me?"

    Genesis 25:29–34
  3. 3

    Married outside the family

    His Hittite wives grieved Isaac and Rebekah.

    Genesis 26:34–35
  4. 4

    Wept for a lost blessing

    He "cried with a great and exceeding bitter cry" but could not undo it.

    Genesis 27:34–38; Hebrews 12:17
  5. 5

    Nursed a grudge

    He hated Jacob and planned to kill him once Isaac was dead.

    Genesis 27:41
  6. 6

    Tried to please his father

    He married Mahalath, Ishmael's daughter, because Canaanite wives displeased Isaac.

    Genesis 28:6–9
  7. 7

    Forgave his brother

    He ran to meet Jacob, embraced him, and said, "I have enough, my brother."

    Genesis 33:4–9
  8. 8

    Founded a nation

    His family grew into the Edomites of mount Seir.

    Genesis 36:1–43

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Esau.

Old Testament

  • Genesis 36:1–43"The generations of Esau, who is Edom"
  • Numbers 20:14–21Edom refuses Israel passage
  • Deuteronomy 23:7Do not abhor an Edomite, "for he is thy brother"
  • Obadiah 10Edom's violence "against thy brother Jacob"
  • Malachi 1:2–3"Yet I loved Jacob, And I hated Esau"

New Testament

  • Romans 9:10–13God's choice "not of works, but of him that calleth"
  • Hebrews 11:20Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau by faith
  • Hebrews 12:16–17"For one morsel of meat sold his birthright"
  • Echo: Luke 15:20A father runs to embrace his returning son

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Esau say about his birthright when he was hungry (Genesis 25:32)?
  2. Understand: Esau had every reason for revenge. What do you think changed his heart by Genesis 33?
  3. Apply: What "bowl of stew" tempts you to trade away what matters most?
  4. Apply: Is there someone you need to meet with grace instead of a grudge?
