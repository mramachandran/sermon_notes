Ezra

The priest and scribe who set his heart to study God's Law, live it and teach it to a people starting over.

Ezra was a priest descended from Aaron and a scholar of the Law of Moses. In 458 BC the Persian king Artaxerxes sent him from Babylon to Jerusalem with gifts for the temple and a commission to teach God's Law. He led a second group of exiles home, trusting God rather than soldiers. Finding that many had married into the surrounding peoples and their worship, he confessed the nation's sin as his own and led a painful reform. Later he read the Law aloud while the Levites helped the people understand. His life shows the power of God's Word taken seriously.

The life of Ezra

Dates approximate (BC). Most date Ezra's arrival to 458 BC (Artaxerxes I); some prefer 398 BC, after Nehemiah. The text presents them working together (Nehemiah 8:9).

  1. c. 458 BC7th year of Artaxerxes

    Sent by the king

    Artaxerxes grants Ezra silver, gold and supplies, and power to appoint judges and teach the Law.

    Ezra 7:6, 11–26
  2. c. 458 BC1st month

    Fasting by the river Ahava

    Ezra sends for Levites and, ashamed to ask the king for soldiers, proclaims a fast for protection.

    Ezra 8:15–23
  3. c. 458 BC5th month

    Safe arrival

    The company reaches Jerusalem unharmed, for "the hand of our God was upon us."

    Ezra 7:8–9; 8:31–36
  4. c. 458–457 BCSoon after

    Grief and reform

    Told of mixed marriages, Ezra tears his garments and prays. The people gather in heavy rain, and the cases are examined.

    Ezra 9:1–10:44
  5. c. 445/444 BC7th month

    Reading the Law

    Ezra reads the Book from morning to midday, and the people keep the Feast of Tabernacles with joy.

    Nehemiah 8:1–18
  6. Later

    At the dedication of the wall

    "Ezra the scribe" goes before one of the two great companies on the walls.

    Nehemiah 12:26, 31–43

Key activities

Ezra's reform in chapter 10 is hard for many modern readers. The Law forbade such marriages because they would turn Israel to other gods (Deuteronomy 7:3–4). Christians differ on how it applies today. Paul tells a believer not to leave an unbelieving spouse who is willing to stay (1 Corinthians 7:12–14).

    Handled money with care

    He weighed the treasure into the priests' hands and had it weighed and recorded again in Jerusalem.

    He tore his clothes and sat "astonied" until the evening sacrifice.

    Ezra 9:3–4
  7. 7

    Confessed as one of the people

    He prayed, "O my God, I am ashamed and blush to lift up my face to thee."

    Ezra 9:5–15

    Led a hard reform

    Urged by Shechaniah, he called the people to confess and separate from the marriages.

    Ezra 10:1–17

    Nehemiah 8:1–6

    He read the Law every day of the Feast of Tabernacles, kept as it had not been since Joshua.

    Nehemiah 8:13–18

For discussion

  1. Observe: What three things did Ezra set his heart to do (Ezra 7:10)?
  2. Understand: Why did the people weep at the Law, and why were they told to rejoice (Nehemiah 8:9–12)?
  3. Apply: Ezra studied, obeyed, then taught. Which do you most need to grow in this week?
  4. Apply: Ezra trusted God rather than soldiers. Where do you need to trust God over your own safety nets?
