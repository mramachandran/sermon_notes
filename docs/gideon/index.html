Gideon

The fearful farmer whom God called a mighty man of valour and used to rout Midian with three hundred men.

Gideon was threshing wheat in a winepress, hiding it from Midianite raiders, when the angel of the LORD greeted him as a "mighty man of valour." Gideon felt weak and asked many questions, and God answered with signs and patience. Gideon tore down his father's altar to Baal, gathered an army, and watched God cut it from thirty-two thousand men to three hundred. With trumpets, jars and torches they put the enemy to flight. He refused to be king, but a golden ephod he made became a snare.

The life of Gideon

Judges gives no calendar dates; on the early chronology the judges span c. 1375–1050 BC. Any date for Gideon is rough.

  1. Seven years

    Midian oppresses Israel

    Midianites and their allies invade each harvest, destroying crops and livestock. Israel hides in caves and cries to the LORD.

    Judges 6:1–6
  2. At Ophrah

    Called by God

    The angel of the LORD finds Gideon threshing wheat. Gideon protests he is the least in his father's house; fire consumes his offering.

    Judges 6:11–24
  3. That night

    Tears down Baal's altar

    He breaks down his father's altar to Baal. The townsmen want him killed, but Joash defends him.

    Judges 6:25–32
  4. In the valley of Jezreel

    Gathers an army

    The Spirit of the LORD comes on Gideon, and he summons the tribes. He asks for signs with a fleece, and God gives them.

    Judges 6:33–40
  5. At the well of Harod

    Three hundred chosen

    God reduces the army from thirty-two thousand to ten thousand, and then to three hundred men who lapped the water.

    Judges 7:1–8
  6. At night

    Midian is routed

    After overhearing a Midianite's dream, Gideon attacks. His men blow trumpets, break jars and shout, and the enemy turns on itself.

    Judges 7:9–22
  7. Across the Jordan

    Pursues the kings

    "Faint, yet pursuing," he captures and kills Zebah and Zalmunna, and punishes Succoth and Penuel.

    Judges 7:23–8:21
  8. Forty years

    Rest, ephod and death

    He refuses the crown but makes an ephod that becomes a snare. The land is quiet forty years, and he dies "in a good old age."

    Judges 8:22–32

Key activities

Christians read the fleece in different ways. Some see it as weak faith that God kindly answered. Others see it as a sincere request for assurance. Scripture records it without comment, since God had already given Gideon his promise (Judges 6:14–16).

  1. 1

    Hid his harvest

    He threshed wheat in a winepress to keep it from the Midianites.

    Judges 6:11
  2. 2

    Questioned God

    He asked, "if the LORD be with us, why then is all this befallen us?"

    Judges 6:13
  3. 3

    Confessed his weakness

    "My family is poor in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house."

    Judges 6:15
  4. 4

    Built an altar

    He named it Jehovah-shalom, "the LORD is peace."

    Judges 6:22–24
  5. 5

    Obeyed by night

    Afraid of his family and the town, he tore down Baal's altar under cover of darkness.

    Judges 6:25–27
  6. 6

    Earned a new name

    His father said, "if he be a god, let him plead for himself," and called him Jerubbaal.

    Judges 6:31–32
  7. 7

    Sought signs

    He asked for dew on the fleece and then on the ground, and God did both.

    Judges 6:36–40
  8. 8

    Trusted God with fewer men

    God said too many soldiers would let Israel say, "Mine own hand hath saved me."

    Judges 7:2–8
  9. 9

    Led a night attack

    His men cried, "The sword of the LORD, and of Gideon."

    Judges 7:16–22
  10. 10

    Calmed Ephraim

    He answered their anger with humility, and "their anger was abated toward him."

    Judges 8:1–3
  11. 11

    Refused the crown

    "I will not rule over you, neither shall my son rule over you: the LORD shall rule over you."

    Judges 8:22–23
  12. 12

    Made a golden ephod

    He set it up in Ophrah, and "all Israel went thither a whoring after it."

    Judges 8:24–27

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Gideon.

Old Testament

  • Judges 6–8The main account
  • Judges 8:33–9:57After his death, Abimelech kills his brothers
  • 1 Samuel 12:11"Jerubbaal" among the deliverers God sent
  • 2 Samuel 11:21Called "Jerubbesheth," Baal replaced by "shame"
  • Psalm 83:9–12Enemies like Oreb, Zeeb, Zebah and Zalmunna
  • Isaiah 9:4; 10:26"The day of Midian" recalled as God's saving power

New Testament

  • Hebrews 11:32–34"Gedeon" first among judges made strong out of weakness
  • Echo: 2 Corinthians 12:9"My strength is made perfect in weakness"
  • Echo: 2 Corinthians 4:7Some link the broken jars to "earthen vessels"

For discussion

  1. Observe: How did God reduce Gideon's army, and what reason did he give (Judges 7:2–7)?
  2. Understand: God called Gideon a "mighty man of valour" while he was hiding. What does that show about how God sees people?
  3. Apply: Gideon's first act of obedience was at home, at his father's altar. What needs to change close to home before you serve more widely?
  4. Apply: Gideon's ephod turned a victory into a snare. What good gift or success could pull your heart away from God?
