Part 7 · Exile and Return · Chapter 110

Haggai and Zechariah

Two prophets who got a discouraged people building again, one with plain rebukes and one with visions of hope.

By 520 BC the temple's foundation had lain untouched for about sixteen years while the exiles built their own homes. Then God raised up two prophets. Haggai spoke short, direct messages: "Consider your ways." Within weeks the work began again. Zechariah, from a priestly family, called the people to return to the Lord and spoke of a King "lowly, and riding upon an ass." The temple was finished in 516/515 BC.

The life of Haggai and Zechariah

Dates approximate (BC), counted by the reign of Darius I (from 522 BC).

  1. c. 520 BC6th month

    The first message

    Haggai says the people live in panelled houses while God's house lies in ruins. Within the month, the work begins again.

    Haggai 1:1–15; Ezra 5:1–2
  2. c. 520 BC7th month

    The promise of glory

    To those who think the new temple is "as nothing," Haggai says God will shake the nations and fill this house with greater glory.

    Haggai 2:1–9
  3. c. 520 BC8th month

    Zechariah's first word

    Zechariah calls the people not to be like their fathers: "Turn ye unto me … and I will turn unto you."

    Zechariah 1:1–6
  4. c. 520 BC9th month

    Haggai's last messages

    Haggai warns that unclean hearts make offerings unclean, promises blessing, and calls Zerubbabel God's signet.

    Haggai 2:10–23
  5. c. 519 BC11th month

    The night visions

    Zechariah sees visions by night, usually counted as eight, including a man measuring Jerusalem and a golden lampstand.

    Zechariah 1:7–6:8
  6. c. 518–515 BCFasting and finishing

    Mercy, then a finished temple

    Zechariah answers a question about fasting: God wants justice, mercy and truth. The temple is finished in Darius's sixth year.

    Zechariah 7:1–8:23; Ezra 6:14–15

Key activities

Zechariah 9–14 has no dates and a different style from chapters 1–8. Many scholars think these chapters come from a later prophet. Others hold that Zechariah wrote the whole book, perhaps later in his life.

  1. 1

    Haggai confronted misplaced priorities

    "Is it time for you … to dwell in your cieled houses, and this house lie waste?"

    Haggai 1:3–11
  2. 2

    Haggai encouraged the builders

    He brought one short message from God: "I am with you, saith the LORD."

    Haggai 1:13; 2:4–5
  3. 3

    Haggai promised greater glory

    God would fill the plain new house with glory and "give peace" in it.

    Haggai 2:6–9
  4. 4

    Haggai taught about holiness

    Using questions for the priests, he showed that holiness does not rub off, but uncleanness does.

    Haggai 2:10–19
  5. 5

    Zechariah called for repentance

    He urged the people to turn back to God instead of repeating their fathers' mistakes.

    Zechariah 1:1–6
  6. 6

    Zechariah saw God's care in visions

    The Lord would be "a wall of fire" around Jerusalem, rebuke Satan and clothe Joshua in clean garments.

    Zechariah 2:5; 3:1–5
  7. 7

    Zechariah crowned Joshua as a sign

    He set crowns on the high priest's head and spoke of "the man whose name is The BRANCH," who would build the temple.

    Zechariah 6:9–15
  8. 8

    Zechariah put mercy above ritual

    He told the people to "shew mercy and compassions every man to his brother."

    Zechariah 7:4–10
  9. 9

    Zechariah announced a humble King

    "Thy King cometh unto thee … lowly, and riding upon an ass."

    Zechariah 9:9

  3. Apply: "Consider your ways." Where is your time or money going that needs rethinking?

The world around them

When Darius I took the Persian throne in 522 BC, revolts broke out across the empire, and he spent his first years putting them down. Some think this unrest lies behind Haggai's promise that God would "shake the heavens and the earth" and overthrow kingdoms (Haggai 2:21–22). In Judah, life was hard. Harvests were poor, money seemed to leak away, and the temple site was a reminder of better days.

Words and places

  • Cieled houses. Houses panelled with wood, a sign of comfort (Haggai 1:4).
  • The BRANCH. A title for the coming king from David's line (Jeremiah 23:5; Zechariah 3:8; 6:12).
  • Night visions. A series of pictures, usually counted as eight, that Zechariah saw by night, each explained by an angel (Zechariah 1:7–6:8).
  • Iddo. A priestly family among the first returnees. Zechariah is linked to it (Ezra 5:1; Nehemiah 12:4, 16).
