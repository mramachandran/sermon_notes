Herod Antipas

The ruler of Galilee who beheaded John the Baptist and mocked Jesus.

Herod Antipas, a son of Herod the Great, ruled Galilee and Perea for more than forty years, the region where Jesus grew up and did most of his ministry. The Gospels show a man pulled in two directions. He feared John the Baptist and liked to hear him, yet imprisoned him for condemning his marriage and beheaded him to keep a rash oath. He wanted to see Jesus do a miracle, yet when Jesus stood before him, Herod mocked him. Jesus called him "that fox." His life shows how curiosity about God can harden into contempt.

The life of Herod Antipas

Dates are approximate. His reign and exile come from Josephus. Gospel events follow the house chronology, with the crucifixion c. AD 30; on the other main view (AD 33), add about three years.

  1. c. 4 BCAfter his father's death

    Becomes tetrarch

    When Herod the Great dies, Rome divides his kingdom among his sons. Antipas receives Galilee and Perea. Luke later dates John's ministry by him: "Herod being tetrarch of Galilee."

    Luke 3:1
  2. c. AD 28–29Galilee

    Imprisons John

    John rebukes Herod for taking Herodias, his brother's wife, "and for all the evils which Herod had done." Herod shuts John up in prison.

    Luke 3:19–20; Mark 6:17–18
  3. c. AD 29His birthday

    Beheads John

    At a birthday feast Herodias' daughter dances, and Herod swears to give her whatever she asks. Prompted by her mother, she asks for John's head. Herod, "exceeding sorry," sends an executioner.

    Mark 6:21–28
  4. c. AD 29Galilee

    Troubled by Jesus

    Hearing of Jesus' works, Herod is perplexed. He says, "It is John, whom I beheaded: he is risen from the dead," and he wants to see Jesus.

    Mark 6:14–16; Luke 9:7–9
  5. c. AD 29–30On the road

    Threatens Jesus

    Some Pharisees warn Jesus that Herod wants to kill him. Jesus replies, "Go ye, and tell that fox," and continues his work on the way to Jerusalem.

    Luke 13:31–33
  6. c. AD 30Crucifixion day

    Mocks Jesus

    Pilate sends Jesus to Herod, who is in Jerusalem. Herod questions him at length, but Jesus answers nothing. Herod and his soldiers mock him, dress him in a gorgeous robe, and send him back.

    Luke 23:6–12

Key activities

Mark 6:17 calls Herodias's first husband Philip, while Josephus calls him Herod. Many think he had both names, and he is not the same as Philip the tetrarch of Luke 3:1. Herodias's daughter is not named in Scripture; Josephus names her Salome.

For discussion

  2. Understand: Herod liked to hear John but would not change. Why is that so dangerous?

  4. Apply: Herod wanted a miracle, not a Master. How can you come to Jesus this week for who he is, not only what he can do?

The world around them

Galilee was a fertile, crowded region of farms, fishing villages and towns. Antipas rebuilt Sepphoris, a few miles from Nazareth, and founded Tiberias on the lake around AD 20, naming it after the emperor. He was not a king, though Mark uses that courtesy title (Mark 6:14). Josephus says he divorced the daughter of Aretas, king of the Nabateans, to marry Herodias. Aretas later defeated him in battle, and some Jews saw this as punishment for killing John. In AD 39 the emperor Caligula removed him and sent him into exile.

Words and places

  • Tetrarch. A ruler of part of a province. The word first meant "ruler of a fourth" (Luke 3:1; 9:7).
  • Herodians. Supporters of the Herod family, who joined the Pharisees against Jesus (Mark 3:6; 12:13).
  • Leaven of Herod. Jesus' warning about the corrupting influence of Herod's ways (Mark 8:15).
  • Beyond Jordan. The region east of the river, part of it called Perea, also ruled by Antipas (Matthew 19:1).
