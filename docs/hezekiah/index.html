Hezekiah

The king who trusted God when the Assyrian army stood at Jerusalem's walls.

Hezekiah was the son of Ahaz, one of Judah's worst kings, yet he became one of its best. In his first month as king he reopened the temple his father had shut, cleansed it, and called all Israel to keep the Passover. He smashed idols and even the bronze serpent Moses had made, because people were burning incense to it. When Sennacherib of Assyria invaded and demanded surrender, Hezekiah spread the enemy's letter before the LORD and prayed, and Jerusalem was saved. When he fell ill, he wept and prayed, and God gave him fifteen more years. Pride later led him to show off his treasures to Babylon's envoys. But Scripture says no king of Judah trusted the LORD like him.

The life of Hezekiah

Dates are approximate (BC). 2 Kings 18:13 fits an accession c. 715 BC; 2 Kings 18:9–10 leads some to date it c. 729 BC, perhaps as co-regent.

  1. c. 715 BCAge 25

    Becomes king

    Hezekiah succeeds Ahaz and does right in the sight of the LORD, as David had done.

    2 Kings 18:1–3; 2 Chronicles 29:1–2
  2. c. 715 BCFirst month

    Reopens the temple

    He repairs the temple doors and has the priests and Levites cleanse the house. Worship is restored.

    2 Chronicles 29:3–36
  3. Early in his reign

    Passover for all Israel

    He invites everyone from Beersheba to Dan. Many in the north mock, but some come, and there is great joy.

    2 Chronicles 30:1–27
  4. Early in his reign

    Tears down idols

    He removes the high places and destroys the bronze serpent, calling it Nehushtan.

    2 Kings 18:4; 2 Chronicles 31:1
  5. c. 701 BC14th year

    Sennacherib invades

    Assyria takes Judah's fortified cities. Hezekiah pays heavy tribute, but an army still comes to Jerusalem.

    2 Kings 18:13–17
  6. c. 701 BCUnder siege

    Prayer and deliverance

    The Rabshakeh mocks Judah's God. Hezekiah prays, and the angel of the LORD strikes the Assyrian camp.

    2 Kings 18:17–19:37
  7. c. 701 BCAge about 39

    Sick unto death

    Isaiah tells him to set his house in order. Hezekiah weeps and prays, and God adds fifteen years to his life.

    2 Kings 20:1–11; Isaiah 38:1–22
  8. c. 686 BCAge 54

    Dies

    He is buried with honour, and his son Manasseh reigns in his place.

    2 Kings 20:21; 2 Chronicles 32:33

Key activities

Sennacherib's own records say he shut Hezekiah up in Jerusalem "like a bird in a cage," but they do not claim that he took the city. Carvings of his siege of Lachish survive from his palace at Nineveh.

  1. 1

    Reopened God's house

    In his first month he opened the temple doors and restored worship.

    2 Chronicles 29:3–19
  2. 2

    Invited the whole nation

    He called Judah and the remnant of the north to one Passover in Jerusalem.

    2 Chronicles 30:1–12
  3. 3

    Prayed for the unready

    For those who ate unclean he prayed, "The good LORD pardon every one."

    2 Chronicles 30:17–20
  4. 4

    Broke the bronze serpent

    He destroyed a holy relic from Moses' day because it had become an idol.

    2 Kings 18:4
  5. 5

    Threw off Assyria's yoke

    The LORD was with him, and he rebelled against the king of Assyria and defeated the Philistines.

    2 Kings 18:7–8
  6. 6

    Prepared the city

    He stopped the outside springs, rebuilt the wall, and piped water into Jerusalem.

    2 Kings 20:20; 2 Chronicles 32:2–5, 30
  7. 7

    Encouraged his people

    "With him is an arm of flesh; but with us is the LORD our God to help us, and to fight our battles."

    2 Chronicles 32:6–8
  8. 8

    Spread the letter before God

    He took Sennacherib's letter into the temple and prayed that all kingdoms would know the LORD.

    2 Kings 19:14–19
  9. 9

    Wept and was healed

    He turned his face to the wall and prayed. God said, "I have heard thy prayer, I have seen thy tears."

    2 Kings 20:2–6
  10. 10

    Showed off his treasures

    He showed Babylon's envoys everything; Isaiah warned it would all go to Babylon.

    2 Kings 20:12–19; 2 Chronicles 32:25–26, 31
  11. 11

    Preserved wisdom

    His officials copied out a collection of Solomon's proverbs.

    Proverbs 25:1

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Hezekiah do with Sennacherib's letter (2 Kings 19:14–19)?
  2. Understand: Hezekiah trusted God under siege but grew proud after he was healed. Why is success sometimes harder to handle than trouble?
  3. Apply: What "letter" or threat could you spread out before God in prayer this week?
  4. Apply: Hezekiah broke even a relic from Moses once it became an idol. Is there something good in your life that has taken God's place?
