Hosea

The prophet whose broken marriage became a picture of God's faithful love.

Hosea prophesied to the northern kingdom of Israel in its last decades, from the prosperous days of Jeroboam II to the years before Samaria fell. God told him to marry Gomer and to give their children names that were messages to the nation. Gomer was unfaithful to him. Later God told him to love her again and buy her back. Hosea's home became a living sermon: Israel had run after other gods as an unfaithful wife runs after lovers, yet God would not let her go. His book is full of grief and anger, but also tenderness. "How shall I give thee up, Ephraim?" asks the LORD. Jesus twice quoted Hosea's message: "I will have mercy, and not sacrifice."

The life of Hosea

Dates are approximate (BC). Hosea tells us almost nothing about his life apart from his marriage, and he does not date its events.

  1. c. 755 BCUnder Jeroboam II

    The call and the marriage

    The word of the LORD comes to Hosea. God tells him to marry, and he takes Gomer as his wife, as a sign of Israel's unfaithfulness.

    Hosea 1:1–3
  2. The following years

    Three children named

    A son is named Jezreel, for the bloodshed of Jehu's house. A daughter is named Lo-ruhamah, "not pitied." A son is named Lo-ammi, "not my people."

    Hosea 1:3–9
  3. Later

    Buys back his wife

    God tells him to love a woman who has been unfaithful. He buys her for fifteen pieces of silver and some barley and asks her to be faithful to him.

    Hosea 3:1–3
  4. c. 750–725 BCIsrael in turmoil

    Warns a failing nation

    Kings are murdered one after another, and Israel turns to Egypt and Assyria for help. Hosea calls this unfaithfulness to God.

    Hosea 7:7–11; 8:4, 9
  5. c. 722 BCFall of Samaria

    The warnings come true

    Assyria captures Samaria and takes Israel into exile, as Hosea had warned. Scripture does not say whether he lived to see it.

    Hosea 13:16; 2 Kings 17:5–6

Key activities

Christians have read Hosea's marriage in different ways. Some think Gomer was already unfaithful when Hosea married her. Others think she became unfaithful later and the story is told looking back. Most think the woman in chapter 3 is Gomer, though the text does not name her.

  1. 1

    Obeyed a hard command

    He married Gomer as God told him, knowing his marriage would carry a message.

    Hosea 1:2–3
  2. 2

    Named his children as signs

    Their names warned Israel of judgment. God then promised to reverse them: "Ye are the sons of the living God."

    Hosea 1:4–10; 2:23
  3. 3

    Spoke of God as a husband

    God promised to win Israel back and said, "I will betroth thee unto me for ever."

    Hosea 2:14–20
  4. 4

    Loved his wife again

    He bought her back and waited for her, a picture of God waiting for Israel.

    Hosea 3:1–5
  5. 5

    Accused the priests

    "My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge," he said, because the priests had rejected knowledge.

    Hosea 4:1–9
  6. 6

    Named Israel's folly

    He warned that those who sow the wind will reap the whirlwind.

    Hosea 7:11; 8:7
  7. 7

    Showed God's heart

    God had loved Israel as a child and called his son out of Egypt, and could not give Ephraim up.

    Hosea 11:1–9
  8. 8

    Called Israel home

    He ended with an invitation: "O Israel, return unto the LORD thy God," and God's promise, "I will heal their backsliding."

    Hosea 14:1–4

Across Scripture

Direct references first. Echoes are study links, not places that name Hosea.

Old Testament

  • Hosea 1–14The book of his marriage and prophecies
  • 2 Kings 14:23–17:23Israel's last kings and the fall of Samaria
  • Echo: Jeremiah 3:1–14; Ezekiel 16God's people as an unfaithful wife

New Testament

  • Matthew 9:13; 12:7Jesus quotes Hosea 6:6: "I will have mercy, and not sacrifice"
  • Matthew 2:15"Out of Egypt have I called my son" applied to Jesus
  • Romans 9:25–26; 1 Peter 2:10"Not my people" become God's people
  • 1 Corinthians 15:55Paul's cry over death echoes Hosea 13:14
  • Luke 23:30; Revelation 6:16Calling on the mountains, as in Hosea 10:8
  • Echo: Ephesians 5:25–27; Revelation 19:7The church as Christ's bride

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Hosea do in Hosea 3:1–3, and what did it cost him?
  2. Understand: Why do you think God chose a marriage to picture his bond with his people?
  3. Apply: God wants mercy more than routine (Hosea 6:6). Where has your faith become routine?
  4. Apply: Whom do you need to keep loving though they hurt you? What is one step you could take?
