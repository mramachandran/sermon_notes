Huldah

The prophetess the king's men consulted when the Book of the Law was found.

Huldah lived in Jerusalem in the days of King Josiah. She was married to Shallum, the keeper of the royal wardrobe, and Scripture calls her "the prophetess." In 622 BC, workers repairing the temple found the Book of the Law. When it was read to Josiah, he tore his clothes in grief and sent his highest officials to ask the LORD what it meant. They went to Huldah. Her answer was clear and brave. Judgment would come on Jerusalem, just as the Book said. But because the king's heart was tender, the disaster would not come in his lifetime. Her words moved Josiah to lead the nation in renewing its covenant with God.

The life of Huldah

Dates are approximate (BC) and agree with the Josiah chapter. All we know of Huldah comes from one scene, told twice.

  1. Before c. 622 BCIn Jerusalem

    A prophetess at home

    Huldah lives in Jerusalem's second quarter with her husband Shallum, who keeps the wardrobe, probably the royal or temple robes.

    2 Kings 22:14; 2 Chronicles 34:22
  2. c. 622 BCJosiah's 18th year

    The Book is found

    Hilkiah the high priest finds the Book of the Law in the temple. Shaphan reads it to the king, who tears his clothes.

    2 Kings 22:8–13
  3. c. 622 BCThe same year

    The king's men come

    Josiah sends Hilkiah, Shaphan, Ahikam, Achbor and Asahiah to inquire of the LORD. They go to Huldah.

    2 Kings 22:12–14; 2 Chronicles 34:20–22
  4. c. 622 BCHer answer

    Judgment and mercy

    She says the Book's curses will fall on Jerusalem, because the people have forsaken God. But Josiah will be gathered to his grave in peace.

    2 Kings 22:15–20; 2 Chronicles 34:23–28
  5. c. 622 BCSoon after

    The covenant renewed

    The king gathers the people, reads the Book to them, and makes a covenant to follow the LORD.

    2 Kings 23:1–3
  6. c. 609 and 586 BCYears later

    Her words come true

    Josiah dies before the disaster, and Jerusalem falls to Babylon about 36 years after her prophecy.

    2 Kings 23:29–30; 25:8–10

Key activities

Huldah said Josiah would go to his grave "in peace," yet he died in battle (2 Kings 23:29). Most readers take her words to mean he would die before the disaster fell, which he did.

  1. 1

    Was sought out

    The king's highest officials came to her for the word of the LORD.

    2 Kings 22:14
  2. 2

    Spoke with authority

    She began, "Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Tell the man that sent you to me."

    2 Kings 22:15
  3. 3

    Confirmed the Book

    She said all the words of the Book the king had read would come true.

    2 Kings 22:16–17
  4. 4

    Did not soften judgment

    She named the reason plainly: the people had forsaken God and burned incense to other gods.

    2 Kings 22:17
  5. 5

    Spoke mercy to the king

    Because Josiah had humbled himself and wept, God had heard him.

    2 Kings 22:18–20
  6. 6

    Set reform in motion

    Her message led straight to the covenant renewal and the cleansing of the land.

    2 Kings 23:1–25

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Huldah.

Old Testament

  • 2 Kings 22:14–20and 2 Chronicles 34:22–28. The two accounts of her prophecy.
  • 2 Kings 23:1–3The covenant renewal that followed.
  • EchoesMiriam and Deborah were earlier prophetesses (Exodus 15:20; Judges 4:4). Isaiah's wife is called "the prophetess" (Isaiah 8:3). God promised to pour out his Spirit so that "your sons and your daughters shall prophesy" (Joel 2:28).

New Testament

  • EchoesAnna the prophetess speaks of the child Jesus in the temple (Luke 2:36–38). Peter quotes Joel's promise at Pentecost (Acts 2:16–18). Philip the evangelist had four daughters who prophesied (Acts 21:9).

For discussion

  1. Observe: What were the two parts of Huldah's message (2 Kings 22:15–20)?
  2. Understand: Why do you think Josiah's officials trusted Huldah to tell them the truth?
  3. Apply: Huldah gave an unwelcome message faithfully. When have you needed to say something true but hard? How did you do it?
  4. Apply: Josiah's tender heart mattered to God. What would it look like for you to respond to Scripture with that kind of heart this week?
