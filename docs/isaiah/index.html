Isaiah

The prophet who saw the Lord high and lifted up, and spoke of judgment, comfort and a coming King.

Isaiah son of Amoz prophesied in Jerusalem for about forty years or more, through the reigns of four kings of Judah. His ministry began with a vision of God's holiness in the temple, and his answer, "Here am I; send me," has echoed through the centuries. He confronted King Ahaz when fear drove him toward Assyria. He stood beside King Hezekiah when the Assyrian army surrounded Jerusalem. He warned of judgment on Judah and the nations, yet he also spoke some of Scripture's most tender words of comfort. His book looks ahead to a child called Immanuel, a Prince of Peace, and a suffering servant wounded for others. The New Testament quotes him more than any other prophet.

The life of Isaiah

Dates are approximate (BC). The events of Isaiah 38–39 are placed around 701 BC because Hezekiah was given fifteen more years. Some date them a little earlier.

  1. c. 740 BCYear Uzziah died

    The vision and call

    Isaiah sees the Lord on a throne and seraphim crying, "Holy, holy, holy." A live coal touches his lips, and he answers God's call.

    Isaiah 6:1–13
  2. c. 735 BCReign of Ahaz

    The sign of Immanuel

    Syria and Israel threaten Jerusalem. Isaiah meets Ahaz at the upper pool with his son and urges him to trust God. He gives the sign of a child called Immanuel.

    Isaiah 7:1–16
  3. c. 734 BCSoon after

    A son as a sign

    His wife bears a son named Maher-shalal-hash-baz. Before the boy can talk, Assyria will plunder Damascus and Samaria.

    Isaiah 8:1–4
  4. c. 711 BCYear of Ashdod

    Walks barefoot

    When Assyria attacks Ashdod, Isaiah goes stripped and barefoot for three years. It is a sign that Egypt and Ethiopia will be led away captive.

    Isaiah 20:1–6
  5. c. 701 BCSennacherib's invasion

    God will defend the city

    Hezekiah sends to him in distress. Isaiah answers that the Assyrian king will not enter Jerusalem. That night the angel of the LORD strikes the camp.

    2 Kings 19:1–7, 20–37
  6. c. 701 BCHezekiah's illness

    Death, then life

    Isaiah tells the king to set his house in order. After Hezekiah prays, Isaiah brings word of fifteen more years and a sign on the sundial.

    2 Kings 20:1–11; Isaiah 38:1–8
  7. c. 701 BCEnvoys from Babylon

    A warning of exile

    Hezekiah shows his treasures to visitors from Babylon. Isaiah foretells that everything will one day be carried there.

    2 Kings 20:12–19; Isaiah 39:1–8

Key activities

Many scholars think later prophets wrote or completed chapters 40–66; many others hold that Isaiah wrote the whole book.

  1. 1

    Saw God's holiness

    In the temple he saw the Lord "high and lifted up" and cried, "Woe is me! for I am undone."

    Isaiah 6:1–5
  2. 2

    Answered the call

    Cleansed by the live coal, he offered himself: "Here am I; send me."

    Isaiah 6:6–8
  3. 3

    Called for justice

    He told Judah to relieve the oppressed. "Though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow."

    Isaiah 1:16–18
  4. 4

    Challenged a frightened king

    He urged Ahaz to trust God instead of foreign powers. "If ye will not believe, surely ye shall not be established."

    Isaiah 7:1–9
  5. 5

    Gave signs through his family

    He said that he and his children were "for signs and for wonders in Israel."

    Isaiah 7:3; 8:1–4, 18
  6. 6

    Foretold a royal child

    A child would be born, called Wonderful, Counsellor, the mighty God, the everlasting Father, the Prince of Peace.

    Isaiah 9:6–7; 11:1–10
  7. 7

    Acted out a warning

    He walked barefoot for three years to warn against trusting Egypt.

    Isaiah 20:2–6
  8. 8

    Strengthened Hezekiah

    When Jerusalem was besieged, he told the king not to be afraid and prayed with him.

    2 Kings 19:6–7; 2 Chronicles 32:20
  9. 9

    Brought news of healing

    He announced Hezekiah's death, then his recovery.

    2 Kings 20:1–7
  10. 10

    Recorded history

    He wrote the acts of Uzziah and of Hezekiah.

    2 Chronicles 26:22; 32:32
  11. 11

    Spoke comfort

    "Comfort ye, comfort ye my people, saith your God."

    Isaiah 40:1–11, 28–31
  12. 12

    Described the suffering servant

    "He was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities."

    Isaiah 52:13–53:12

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Isaiah see and hear before he said, "Here am I; send me" (Isaiah 6:1–8)?
  2. Understand: Why do you think a vision of God's holiness made Isaiah feel "undone" before it made him ready to serve?
  3. Apply: Ahaz trusted a powerful ally more than God. What do you lean on when you are afraid?
  4. Apply: Isaiah spoke hard truth and deep comfort. Who needs one of those from you this week?
