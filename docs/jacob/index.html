Part 2 · The Patriarchs · Chapter 19

Jacob

The schemer who wrestled with God, limped away blessed, and gave his new name, Israel, to a nation.

Jacob was born grasping his twin brother's heel, and for much of his life he kept grasping. He bought Esau's birthright and stole his father's blessing, then ran for his life. On the road God met him in a dream and promised never to leave him. In Haran the deceiver was himself deceived by Laban. Coming home, he wrestled through the night and would not let go without a blessing. He left with a limp and a new name, Israel, and his twelve sons became the twelve tribes. "The God of Jacob" gives hope to every flawed believer.

The life of Jacob

Ages come from Genesis. The traditional chronology (from 1 Kings 6:1) has Jacob entering Egypt c. 1876 BC; many scholars place the patriarchs c. 2000–1700 BC.

  1. Isaac age 60

    Born second

    Jacob is born holding Esau's heel and grows up "a plain man, dwelling in tents."

    Genesis 25:21–27
  2. Isaac old and blind

    Takes the blessing

    In Esau's clothes Jacob says, "I am Esau thy firstborn." Esau vows to kill him, and Jacob flees.

    Genesis 27:1–28:5
  3. On the road

    Dreams at Bethel

    He sees a ladder to heaven. God renews Abraham's promises: "I am with thee." Jacob makes a vow.

    Genesis 28:10–22
  4. In Haran

    Twenty years with Laban

    Given Leah instead of Rachel, he serves fourteen years for his wives and six for flocks.

    Genesis 29:1–31:55
  5. At the Jabbok

    Wrestles and is renamed

    Alone at night, he wrestles a man until dawn and is renamed Israel. Esau embraces him.

    Genesis 32:1–33:17
  6. Back in Canaan

    Loses Rachel and Joseph

    Rachel dies bearing Benjamin. His sons sell Joseph, and Jacob refuses to be comforted.

    Genesis 35:16–20; 37:2–35
  7. Age 130

    Goes down to Egypt

    God says, "Fear not to go down into Egypt." Jacob stands before Pharaoh and blesses him.

    Genesis 45:25–47:10
  8. Age 147

    Blesses his sons and dies

    He blesses Joseph's sons and his twelve sons, and is buried at Machpelah.

    Genesis 47:28–50:13

Key activities

The man who wrestled Jacob is not named. Jacob says he has "seen God face to face" (Genesis 32:30), and Hosea 12:4 calls him "the angel." Many Christians see this as an appearance of God in human form; others see an angel sent by God.

  1. 1

    Bought the birthright

    He made hungry Esau swear it away for bread and lentil stew.

    Genesis 25:29–34
  2. 2

    Deceived his father

    He lied three times, even saying, "The LORD thy God brought it to me."

    Genesis 27:18–29
  3. 3

    Made a vow at Bethel

    He promised that the LORD would be his God and pledged a tenth of all God gave him.

    Genesis 28:16–22
  4. 4

    Served for love

    His seven years for Rachel "seemed unto him but a few days, for the love he had to her."

    Genesis 29:18–20
  5. 5

    Was deceived himself

    On the wedding night Laban gave him Leah instead of Rachel.

    Genesis 29:21–30
  6. 6

    Fathered twelve sons

    Through Leah, Rachel, Bilhah and Zilpah, amid bitter rivalry between the sisters, the family of Israel was born.

    Genesis 29:31–30:24; 35:16–18
  7. 7

    Prospered under pressure

    Though Laban changed his wages ten times, God made him rich.

    Genesis 30:25–43; 31:38–42
  8. 8

    Prayed in fear

    "I am not worthy of the least of all the mercies."

    Genesis 32:9–12
  9. 9

    Wrestled for a blessing

    "I will not let thee go, except thou bless me." He limped away renamed Israel.

    Genesis 32:24–32
  10. 10

    Returned to Bethel

    He told his household to put away their foreign gods and built an altar where God had first met him.

    Genesis 35:1–15
  11. 11

    Favored Joseph

    He loved Joseph most and gave him a coat of many colours, which stirred his brothers' hatred.

    Genesis 37:3–4
  12. 12

    Blessed by faith

    Dying, he blessed Joseph's sons and spoke over each of his own sons.

    Genesis 48:1–49:33; Hebrews 11:21

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Jacob.

Old Testament

  • Genesis 25–50The main account of his life
  • Deuteronomy 26:5"A Syrian ready to perish was my father," most take as Jacob
  • Psalm 46:7"The God of Jacob is our refuge"
  • Hosea 12:3–4, 12He took his brother by the heel, wept and prayed

New Testament

  • Matthew 8:11He will sit with Abraham and Isaac in the kingdom
  • John 1:51"Angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man"
  • John 4:5–12Jesus sits at "Jacob's well"
  • Romans 9:10–13God chose Jacob before the twins were born
  • Hebrews 11:21He blessed Joseph's sons and "worshipped"
  • Echo: 2 Corinthians 12:9Strength made perfect in weakness

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did God promise Jacob at Bethel, though he was running from his own wrongdoing (Genesis 28:13–15)?
  2. Understand: Why do you think God let Jacob walk away with a limp?
  3. Apply: "I will not let thee go, except thou bless me." What should you keep praying about?
  4. Apply: Jacob repeated his parents' favoritism. What family pattern do you want to stop with you?
