James, brother of Jesus

The brother who did not believe, then became a pillar of the Jerusalem church.

James grew up in the same house in Nazareth as Jesus, yet John says plainly that Jesus' brothers did not believe in him. After the resurrection Jesus appeared to James, and everything changed. Within a few years James was a leader of the Jerusalem church, one of the "pillars." At the Jerusalem council he gave the ruling that welcomed Gentile believers without making them keep the whole law of Moses. The letter of James, traditionally credited to him, is a plain call to live out what we believe.

The life of James, brother of Jesus

Dates are approximate (AD). Acts and Galatians give the order of events, but not exact years. His death date comes from the Jewish historian Josephus.

  1. c. AD 28–30Jesus' ministry

    Does not believe

    James and his brothers watch Jesus' public work from close by. They urge him to show himself openly, and John adds that "neither did his brethren believe in him."

    John 7:3–5; Mark 3:21, 31–35
  2. c. AD 30After the resurrection

    Meets the risen Lord

    Paul lists James among the witnesses of the resurrection. The risen Jesus appears to him personally. Scripture does not tell us what was said.

    1 Corinthians 15:7
  3. c. AD 30Before Pentecost

    Prays with the believers

    In the upper room in Jerusalem, the brothers of Jesus pray with the apostles, the women and Mary his mother. They are now among his followers.

    Acts 1:13–14
  4. c. AD 36–38Paul's first visit

    Meets Paul

    Three years after his conversion, Paul spends fifteen days with Peter in Jerusalem. The only other leader he sees is "James the Lord's brother."

    Galatians 1:18–19
  5. AD 44Peter's escape

    A recognized leader

    After an angel frees Peter from Herod's prison, Peter tells the believers to report the news "unto James, and to the brethren," then leaves the city.

    Acts 12:17
  6. c. AD 49The Jerusalem council

    Gives the ruling

    When the church debates whether Gentile believers must be circumcised, James speaks last. He quotes Amos and proposes a letter asking only a few things of them.

    Acts 15:13–29
  7. c. AD 57Paul's last visit

    Welcomes Paul

    Paul reports what God has done among the Gentiles. James and the elders glorify God, then advise him on how to answer rumours among Jewish believers.

    Acts 21:17–26

Key activities

Christians have understood "the Lord's brother" in different ways. Many Protestants read it as a younger son of Mary and Joseph. Eastern Orthodox tradition sees a son of Joseph by an earlier marriage, and Roman Catholic tradition often a close relative or cousin.

  1. 1

    Doubted, then believed

    He did not believe during Jesus' ministry, but he was among the believers after the resurrection.

    John 7:5; Acts 1:14
  2. 2

    Saw the risen Christ

    Jesus appeared to him by name.

    1 Corinthians 15:7
  3. 3

    Led the Jerusalem church

    Peter sent word to him, and Paul called him a pillar.

    Acts 12:17; Galatians 2:9
  4. 4

    Welcomed Gentiles

    He judged "that we trouble not them, which from among the Gentiles are turned to God."

    Acts 15:19
  5. 5

    Gave Paul the right hand of fellowship

    With Peter and John he agreed that Paul and Barnabas should go to the Gentiles.

    Galatians 2:9
  6. 6

    Wrote to scattered believers

    The letter of James opens with "James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ."

    James 1:1
  7. 7

    Urged a working faith

    He called believers to be "doers of the word, and not hearers only."

    James 1:22; 2:14–26

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Jesus' brothers say and do in John 7:3–5?
  2. Understand: Why do you think the risen Jesus appeared to James in person (1 Corinthians 15:7)?
  3. Apply: James once doubted his own brother. Who in your family is far from faith, and how can you pray for them?
  4. Apply: Faith without works is dead. What one concrete act this week would put your faith into practice?
