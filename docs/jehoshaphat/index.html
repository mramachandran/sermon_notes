Jehoshaphat

A godly king who taught his people God's law, prayed his way out of disaster, and kept making friends with the wrong family.

Jehoshaphat, son of Asa, was one of Judah's best kings. He sought God, sent teachers across the land with the book of the law, and reformed the courts. When a vast army invaded, he admitted he did not know what to do and turned to God in prayer, and Judah won without striking a blow. Yet he tied his family to Ahab's by marriage, fought beside Ahab, and later partnered with Ahab's sons. A prophet asked him, "Shouldest thou help the ungodly?" His story shows real faith beside a real weakness, and how alliances shape later generations.

The life of Jehoshaphat

Dates are approximate (BC). He probably reigned with his sick father Asa from c. 872 BC, alone from c. 870 BC.

  1. c. 872–870 BCAge 35

    Becomes king

    He begins to reign in the fourth year of Ahab of Israel. He walks in the first ways of David, seeks God rather than the Baals, and strengthens Judah against Israel.

    1 Kings 22:41–43; 2 Chronicles 17:1–6
  2. 3rd year of his reign

    Sends out teachers

    He sends princes, Levites and priests through the cities of Judah, carrying the book of the law of the LORD and teaching the people.

    2 Chronicles 17:7–9
  3. Early reign

    Alliance with Ahab

    Rich and honoured, he joins himself to Ahab by marriage. His son Jehoram marries Ahab's daughter, a match that will bring Baal worship into Judah's royal house.

    2 Chronicles 18:1; 2 Kings 8:18
  4. c. 853 BC

    Ramoth-gilead

    He joins Ahab against Syria. Syrian captains mistake him for Ahab; he cries out, and God turns them away.

    1 Kings 22:1–38; 2 Chronicles 18:1–34
  5. After the battle

    Rebuked, then reforms

    Jehu son of Hanani rebukes him for helping the ungodly. Jehoshaphat goes out among the people to bring them back to God and sets judges in every fortified city.

    2 Chronicles 19:1–11
  6. Later

    Victory without a battle

    Moab, Ammon and men of Mount Seir invade. He proclaims a fast and prays with all Judah. Singers lead the army out, and the invaders destroy one another.

    2 Chronicles 20:1–30
  7. c. 848 BC

    Dies

    After twenty-five years as king he is buried with his fathers in the city of David. His son Jehoram reigns in his place.

    1 Kings 22:50; 2 Chronicles 21:1

Key activities

Kings says the high places were not taken away (1 Kings 22:43), while Chronicles says he removed them (2 Chronicles 17:6), then also notes they remained (2 Chronicles 20:33). Many readers think he removed some, or removed pagan shrines while others survived.

  1. 1

    Taught the law

    He sent officials and Levites through Judah to teach from the book of the law.

    2 Chronicles 17:7–9
  2. 2

    Made a risky alliance

    He told Ahab, "I am as thou art, and my people as thy people."

    1 Kings 22:4; 2 Chronicles 18:3
  3. 3

    Asked for a true prophet

    After about four hundred prophets all promised victory, he asked, "Is there not here a prophet of the LORD besides?"

    1 Kings 22:5–8
  4. 4

    Took correction

    After Jehu's rebuke he went out himself, from Beersheba to Mount Ephraim, to bring the people back to God.

    2 Chronicles 19:1–4
  5. 5

    Reformed justice

    He told the judges, "ye judge not for man, but for the LORD," and warned them against bribes.

    2 Chronicles 19:5–11
  6. 6

    Prayed in helplessness

    Facing invasion, he stood in the temple and admitted, "neither know we what to do."

    2 Chronicles 20:3–13
  7. 7

    Sent singers ahead

    He appointed singers to go before the army, praising God's mercy.

    2 Chronicles 20:20–22
  8. 8

    Built ships that broke

    He joined Ahaziah of Israel in a shipping venture. A prophet warned him, and the ships were wrecked.

    2 Chronicles 20:35–37; 1 Kings 22:48–49
  9. 9

    Marched against Moab

    He joined Joram of Israel and the king of Edom. Elisha helped them only for Jehoshaphat's sake.

    2 Kings 3:4–27

For discussion

  1. Observe: What three things did Jehoshaphat admit in his prayer (2 Chronicles 20:12)?
  2. Understand: Jehoshaphat was a godly king, yet he kept joining himself to Ahab's family. Why might a good person keep making the same mistake?
  3. Apply: "Neither know we what to do: but our eyes are upon thee." Where do you need to pray that prayer this week?
  4. Apply: Jehoshaphat's alliances affected his children and grandchildren. What partnership or friendship in your life needs a hard look?

The world around them

Moab, Ammon and Edom (Mount Seir) lay east and south of the Dead Sea. The invaders of 2 Chronicles 20 came around the sea and up toward En-gedi, an oasis on its western shore. In 1868 a stone inscription of Mesha, king of Moab, was found east of the Dead Sea. It describes Moab throwing off Israel's rule, which matches the revolt in 2 Kings 3:4–5. Ezion-geber, on the Gulf of Aqaba, was Judah's port for trade by sea to the south.

Words and places

  • En-gedi. A spring-fed oasis on the western shore of the Dead Sea (2 Chronicles 20:2).
  • Valley of Berachah. "Valley of blessing," where Judah gathered to praise God after the victory (2 Chronicles 20:26).
  • Ezion-geber. A port at the northern tip of the Gulf of Aqaba (1 Kings 22:48).
  • Valley of Jehoshaphat. A name in Joel for the place of God's final judgment. Whether it refers to this king is debated (Joel 3:2, 12).
