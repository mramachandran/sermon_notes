Jephthah

An outcast who became Gilead's deliverer, and whose rash vow ended in grief.

Jephthah was a warrior from Gilead, east of the Jordan. He was the son of a harlot, and his half-brothers drove him from home. He gathered a band of men in the land of Tob. Years later, when the Ammonites pressed Israel, the elders who had rejected him came asking for help. Jephthah became their head, argued Israel's case with the king of Ammon, and then won a great victory when the Spirit of the LORD came upon him. But before the battle he made a vow, and his only daughter was the first to come out to meet him. His story holds real faith and tragic rashness side by side. Hebrews still names him among the heroes of faith.

The life of Jephthah

No calendar dates. Jephthah's "300 years" (Judges 11:26) is cited for the early Exodus date; others take it as a round number.

  1. Before his call

    Ammon oppresses Gilead

    For eighteen years the Philistines and Ammonites oppress Israel. Israel cries out, puts away its idols, and serves the LORD again.

    Judges 10:6–16
  2. Early life

    Driven out

    Gilead's sons by his wife thrust him out. He flees to the land of Tob, where "vain men" gather to him.

    Judges 11:1–3
  3. Ammon attacks

    Called home

    The elders of Gilead ask him to be their captain. He agrees if he becomes their head, sealed before the LORD in Mizpeh.

    Judges 11:4–11
  4. Before the battle

    Argues Israel's case

    He tells the king of Ammon that Israel took the land from the Amorites, and leaves the case to "the LORD the Judge."

    Judges 11:12–28
  5. The campaign

    Vows and wins

    The Spirit of the LORD comes upon him. He vows that whatever comes out of his house to meet him will be the LORD's, offered as a burnt offering. He defeats Ammon across twenty cities.

    Judges 11:29–33
  6. Coming home

    His daughter meets him

    His only child comes out with timbrels and dances. He tears his clothes. She asks for two months to mourn her virginity on the mountains, and then he does "according to his vow."

    Judges 11:34–40
  7. Afterward

    War with Ephraim

    Ephraim complains that it was not called to fight. Jephthah's men hold the Jordan fords and test fugitives with the word "Shibboleth." He judges Israel six years, dies, and is buried in Gilead.

    Judges 12:1–7

Key activities

Readers differ on what Jephthah's vow meant for his daughter. Many believe he offered her as a burnt offering, a grave sin the law forbade; others believe he gave her to lifelong, unmarried service of the LORD, pointing to the stress on her virginity (Judges 11:37–39).

  1. 1

    Rose from rejection

    Thrust out by his brothers, he became leader of a band of men in Tob.

    Judges 11:1–3
  2. 2

    Made terms with the elders

    He agreed to fight only if Gilead made him head, and he spoke all his words before the LORD in Mizpeh.

    Judges 11:4–11
  3. 3

    Tried words before war

    He sent messengers to the king of Ammon and argued from Israel's history.

    Judges 11:12–27
  4. 4

    Trusted the Judge

    He left the dispute with "the LORD the Judge."

    Judges 11:27
  5. 5

    Led by the Spirit

    The Spirit of the LORD came upon him, and he passed through Gilead and Manasseh to meet Ammon.

    Judges 11:29
  6. 6

    Made a rash vow

    He promised God whatever first met him as a burnt offering if he gave victory.

    Judges 11:30–31
  7. 7

    Won the victory

    The LORD delivered Ammon into his hands, from Aroer to Minnith.

    Judges 11:32–33
  8. 8

    Kept his word in grief

    "I have opened my mouth unto the LORD, and I cannot go back."

    Judges 11:35
  9. 9

    Fought fellow Israelites

    When Ephraim threatened him, forty-two thousand Ephraimites fell at the Jordan.

    Judges 12:1–6

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Jephthah.

  • Numbers 21:21–31; Deuteronomy 2:26–36The history he retold to Ammon
  • Echo: Ecclesiastes 5:5Better not to vow than to vow and not pay
  • Echo: Genesis 22:11–12God stopped Abraham's hand over Isaac

  • Hebrews 11:32–33Named among those who "through faith subdued kingdoms"

  • Echo: Matthew 21:42"The stone which the builders rejected"

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did the elders of Gilead promise Jephthah, and where was it sealed (Judges 11:8–11)?
  2. Understand: Jephthah had real faith (Hebrews 11:32), yet he made a rash vow. How can both be true of the same person?
  3. Apply: Jephthah was rejected by his family and later called back. How might God use a hard part of your past?
  4. Apply: Is there a promise you made quickly, to God or to someone else, that you need to think through or keep this week?
