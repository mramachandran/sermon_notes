Jeremiah

The weeping prophet who warned Jerusalem for forty years and promised a new covenant.

Jeremiah was a young man from a priestly family in Anathoth when God called him in King Josiah's thirteenth year. For about forty years, through Judah's last kings, he warned that Babylon would destroy Jerusalem unless the people turned back to God. Few listened. He was beaten, put in the stocks, thrown into a muddy cistern and accused of treason. He wept and argued honestly with God. Yet he bought a field during the siege as a sign of hope, and foretold a new covenant written on the heart. He saw Jerusalem fall and ended his days in Egypt.

The life of Jeremiah

Dates are approximate (BC). The book is not in time order, so entries gather events from different chapters.

  1. c. 627 BC13th year of Josiah

    Called as a youth

    He says he is only a child. God touches his mouth and sets him over nations.

    Jeremiah 1:1–10
  2. c. 609 BCStart of Jehoiakim's reign

    The temple sermon

    He warns that the temple will not save lawbreakers. Priests demand his death, but Ahikam protects him.

    Jeremiah 7:1–15; 26:1–24
  3. c. 605–604 BCJehoiakim's 4th–5th years

    The scroll burned

    Baruch reads Jeremiah's words in the temple. The king cuts up the scroll and burns it, and Jeremiah dictates it again.

    Jeremiah 36:1–32
  4. c. 594 BC4th year of Zedekiah

    The yoke

    Jeremiah wears a yoke and tells the nations to serve Babylon. Hananiah breaks it and dies that year.

    Jeremiah 27:2–12; 28:1–17
  5. c. 588 BCDuring the siege

    Prison and the cistern

    Arrested as a deserter, he is lowered into a muddy cistern, and Ebed-melech pulls him out.

    Jeremiah 37:11–38:13
  6. c. 587 BC10th year of Zedekiah

    Buys a field

    From prison he buys his cousin's field at Anathoth as a sign that fields will again be bought.

    Jeremiah 32:1–15
  7. c. 586 BCJerusalem falls

    Set free

    The Babylonians release him, and he stays with Gedaliah at Mizpah.

    Jeremiah 39:1–14; 40:1–6
  8. After c. 586 BCIn Egypt

    Carried away

    After Gedaliah is murdered, the survivors flee to Egypt and take Jeremiah and Baruch with them.

    Jeremiah 41:1–43:7; 44:1

Key activities

Jeremiah is often called "the weeping prophet." Tradition names him as the author of Lamentations, though the book itself does not name its author.

  1. 1

    Accepted a hard call

    He felt too young, but God said, "Behold, I have put my words in thy mouth."

    Jeremiah 1:6–9
  2. 2

    Preached at the temple gate

    He told Judah to amend their ways, and warned that the temple had become "a den of robbers."

    Jeremiah 7:1–11
  3. 3

    Acted out God's message

    He used a linen girdle, a potter's clay, a broken bottle and a wooden yoke as signs.

    Jeremiah 13:1–11; 18:1–6; 19:1–11; 27:2
  4. 4

    Suffered for the truth

    Pashur the priest had him beaten and put in the stocks.

    Jeremiah 20:1–2
  5. 5

    Poured out his heart

    He complained bitterly to God, yet said the word was like "a burning fire shut up in my bones."

    Jeremiah 20:7–18
  6. 6

    Wrote to the exiles

    He told them to build houses, plant gardens and seek the peace of Babylon, for they would return after seventy years.

    Jeremiah 29:4–14
  7. 7

    Promised a new covenant

    God would write his law in their hearts and remember their sin no more.

    Jeremiah 31:31–34
  8. 8

    Bought a field in a war zone

    He paid for land the Babylonians were about to take, trusting that nothing was too hard for God.

    Jeremiah 32:6–27
  9. 9

    Spoke plainly to a weak king

    He told Zedekiah in secret that surrender to Babylon would save the city.

    Jeremiah 38:14–23
  10. 10

    Stayed with those left behind

    Offered a place in Babylon, he chose to stay with those left in the land.

    Jeremiah 40:1–6
  11. 11

    Warned against fleeing to Egypt

    He told the survivors to remain in Judah, but they would not listen.

    Jeremiah 42:7–43:4

For discussion

  1. Observe: What excuse did Jeremiah give, and how did God answer it (Jeremiah 1:6–9)?
  2. Understand: Why do you think God had Jeremiah buy a field just before Jerusalem fell (Jeremiah 32:6–15)?
  3. Apply: Jeremiah told God exactly how he felt. What would it look like for you to be that honest with God in prayer?
  4. Apply: The exiles were told to seek the good of the city where they lived. What is one way you could do that where you live?
