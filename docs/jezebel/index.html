Jezebel

The foreign queen who brought Baal to Israel's throne and used power without conscience.

Jezebel was a princess of Sidon who married Ahab, king of Israel. She set out to make Baal the god of Israel. She fed hundreds of Baal's prophets at her own table and hunted down the prophets of the LORD. After Elijah's victory on Mount Carmel, one message from her sent him fleeing. When Ahab could not buy Naboth's vineyard, she arranged a mock trial and had Naboth stoned. Elijah foretold a shameful end for her, and years later Jehu fulfilled it at Jezreel. Her name became a byword, and the book of Revelation uses it for a false teacher who led God's people astray.

The life of Jezebel

Dates are approximate (BC) and follow the house chronology. Scripture does not say when she married Ahab or how old she was.

  1. By c. 874 BC

    Marries Ahab

    A Phoenician princess, daughter of Ethbaal of Sidon, marries Ahab of Israel. Ahab begins to serve Baal and builds a temple and altar for him in Samaria.

    1 Kings 16:31–32
  2. c. 860s BC

    Kills the LORD's prophets

    Jezebel cuts off the prophets of the LORD, and Obadiah hides a hundred of them in caves. Meanwhile, 450 prophets of Baal and 400 prophets of the groves eat at her table.

    1 Kings 18:4, 13, 19
  3. After Carmel

    Threatens Elijah

    Ahab tells her about the fire from heaven and the death of Baal's prophets. She swears to kill Elijah by the same time the next day, and the prophet runs for his life.

    1 Kings 19:1–3
  4. Later

    Plots against Naboth

    Finding Ahab sulking, she writes letters in his name, arranges false witnesses, and has Naboth stoned. Elijah declares that the dogs will eat Jezebel by the wall of Jezreel.

    1 Kings 21:5–23
  5. c. 853–841 BC

    Queen mother

    After Ahab dies, his sons Ahaziah and Joram reign in turn. She remains a powerful presence, and Jehu later names her idolatry as the reason there can be no peace.

    1 Kings 22:51–53; 2 Kings 9:22
  6. c. 841 BC

    Dies at Jezreel

    She paints her face, dresses her hair, and taunts Jehu from a window. Her own servants throw her down. When men go to bury her, only her skull, feet and palms remain.

    2 Kings 9:30–37

Key activities

Scripture never shows Jezebel in worship or in private. It shows her using power. Read her story alongside Ahab's, because each shaped the other.

  1. 1

    Brought Baal to court

    Through her marriage, Baal worship became the royal religion in Samaria. Kings says she "stirred up" Ahab to evil.

    1 Kings 16:31–33; 21:25
  2. 2

    Persecuted the prophets

    She cut off the prophets of the LORD, and some survived only because Obadiah hid them.

    1 Kings 18:4, 13
  3. 3

    Fed the prophets of Baal

    Eight hundred and fifty prophets of Baal and of the groves ate at her table.

    1 Kings 18:19
  4. 4

    Vowed to kill Elijah

    Her message began, "So let the gods do to me, and more also," and Elijah fled to the wilderness.

    1 Kings 19:2–3
  5. 5

    Mocked her husband

    "Dost thou now govern the kingdom of Israel?" she asked, and took matters into her own hands.

    1 Kings 21:5–7
  6. 6

    Used the law to murder

    She sealed letters with Ahab's seal, proclaimed a fast, and set two worthless men to accuse Naboth of blaspheming God and the king.

    1 Kings 21:8–14
  7. 7

    Faced death defiantly

    She greeted Jehu with a taunt: "Had Zimri peace, who slew his master?"

    2 Kings 9:30–31

The world around them

Sidon and Tyre were Phoenician cities on the coast north of Israel, in what is now Lebanon. They were rich trading ports whose ships sailed across the Mediterranean. Baal was a Canaanite storm god, believed to send rain and harvests. Many readers note that a drought struck at the very heart of his claims. The Jewish historian Josephus, quoting an older writer, says Jezebel's father Ethbaal had been a priest of Astarte who seized the throne of Tyre. Scripture calls him simply king of the Zidonians.

Words and places

  • Zidonians. The people of Sidon, a leading Phoenician city (1 Kings 16:31).
  • Baal. A Hebrew word meaning "lord" or "master," used for the Canaanite storm god (1 Kings 16:31–32).
  • Sons of Belial. A KJV phrase for worthless or wicked men (1 Kings 21:10, 13).
  • Jezreel. A royal city overlooking the valley of Jezreel. Naboth's vineyard lay next to Ahab's palace there (1 Kings 21:1).
