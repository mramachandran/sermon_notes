John Mark

The young helper who turned back, got a second chance, and became "profitable ... for the ministry."

John Mark grew up in Jerusalem, in a home where the first believers gathered to pray. He was a cousin of Barnabas, and he set out with Barnabas and Paul on their first missionary journey. Partway through, at Perga, he left them and went home. That choice later caused a sharp quarrel that split Paul and Barnabas. Barnabas gave Mark a second chance, and years later Paul himself called Mark a fellow worker and asked for him near the end of his life. Peter called him "Marcus my son." Early church tradition names him as the writer of the Gospel of Mark. His story is one of failure, patience and restoration.

The life of John Mark

Dates are approximate (AD) and follow Paul's journeys. The order of events after Acts 15 is pieced together from the letters.

  1. c. AD 44Jerusalem

    A praying household

    Freed from prison, Peter goes to "the house of Mary the mother of John, whose surname was Mark," where many are praying.

    Acts 12:1–17
  2. c. AD 44–46Antioch and Cyprus

    Helper on the first journey

    Barnabas and Saul take Mark to Antioch, then on to Cyprus, "and they had also John to their minister."

    Acts 12:25; 13:1–5
  3. c. AD 46–47Perga

    Turns back

    At Perga in Pamphylia, John leaves them and returns to Jerusalem. Luke does not say why.

    Acts 13:13
  4. c. AD 49Antioch

    The sharp split

    Paul refuses to take Mark again. He and Barnabas part, and Barnabas sails with Mark to Cyprus.

    Acts 15:36–39
  5. c. AD 60–62With Paul in prison

    A fellow worker

    Mark is with Paul again, one of the few Jewish co-workers who have been "a comfort unto me."

    Colossians 4:10–11; Philemon 24
  6. c. AD 60s"Babylon"

    With Peter

    Peter sends greetings from "Marcus my son," probably meaning a son in the faith. Many take "Babylon" as a code name for Rome.

    1 Peter 5:13
  7. c. AD 64–67Paul's last letter

    Sent for

    Near the end of his life, Paul asks Timothy to bring Mark, "for he is profitable to me for the ministry."

    2 Timothy 4:11

Key activities

Scripture never explains why John Mark left at Perga or how he and Paul were reconciled. It records only the break and, years later, Paul's warm words.

  1. 1

    Grew up among praying believers

    His mother's house was a gathering place where the church prayed for Peter's release.

    Acts 12:12
  2. 2

    Went with Barnabas and Saul

    He traveled from Jerusalem to Antioch with the two leaders.

    Acts 12:25
  3. 3

    Served as an assistant

    On Cyprus he helped the missionaries in their work.

    Acts 13:5
  4. 4

    Left the mission

    At Perga he departed and went back to Jerusalem, a choice Paul still held against him later.

    Acts 13:13; 15:38
  5. 5

    Received a second chance

    Barnabas, his cousin, insisted on taking him again and took him to Cyprus when Paul would not.

    Acts 15:37–39; Colossians 4:10
  6. 6

    Worked alongside Paul again

    Mark became one of Paul's "fellowlabourers" during his imprisonment.

    Colossians 4:10–11; Philemon 24
  7. 7

    Proved useful in ministry

    Paul asked for Mark because he was "profitable" to him for the ministry.

    2 Timothy 4:11
  8. 8

    Stood by Peter

    Peter called him "Marcus my son."

    1 Peter 5:13

For discussion

  1. Observe: How does Paul speak about Mark in Acts 15:38 compared with 2 Timothy 4:11?
  2. Understand: What was each of Paul and Barnabas trying to protect in their view of Mark (Acts 15:36–39)?
  3. Apply: Who in your life needs a second chance from you?
  4. Apply: Where do you need to stop letting a past failure define you?
