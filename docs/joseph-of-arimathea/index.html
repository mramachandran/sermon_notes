Joseph of Arimathea

A rich member of the council who went boldly to Pilate and laid Jesus in his own tomb.

Joseph of Arimathea was a wealthy member of the Jewish council and a secret disciple of Jesus, afraid of the other leaders. He had not agreed with the council's plan to kill Jesus. On the afternoon Jesus died, with the sabbath hours away, Joseph stepped out of the shadows and boldly asked Pilate for the body. With Nicodemus he wrapped it in linen and spices and laid it in his own new rock tomb. All four Gospels tell of him, and his tomb became the place where the resurrection was first announced.

The life of Joseph of Arimathea

The date follows the house chronology, with the crucifixion c. AD 30; on the other main view, AD 33. Joseph appears only on the day of Jesus' death, though Luke tells us how he had voted before.

  1. Before the crucifixionIn the council

    Refuses to consent

    Joseph is "a good man, and a just," who waits for the kingdom of God. He has not agreed with the council's plan and action against Jesus.

    Luke 23:50–51
  2. c. AD 30Friday afternoon

    Goes boldly to Pilate

    It is the day of preparation, the day before the sabbath. Joseph goes in to Pilate and asks for the body. Pilate checks with the centurion that Jesus is dead, then gives it to him.

    Mark 15:42–45
  3. c. AD 30Before sunset

    Takes down the body

    Joseph buys fine linen and takes the body down. With Nicodemus, who brings myrrh and aloes, he winds it in linen with the spices, as the Jews bury their dead.

    Mark 15:46; John 19:38–40
  4. c. AD 30Before the sabbath

    Lays Jesus in his tomb

    In a garden near the place of crucifixion, he lays Jesus in "his own new tomb, which he had hewn out in the rock." He rolls a great stone to the door and departs.

    Matthew 27:59–60; John 19:41–42

The world around them

Wealthy families in Jerusalem were buried in tombs cut into the rock outside the city walls. A body was laid on a shelf or bench inside. Later, when only bones remained, they were often gathered into a stone box called an ossuary. A large stone was rolled across the low entrance. Jewish burial was quick, usually on the day of death, and the law required that a body hanged on a tree be buried before night (Deuteronomy 21:22–23). With the sabbath beginning at sunset, Joseph had only a few hours.

Tradition

Medieval legends say Joseph brought the cup of the Last Supper, the Holy Grail, to Britain and founded a church at Glastonbury in England. These stories appear more than a thousand years after the Gospels and are not Scripture.

Words and places

  • Counsellor. A member of the council, the Sanhedrin (Mark 15:43; Luke 23:50).
  • Preparation. The day before the sabbath, when food and other needs were made ready (Mark 15:42).
  • Sepulchre. A tomb (Matthew 27:60–61).
  • Arimathaea. Joseph's home town, "a city of the Jews" (Luke 23:51).
