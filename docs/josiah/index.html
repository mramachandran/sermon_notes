Josiah

The boy king who found the Book of the Law and turned Judah back to God.

Grandson of Manasseh, the most wicked of Judah's kings, Josiah became its most wholehearted reformer. Named in prophecy three centuries before his birth, he took the throne at eight. When a forgotten scroll of God's Law was found in the temple, he wept, gathered the nation, and led the most thorough return to God in Judah's history.

The life of Josiah

Dates are approximate (BC). Years count from the start of his reign.

  1. c. 930 BC300 yrs before

    Prophesied by name

    A man of God at Bethel foretells that a son of David named Josiah will burn priests' bones on Jeroboam's altar.

    1 Kings 13:1–3
  2. c. 640 BCAge 8

    Crowned king

    After servants assassinate his father Amon, the people of the land make young Josiah king.

    2 Kings 21:23–22:1
  3. c. 632 BCAge 16

    Begins to seek God

    While still young, he begins to seek the God of David his father.

    2 Chronicles 34:3
  4. c. 628 BCAge 20

    Purges the land

    He tears down high places, Asherah poles and idols in Judah, Jerusalem and the northern tribes.

    2 Chronicles 34:3–7
  5. c. 627 BC13th year

    Jeremiah is called

    The word of the LORD comes to young Jeremiah; Zephaniah also prophesies.

    Jeremiah 1:1–2; Zephaniah 1:1
  6. c. 622 BCAge 26

    The Book is found

    Hilkiah finds the Book of the Law in the temple. Josiah hears it, tears his clothes, and consults Huldah.

    2 Kings 22:3–20
  7. c. 622 BC18th year

    Covenant and Passover

    He reads the Book to all the people, cleanses the land, and keeps a Passover unmatched since Samuel.

    2 Kings 23:1–25
  8. c. 609 BCAge 39

    Death at Megiddo

    Ignoring Pharaoh Neco's warning, he is shot by archers and dies. Jeremiah laments him.

    2 Chronicles 35:20–25

Key activities

Kings tells the reforms after the Book is found; Chronicles puts the first purge six years earlier.

  1. 1

    Sought God while young

    His faith began as a personal search at sixteen.

    2 Chronicles 34:3
  2. 2

    Purged Judah and the north

    He broke down altars, Asherah poles and images and ground them to dust.

    2 Chronicles 34:3–7
  3. 3

    Repaired the temple

    He paid workers so trusted that no accounting was asked.

    2 Kings 22:3–7
  4. 4

    Heard the Book and wept

    He tore his clothes when the rediscovered Law was read to him.

    2 Kings 22:8–13
  5. 5

    Inquired of Huldah

    Judgment would come, but not in his days, because his heart was tender.

    2 Kings 22:14–20
  6. 6

    Renewed the covenant

    He read the whole Book aloud and pledged the nation to keep it.

    2 Kings 23:1–3
  7. 7

    Cleansed the temple

    He burned the vessels made for Baal, Asherah and the stars in the Kidron fields.

    2 Kings 23:4–7
  8. 8

    Ended the high places

    He deposed idol priests and tore down shrines, including Solomon's.

    2 Kings 23:5, 8–9, 13–14
  9. 9

    Stopped child sacrifice and the occult

    He defiled Topheth and removed the mediums and household idols.

    2 Kings 23:10–12, 24
  10. 10

    Fulfilled prophecy at Bethel

    He burned bones on Jeroboam's altar but spared the prophet's tomb.

    2 Kings 23:15–20
  11. 11

    Kept the great Passover

    There had been none like it since Samuel.

    2 Kings 23:21–23; 2 Chronicles 35:1–19
  12. 12

    Fell at Megiddo

    He would not heed Pharaoh Neco's warning, and he was shot and died.

    2 Chronicles 35:20–24

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Josiah do the moment he heard the Book of the Law (2 Kings 22:11–13)?
  2. Understand: Huldah said judgment would still come. Why do you think Josiah reformed the nation anyway?
  3. Apply: Josiah broke with his family's pattern. What habit would you like to break with God's help?
  4. Apply: Is there a "lost book" in your life, such as Scripture or prayer? What is one step toward it this week?
