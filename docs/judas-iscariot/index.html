Judas Iscariot

The chosen apostle who betrayed his Master for thirty pieces of silver.

Judas Iscariot was one of the Twelve. Jesus chose him, sent him out to preach and heal, and trusted him with the group's money. He saw the miracles and heard the teaching as closely as Peter and John did. Yet he became the man who handed Jesus over to his enemies. John says Judas was a thief who stole from the common purse. Luke and John say Satan entered him. For thirty pieces of silver he led an armed crowd to Gethsemane and marked Jesus with a kiss. When he saw Jesus condemned, he tried to return the money, and then he took his own life. His story is one of the most sobering in Scripture. It shows that being close to Jesus is not the same as belonging to him.

The life of Judas Iscariot

Dates are approximate (AD). The crucifixion is dated AD 30 or AD 33; this study uses c. AD 30. The last five entries fall within one week.

  1. c. AD 28On a mountain

    Chosen as an apostle

    Jesus names the Twelve after a night of prayer. In each Gospel list Judas Iscariot is named last and marked as the one who would betray him.

    Luke 6:12–16; Mark 3:19
  2. c. AD 29Capernaum

    Singled out

    When many disciples turn away, Jesus says, "Have not I chosen you twelve, and one of you is a devil?" John explains that he meant Judas.

    John 6:66–71
  3. c. AD 30Bethany

    Objects to the ointment

    Mary anoints Jesus with costly ointment. Judas asks why it was not sold and given to the poor. John says he cared not for the poor but was a thief.

    John 12:1–8
  4. c. AD 30Jerusalem

    Strikes a deal

    Judas goes to the chief priests and asks what they will give him to hand Jesus over. They agree on thirty pieces of silver, and he watches for his chance.

    Matthew 26:14–16; Luke 22:3–6
  5. c. AD 30The last supper

    Goes out into the night

    Jesus says one of them will betray him and gives Judas the sop. Satan enters him, Jesus says, "That thou doest, do quickly," and Judas goes out.

    John 13:21–30
  6. c. AD 30Gethsemane

    Betrays with a kiss

    Judas arrives with a great multitude carrying swords and staves. He greets Jesus, "Hail, master," and kisses him, the agreed sign for the arrest.

    Matthew 26:47–50
  7. c. AD 30Jerusalem

    Dies in despair

    Seeing Jesus condemned, Judas brings back the silver and says he has betrayed innocent blood. The priests will not take it back. He throws it down in the temple and hangs himself.

    Matthew 27:3–5

Key activities

Matthew says Judas hanged himself and the priests bought a potter's field with the money. Acts says Judas "purchased a field" with it and, "falling headlong," burst open. Many readers combine the two accounts; others see two independent reports. Both call the place the field of blood.

  1. 1

    Was chosen and sent

    He was among the Twelve whom Jesus sent to preach and heal.

    Matthew 10:1–5
  2. 2

    Kept the bag

    He held the group's money and stole from it.

    John 12:6; 13:29
  3. 3

    Complained about Mary's gift

    He called her ointment a waste that could have been sold for three hundred pence.

    John 12:4–5
  4. 4

    Let Satan in

    Scripture says the devil put betrayal into his heart and that Satan entered him.

    Luke 22:3; John 13:2, 27
  5. 5

    Sold his Master

    He asked the chief priests, "What will ye give me, and I will deliver him unto you?"

    Matthew 26:15
  6. 6

    Had his feet washed

    Jesus washed the disciples' feet, yet said, "Ye are not all clean," knowing who would betray him.

    John 13:5, 10–11
  7. 7

    Asked if it was he

    He asked, "Master, is it I?" and Jesus answered, "Thou hast said."

    Matthew 26:25
  8. 8

    Left the supper

    He took the sop and went out, "and it was night."

    John 13:30
  9. 9

    Kissed Jesus

    He used a sign of friendship to point out Jesus to the arresting crowd. Jesus called him "Friend."

    Matthew 26:48–50
  10. 10

    Regretted too late

    He said, "I have sinned in that I have betrayed the innocent blood," and brought back the silver.

    Matthew 27:3–4
  11. 11

    Took his own life

    He went out and hanged himself.

    Matthew 27:5
  12. 12

    Left an empty place

    Peter said Judas had gone "to his own place," and Matthias took his office among the apostles.

    Acts 1:16–26

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Judas.

Old Testament

  • Psalm 41:9The friend who ate bread, applied by Jesus (John 13:18)
  • Psalm 69:25; 109:8Applied by Peter to Judas's empty office (Acts 1:20)
  • Zechariah 11:12–13Thirty pieces cast to the potter; Matthew cites "Jeremy" (27:9)
  • Echo: Genesis 37:26–28Judah sells his brother Joseph for silver
  • Echo: 2 Samuel 17:23Ahithophel betrays David and hangs himself

New Testament

  • Matthew 26:24Better for that man if he had not been born
  • John 17:12None lost "but the son of perdition"
  • Acts 1:16–25Peter recounts his end; a replacement is chosen
  • TraditionHis motives are debated; Scripture names greed and Satan's work

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Judas do after he saw that Jesus was condemned (Matthew 27:3–5)?
  2. Understand: Peter and Judas both failed Jesus that night. Why did their stories end so differently?
  3. Apply: Judas was stealing from the bag long before the betrayal. What hidden habit do you need to bring into the light?
  4. Apply: The priests told Judas, "see thou to that." How could you point someone full of regret to Jesus this week?
