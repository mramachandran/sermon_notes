The lesser-known apostles

Faithful names on the list: James son of Alphaeus, Judas (Thaddaeus), Simon the Zealot, and Matthias.

Every list of the Twelve ends with a few names we know almost nothing about. James the son of Alphaeus is never quoted. Judas, "not Iscariot," asks Jesus one question at the last supper. Simon is remembered only by his label, "the Canaanite" or "Zelotes," which probably marks him as zealous for Israel's cause. After the resurrection Matthias is chosen to take the place of Judas Iscariot. These men were sent out with the same authority as Peter and John. Their place on the list reminds us that most of Christ's servants work without fame.

The life of The lesser-known apostles

Dates are approximate (AD). Jesus' ministry is dated c. AD 27/28–30 in this study, and the crucifixion c. AD 30. Scripture gives no ages.

  1. c. AD 28On a mountain

    Chosen as apostles

    After a night of prayer, Jesus chooses twelve from among his disciples. James son of Alphaeus, Judas and Simon are named among them.

    Luke 6:12–16
  2. c. AD 29Galilee

    Sent out two by two

    Jesus gives the Twelve power over unclean spirits and sends them to preach repentance, heal the sick, and anoint with oil.

    Mark 6:7–13; Matthew 10:1–15
  3. c. AD 30The last supper

    Judas asks a question

    Judas, "not Iscariot," asks why Jesus will show himself to the disciples and not to the world. Jesus speaks of love, obedience and his dwelling with them.

    John 14:21–23
  4. c. AD 30Jerusalem

    Wait in the upper room

    After the ascension James, Judas and Simon pray with the other apostles, the women, and Jesus' mother and brothers.

    Acts 1:13–14
  5. c. AD 30Jerusalem

    Matthias is chosen

    Peter calls for a witness of the resurrection to replace Judas Iscariot. After prayer the lot falls on Matthias, who is numbered with the eleven apostles.

    Acts 1:15–26

Key activities

The names in the lists differ slightly. Most readers take Thaddaeus, Lebbaeus and "Judas of James" as one man known by more than one name. Luke's Greek says only "Judas of James," so he may be James's son rather than his brother; the KJV supplies "brother."

The world around them

Jewish Palestine in Jesus' day was restless under Roman rule. Some Jews looked for a Messiah who would drive out the Romans by force. In AD 6 Judas the Galilean led a revolt against a Roman census (Acts 5:37). Josephus later names the Zealots as a party in the war with Rome that began in AD 66. Whether that organized party existed in Jesus' day is debated. Simon's title may mean he belonged to such a group, or simply that he was zealous for God's law.

Words and places

  • Zelotes. A Greek word for "zealot," a person on fire for a cause (Luke 6:15).
  • Canaanite. In Matthew 10:4 this probably comes from an Aramaic word for "zealous," not from the land of Canaan.
  • Alphaeus. The father of James; Levi's father has the same name, but Scripture does not say they were brothers (Mark 2:14; 3:18).
  • Casting lots. A way of seeking God's choice, used before the Spirit was given at Pentecost (Acts 1:26).
