Lot

Abraham's nephew, who chose the well-watered plain and was pulled out of Sodom by the hand.

Lot was Abraham's nephew, the son of his brother Haran, who died in Ur. He traveled with his uncle into Canaan and grew rich alongside him. When their herds became too large to share the land, Abram let Lot choose first. Lot chose the green plain near Sodom, a city known for great wickedness. Step by step he moved closer, until he sat in Sodom's gate. When God judged the city, angels had to take Lot by the hand and lead him out. Yet the New Testament calls him "just Lot," a righteous man troubled by the evil around him.

The life of Lot

Genesis gives no dates or ages for Lot. The order below follows the text.

  1. In Ur

    Loses his father

    Lot's father Haran dies in Ur of the Chaldees. Terah takes his grandson Lot with Abram and Sarai toward Canaan, and they settle in Haran.

    Genesis 11:27–31
  2. Leaving Haran

    Goes with Abram

    When Abram obeys God's call, "Lot went with him." They arrive in Canaan and later return together from Egypt, both rich in flocks and herds.

    Genesis 12:4–5; 13:1–5
  3. Near Bethel

    Chooses the plain

    Their herdsmen quarrel. Abram offers Lot his pick of the land, and Lot chooses the well-watered plain of the Jordan and pitches his tent toward Sodom.

    Genesis 13:6–13
  4. In Sodom

    Taken captive

    Four eastern kings defeat Sodom and carry off Lot, now living in the city. Abram pursues them, rescues Lot and brings back his goods.

    Genesis 14:11–16
  5. In Sodom

    Receives two angels

    Lot takes two visitors into his house. The men of the city surround it, and the angels strike them with blindness and warn Lot to leave.

    Genesis 19:1–14
  6. At dawn

    Led out

    Lot lingers, and the angels take him, his wife and his daughters by the hand. Fire falls on Sodom and Gomorrah. His wife looks back and becomes a pillar of salt.

    Genesis 19:15–26
  7. In a cave

    A shameful end

    Afraid to stay in Zoar, Lot lives in a mountain cave. His daughters make him drunk and conceive by him. Their sons become the fathers of Moab and Ammon.

    Genesis 19:30–38

Key activities

Genesis 19:8 records that Lot offered his daughters to the mob to protect his guests. Scripture reports this without approving it. Many readers see in it how deeply life in Sodom had shaped him.

  1. 1

    Followed his uncle

    He went with Abram from Haran into Canaan and shared in his blessing.

    Genesis 12:4–5; 13:5
  2. 2

    Chose by sight

    He "lifted up his eyes" and chose the richest land, even though the men of Sodom were "wicked and sinners before the LORD exceedingly."

    Genesis 13:10–13
  3. 3

    Moved into Sodom

    He first pitched his tent toward Sodom, then lived in the city and sat in its gate.

    Genesis 13:12; 14:12; 19:1
  4. 4

    Welcomed strangers

    He urged two travelers to stay under his roof and baked them a meal.

    Genesis 19:1–3
  5. 5

    Stood at the door

    He went out to the crowd and pleaded, "Do not so wickedly," but his own offer was shameful.

    Genesis 19:4–9
  6. 6

    Warned his family

    He urged his sons-in-law to flee, but they thought he was joking.

    Genesis 19:12–14
  7. 7

    Lingered

    Even at the last moment he hesitated, and the angels pulled him out "the LORD being merciful unto him."

    Genesis 19:15–16
  8. 8

    Bargained for Zoar

    He asked to flee to a small town instead of the mountains, and the angel spared it for his sake.

    Genesis 19:17–23

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Lot.

Old Testament

  • Genesis 18:22–33Abraham pleads for Sodom, where Lot lives
  • Genesis 19:29"God remembered Abraham, and sent Lot out"
  • Deuteronomy 2:9, 19Land given to "the children of Lot"
  • Psalm 83:8The "children of Lot" among Israel's enemies
  • Echo: Ruth 4:13–17Ruth the Moabitess, ancestor of David

New Testament

  • Luke 17:28–32"The days of Lot"; "Remember Lot's wife"
  • 2 Peter 2:6–9God "delivered just Lot," vexed by the wicked's way of life
  • Echo: Hebrews 11:10Abraham "looked for a city which hath foundations"

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Lot see when he chose the plain, and what does Genesis add right after (Genesis 13:10–13)?
  2. Understand: Peter calls Lot "righteous." How does that fit with what Genesis shows about his choices?
  3. Apply: Lot moved toward Sodom one step at a time. What small step toward something harmful do you need to reverse?
  4. Apply: Lot lingered even when rescue was offered. Where are you delaying something God has already made clear?
