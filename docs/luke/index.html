Luke

"The beloved physician," Paul's loyal companion, and by early tradition the writer of Luke and Acts.

Luke is named only three times in the New Testament, all in Paul's letters. Paul calls him "the beloved physician" and a fellow worker, and in Paul's last imprisonment Luke is the one friend still with him. The early church also named Luke as the author of the Gospel of Luke and the Acts of the Apostles, which together make up about a quarter of the New Testament. Neither book names its writer, but in parts of Acts the narrator says "we," as if he traveled with Paul. If Luke is that writer, he crossed into Europe with Paul, went with him to Jerusalem, and survived a shipwreck on the way to Rome. His life shows careful work and faithful friendship.

The life of Luke

Dates are approximate (AD). If Luke wrote Acts, the "we" passages (Acts 16:10–17; 20:5–21:18; 27:1–28:16) show when he was with Paul. That link is the traditional view, not a statement of the text.

  1. c. AD 49–50Troas

    "We" join Paul

    After Paul's vision of the man of Macedonia, the narrator writes, "immediately we endeavoured to go into Macedonia." The first "we" passage runs through the founding of the church at Philippi.

    Acts 16:10–17
  2. c. AD 57Philippi to Jerusalem

    The collection journey

    The "we" resumes as Paul sails from Philippi after the days of unleavened bread, through Troas and Miletus, then on to Tyre, Caesarea and Jerusalem.

    Acts 20:5–21:18
  3. c. AD 57–59Caesarea

    Two years of waiting

    Paul is held in Caesarea under Felix and then Festus. The "we" returns when the voyage begins there, so many think Luke stayed nearby, perhaps gathering eyewitness accounts.

    Acts 24:27; 27:1
  4. c. AD 59–60At sea

    Storm and shipwreck

    The detailed account of the voyage, the long storm and the wreck on Malta reads like an eyewitness report. On the island Paul heals many of the sick.

    Acts 27:1–28:10
  5. c. AD 60–62Rome

    With Paul in prison

    Paul sends greetings from "Luke, the beloved physician" and names "Lucas" among his fellow workers.

    Acts 28:16; Colossians 4:14; Philemon 24
  6. c. AD 64–67Rome

    The last friend

    In Paul's final letter, others have gone away. "Only Luke is with me."

    2 Timothy 4:10–11

Key activities

Neither the Gospel of Luke nor Acts names its author. From the late second century, writers such as Irenaeus name Luke, and many scholars accept this. Others think the author was a later Christian who used a travel source for the "we" passages.

  1. 1

    Investigated carefully

    The writer drew on eyewitnesses and set out to write "in order."

    Luke 1:1–3
  2. 2

    Wrote so others could be sure

    He addressed his work to Theophilus, "that thou mightest know the certainty" of what he had been taught.

    Luke 1:3–4; Acts 1:1
  3. 3

    Crossed into Europe with Paul

    He joined the mission from Troas to Philippi.

    Acts 16:10–12
  4. 4

    Traveled to Jerusalem with Paul

    He was part of the group that went up with Paul and the collection.

    Acts 20:5–6; 21:15–18
  5. 5

    Survived a shipwreck

    He sailed with Paul the prisoner and was wrecked on Malta.

    Acts 27:1, 41–44; 28:1
  6. 6

    Served as a physician

    Paul calls him "the beloved physician."

    Colossians 4:14
  7. 7

    Labored alongside Paul

    Paul lists him among his "fellowlabourers."

    Philemon 24
  8. 8

    Stayed when others left

    In Paul's last imprisonment Luke alone remained with him.

    2 Timothy 4:10–11

The world around them

Luke and Acts are both dedicated to "most excellent Theophilus," perhaps a man of rank or a patron (Luke 1:3; Acts 1:1). Ancient historians often dedicated their works in this way. Physicians were common in the Roman world. Some were highly trained, and some were slaves or former slaves. The author of Luke–Acts writes polished Greek and pays close attention to officials, places and sea travel. Inscriptions have confirmed some of these details, such as the title "politarchs" for the rulers of Thessalonica (Acts 17:6, KJV "rulers of the city").

Words and places

  • Physician. A doctor (Colossians 4:14; Luke 4:23).
  • Theophilus. The man to whom Luke and Acts are addressed. The name means "friend of God" (Luke 1:3; Acts 1:1).
  • Treatise. A written account. Acts calls the Gospel "the former treatise" (Acts 1:1).
  • Melita. The KJV name for Malta, where Paul's ship was wrecked (Acts 28:1).
