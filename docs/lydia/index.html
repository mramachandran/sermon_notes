Lydia

The businesswoman by the river "whose heart the Lord opened."

Lydia was a trader in purple cloth from the city of Thyatira, living in Philippi when Paul first crossed into Europe. She already worshipped the God of Israel. One sabbath she was with a group of women praying by a river outside the city when Paul and his companions sat down and spoke to them. As she listened, the Lord opened her heart. She and her household were baptized, and she insisted that the missionaries stay in her home. Later the believers gathered at her house. She is the first convert Acts records in Europe, and her faith moved quickly from hearing to hospitality.

The life of Lydia

Dates are approximate (AD). Lydia's story takes place during Paul's second journey, c. AD 49–52. Acts records only a few days of her life.

  1. Before Paul came

    From Thyatira to Philippi

    Lydia, originally from Thyatira, has a house in Philippi and trades in purple. She already worships the God of Israel.

    Acts 16:14–15
  2. c. AD 49–50Troas

    A call to Macedonia

    Paul sees a vision of a man of Macedonia praying, "Come over into Macedonia, and help us." The team sails for Europe and reaches Philippi.

    Acts 16:9–12
  3. c. AD 49–50A sabbath by the river

    She listens

    Outside the city, by a river "where prayer was wont to be made," women gather. Paul and his companions sit down and speak to them, and Lydia listens.

    Acts 16:13–14
  4. The same season

    Heart opened, household baptized

    The Lord opens Lydia's heart to take in Paul's message. She and her household are baptized.

    Acts 16:14–15
  5. During their stay

    Opens her home

    She urges the travelers, "If ye have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, come into my house, and abide there." They accept.

    Acts 16:15
  6. After the prison

    Hosts the church

    Freed from prison, Paul and Silas go to Lydia's house, see the believers, comfort them, and leave the city.

    Acts 16:40

Key activities

Acts does not say whether Lydia was married, widowed or single. That she had her own house and household suggests she was its head, but Scripture gives no more detail.

The world around them

Purple dye was one of the most expensive products of the ancient world. The finest came from sea snails, while cheaper purple came from plant dyes such as madder root. Purple cloth was worn by the wealthy and powerful (Luke 16:19). Thyatira was known for its dyers, and inscriptions from the city mention a dyers' guild. Philippi seems to have had few Jews. Many scholars think the believers met by the river because the city had no synagogue.

Words and places

  • Seller of purple. A dealer in purple dye or purple-dyed cloth, a luxury trade (Acts 16:14).
  • Thyatira. A city in western Asia Minor, later home to one of the seven churches of Revelation (Revelation 2:18).
  • Worshipped God. A phrase often used for Gentiles who worshipped Israel's God without fully converting to Judaism (Acts 16:14; 18:7).
  • Constrained. Urged strongly, refusing to take no for an answer (Acts 16:15; Luke 24:29).
