The Magi

Wise men from the east who followed a star, worshipped the child Jesus, and went home another way.

Matthew tells of "wise men from the east" who arrived in Jerusalem asking for the newborn King of the Jews. They had seen his star and come to worship him, and their question alarmed Herod. Micah's prophecy pointed them to Bethlehem, where they bowed down and gave the child gold, frankincense and myrrh. Warned in a dream, they went home another way. Matthew does not say how many there were, name them, or call them kings; those details are later tradition. Foreigners were among the first to worship Jesus, while Jerusalem's scholars are not said to have gone.

The life of The Magi

Dates are approximate (BC). Herod's order about boys "two years old and under" suggests the visit came some months after the birth.

  1. In the east

    They see the star

    The wise men see a star they link to the birth of the King of the Jews. They set out on a long journey to find and worship him.

    Matthew 2:1–2
  2. c. 6–4 BCJerusalem

    They ask for the King

    They arrive in Jerusalem asking where the newborn King is. Herod is troubled, and all Jerusalem with him. The chief priests and scribes point to Bethlehem from the prophet Micah.

    Matthew 2:1–6; Micah 5:2
  3. c. 6–4 BCBefore Herod

    A secret meeting

    Herod calls the wise men privately and finds out exactly when the star appeared. He sends them to Bethlehem, asking them to report back so that he may worship too.

    Matthew 2:7–8
  4. c. 6–4 BCBethlehem

    They worship the child

    The star goes before them and stops over the place. Rejoicing greatly, they enter the house, fall down and worship Jesus, and open their gifts.

    Matthew 2:9–11
  5. c. 6–4 BCThe journey home

    Another way

    Warned by God in a dream not to return to Herod, they depart into their own country by another way.

    Matthew 2:12

Key activities

Christians and scholars differ on what the star was: a miracle, a comet, a new star, or a meeting of planets, such as a conjunction of Jupiter and Saturn in 7 BC. Matthew does not explain it.

  1. 1

    Watched and responded

    They noticed a sign in the heavens and acted on it, travelling far to find the King.

    Matthew 2:1–2
  2. 2

    Asked openly

    They came to the capital and asked plainly for the newborn King of the Jews.

    Matthew 2:2
  3. 3

    Were guided by Scripture

    The prophet's words about Bethlehem showed them where to go next.

    Matthew 2:4–6, 9
  4. 4

    Rejoiced

    When they saw the star again, "they rejoiced with exceeding great joy."

    Matthew 2:10
  5. 5

    Worshipped the child

    They found him with Mary his mother, fell down and worshipped him.

    Matthew 2:11
  6. 6

    Gave costly gifts

    They "presented unto him gifts; gold, and frankincense, and myrrh."

    Matthew 2:11
  7. 7

    Obeyed God's warning

    They did not return to Herod but went home another way.

    Matthew 2:12

Across Scripture

Direct references first. Echoes are study links, not places that name the Magi.

Old Testament

  • Micah 5:2A ruler from Bethlehem, quoted to Herod (Matthew 2:6)
  • Numbers 24:17"A Star out of Jacob," which many link with the star
  • Isaiah 60:3, 6; Psalm 72:10–11Kings bring gold and incense; shaped the "kings" idea

New Testament

  • Matthew 2:1–16The only account of them
  • Acts 8:9; 13:6–8The same word family for Simon and Elymas the sorcerers
  • TraditionNames Caspar, Melchior and Balthasar (c. 6th century) and Epiphany; not Scripture

For discussion

  1. Observe: What three gifts did the wise men bring (Matthew 2:11)?
  2. Understand: The wise men travelled far to worship, while the scribes in Jerusalem knew the prophecy but are not said to have gone. What does this contrast suggest?
  3. Apply: The wise men gave their best to Jesus. What could you offer him this week, in time, money or talent?
  4. Apply: They went home "another way." How has meeting Jesus changed the direction of your life?
