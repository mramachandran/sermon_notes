Part 6 · The Divided Kingdom · Chapter 97

Manasseh

Judah's most wicked king, who was humbled in chains and found that God still hears.

Manasseh was the son of good King Hezekiah, but he undid almost everything his father had done. He became king at twelve and reigned fifty-five years, longer than any other king of Judah. He set up altars to Baal and the stars inside the temple, practised sorcery, made his children pass through the fire, and filled Jerusalem with innocent blood. Kings says his sins sealed Judah's fate. Yet Chronicles adds a surprising turn. Taken to Babylon in chains by the Assyrians, Manasseh humbled himself and prayed, and God brought him home. He spent his last years trying to repair the damage.

The life of Manasseh

Dates are approximate (BC). Many scholars think Manasseh reigned with his father from c. 697 BC and alone from c. 686 BC. Scripture does not say when he was taken to Babylon.

  1. c. 697 BCAge 12

    Becomes king

    Manasseh begins his reign of fifty-five years in Jerusalem.

    2 Kings 21:1; 2 Chronicles 33:1
  2. c. 686 BCSole king

    Undoes his father's reforms

    He rebuilds the high places, raises altars to Baal, makes an Asherah pole, and worships the stars, even in the temple courts.

    2 Kings 21:2–7; 2 Chronicles 33:2–7
  3. During his reign

    Deaf to the prophets

    The LORD warns through his prophets that he will wipe Jerusalem "as a man wipeth a dish." Manasseh and his people will not listen.

    2 Kings 21:10–15; 2 Chronicles 33:10
  4. During his reign

    Innocent blood

    He sheds so much innocent blood that Jerusalem is filled with it from one end to the other.

    2 Kings 21:16
  5. Date unknown

    Captive and humbled

    The captains of the king of Assyria bind him with fetters and carry him to Babylon. In distress he humbles himself and prays, and God restores him to Jerusalem.

    2 Chronicles 33:11–13
  6. After his return

    Repairs the damage

    He removes the foreign gods from the temple, restores the LORD's altar, and commands Judah to serve the LORD. He also strengthens Jerusalem's walls.

    2 Chronicles 33:14–17
  7. c. 642 BCAge 67

    Dies

    He is buried in the garden of his own house, and his son Amon reigns.

    2 Kings 21:18; 2 Chronicles 33:20

Key activities

Kings does not mention Manasseh's repentance. It stresses that his sins were so great that judgment on Judah could not be turned back (2 Kings 23:26; 24:3–4). Chronicles adds his captivity and prayer. The two accounts stand side by side.

  1. 1

    Rebuilt what his father tore down

    He restored the high places Hezekiah had destroyed.

    2 Kings 21:3
  2. 2

    Defiled the temple

    He built altars for the host of heaven in both temple courts and set a carved idol in the house of God.

    2 Kings 21:4–7; 2 Chronicles 33:4–7
  3. 3

    Gave his children to the fire

    He made his children pass through the fire in the valley of the son of Hinnom and turned to witchcraft and mediums.

    2 Kings 21:6; 2 Chronicles 33:6
  4. 4

    Led Judah astray

    He led the people to do more evil than the nations God had driven out.

    2 Kings 21:9; 2 Chronicles 33:9
  5. 5

    Shed innocent blood

    He "shed innocent blood very much, till he had filled Jerusalem from one end to another."

    2 Kings 21:16
  6. 6

    Humbled himself in chains

    In Babylon he "besought the LORD his God, and humbled himself greatly."

    2 Chronicles 33:11–12
  7. 7

    Knew the LORD was God

    God heard him and brought him home, and he learned who God really was.

    2 Chronicles 33:13
  8. 8

    Tried to undo the harm

    He removed the idols, restored true worship, and built up Jerusalem's defences. The people still sacrificed at the high places, though now to the LORD.

    2 Chronicles 33:14–17

For discussion

  1. Observe: What happened to Manasseh in Babylon, and what did he do there (2 Chronicles 33:11–13)?
  2. Understand: Manasseh was forgiven, yet the harm he did lived on after him. How do both of those things fit together?
  3. Apply: Is there anyone you think is too far gone for God? How does Manasseh's story challenge that?
  4. Apply: Manasseh tried to repair what he had broken. Is there something you need to put right, and what is the first step?
