Martha, Mary and Lazarus

A family in Bethany whom Jesus loved, and whose brother he called out of the grave.

Martha, Mary and their brother Lazarus lived in Bethany, just outside Jerusalem, and their home was a place of welcome for Jesus. John says simply that "Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus." Luke shows Martha busy serving and Mary sitting at Jesus' feet. John tells how Lazarus died, how Jesus wept with the sisters, and how he called Lazarus out of the tomb after four days. Days later Mary poured costly ointment on Jesus' feet. This family shows friendship with Jesus in ordinary life, in grief, and in worship.

The life of Martha, Mary and Lazarus

Dates are approximate (AD), with the crucifixion c. AD 30 (on the AD 33 view, add three years). Luke gives no date and does not name the village.

  1. c. AD 29A village visit

    Martha welcomes Jesus

    Mary sits at his feet while Martha is "cumbered about much serving." Jesus says Mary has chosen "that good part."

    Luke 10:38–42
  2. c. AD 30Winter

    Lazarus falls sick

    The sisters send word to Jesus beyond the Jordan. He says the sickness is for God's glory and stays two more days.

    John 10:40; 11:1–6
  3. c. AD 30Four days later

    Martha meets Jesus

    Jesus tells her, "I am the resurrection, and the life." She confesses him as the Christ, the Son of God.

    John 11:17–27
  4. c. AD 30The same day

    Jesus weeps

    Mary falls at his feet with the same words as Martha. Seeing the mourners weeping, Jesus groans in spirit and weeps.

    John 11:28–37
  5. c. AD 30The same day

    Lazarus comes forth

    The stone is taken away, and Jesus cries, "Lazarus, come forth." The dead man walks out in graveclothes.

    John 11:38–44
  6. c. AD 30Soon after

    The council plots

    Many believe, but the council, led by Caiaphas, plans Jesus' death, and Jesus withdraws to Ephraim.

    John 11:45–54
  7. c. AD 30Six days before Passover

    Supper and anointing

    Martha serves and Mary anoints Jesus' feet with spikenard. Crowds come to see Lazarus, and the priests plot to kill him too.

    John 12:1–11

Key activities

Matthew 26:6–13 and Mark 14:3–9 tell of a woman who anoints Jesus' head at the house of Simon the leper in Bethany. Most readers think this is the same event as John 12, where the woman is Mary. Luke 7:36–50 is a different anointing, in Galilee.

  1. 1

    Martha opened her home

    She received Jesus into her house as a guest.

    Luke 10:38
  2. 2

    Mary sat at Jesus' feet

    She took the place of a disciple and listened to his word.

    Luke 10:39
  3. 3

    Martha spoke her frustration

    "Lord, dost thou not care that my sister hath left me to serve alone?" Jesus answered her gently: "one thing is needful."

    Luke 10:40–42
  4. 4

    The sisters sent for Jesus

    They brought their need straight to him and trusted his love.

    John 11:3
  5. 5

    Martha confessed her faith

    In grief she still said, "I believe that thou art the Christ, the Son of God."

    John 11:21–27
  6. 6

    Mary brought her tears

    She fell at Jesus' feet and wept, and Jesus wept with her.

    John 11:32–35
  7. 7

    Lazarus was raised

    At Jesus' command he came out of the tomb, and Jesus said, "Loose him, and let him go."

    John 11:43–44
  8. 8

    Many believed

    The miracle led many to put their faith in Jesus.

    John 11:45; 12:11
  9. 9

    Martha served again

    At the supper in Bethany she served, as she had before.

    John 12:2
  10. 10

    Mary anointed Jesus

    She poured a pound of spikenard on his feet and wiped them with her hair. Jesus said, "against the day of my burying hath she kept this."

    John 12:3–8
  11. 11

    Lazarus became a target

    Because his new life drew people to Jesus, the chief priests planned to kill him as well.

    John 12:10–11

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names this family.

Old Testament

  • Echo: 1 Kings 17:17–24; 2 Kings 4:32–37Elijah and Elisha raise dead sons
  • Echo: Ezekiel 37:12–14God opens graves and gives his Spirit

New Testament

  • Luke 10:38–42Martha serves and Mary listens
  • John 11:1–12:19Lazarus raised; the council plots; Mary anoints Jesus
  • Matthew 26:6–13; Mark 14:3–9The anointing at Bethany, told as a memorial
  • Luke 16:19–31A different Lazarus, the beggar in a parable
  • Echo: 1 Thessalonians 4:13–14Not sorrowing as those with no hope
  • TraditionLegends take Lazarus to Cyprus or Provence; merging Mary with Magdalene is unscriptural

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Martha and Mary each say to Jesus when he arrived (John 11:21, 32)?
  2. Understand: Jesus knew he would raise Lazarus, yet he wept (John 11:35). What does that show about him?
  3. Apply: Are you more like Martha, busy serving, or Mary, sitting to listen? What would one step toward the other look like?
  4. Apply: Martha believed in the middle of grief. How could you come alongside someone grieving this week?
