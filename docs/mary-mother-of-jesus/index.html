Mary, mother of Jesus

The woman of Nazareth who said yes to God, raised his Son, stood at the cross, and prayed with the first church.

Mary was an ordinary Jewish woman of Nazareth, engaged to Joseph, when Gabriel told her she would conceive by the Holy Ghost and bear the Son of God. She asked one honest question and then gave herself to God's plan. Her life held great joy and deep pain: a birth in Bethlehem, flight to Egypt, and later the cross. Scripture shows her pondering what she did not yet understand, pointing others to her Son, and praying with his followers after he rose. Christians honour her in different ways, but all can learn from her trust and faithfulness.

The life of Mary, mother of Jesus

Dates are approximate (BC or AD). Scripture does not give Mary's age at any point.

  1. c. 7–5 BCIn Nazareth

    Gabriel's message

    The angel tells Mary she will bear Jesus, the Son of the Highest. She asks how, hears of the Holy Ghost, and consents.

    Luke 1:26–38
  2. c. 7–5 BCHill country of Judah

    Visits Elizabeth

    Elizabeth blesses her, and Mary answers with a song of praise, often called the Magnificat.

    Luke 1:39–56
  3. c. 6–4 BCBethlehem

    Gives birth to Jesus

    She lays her firstborn son in a manger. Shepherds come, and Mary keeps these things in her heart.

    Luke 2:1–20
  4. c. 6–4 BCForty days old

    The temple

    They offer the poor family's two birds. Simeon warns that a sword will pierce her own soul.

    Luke 2:21–39; Leviticus 12:2–8
  5. c. 6–4 BCUnder Herod

    Flight to Egypt

    After the wise men's visit, Joseph takes Mary and Jesus to Egypt, then settles in Nazareth.

    Matthew 2:1–23
  6. Jesus aged 12

    Lost and found

    After three days' searching they find Jesus in the temple, about his Father's business.

    Luke 2:41–51
  7. c. AD 28–30Jesus' ministry

    Cana and after

    At Cana she tells the servants to do whatever Jesus says. Later she and his brothers seek him.

    John 2:1–12; Mark 3:31–35
  8. c. AD 30 or 33Cross and upper room

    Faithful to the end

    Jesus entrusts her to the disciple he loved. After the ascension she prays with the apostles.

    John 19:25–27; Acts 1:14

Key activities

Christians differ over Mary's later role. Roman Catholic and Orthodox Christians honour her in ways many Protestants do not. This chapter follows what the text says.

  1. 1

    Believed God's word

    Told she would bear God's Son, she answered, "Behold the handmaid of the Lord." Elizabeth called her blessed because she believed.

    Luke 1:38, 45
  2. 2

    Asked an honest question

    She asked, "How shall this be, seeing I know not a man?" and was answered, "For with God nothing shall be impossible."

    Luke 1:34–37
  3. 3

    Sang of God's mercy

    Her song praises God who scatters the proud, lifts the lowly, fills the hungry, and remembers his promise to Abraham.

    Luke 1:46–55
  4. 4

    Faced hardship and possible disgrace

    She conceived before her marriage was completed and gave birth far from home, with "no room for them in the inn."

    Matthew 1:18–19; Luke 2:4–7
  5. 5

    Pondered in her heart

    Twice Luke says she treasured up what she saw and heard, even when she did not understand it.

    Luke 2:19, 51
  6. 6

    Kept the Law

    She and Joseph circumcised Jesus, presented him in the temple, and went up to Jerusalem every year for Passover.

    Luke 2:21–24, 41
  7. 7

    Fled as a refugee

    She went with Joseph to Egypt to save the child from Herod.

    Matthew 2:13–15
  8. 8

    Pointed others to Jesus

    At Cana she said, "Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it."

    John 2:5
  9. 9

    Stood by the cross

    She was near the cross with other women when most of the disciples had fled.

    John 19:25; Mark 14:50
  10. 10

    Prayed with the church

    She continued in prayer with the apostles, the women and Jesus' brothers in Jerusalem.

    Acts 1:14

Across Scripture

Direct references first. Echoes are study links. Christians differ on whether Jesus' "brethren" were Mary's children, Joseph's, or cousins; stories of her later life and assumption are later tradition, not Scripture.

Old Testament

  • Isaiah 7:14"A virgin shall conceive," fulfilled in Mary (Matthew 1:22–23)
  • Echo: 1 Samuel 2:1–10Hannah's song, strongly echoed in Mary's
  • Echo: Genesis 3:15The promised seed, fulfilled in her Son (Galatians 4:4)

New Testament

  • Matthew 1:16–25; 2:11–21Joseph's wife and mother of the child Jesus
  • Luke 1:26–56; 2:1–52The fullest account of her story
  • Mark 3:31–35; 6:3She seeks Jesus; he is "the son of Mary"
  • John 2:1–12; 19:25–27At the first sign and at the cross
  • Acts 1:14Her last mention by name

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Mary say to Gabriel, and what did she ask (Luke 1:34, 38)?
  2. Understand: Simeon warned that a sword would pierce Mary's soul (Luke 2:35). Where in her story do you see that happen?
  3. Apply: Mary "pondered" what she did not understand. What is one thing God is doing in your life that you need to ponder rather than rush to explain?
  4. Apply: "Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it." What is one thing Jesus has said that you will obey this week?
