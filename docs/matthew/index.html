Matthew

The tax collector who left his table to follow Jesus and threw a party for sinners.

Matthew, also called Levi, collected taxes by the Sea of Galilee, probably at Capernaum. To most Jews of his day, that made him a collaborator and a cheat. Jesus walked past his tax booth and said, "Follow me." Matthew left it all and followed. Then he threw a great feast so his fellow tax collectors could meet Jesus too. When the Pharisees objected, Jesus answered that the sick, not the healthy, need a doctor. No Gospel records a word Matthew spoke, yet the church has long linked his name with the first Gospel.

The life of Matthew

Dates are approximate (AD). Jesus' ministry is dated c. AD 27/28–30 in this study. Scripture does not give Matthew's age.

  1. c. AD 28Capernaum

    Called from the tax booth

    Walking by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus sees Levi the son of Alphaeus sitting at the receipt of custom. At the words "Follow me," he leaves everything and follows.

    Mark 2:13–14; Luke 5:27–28
  2. c. AD 28His own house

    Hosts a great feast

    Levi makes a great feast for Jesus, and many tax collectors and sinners sit down to eat with him and his disciples.

    Luke 5:29; Matthew 9:10
  3. c. AD 28His own house

    Hears Jesus defend him

    The Pharisees ask why Jesus eats with publicans and sinners. Jesus answers that he came to call sinners to repentance.

    Matthew 9:11–13
  4. c. AD 28On a mountain

    Chosen as an apostle

    Jesus names the Twelve. In Matthew's Gospel the list calls him "Matthew the publican," keeping his old label.

    Matthew 10:2–4; Luke 6:13–16
  5. c. AD 30Jerusalem

    Waits in the upper room

    After the ascension Matthew is with the other apostles and the women, praying and waiting for the Spirit.

    Acts 1:13–14

Key activities

Mark and Luke call him Levi, while Matthew's Gospel calls him Matthew. Most readers take these as two names for one man, as with Simon and Peter.

  2. 2

The world around them

Capernaum stood on the north shore of the Sea of Galilee, near a trade road and the border between the lands of Herod Antipas and his brother Philip. Matthew probably collected tolls on goods for Herod Antipas. Tax collection was usually farmed out. A collector paid for the right and made his living from whatever he could charge above the official rate. Many grew rich by cheating (Luke 3:12–13; 19:8). Religious Jews saw them as traitors who worked for foreign powers and as unclean because of their dealings with Gentiles.

Words and places

  • Publican. A tax or toll collector; in the Gospels the word is often paired with "sinners" (Matthew 9:10–11).
  • Receipt of custom. The tax booth or toll office where duties were paid (Matthew 9:9).
  • Capernaum. A lakeside town that became the base of Jesus' ministry in Galilee (Matthew 4:13; Mark 2:1).
  • Pharisees. A strict religious party devoted to keeping the Law and the traditions of the elders (Matthew 9:11).
