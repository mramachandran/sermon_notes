Mephibosheth

Jonathan's lame son, who was brought from hiding to eat at the king's table.

Mephibosheth was the son of Jonathan and grandson of King Saul. He was five when his father and grandfather died on Mount Gilboa. His nurse fled with him in panic, he fell, and he was lame in both feet for life. He grew up in hiding, with every reason to fear David, since a new king often wiped out the old king's family. Instead, David sought someone from Saul's house to show kindness to "for Jonathan's sake." He gave Mephibosheth back his land and a place at the royal table: a clear picture of grace, given because of a promise.

The life of Mephibosheth

Dates are approximate (BC). He was five when Saul and Jonathan died (c. 1010 BC), so he was born c. 1015 BC. Later events cannot be dated closely.

  1. c. 1010 BCAge 5

    Lamed in the escape

    Word comes from Jezreel that Saul and Jonathan are dead. His nurse picks him up and flees, but in her haste he falls and becomes lame.

    2 Samuel 4:4
  2. Growing up

    Hidden in Lo-debar

    He lives in the house of Machir son of Ammiel, at Lo-debar east of the Jordan. By the time David finds him, he has a young son named Micha.

    2 Samuel 9:4, 12
  3. David's reign

    Brought to the king

    David asks whether anyone is left of Saul's house. Saul's servant Ziba names Mephibosheth. David restores Saul's land to him and seats him at the king's table "as one of the king's sons."

    2 Samuel 9:1–13
  4. Absalom's revolt

    Accused by Ziba

    As David flees Jerusalem, Ziba meets him with supplies and claims that Mephibosheth has stayed behind, hoping to regain Saul's kingdom. David gives Ziba all that was Mephibosheth's.

    2 Samuel 16:1–4
  5. The king's return

    Meets David

    Mephibosheth comes out unwashed, with feet undressed and beard untrimmed, as he has been since David left. He says Ziba deceived him. David divides the land between them.

    2 Samuel 19:24–30
  6. The famine years

    Spared

    A three-year famine is traced to Saul's wrong against the Gibeonites. When they demand seven of Saul's descendants, David spares Mephibosheth because of his oath to Jonathan.

    2 Samuel 21:1–9

Key activities

Ziba and Mephibosheth gave David opposite stories (2 Samuel 16:1–4; 19:24–30). Many readers believe Mephibosheth, since his long mourning shows loyalty. Others think Ziba may have been telling the truth. David himself seemed unsure and divided the land, and Scripture does not settle it.

  1. 1

    Survived a disaster as a child

    He lived through his family's fall, but it left him lame.

    2 Samuel 4:4
  2. 2

    Came when the king called

    He came before David, fell on his face and said, "Behold thy servant!"

    2 Samuel 9:6
  3. 3

    Saw himself as unworthy

    "What is thy servant, that thou shouldest look upon such a dead dog as I am?"

    2 Samuel 9:8
  4. 4

    Ate at the king's table

    He lived in Jerusalem and ate "continually at the king's table."

    2 Samuel 9:11–13
  5. 5

    Mourned while the king was away

    He did not care for his feet, trim his beard or wash his clothes until David returned in peace.

    2 Samuel 19:24
  6. 6

    Cared more for the king than the land

    "Yea, let him take all, forasmuch as my lord the king is come again in peace unto his own house."

    2 Samuel 19:30

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did David give Mephibosheth, and why (2 Samuel 9:1, 7)?
  2. Understand: Mephibosheth called himself a "dead dog." How does his view of himself make David's kindness stand out?
  3. Apply: David kept a promise to a friend long after the friend was gone. Is there a promise you have made that still needs keeping?
  4. Apply: Who is "hidden in Lo-debar" around you, overlooked or afraid, and how could you welcome them to your table?

The world around them

In the ancient Near East a new dynasty often put to death the family of the old king to stop rival claims. That is why Mephibosheth hid, and why David's kindness was so surprising. To "eat at the king's table" meant being fed and honored as part of the royal household. Lo-debar was a town in Gilead, east of the Jordan, far from Jerusalem. Its name probably means "no pasture."

Words and places

  • Lo-debar. A town in Gilead where he grew up (2 Samuel 9:4).
  • Ziba. A servant of Saul's house who managed Mephibosheth's land (2 Samuel 9:9–10).
  • Merib-baal. His name in Chronicles. Many scholars think later writers changed "baal" to "bosheth," meaning "shame," to avoid the name of the idol Baal (1 Chronicles 8:34).
  • Dead dog. A phrase for someone worthless (2 Samuel 9:8).
