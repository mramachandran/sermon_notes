Part 6 · The Divided Kingdom · Chapter 95

Micah

A country prophet who named the sins of the powerful and pointed to a ruler from Bethlehem.

Micah came from Moresheth, a small town in the lowlands of Judah, far from the palaces of Jerusalem. He prophesied in the days of Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, at the same time as Isaiah. He saw what the powerful were doing to ordinary families: seizing fields and houses, bribing judges, and hiring prophets to say what they wanted to hear. Micah warned that Samaria would fall and that even Jerusalem would be ploughed like a field. A century later, his words were remembered at Jeremiah's trial, because King Hezekiah had listened and repented. Micah also foretold a ruler from little Bethlehem, and he summed up God's will in one of the Bible's best-known sentences: do justly, love mercy, walk humbly with God.

The life of Micah

Dates are approximate (BC). Micah does not date his individual messages, so the order below is partly a best guess.

  1. Before his callAt Moresheth

    A man from the lowlands

    Micah grows up in a farming town southwest of Jerusalem, close to the border with the Philistines.

    Micah 1:1, 14
  2. c. 735 BCDays of Jotham

    The word comes

    The word of the LORD comes to Micah concerning Samaria and Jerusalem.

    Micah 1:1
  3. Before 722 BCWarning to the north

    Samaria will fall

    He announces that Samaria will become a heap in the field, and that the same judgment is coming to the gate of Jerusalem.

    Micah 1:2–9
  4. c. 715–701 BCDays of Hezekiah

    Zion will be ploughed

    He warns that Jerusalem will be ploughed like a field for its leaders' sins. Hezekiah fears the LORD and prays, and the disaster is held back.

    Micah 3:9–12; Jeremiah 26:18–19
  5. Undated

    A ruler from Bethlehem

    He foretells that a ruler in Israel will come from Bethlehem Ephratah, "whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting."

    Micah 5:2–5
  6. c. 609 BCAbout a century later

    Quoted to save Jeremiah

    When priests and prophets demand Jeremiah's death, some elders recall Micah's words and Hezekiah's response.

    Jeremiah 26:1, 16–19, 24

Key activities

Micah 4:1–3 closely matches Isaiah 2:2–4. Scholars differ on whether one prophet borrowed from the other or both used an older saying. Micah is also a different man from Micaiah, who spoke to King Ahab (1 Kings 22).

  1. 1

    Lamented his people's ruin

    He said he would wail and go stripped and barefoot because Judah's wound was incurable.

    Micah 1:8–9
  2. 2

    Exposed land grabs

    He condemned those who lie awake planning evil and at morning light seize fields and houses.

    Micah 2:1–2
  3. 3

    Confronted leaders

    He rebuked rulers who judged for bribes, priests who taught for hire, and prophets who divined for money.

    Micah 3:1–11
  4. 4

    Spoke with God's power

    Unlike the false prophets, he said he was full of power by the Spirit of the LORD, to declare Israel's sin.

    Micah 3:5–8
  5. 5

    Promised peace

    He foresaw a day when nations would "beat their swords into plowshares" and everyone would sit under his own vine and fig tree.

    Micah 4:1–4
  6. 6

    Pointed to Bethlehem

    He foretold a ruler from the small town of Bethlehem who would shepherd God's people.

    Micah 5:2–4
  7. 7

    Summed up God's will

    Not endless sacrifices, but justice, mercy and humble walking with God.

    Micah 6:6–8
  8. 8

    Waited for God

    "Therefore I will look unto the LORD; I will wait for the God of my salvation."

    Micah 7:7
  9. 9

    Praised God's mercy

    "Who is a God like unto thee, that pardoneth iniquity?" He will cast all their sins "into the depths of the sea."

    Micah 7:18–19

For discussion

  1. Observe: What three things does God require in Micah 6:8?
  2. Understand: Why do you think Micah links worship so closely with how people treat their neighbours?
  3. Apply: Which of the three, justice, mercy or humility, is hardest for you right now? What would one step look like?
  4. Apply: Micah's words changed a king. Whose words has God used to change your direction, and whom could you encourage this week?
