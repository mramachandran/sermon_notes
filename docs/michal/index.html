Michal

Saul's daughter, who loved David, saved his life, and later despised him.

Michal, King Saul's younger daughter, loved David, and Saul used her love as a trap. When her father's men came to kill David, Michal let him down through a window and bought him time with a trick. But while David was a fugitive, Saul gave her to another man. Years later David demanded her back, and her second husband followed her weeping. When David danced before the ark, Michal looked down from a window and despised him. Bitterness had closed her heart to joy.

The life of Michal

Dates are approximate and rough (BC). Her story runs from Saul's court to the early years of David's reign in Jerusalem.

  1. Saul's court

    Loves David

    "Michal Saul's daughter loved David," and Saul is pleased. He asks as a bride-price proof that David has killed a hundred Philistines, hoping David will die. David brings double, and they marry.

    1 Samuel 18:20–28
  2. One night

    Saves David's life

    Saul sends men to watch David's house and kill him in the morning. Michal lets David down through a window, puts an image in his bed with goats' hair at its head, and says he is sick.

    1 Samuel 19:11–17
  3. While David is a fugitive

    Given to another man

    With David on the run in the wilderness, Saul gives Michal as wife to another man, Phalti son of Laish, of Gallim. Scripture does not record what Michal thought of it.

    1 Samuel 25:44
  4. David at Hebron

    Taken back

    When Abner offers to bring Israel over to David, David demands Michal's return. Ish-bosheth takes her from Phaltiel, who follows weeping as far as Bahurim until Abner sends him home.

    2 Samuel 3:12–16
  5. In Jerusalem

    Despises David

    As the ark comes into the city, Michal sees David dancing and despises him. She mocks him when he comes home, and David answers her sharply.

    2 Samuel 6:16, 20–23

Key activities

2 Samuel 21:8 in the KJV mentions "the five sons of Michal the daughter of Saul" brought up for Adriel. But 1 Samuel 18:19 says Merab married Adriel, and 2 Samuel 6:23 says Michal had no child. A few Hebrew manuscripts and some Greek copies read Merab in 21:8, and most modern translations follow them. Others explain "Michal" as the aunt who raised them.

  1. 1

    Loved David

    Scripture says twice that Michal loved David, and Saul saw it.

    1 Samuel 18:20, 28
  2. 2

    Chose her husband over her father

    She warned David, "If thou save not thy life to night, to morrow thou shalt be slain."

    1 Samuel 19:11
  3. 3

    Deceived Saul's men

    She helped David escape through a window and used an image in the bed to delay the soldiers.

    1 Samuel 19:12–16
  4. 4

    Lied to protect herself

    When Saul confronted her, she claimed David had threatened to kill her.

    1 Samuel 19:17
  5. 5

    Was passed between men

    She was given to Phalti and then taken back to David, with no word of her own recorded.

    1 Samuel 25:44; 2 Samuel 3:13–16
  6. 6

    Despised worship

    She scorned David's dancing: "How glorious was the king of Israel to day." David replied, "I will yet be more vile than thus."

    2 Samuel 6:20–22

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Michal.

  • 1 Samuel 18:20–28; 19:11–17; 25:44Her marriage to David, her rescue of him, and her marriage to Phalti.
  • 2 Samuel 3:13–16She is returned to David.
  • 2 Samuel 6:16–23and 1 Chronicles 15:29. She despises David's dancing before the ark.

  • Echo: Mark 14:3–9Jesus defends a woman criticized for her costly worship

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Michal do for David in 1 Samuel 19, and what did she say to him in 2 Samuel 6?
  2. Understand: What might have happened in Michal's heart between those two scenes?
  3. Apply: Michal watched worship from a window. What keeps you at a distance from wholehearted worship?
  4. Apply: Is there an old hurt that is making you critical of someone else's joy? What step toward healing could you take?
