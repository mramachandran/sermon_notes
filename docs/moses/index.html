Moses

The reluctant shepherd who led Israel out of Egypt and spoke with God face to face.

Moses was born a Hebrew slave under a death sentence, raised in Pharaoh's household, and trained as a shepherd in the desert. At eighty he met God in a burning bush and was sent to say, "Let my people go." Through him God brought Israel out of slavery, gave the Law at Sinai, and built a nation in the wilderness. He was prophet, lawgiver and intercessor, yet also a man who argued, lost his temper, and was kept out of the Promised Land. The New Testament names him more than any other Old Testament person.

The life of Moses

Dates are approximate (BC), following the early Exodus date, c. 1446 BC (1 Kings 6:1). Many scholars prefer c. 1260 BC, about 185 years later.

  1. c. 1526 BCBirth

    Hidden and drawn out

    Hidden from Pharaoh's decree in a basket among the reeds, he is found and raised by Pharaoh's daughter.

    Exodus 1:22–2:10
  2. c. 1486 BCAge 40

    Flees to Midian

    He kills an Egyptian who was beating a Hebrew, and flees when Pharaoh seeks his life.

    Exodus 2:11–15; Acts 7:23–29
  3. c. 1486–1446 BCAge 40 to 80

    Shepherd in Midian

    He marries Zipporah and keeps Jethro's flock in the desert for forty years.

    Exodus 2:16–22; 3:1; Acts 7:29–30
  4. c. 1446 BCAge 80

    The burning bush

    At Horeb God reveals his name, "I AM THAT I AM," and sends a reluctant Moses to Egypt.

    Exodus 3:1–4:17; 7:7
  5. c. 1446 BCAge 80

    Out of Egypt

    Ten plagues fall and Israel leaves after the Passover, crossing the sea on dry ground.

    Exodus 5:1–15:21
  6. c. 1446–1445 BCAt Sinai

    Law and tabernacle

    He receives the Law, breaks the tablets over the golden calf, and builds the tabernacle.

    Exodus 19–40
  7. c. 1445 BCSecond year

    Turned back at Kadesh

    Ten spies spread fear, and that generation is sentenced to wander forty years.

    Numbers 10:11; 13:1–14:38
  8. c. 1406 BCAge 120

    Death on Nebo

    He commissions Joshua, sees the land from Mount Nebo, and dies; God buries him.

    Deuteronomy 31–34

Key activities

Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers and Deuteronomy tell Moses' story together. Deuteronomy 1–4 retells some events from Moses' own point of view.

  1. 1

    Chose God's people over Egypt

    He refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter.

    Exodus 2:11–12; Hebrews 11:24–26
  2. 2

    Protected the helpless

    At a desert well he defended seven sisters and watered their flock.

    Exodus 2:16–17
  3. 3

    Argued with God, then obeyed

    "I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue." God answered each objection.

    Exodus 3:11–4:17
  4. 4

    Confronted Pharaoh

    He carried one message from God: let my people go.

    Exodus 5:1–11:10
  5. 5

    Led the first Passover

    Each household marked its doorposts with a lamb's blood.

    Exodus 12:1–28
  6. 6

    Stood firm at the sea

    "Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD."

    Exodus 14:13–15:18
  7. 7

    Shared the load

    He appointed judges on Jethro's advice; later seventy elders helped him.

    Exodus 18:13–26; Numbers 11:16–17, 24–29
  8. 8

    Received the Law

    On Sinai he received the Ten Commandments and sealed the covenant with blood.

    Exodus 19:1–24:8; 20:1–17
  9. 9

    Stood in the gap

    After the golden calf he asked to be blotted out of God's book in Israel's place.

    Exodus 32:11–14, 30–32; Psalm 106:23
  10. 10

    Spoke with God as a friend

    "The LORD spake unto Moses face to face, as a man speaketh unto his friend."

    Exodus 33:11, 18–23; 34:5–8
  11. 11

    Struck the rock

    At Meribah he struck the rock in anger and was barred from the land.

    Numbers 20:7–12
  12. 12

    Handed on the work

    He laid his hands on Joshua and urged Israel to "choose life."

    Numbers 27:18–23; Deuteronomy 30:19; 31:7–8

For discussion

  1. Observe: What reasons did Moses give for not going back to Egypt (Exodus 3:11–4:13)?
  2. Understand: Why do you think God kept Moses out of the land (Numbers 20:7–12)?
  3. Apply: Who could help carry something you are holding alone, as Jethro helped Moses?
  4. Apply: Moses pleaded for people who let him down. Who could you pray for by name?
