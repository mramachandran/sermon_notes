Nadab and Abihu

Aaron's two oldest sons, who saw God on the mountain and died for offering "strange fire."

Nadab and Abihu were the two oldest sons of Aaron, Israel's first high priest. Few were given more privilege. They climbed Sinai with Moses and the elders, saw the God of Israel, and ate and drank in his presence. They were ordained as priests and served at the first sacrifices in the tabernacle. Then, on that very day or soon after, they offered "strange fire before the LORD, which he commanded them not," and fire from the LORD killed them. Their story shows that God's nearness must be treated as holy.

The life of Nadab and Abihu

Dates are approximate (BC) and follow the early Exodus date, c. 1446 BC. On the late date, move them about 185 years later. Leviticus 10 seems to follow straight after the eighth day of the ordination (Leviticus 9:1), but the exact day is not stated.

Key activities

Scripture does not say exactly what made the fire "strange." Suggestions include fire not taken from the altar, incense offered at the wrong time or place, or acting without being told. Because God warns priests against wine right afterward (Leviticus 10:8–11), some think they were drunk. The text does not settle it.

For discussion

  3. Apply: Nadab and Abihu had been given great closeness to God. Where might you have grown careless with worship or prayer?

The world around them

Priests burned incense on a golden altar in the tabernacle each morning and evening (Exodus 30:7–8). A censer was a small pan for carrying burning coals. On the Day of Atonement, the high priest took coals "from off the altar before the LORD" (Leviticus 16:12). Many think this rule explains what went wrong: Nadab and Abihu used fire from somewhere else. In the ancient world, temples were often run by priestly families. Israel's priesthood was also a family office, which is why the death of two heirs was so shocking.

Tradition

Some Jewish teachers suggested that Nadab and Abihu were proud, or eager to lead before their time. These are later guesses, not statements of Scripture.

Words and places

  • Censer. A pan or firepan for carrying hot coals and burning incense (Leviticus 10:1; 16:12).
  • Incense. A sweet-smelling mixture burned before God, often a picture of prayer (Exodus 30:34–38; Psalm 141:2).
  • Strange fire. Fire that was "unauthorized" or not the kind God had commanded (Leviticus 10:1).
  • Sanctified. Treated as holy, or set apart (Leviticus 10:3).
