Nathan the prophet

The prophet who brought David God's greatest promise and his hardest rebuke.

Nathan was a prophet at David's court in Jerusalem. Scripture tells us nothing of his family or background, but it shows him at three turning points in David's reign. First, he brought God's promise that David's house and throne would last for ever. Second, after David's sin with Bathsheba and the killing of Uriah, Nathan told the king a simple story about a rich man who stole a poor man's only lamb. When David burned with anger, Nathan said, "Thou art the man." Third, in David's old age, Nathan acted quickly to make sure Solomon, not Adonijah, took the throne. Nathan shows what it means to speak God's word faithfully, whether the message is comfort or confrontation, and to do it with wisdom and courage.

The life of Nathan the prophet

Dates are approximate (BC). Scripture gives no dates for Nathan's first two scenes. They fall in David's reign in Jerusalem (c. 1003–970 BC). The last scene is c. 970 BC.

  1. David at rest in Jerusalem

    Brings God's covenant

    David wants to build a house for the ark. Nathan first says, "Go, do all that is in thine heart." That night God tells Nathan that David's son will build it, and God will build David a lasting house.

    2 Samuel 7:1–17
  2. After Uriah's death

    Confronts the king

    God sends Nathan to David. He tells the story of the rich man and the poor man's lamb, then says, "Thou art the man." David confesses, and Nathan announces both forgiveness and consequences.

    2 Samuel 12:1–15
  3. At Solomon's birth

    Names the child

    God sends word by Nathan, and through him the baby is named Jedidiah, "beloved of the LORD." The LORD loves the child.

    2 Samuel 12:24–25
  4. c. 970 BC

    Warns of Adonijah

    Adonijah proclaims himself king and leaves Nathan out. Nathan tells Bathsheba to remind David of his promise, then comes in himself to confirm her words.

    1 Kings 1:5–27
  5. c. 970 BC

    Anoints Solomon

    At David's command, Nathan and Zadok take Solomon on the king's mule to Gihon and anoint him king. The trumpet sounds, and the people shout, "God save king Solomon."

    1 Kings 1:32–45

Key activities

2 Chronicles 29:25 says the temple music was arranged by command of David, Gad the seer and Nathan the prophet. 1 Kings 4:5 names two officials who were sons of a Nathan, but it is not certain which Nathan is meant.

  1. 1

    Brought back a changed message

    He first told David to go ahead with the temple. After God spoke to him that night, he returned with God's own answer.

    2 Samuel 7:3–5
  2. 2

    Delivered God's covenant

    He told David that God would raise up his son and establish his throne for ever.

    2 Samuel 7:8–17
  3. 3

    Told a story that opened a heart

    "The rich man had exceeding many flocks and herds: But the poor man had nothing, save one little ewe lamb."

    2 Samuel 12:1–4
  4. 4

    Named the sin plainly

    He said, "Thou art the man," and listed what God had given David and what David had done.

    2 Samuel 12:7–12
  5. 5

    Announced forgiveness

    When David said, "I have sinned against the LORD," Nathan replied, "The LORD also hath put away thy sin; thou shalt not die."

    2 Samuel 12:13
  6. 6

    Protected God's choice of king

    He planned with Bathsheba to warn David about Adonijah.

    1 Kings 1:11–27
  7. 7

    Anointed Solomon

    With Zadok the priest, he anointed Solomon at Gihon.

    1 Kings 1:38–45

For discussion

  1. Observe: How did Nathan approach David in 2 Samuel 12:1–7, and how did David react before he knew the story was about him?
  2. Understand: Nathan's first answer in 2 Samuel 7:3 was changed by God's word that night. What does it show that he went back to David with a different message?
  3. Apply: Nathan loved David enough to confront him. Who in your life has that freedom, and do you give it to them?
  4. Apply: Is there a hard but loving word you need to speak to someone? How could you say it wisely?

The world around them

Prophets in Israel spoke for God to the people, and often to the king himself. Samuel had anointed and rebuked Saul, and Gad the seer also advised David (1 Samuel 22:5; 2 Samuel 24:11). In many ancient kingdoms, court prophets told kings what they wanted to hear. Israel's prophets were expected to speak God's word even when it cost them. Parables and word-pictures were one of their tools, drawing the listener in before the point was made.

Words and places

  • Prophet. One who speaks for God (2 Samuel 7:2).
  • Ewe lamb. A female lamb, in Nathan's story a family pet as dear as a daughter (2 Samuel 12:3).
  • Gihon. A spring just outside Jerusalem's walls, where Solomon was anointed (1 Kings 1:33, 38).
  • Jedidiah. The name given to Solomon through Nathan, "beloved of the LORD" (2 Samuel 12:25).
