Nehemiah

The king's cupbearer who prayed, planned and rebuilt Jerusalem's walls in fifty-two days.

Nehemiah was cupbearer to the Persian king Artaxerxes I. Hearing that Jerusalem's walls lay in ruins, he wept and prayed. Then, with a quick silent prayer, he asked the king to send him to rebuild the city. As governor he rallied the people, and despite enemies the wall was finished in fifty-two days. He also defended the poor and later cleansed the temple. His memoir is full of short prayers: "Remember me, O my God, for good."

The life of Nehemiah

Dates approximate (BC), counted from Artaxerxes I (465–424 BC); scholars differ by a year.

  1. c. 446/445 BC20th year, Chisleu

    Bad news from Jerusalem

    In Shushan, Nehemiah hears that Jerusalem's wall is broken down. He weeps, fasts and prays.

    Nehemiah 1:1–11
  2. c. 445 BC20th year, Nisan

    The king's question

    When the king asks why he is sad, he prays silently, then asks to be sent to rebuild.

    Nehemiah 2:1–8
  3. c. 445/444 BCOn arrival

    A night inspection

    He views the ruined walls by night, and the leaders answer, "Let us rise up and build."

    Nehemiah 2:11–20
  4. c. 445/444 BCDuring the building

    Trowel, sword and justice

    He arms the builders against attack and makes the nobles return what they took from the poor.

    Nehemiah 4:1–23; 5:1–13
  5. c. 445/444 BC25th of Elul

    The wall finished

    Despite traps and threats, the wall is completed in fifty-two days, "wrought of our God."

    Nehemiah 6:1–16
  6. c. 433/432 BC and later

    Second term and reforms

    Returning to Jerusalem, he throws out Tobiah's goods, guards the Sabbath and confronts mixed marriages.

    Nehemiah 13:4–31

Key activities

Nehemiah could be fierce. When he found Jews married to foreign wives, he "contended with them, and cursed them, and smote certain of them, and plucked off their hair" (Nehemiah 13:25). Some readers admire his zeal for God. Others see a leader whose methods went too far. The text records it without comment.

    Wept and prayed first

    He mourned for days and confessed the sins of Israel, including his own family's.

    Nehemiah 1:4–11

    When the king asked what he wanted, he sent up a silent prayer before he answered.

    He asked for letters of safe passage and for timber for the gates, the wall and his house.

    Nehemiah 2:17–18

    Nehemiah 3:1–32

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Nehemiah do in the moment between the king's question and his answer (Nehemiah 2:4–5)?
  2. Understand: Nehemiah both prayed and set a guard (Nehemiah 4:9). How do trusting God and taking practical action fit together?
  3. Apply: Nehemiah was moved by news of broken walls far away. What need is God stirring your heart about, and what first step could you take?
  4. Apply: His enemies tried to pull him away from "a great work." What distractions keep you from what God has given you to do, and how can you say no to one this week?

Tradition

The book of 2 Maccabees, in the Apocrypha, says Nehemiah founded a library of sacred books (2 Maccabees 2:13). In the Hebrew Bible, Ezra and Nehemiah were long counted as one book.
