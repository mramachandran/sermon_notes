Part 6 · The Divided Kingdom · Chapter 82

Obadiah, Ahab's steward

A quiet believer in a wicked court who hid a hundred prophets under the queen's nose.

Obadiah ran King Ahab's royal household, one of the highest offices in Israel, in a court where Jezebel was killing the LORD's prophets. Yet he "feared the LORD greatly." While the queen hunted the prophets, Obadiah hid a hundred of them in caves and fed them through a famine. Meeting Elijah, he is glad, afraid and a little defensive at once. He did not resign in protest, but he did not bow to Baal either.

The life of Obadiah, Ahab's steward

Dates are approximate. The drought falls in Ahab's reign (c. 874–853 BC), probably in the 860s BC. Kings gives no exact year.

  1. Ahab's reign

    Serves at court

    Obadiah is governor over King Ahab's house, a senior royal official. Yet Scripture says he feared the LORD greatly and had done so since he was young.

    1 Kings 18:3, 12
  2. When Jezebel strikes

    Hides a hundred prophets

    As Jezebel cuts off the prophets of the LORD, Obadiah takes a hundred of them. He hides them by fifty in a cave and feeds them with bread and water.

    1 Kings 18:4, 13
  3. Third year of drought

    Searches for grass

    The famine in Samaria is severe. Ahab and Obadiah divide the land between them and go looking for springs and grass to keep the horses and mules alive.

    1 Kings 18:1–6
  4. On the road

    Meets Elijah

    Elijah suddenly stands before him. Obadiah recognizes him, falls on his face, and is told to go and tell Ahab, "Behold, Elijah is here."

    1 Kings 18:7–8
  5. The same hour

    Pleads his fear

    Obadiah fears that the Spirit will carry Elijah away and Ahab will kill the messenger. He reminds Elijah how he saved the prophets. Elijah swears to appear before Ahab that day.

    1 Kings 18:9–15
  6. That day

    Brings Ahab

    Obadiah goes to Ahab and tells him, and Ahab goes to meet Elijah. The meeting leads straight to the contest on Mount Carmel.

    1 Kings 18:16–20

Key activities

Scripture never says whether Ahab or Jezebel knew of Obadiah's faith. The text simply sets his fear of God beside his high office and lets both stand.

  1. 1

    Feared God in a hostile court

    He served a king who worshipped Baal, yet he kept faith with the LORD.

    1 Kings 18:3
  2. 2

    Hid a hundred prophets

    At great personal risk, he hid them in caves while Jezebel was killing the LORD's prophets.

    1 Kings 18:4
  3. 3

    Fed them through a famine

    He kept them supplied with bread and water when food and water were scarce.

    1 Kings 18:4, 13
  4. 4

    Did his job faithfully

    He went out on the king's errand to search for grass and water.

    1 Kings 18:5–6
  5. 5

    Honoured the prophet

    When he met Elijah, he fell on his face and called him "my lord Elijah."

    1 Kings 18:7
  6. 6

    Spoke his fear honestly

    He told Elijah plainly that the errand could cost him his life.

    1 Kings 18:9–14
  7. 7

    Carried the message

    Once Elijah promised to appear, Obadiah went and told Ahab.

    1 Kings 18:15–16

For discussion

  1. Observe: How did Obadiah describe himself and his past to Elijah (1 Kings 18:12–13)?
  2. Understand: Obadiah served Ahab and also protected God's prophets. Was he compromising or being wise? What in the text helps you decide?
  3. Apply: Obadiah worked inside a system run by people who opposed God. Where do you live or work under leaders who do not share your faith? What does faithfulness look like there?
  4. Apply: Obadiah's courage was quiet and practical: bread and water, day after day. Who needs quiet, practical help from you this week?

The world around them

The man "over the house" was the palace steward, one of the highest officials in the kingdom. Solomon had one (1 Kings 4:6), and so did later kings of Judah (Isaiah 22:15). Such a man managed the king's household, lands and supplies, which may explain how Obadiah could feed a hundred people in a famine. The limestone hills of the region are full of caves. Horses and mules mattered because chariots were the heart of a king's army.

Tradition

The Talmud identifies him with the prophet who wrote the book of Obadiah, but most scholars think they were different men. The historian Josephus says the prophet's widow in 2 Kings 4:1 was Obadiah's wife. Scripture does not name her husband.

Words and places

  • Governor of the house. The royal steward, who ran the palace and the king's estates (1 Kings 4:6; 18:3).
  • Obadiah. A common name. About a dozen different men in the Old Testament are called Obadiah (1 Chronicles 3:21; Ezra 8:9).
  • Carried by the Spirit. Some people believed God's Spirit might suddenly carry Elijah elsewhere (1 Kings 18:12; 2 Kings 2:16).
