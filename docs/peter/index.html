Peter

The fisherman who fell hard, was restored, and became a rock for the early church.

Simon was a Galilean fisherman when his brother Andrew brought him to Jesus, who named him Cephas, or Peter, "a stone." Peter speaks first, acts fast, and sometimes gets it badly wrong. He confessed Jesus as the Christ, then tried to talk him out of the cross. He promised to die with him, then denied him three times. Yet the risen Jesus restored him, and in Acts Peter preaches at Pentecost, heals in Jesus' name, and opens the gospel to the Gentiles. Two New Testament letters bear his name.

The life of Peter

Dates approximate (AD); this study dates the crucifixion c. AD 30 (some prefer AD 33).

  1. c. AD 27–28By the Jordan

    Meets Jesus

    Andrew brings his brother Simon to Jesus, who names him Cephas, a stone.

    John 1:40–42
  2. c. AD 28Sea of Galilee

    Leaves his nets

    After a net-breaking catch at Jesus' word, he leaves everything to follow him.

    Luke 5:1–11
  3. c. AD 29Caesarea Philippi

    Confesses the Christ

    Peter calls Jesus the Christ, then rebukes him for speaking of the cross.

    Matthew 16:13–23
  4. c. AD 30The high priest's courtyard

    Denies Jesus

    Peter three times denies knowing him, then goes out and weeps bitterly.

    Luke 22:31–34, 54–62
  5. c. AD 30By the sea

    Restored by the risen Lord

    Three times Jesus asks, "Lovest thou me?" and charges him to feed his flock.

    John 21:1–19
  6. c. AD 30Jerusalem

    Preaches at Pentecost

    Filled with the Spirit, he preaches Christ; about three thousand are baptized.

    Acts 2:1–41
  7. AD 44Jerusalem

    Freed from prison

    After Herod Agrippa I kills James, an angel leads the jailed Peter out.

    Acts 12:1–17
  8. c. AD 49Jerusalem

    Speaks at the council

    Peter says God saves Gentiles and Jews alike by grace.

    Acts 15:6–11

Key activities

After Acts 15 Peter drops out of the narrative; Scripture does not record his later travels or death.

  1. 1

    Left his nets

    "Depart from me; for I am a sinful man, O Lord."

    Luke 5:4–11
  2. 2

    Walked on the water

    Sinking in the wind, he cried, "Lord, save me."

    Matthew 14:28–31
  3. 3

    Spoke for the Twelve

    "Lord, to whom shall we go? thou hast the words of eternal life."

    John 6:66–69
  4. 4

    Confessed and then stumbled

    He rebuked Jesus for speaking of suffering: "Get thee behind me, Satan."

    Matthew 16:16–23
  5. 5

    Saw the Transfiguration

    He offered to build three tabernacles, not knowing what to say.

    Mark 9:2–8
  6. 6

    Drew his sword

    He cut off Malchus's ear, and Jesus told him to put the sword away.

    John 18:10–11
  7. 7

    Denied his Lord

    Three times in one night he said he did not know Jesus.

    Mark 14:66–72
  8. 8

    Was restored

    The risen Jesus appeared to him and later gave him a threefold charge to feed his sheep.

    Luke 24:34; John 21:15–17
  9. 9

    Preached and healed

    At the temple gate he told a lame man, "Silver and gold have I none," and raised him up in Jesus' name.

    Acts 3:1–16
  10. 10

    Stood before the council

    With the apostles he answered, "We ought to obey God rather than men."

    Acts 4:1–20; 5:29
  11. 11

    Welcomed Cornelius

    A vision at Joppa taught him that God is no respecter of persons, and he had a Roman centurion's household baptized.

    Acts 10:1–48; 11:1–18
  12. 12

    Wrote to scattered believers

    The letter of 1 Peter encourages Christians facing suffering to hope and to do good.

    1 Peter 1:1–9

Across Scripture

Direct references first. Tradition: martyred in Rome under Nero, c. AD 64–67 (hinted in John 21:18–19); the upside-down cross is a later story.

Old Testament

  • Joel 2:28–32Quoted at Pentecost (Acts 2:16–21)
  • Psalm 16:8–11; 110:1Preached of Jesus' resurrection (Acts 2:25–35)
  • Psalm 118:22; Isaiah 28:16The rejected corner stone (1 Peter 2:6–7)
  • Echo: Jonah 1:3Jonah fled Gentiles from Joppa; Peter is sent from there

New Testament

  • Matthew 16:18"Upon this rock": Peter, his confession, or Christ? Views differ
  • 1 Corinthians 15:5The risen Lord "was seen of Cephas"
  • Galatians 1:18; 2:7–14Paul visits him, and later rebukes him at Antioch
  • 1 Peter 5:1, 13; 2 Peter 1:16–18"Babylon" often read as Rome; 2 Peter's authorship debated

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Jesus ask Peter three times by the sea (John 21:15–17)?
  2. Understand: Why was the cross so hard for Peter to accept?
  3. Apply: Peter wept but did not stay away. What helps you come back to God after failing?
  4. Apply: God shows no partiality. Who outside your usual circle could you welcome this week?
