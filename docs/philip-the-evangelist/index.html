Philip the evangelist

The table-server who carried the gospel to Samaria, to an African official, and down the coast.

Philip was one of the seven chosen to care for the widows in the Jerusalem church. When persecution broke out after Stephen's death, he went to Samaria, a place most Jews avoided, and preached Christ with great results. Then an angel sent him to a desert road, where he met an Ethiopian official reading Isaiah. Philip pointed him to Jesus and baptized him, then preached town to town up to Caesarea. Twenty years later he was still there, "Philip the evangelist," with four daughters who prophesied.

The life of Philip the evangelist

Dates are approximate (AD). Acts gives no years. The mission to Samaria follows Stephen's death, c. AD 33–35, and Paul's visit to Caesarea is c. AD 57.

  1. Early churchJerusalem

    Chosen as one of the seven

    When Greek-speaking widows are neglected, the church chooses seven men full of the Spirit and wisdom. Philip is named second, after Stephen. The apostles pray and lay hands on them.

    Acts 6:1–6
  2. c. AD 33–35Samaria

    Preaches Christ

    Persecution scatters the believers. Philip goes down to the city of Samaria and preaches Christ. Crowds listen, unclean spirits are cast out, the lame are healed, and "there was great joy in that city."

    Acts 8:1–8
  3. Samaria

    Baptizes many, including Simon

    Men and women believe and are baptized. Even Simon, a well-known sorcerer, believes and follows Philip. Peter and John come from Jerusalem, and the Samaritans receive the Holy Ghost.

    Acts 8:9–17
  4. The Gaza road

    Meets the Ethiopian

    An angel sends Philip south to a desert road. He finds a treasurer of the queen of Ethiopia reading Isaiah, explains the passage, preaches Jesus, and baptizes him in roadside water.

    Acts 8:26–38
  5. Azotus to Caesarea

    Preaches along the coast

    The Spirit catches Philip away. He is found at Azotus and preaches in all the cities along the way until he comes to Caesarea.

    Acts 8:39–40
  6. c. AD 57Caesarea

    Hosts Paul

    At the end of his third journey, Paul and his companions stay many days in Philip's house. The prophet Agabus comes and warns Paul about what awaits him in Jerusalem.

    Acts 21:8–14

Key activities

The KJV includes Acts 8:37, the Ethiopian's confession of faith. Many of the oldest Greek manuscripts do not have this verse, so some modern translations put it in a footnote.

  1. 1

    Served the widows

    He was trusted to oversee the daily care of the church's needy widows.

    Acts 6:3–5
  2. 2

    Crossed a cultural line

    He took the gospel to Samaria, and "there was great joy in that city."

    Acts 8:5–8
  3. 3

    Did signs

    Unclean spirits came out, and many who were palsied and lame were healed.

    Acts 8:6–7, 13
  4. 4

    Obeyed without delay

    Told to go to a desert road, "he arose and went."

    Acts 8:26–27
  5. 5

    Ran to the chariot

    The Spirit said, "Go near, and join thyself to this chariot," and Philip ran to it.

    Acts 8:29–30
  6. 6

    Explained Scripture

    He asked, "Understandest thou what thou readest?" and preached Jesus from Isaiah 53.

    Acts 8:30–35
  7. 7

    Baptized the Ethiopian

    The official went on his way rejoicing.

    Acts 8:36–39
  8. 8

    Kept preaching

    He preached in every city from Azotus to Caesarea, and years later was still known as the evangelist.

    Acts 8:40; 21:8
