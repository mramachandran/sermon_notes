Pontius Pilate

The Roman governor who found no fault in Jesus and handed him over to be crucified.

Pontius Pilate was the Roman governor of Judea from AD 26 to 36. He held the power of life and death, and so Jesus' trial ended before him. All four Gospels show a man who questioned Jesus, found no fault in him, and tried several ways to let him go. He sent him to Herod, offered the crowd Barabbas, and had him scourged in the hope that it would be enough. In the end he gave way to pressure, washed his hands, and handed Jesus over to be crucified. He is the only Roman official named in the ancient Christian creeds. His question, "What is truth?", still hangs in the air.

The life of Pontius Pilate

His years in office (AD 26–36) come from Josephus. Gospel events follow the house chronology, with the crucifixion c. AD 30; on the other main view, AD 33.

  1. c. AD 28–29Governor of Judaea

    Named in Luke's dating

    Luke dates John the Baptist's call to the fifteenth year of the emperor Tiberius, "Pontius Pilate being governor of Judaea."

    Luke 3:1
  2. Before AD 30Jerusalem

    Blood at the altar

    Some tell Jesus of Galileans "whose blood Pilate had mingled with their sacrifices." Jesus uses the news to call his hearers to repent. Scripture gives no other details.

    Luke 13:1–3
  3. c. AD 30Friday dawn

    Questions Jesus

    The chief priests bring Jesus, accused of claiming to be a king. Pilate examines him and finds no fault. Hearing that he is a Galilean, he sends him to Herod Antipas.

    Luke 23:1–7; John 18:28–38
  4. c. AD 30Friday morning

    Offers Barabbas

    Hoping to release Jesus, he offers the crowd a choice of prisoner. His wife sends a warning after a dream. The chief priests stir the crowd, who ask for Barabbas.

    Matthew 27:15–21
  5. c. AD 30Friday morning

    Washes his hands

    He has Jesus scourged and says, "Behold the man!" Fearing a riot and the charge of disloyalty to Caesar, he washes his hands before the crowd and hands Jesus over.

    John 19:1–16; Matthew 27:24–26
  6. c. AD 30Friday and Saturday

    Title, body and guard

    He refuses to change the title "THE KING OF THE JEWS," grants Jesus' body to Joseph of Arimathea, and lets the chief priests seal and guard the tomb.

    John 19:19–22, 38; Matthew 27:62–66

Key activities

Jesus told Pilate, "Thou couldest have no power at all against me, except it were given thee from above" (John 19:11). The Gospels show Pilate as both unwilling and responsible, and Acts 4:27–28 places the whole event within God's purpose.

  1. 1

    Examined Jesus

    He asked, "Art thou the King of the Jews?" and heard that Jesus' kingdom was not of this world.

    John 18:33–37
  2. 2

    Asked about truth

    He asked, "What is truth?" and went out without waiting for an answer.

    John 18:38
  3. 3

    Declared him innocent

    Three times he said he found no fault in Jesus.

    Luke 23:4, 14, 22
  4. 4

    Sent him to Herod

    He tried to pass the case to Herod Antipas, who sent Jesus back.

    Luke 23:6–12
  5. 5

    Offered Barabbas

    He hoped the crowd would choose Jesus, but they chose Barabbas.

    Mark 15:6–15
  6. 6

    Washed his hands

    He said, "I am innocent of the blood of this just person: see ye to it."

    Matthew 27:24
  7. 7

    Gave in to pressure

    When the crowd cried, "If thou let this man go, thou art not Caesar's friend," he delivered Jesus to be crucified.

    John 19:12–16

For discussion

  2. Understand: Pilate washed his hands, but was he free of responsibility? Why or why not?
  3. Apply: Pilate knew what was right but feared the crowd. Where do you feel pressure to go along with others against your conscience?
  4. Apply: Pilate asked, "What is truth?" and walked away. What truth do you need to stop and face this week?

The world around them

Judea came under direct Roman rule in AD 6. Its governors, called prefects in Pilate's day, lived at Caesarea and came up to Jerusalem at the feasts to keep order. A stone found at Caesarea in 1961 bears Pilate's name and his title, prefect of Judea. Josephus and Philo describe him as stubborn and harsh. In AD 36 he was removed after an attack on a crowd of Samaritans and sent to Rome. The Roman historian Tacitus also records that Christ was put to death under Pilate.

Tradition

Early Christian writers told different stories about Pilate's end. Eusebius says he took his own life. In later tradition his wife is named Claudia Procula, and the Ethiopian church honours both of them as saints. None of this is in Scripture.

Words and places

  • Governor. The Roman ruler of the province; his title in Pilate's day was prefect (Luke 3:1; Matthew 27:2).
  • Hall of judgment. The governor's headquarters in Jerusalem, also called the praetorium (John 18:28; Mark 15:16).
  • Scourge. To beat with a whip, often before crucifixion (John 19:1).
  • Gabbatha. "The Pavement," where Pilate sat on the judgment seat (John 19:13).
