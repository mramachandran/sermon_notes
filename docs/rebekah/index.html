Rebekah

The bold young woman who said "I will go," and later took her family's future into her own hands.

Rebekah enters the Bible at a well, drawing water for a stranger and all ten of his camels. He was Abraham's servant, sent to find a wife for Isaac, and he saw her kindness as God's answer to prayer. Asked whether she would leave home at once, she said simply, "I will go." She became Isaac's beloved wife and received a word from God about her twin sons. Later she planned the deception that won Jacob his father's blessing. It cost her dearly: she sent Jacob away, and Genesis never records that she saw him again.

The life of Rebekah

Genesis gives no ages for Rebekah. Isaac's ages come from Genesis 25:20 and 25:26.

  1. At the well

    Waters the camels

    Abraham's servant prays for a sign. Before he finishes, Rebekah comes out, gives him a drink, and offers to draw water for all his camels.

    Genesis 24:12–27
  2. At home

    Agrees to go

    Her brother Laban and father Bethuel agree that "the thing proceedeth from the LORD." Asked if she will go, she agrees, and her family blesses her.

    Genesis 24:28–61
  3. Isaac age 40

    Marries Isaac

    She sees Isaac in the field, covers herself with a veil, and becomes his wife. He loves her and is comforted after his mother's death.

    Genesis 24:62–67; 25:20
  4. Isaac age 60

    Bears twins

    After twenty years of barrenness, Isaac prays and she conceives. The children struggle within her, so she asks the LORD why. He tells her, "The elder shall serve the younger."

    Genesis 25:21–26
  5. In Gerar

    Called a sister

    During a famine Isaac tells the men of Gerar that she is his sister, until King Abimelech sees them together and protects her.

    Genesis 26:6–11
  6. Isaac old and blind

    Plans the deception

    She overhears Isaac promise Esau a blessing. She dresses Jacob in Esau's clothes, covers his hands with goatskins, and cooks the meal herself.

    Genesis 27:1–17
  7. After the blessing

    Sends Jacob away

    Learning that Esau plans to kill Jacob, she urges Jacob to flee to Laban and persuades Isaac to send him there to find a wife.

    Genesis 27:41–28:5

Key activities

Many readers believe Rebekah acted because of God's word that "the elder shall serve the younger" (Genesis 25:23). Genesis does not say so. It shows both God's purpose and the harm done by deceit.

  1. 1

    Served a stranger

    She gave a traveler a drink and then offered, unasked, to water all his camels too.

    Genesis 24:17–20
  2. 2

    Chose to go

    She left her family at once for a husband she had never met.

    Genesis 24:57–61
  3. 3

    Comforted Isaac

    She became the wife Isaac loved, and she comforted him after Sarah's death.

    Genesis 24:67
  4. 4

    Took her question to God

    When her pregnancy troubled her, "she went to enquire of the LORD."

    Genesis 25:22–23
  5. 5

    Loved Jacob

    "Rebekah loved Jacob," while Isaac loved Esau.

    Genesis 25:28
  6. 6

    Grieved over Esau's wives

    His Hittite wives were "a grief of mind unto Isaac and to Rebekah."

    Genesis 26:34–35; 27:46
  7. 7

    Directed the deception

    She told Jacob, "Upon me be thy curse, my son: only obey my voice."

    Genesis 27:5–17
  8. 8

    Protected Jacob

    She sent him away to Haran "a few days" that became twenty years.

    Genesis 27:42–45; 31:41

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Rebekah.

Old Testament

  • Genesis 22:20–23Her birth is reported to Abraham: "Bethuel begat Rebekah."
  • Genesis 24–27This is the main account of her life.
  • Genesis 35:8"Deborah Rebekah's nurse" dies and is buried near Bethel.
  • Genesis 49:31Jacob says, "There they buried Isaac and Rebekah his wife."

New Testament

  • Romans 9:10–13Paul points out that God's word came to Rebecca before her sons were born, "neither having done any good or evil," so that God's choice would rest "not of works, but of him that calleth."
  • EchoesLike the servant at the well, Jesus meets a woman at a well (John 4:6–7). Mary, mother of Jesus, also gives a ready yes to God's call: "be it unto me according to thy word" (Luke 1:38).

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Rebekah do for Abraham's servant beyond what he asked (Genesis 24:17–20)?
  2. Understand: God had told Rebekah that the elder would serve the younger. Do you think she needed to scheme for it to happen? Why or why not?
  3. Apply: Rebekah said, "I will go." Where is God asking you for a ready yes right now?
  4. Apply: Rebekah favored one son. How can you show each person in your family that they are loved?

The world around them

Marriages were arranged between families, usually within the wider clan. Abraham wanted Isaac's wife to come from his own relatives, not from the Canaanites around him. Gifts of gold and silver jewelry and fine clothing sealed the agreement. Camels were costly animals, and a thirsty camel can drink a great deal of water. By offering to water ten of them, Rebekah was volunteering for hard, heavy work. The journey from Mesopotamia to the south of Canaan was long.

Words and places

  • Padan-aram. A region of upper Mesopotamia around Haran, where Abraham's relatives lived (Genesis 25:20; 28:2).
  • Earring. The servant put the gold ring "upon her face," probably a nose ring (Genesis 24:22, 47).
  • Birthright. The firstborn son's special share of inheritance and family leadership (Genesis 25:31–34).
  • Savoury meat. A tasty dish of cooked game or goat's meat (Genesis 27:4, 9).
