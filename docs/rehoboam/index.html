Part 6 · The Divided Kingdom · Chapter 75

Rehoboam

Solomon's son, who took the wrong advice and lost ten tribes in a single day.

Rehoboam was the son of Solomon and the first king of Judah after the kingdom divided. He came to the throne at forty-one, heir to the richest court Israel had known. The northern tribes asked him to lighten the heavy load his father had laid on them. He listened to his young friends instead of his father's older advisers and answered harshly. Ten tribes walked away and made Jeroboam I their king. Rehoboam spent the rest of his seventeen-year reign fortifying a smaller kingdom, drifting from God, and paying tribute to an Egyptian invader. Chronicles adds that he humbled himself when judgment came, and God eased his hand.

The life of Rehoboam

Dates are approximate (BC) and follow the house chronology for the divided kingdom. Years are counted from the start of his reign.

  1. c. 931 BCAge 41

    Goes to Shechem

    After Solomon's death, all Israel gathers at Shechem to make Rehoboam king. Jeroboam, back from Egypt, speaks for the tribes: ease the heavy yoke, and we will serve you.

    1 Kings 11:43–12:5
  2. c. 931 BCThree days later

    Rejects the elders

    The old men who served Solomon urge him to serve the people. His young friends urge him to be harsher still. He takes their advice and threatens to chastise the tribes with scorpions.

    1 Kings 12:6–15
  3. c. 931 BCSoon after

    The kingdom splits

    The northern tribes reject David's house. They stone Adoram, his taskmaster, and Rehoboam flees to Jerusalem by chariot. He musters 180,000 men for war, but Shemaiah tells him to stand down, and he obeys.

    1 Kings 12:16–24
  4. c. 931–928 BCYears 1–3

    Strengthens Judah

    He fortifies cities across Judah and Benjamin. Priests and Levites from the north move south, and for three years the kingdom walks in the way of David and Solomon.

    2 Chronicles 11:5–17
  5. c. 926 BC5th year

    Shishak invades

    Once secure, Rehoboam and Judah forsake God's law. Shishak of Egypt takes the fortified cities and strips the temple and palace, including Solomon's gold shields. The leaders humble themselves, and God spares Jerusalem.

    1 Kings 14:25–28; 2 Chronicles 12:1–12
  6. c. 913 BCAge 58

    Dies

    After seventeen years marked by constant war with Jeroboam, he is buried in the city of David. His son Abijah reigns in his place.

    1 Kings 14:29–31; 2 Chronicles 12:13–16

Key activities

Kings tells the story of the split and Judah's sin. Chronicles adds his building, his large family, and his repentance when Shishak came.

  1. 1

    Went to Shechem to be crowned

    He went north to Shechem, the old meeting place of the tribes, where all Israel had gathered to make him king.

    1 Kings 12:1
  2. 2

    Asked for time and advice

    He sent the people away for three days and consulted both the old men and the young men.

    1 Kings 12:5–11
  3. 3

    Answered roughly

    He told the people, "my father made your yoke heavy, and I will add to your yoke." Ten tribes rebelled that day.

    1 Kings 12:12–16
  4. 4

    Obeyed the word to stand down

    When Shemaiah said, "this thing is from me," Rehoboam and his army went home instead of fighting.

    1 Kings 12:21–24; 2 Chronicles 11:1–4
  5. 5

    Built a line of fortresses

    He fortified Bethlehem, Lachish, Hebron and other towns, and stocked them with food, oil, wine, shields and spears.

    2 Chronicles 11:5–12
  6. 6

    Welcomed exiles from the north

    Priests, Levites and faithful people who rejected Jeroboam's calves came to Jerusalem to worship.

    2 Chronicles 11:13–17
  7. 7

    Built a large household

    He had eighteen wives and sixty concubines. He loved Maachah, Absalom's daughter, most and chose her son Abijah as heir.

    2 Chronicles 11:18–23
  8. 8

    Forsook the law

    Once strong, he and all Israel with him forsook God's law. Judah built high places and images on every high hill.

    1 Kings 14:22–24; 2 Chronicles 12:1
  9. 9

    Humbled himself

    When Shemaiah announced judgment through Shishak, the king and princes said, "The LORD is righteous." God granted them some deliverance.

    2 Chronicles 12:5–7, 12
  10. 10

    Replaced gold with brass

    He made brass shields to replace the gold ones Shishak had carried off.

    1 Kings 14:26–28

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did the older men advise, and what did the young men advise (1 Kings 12:6–11)?
  2. Understand: The text says the split was Rehoboam's choice and also "from the LORD" (1 Kings 12:15, 24). How can both be true?
  3. Apply: Whose advice do you tend to seek when you face a hard decision? Do they ever tell you what you would rather not hear?
  4. Apply: Rehoboam drifted from God once he felt strong (2 Chronicles 12:1). What habit would keep you close to God in success?

The world around them

The tribes had never been fully one. David first reigned over Judah alone at Hebron, and the northern cry "to your tents, O Israel" had been heard once before, in Sheba's revolt (2 Samuel 20:1). Solomon's building projects relied on forced labour and heavy taxes (1 Kings 5:13–14; 12:4). Shishak is usually identified with Shoshenq I, founder of a new Egyptian dynasty. A victory relief he carved at the temple of Karnak lists many towns in the land that he claimed to have taken.

Words and places

  • Shechem. An old city in the hill country of Ephraim, where Joshua renewed the covenant (Joshua 24:1; 1 Kings 12:1).
  • Scorpions. Probably a harsher kind of whip, perhaps with barbs or spikes. The exact meaning is uncertain (1 Kings 12:11).
  • Adoram. The officer over the forced labour, probably the same man as Adoniram under Solomon (1 Kings 4:6; 12:18).
  • High places. Hilltop shrines, often linked with idols, that Judah's kings were repeatedly judged for keeping (1 Kings 14:23).
