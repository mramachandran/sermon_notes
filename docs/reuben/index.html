Reuben

Jacob's firstborn, a man of good intentions who lost his place through one grave sin.

Reuben was the eldest of Jacob's twelve sons, born to Leah in Haran. As firstborn he should have led the family. He often meant well. He tried to rescue Joseph from his brothers, spoke up about their guilt, and offered his own sons as a pledge for Benjamin's safety. But he also committed a grave sin against his father, and at Jacob's deathbed he heard the words "Unstable as water, thou shalt not excel." The firstborn's rights passed to Joseph's sons, and leadership to Judah. His life shows that good intentions matter, but so do steady faithfulness and self-control.

The life of Reuben

Genesis gives no dates or ages for Reuben. The order below follows the text. He was born early in Jacob's twenty years in Haran (Genesis 29:32; 31:41).

  1. In Haran

    Jacob's firstborn

    Leah bears her first son. She names him Reuben, saying, "Surely the LORD hath looked upon my affliction; now therefore my husband will love me."

    Genesis 29:31–32
  2. Wheat harvest

    Brings mandrakes

    Young Reuben finds mandrakes in the field and brings them to his mother. Leah and Rachel bargain over them.

    Genesis 30:14–16
  3. Beyond the tower of Edar

    Sins against his father

    After Rachel's death, Reuben sleeps with Bilhah, his father's concubine. Genesis says only, "and Israel heard it."

    Genesis 35:21–22
  4. At Dothan

    Tries to save Joseph

    The brothers plot to kill Joseph. Reuben talks them into throwing him into a pit instead, planning to rescue him. He returns to find Joseph gone.

    Genesis 37:18–30
  5. In Egypt

    Names the brothers' guilt

    When the governor of Egypt treats them harshly, Reuben reminds his brothers that he had warned them not to sin against the boy.

    Genesis 42:21–24
  6. Back in Canaan

    Offers his sons

    Jacob refuses to send Benjamin to Egypt. Reuben says, "Slay my two sons, if I bring him not to thee." Jacob still refuses.

    Genesis 42:35–38
  7. Jacob's deathbed

    Loses the firstborn's place

    Jacob calls him "my might, and the beginning of my strength," but says he will not excel, because he defiled his father's bed.

    Genesis 49:3–4

Key activities

Some readers see Reuben's sin with Bilhah as lust. Others think he meant to stop Bilhah, Rachel's maid, from taking Rachel's place above his mother Leah, or to claim leadership early. Genesis gives no motive.

    He found mandrakes in the wheat harvest and gave them to Leah.

    Genesis 30:14
  2. 2

    Dishonored his father

    He slept with Bilhah, his father's concubine, and Jacob heard of it.

    Genesis 35:22

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Reuben plan to do for Joseph (Genesis 37:22), and what happened instead?
  2. Understand: Reuben meant well, yet is remembered as "unstable." What was missing?
  3. Apply: Where do you need to speak up now rather than wait?
  4. Apply: Reuben's tribe was still blessed (Deuteronomy 33:6). How does God's mercy give you hope after failure?

The world around them

In the ancient Near East the firstborn son usually led the family after his father died and received a double share of the inheritance (Deuteronomy 21:17). A concubine was a secondary wife, often a former servant, with fewer rights than a full wife. For a son to take his father's concubine was more than a private sin. It could be seen as a bold claim to his father's place and authority, as when Absalom later did the same to David (2 Samuel 16:21–22).

Words and places

  • Firstborn. The eldest son, who normally received a double portion and led the family (Deuteronomy 21:17).
  • Concubine. A secondary wife with fewer rights, often a servant (Genesis 35:22).
  • Dothan. A town north of Shechem where the brothers were grazing the flocks when they sold Joseph (Genesis 37:17).
  • Unstable as water. Like boiling or rushing water, uncontrolled and unreliable (Genesis 49:4).
