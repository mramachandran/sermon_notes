Ruth

The foreign widow whose loyalty placed her in the family line of King David.

Ruth was a Moabite, an outsider to Israel, who lost her husband and had every reason to go home. Instead she bound herself to her bitter, grieving mother-in-law and to Naomi's God. In the fields of Bethlehem her quiet faithfulness met the kindness of Boaz, a kinsman who redeemed the family's land and married her. Her son became the grandfather of David.

The life of Ruth

The book gives places and seasons rather than exact years. The date is approximate.

  1. c. 1100 BCDays of the judges

    Famine drives a family to Moab

    Elimelech of Bethlehem moves to Moab with his wife Naomi and their sons Mahlon and Chilion.

    Ruth 1:1–2
  2. In MoabAbout 10 years

    Marriage and loss

    Ruth marries Mahlon; Orpah marries Chilion. Elimelech dies, then both sons, leaving three widows.

    Ruth 1:3–5; 4:10
  3. On the road

    The great choice

    Naomi urges both daughters-in-law to go home. Orpah kisses her and returns; Ruth clings to Naomi and vows to stay.

    Ruth 1:6–18
  4. BethlehemBarley harvest

    Arrival

    The town stirs at their return. Naomi asks to be called Mara, "bitter," for the Almighty has dealt bitterly with her.

    Ruth 1:19–22
  5. Boaz's fieldBarley and wheat harvests

    Gleaning

    Ruth gleans behind the reapers. Boaz, a relative of Elimelech, protects her and lets extra grain fall for her.

    Ruth 2:1–23
  6. Threshing floorEnd of harvest

    A request at midnight

    On Naomi's advice Ruth lies at Boaz's feet and asks him to spread his covering over her as near kinsman.

    Ruth 3:1–18
  7. City gateThe next day

    Redeemed

    A nearer kinsman declines. Boaz buys Elimelech's land before the elders and takes Ruth as his wife.

    Ruth 4:1–12
  8. BethlehemLater

    A son for Naomi

    Ruth bears Obed. The women bless Naomi; Obed becomes the father of Jesse, the father of David.

    Ruth 4:13–22

Key activities

What Ruth did, in the order the book tells it.

  1. 1

    Married into an Israelite family

    As a Moabite she joined a family from Bethlehem living in her country.

    Ruth 1:4; 4:10
  2. 2

    Stayed when she could have left

    Orpah went back to her people; Ruth clung to Naomi.

    Ruth 1:14
  3. 3

    Chose Naomi's God

    She vowed to share Naomi's people, her God, and even her grave.

    Ruth 1:16–17
  4. 4

    Took the initiative to provide

    She asked to glean in the fields so the two widows could eat.

    Ruth 2:2
  5. 5

    Worked from morning to evening

    The overseer noticed she had barely rested since dawn.

    Ruth 2:7, 17
  6. 6

    Answered kindness with humility

    She bowed before Boaz and asked why a foreigner had found favour.

    Ruth 2:10, 13
  7. 7

    Shared what she was given

    She brought home her grain and saved food from her meal for Naomi.

    Ruth 2:14, 18
  8. 8

    Trusted Naomi's counsel

    "All that thou sayest unto me I will do."

    Ruth 3:5
  9. 9

    Asked Boaz to redeem

    She appealed to his role as near kinsman, and he called her a virtuous woman.

    Ruth 3:9–11
  10. 10

    Became a mother in David's line

    Her son Obed was the grandfather of Israel's greatest king.

    Ruth 4:13, 17

For discussion

  1. Observe: What reasons did Naomi give for sending her daughters-in-law home (Ruth 1:8–13)?
  2. Understand: Why do you think Ruth stayed when Orpah, just as loving, went back?
  3. Apply: Ruth's faith showed up as hard work and loyalty. Where could yours show up that way this week?
  4. Apply: Who is an "outsider" in your church or neighbourhood you could welcome as Boaz welcomed Ruth?
