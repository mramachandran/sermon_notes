Part 2 · The Patriarchs · Chapter 11

Sarah

The barren wife who laughed at God's promise, then laughed for joy when it came true.

Sarah was Abraham's wife and half-sister, and she shared every step of his journey of faith. She is introduced with one hard line: "But Sarai was barren; she had no child." She left home at sixty-five, was twice taken into a king's household because of her husband's fear, and tried to build a family through Hagar, with painful results. When God said she would bear a son at ninety, she laughed. Yet God kept his word, and Isaac, whose name sounds like "laughter," was born. Scripture remembers her doubts honestly, but names her among the heroes of faith.

The life of Sarah

Ages are worked out from Genesis 17:17. Many scholars place the patriarchs c. 2000–1700 BC.

  1. In Ur

    Married and barren

    Sarai marries Abram; she is barren. She leaves Ur with Terah's family and settles in Haran.

    Genesis 11:29–31
  2. Age 65

    Leaves Haran

    When God calls Abram, Sarai goes with him into Canaan, leaving home and relatives behind.

    Genesis 12:4–5
  3. In Egypt

    Taken by Pharaoh

    Abram has her say she is his sister. She is taken until the LORD sends plagues.

    Genesis 12:10–20
  4. Age 75

    Gives Hagar to Abram

    When Hagar conceives and despises her, Sarai treats her harshly, and Hagar runs away.

    Genesis 16:1–6
  5. Age 89

    Named Sarah; laughs

    God promises she will be "a mother of nations." At Mamre she overhears and laughs within herself.

    Genesis 17:15–21; 18:9–15
  6. In Gerar

    Taken again

    King Abimelech takes her; God warns him in a dream, and he returns her untouched.

    Genesis 20:1–18
  7. Age 90

    Isaac is born

    She says, "God hath made me to laugh, so that all that hear will laugh with me."

    Genesis 21:1–7
  8. Age 127

    Dies at Hebron

    Abraham weeps for her and buries her in the cave of Machpelah.

    Genesis 23:1–20

Key activities

In the KJV, Hebrews 11:11 speaks of Sarah's own faith. Some scholars read the Greek as describing Abraham's faith, with Sarah named alongside him. Either way, the verse honors the promise God kept to them both.

  1. 1

    Left home by faith

    She went with Abram from Haran into Canaan, sharing his wandering life in tents.

    Genesis 12:5; Hebrews 11:9
  2. 2

    Went along with a half-truth

    She was Abram's half-sister, and she agreed to hide that she was his wife, first in Egypt and then in Gerar.

    Genesis 12:11–15; 20:2, 5, 12–13
  3. 3

    Was protected by God

    Twice God struck the royal house that took her, and twice she was given back.

    Genesis 12:17; 20:3–7, 18
  4. 4

    Tried to build a family her own way

    She said, "It may be that I may obtain children by her," and gave Hagar to Abram.

    Genesis 16:1–3
  5. 5

    Blamed and afflicted

    When Hagar looked down on her, she said to Abram, "My wrong be upon thee," and dealt hardly with Hagar.

    Genesis 16:4–6
  6. 6

    Received a new name

    God called her Sarah and promised that kings would come from her.

    Genesis 17:15–16
  7. 7

    Laughed and denied it

    She laughed at the idea of a child in old age. Then, afraid, she said, "I laughed not." God asked, "Is any thing too hard for the LORD?"

    Genesis 18:9–15
  8. 8

    Bore Isaac

    She conceived and bore a son at ninety, and her laughter of doubt became laughter of joy.

    Genesis 21:1–7
  9. 9

    Demanded Hagar's departure

    At Isaac's weaning feast she saw Ishmael mocking and said, "Cast out this bondwoman and her son." God told Abraham to listen to her.

    Genesis 21:8–12
  10. 10

    Was mourned and buried

    Abraham wept for her and bought the cave of Machpelah as her grave.

    Genesis 23:1–2, 19

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Sarah.

Old Testament

  • Genesis 11:29–23:20The main account of her life
  • Genesis 24:67Rebekah brought into "his mother Sarah's tent"
  • Genesis 25:10; 49:31Buried with Abraham at Machpelah
  • Isaiah 51:2"Unto Sarah that bare you"

New Testament

  • Romans 4:19; 9:9"The deadness of Sara's womb"; the promised son
  • Galatians 4:21–31The free woman in Paul's allegory, unnamed
  • Hebrews 11:11"Through faith also Sara herself received strength"
  • 1 Peter 3:5–6A model for wives: "Even as Sara obeyed Abraham"
  • Echo: Luke 1:36–37"With God nothing shall be impossible"

For discussion

  1. Observe: How did Sarah respond when she overheard the promise of a son (Genesis 18:10–15)?
  2. Understand: Sarah laughed in doubt, yet Hebrews 11:11 praises her faith. How can both be true?
  3. Apply: Where are you tempted to force an answer instead of waiting on God?
  4. Apply: "Is any thing too hard for the LORD?" What situation do you need to bring to him this week?
