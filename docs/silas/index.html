Silas

The Jerusalem leader who became Paul's partner and sang hymns in a Philippian jail.

Silas was a respected leader and prophet in the Jerusalem church. After the council in Jerusalem, the apostles chose him to carry their letter to the Gentile believers in Antioch. When Paul and Barnabas parted ways, Paul chose Silas as his new partner, and together they carried the gospel into Europe. At Philippi they were beaten and locked in the stocks, yet at midnight they prayed and sang praises to God. Silas helped plant churches in Philippi, Thessalonica, Berea and Corinth, and his name stands beside Paul's at the start of two letters. Paul's letters call him Silvanus. Later he appears with Peter. He is a picture of steady, faithful partnership.

The life of Silas

Dates are approximate (AD) and follow Paul's second journey, c. AD 49–52.

  1. c. AD 49Jerusalem

    Chosen by the council

    After the council, the apostles and elders choose Judas Barsabas and Silas, "chief men among the brethren," to carry their letter to the Gentile believers in Antioch.

    Acts 15:22–29
  2. c. AD 49Antioch

    Prophet and encourager

    Judas and Silas, "being prophets also themselves," encourage and strengthen the believers with many words. The KJV adds that Silas chose to stay on in Antioch.

    Acts 15:30–34
  3. c. AD 49Antioch

    Paul's new partner

    After Paul and Barnabas part ways, Paul chooses Silas. The church commends them to God's grace, and they strengthen the churches of Syria and Cilicia.

    Acts 15:36–41
  4. c. AD 49–50Philippi

    Singing in prison

    Paul and Silas are beaten and put in the stocks after Paul frees a slave girl from a spirit. At midnight they pray and sing. An earthquake opens the doors, and the jailer believes.

    Acts 16:16–34
  5. c. AD 50Thessalonica and Berea

    Driven onward

    Their preaching wins converts but stirs up a mob. Sent away by night to Berea, Silas stays there with Timothy while Paul goes on to Athens.

    Acts 17:1–15
  6. c. AD 50–52Corinth

    Rejoins Paul

    Silas and Timothy arrive from Macedonia, and Paul, "pressed in the spirit," testifies that Jesus is the Christ. Silas is named with Paul in both letters to the Thessalonians.

    Acts 18:5; 1 Thessalonians 1:1
  7. LaterWith Peter

    A faithful brother

    Peter writes his first letter "by Silvanus, a faithful brother unto you."

    1 Peter 5:12

Key activities

Acts 15:34 is missing from many of the oldest manuscripts, so most modern translations leave it out. Silvanus is the longer Latin form of the name Silas.

  1. 1

    Carried the council's letter

    He took the decision of the apostles and elders to Antioch and confirmed it in person.

    Acts 15:22, 27
  2. 2

    Encouraged the church as a prophet

    He "exhorted the brethren with many words, and confirmed them."

    Acts 15:32
  3. 3

    Joined Paul's mission

    He took Barnabas's place as Paul's partner on the second journey.

    Acts 15:40
  4. 4

    Delivered the council's decrees

    With Paul and Timothy he passed on the Jerusalem decisions, and the churches grew daily.

    Acts 16:4–5
  5. 5

    Suffered at Philippi

    He was beaten with rods and fastened in the stocks without a trial.

    Acts 16:22–24, 37
  6. 6

    Praised God at midnight

    He and Paul prayed and sang while the other prisoners listened.

    Acts 16:25
  7. 7

    Helped the jailer believe

    They told him, "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved, and thy house."

    Acts 16:30–34
  8. 8

    Planted churches

    He preached in Thessalonica, Berea and Corinth.

    Acts 17:4, 10; 2 Corinthians 1:19
  9. 9

    Helped write letters

    His name opens both letters to the Thessalonians, and Peter wrote "by Silvanus."

    1 Thessalonians 1:1; 2 Thessalonians 1:1; 1 Peter 5:12

The world around them

Philippi was a Roman colony, a small piece of Rome planted in Macedonia, where many settlers were retired soldiers and their families (Acts 16:12, 21). Roman law forbade beating a Roman citizen without a trial. That is why the magistrates grew afraid when they learned Paul and Silas were citizens (Acts 16:37–39). From Philippi the travelers probably followed the Via Egnatia, a great Roman road, west through Amphipolis and Apollonia to Thessalonica (Acts 17:1).

Words and places

  • Chief men. Leading, respected members of the church (Acts 15:22).
  • Stocks. A wooden frame that locked a prisoner's feet in place (Acts 16:24).
  • Philippi. A Roman colony and leading city of Macedonia, in northern Greece (Acts 16:12).
  • Silvanus. The longer Latin form of Silas's name used in the letters (2 Corinthians 1:19).
