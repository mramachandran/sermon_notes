Simon of Cyrene

A passer-by from Africa who was made to carry Jesus' cross.

Simon of Cyrene appears in only one verse in each of three Gospels. He was a man from Cyrene in North Africa, coming into Jerusalem "out of the country," when he met the procession leading Jesus to be crucified. The Roman soldiers seized him and forced him to carry Jesus' cross. Luke adds that he carried it "after Jesus," walking behind him. Mark names Simon's sons, Alexander and Rufus, as if his first readers knew them. That small detail has led many to think Simon's family later became believers. Simon did not choose his burden, but it put him closer to Jesus on the road to the cross than anyone else. His brief moment has become a picture of what it means to follow Jesus.

The life of Simon of Cyrene

The date follows the house chronology, with the crucifixion c. AD 30; on the other main view, AD 33. Scripture tells only this one episode of Simon's life.

  1. Earlier

    A man of Cyrene

    Simon comes from Cyrene in North Africa. Scripture does not say whether he lived in Jerusalem or had come as a pilgrim for Passover. Jews from Cyrene were among those in Jerusalem at the feasts.

    Mark 15:21; Acts 2:10
  2. c. AD 30Friday morning

    Coming in from the country

    As the soldiers lead Jesus out to be crucified, Simon is passing by, "coming out of the country," and meets the procession on the road.

    Mark 15:20–21; Luke 23:26
  3. The same hour

    Compelled to carry the cross

    The soldiers lay hold on Simon and compel him to bear Jesus' cross. Luke says they laid it on him "that he might bear it after Jesus."

    Matthew 27:32; Luke 23:26
  4. The same hour

    At Golgotha

    They bring Jesus to Golgotha, which is, being interpreted, "The place of a skull." There they offer him wine mingled with myrrh and crucify him.

    Mark 15:22–24

Key activities

John 19:17 says Jesus went out "bearing his cross," while the other Gospels say Simon carried it. Many readers conclude that Jesus carried it at first and Simon took it over on the way.

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Simon of Cyrene.

Old Testament

  • EchoesIsaac carries the wood for the offering up the mountain (Genesis 22:6). "Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows" (Isaiah 53:4).

New Testament

  • Matthew 27:32; Mark 15:21; Luke 23:26Three short accounts of Simon carrying the cross.
  • John 19:17Jesus goes out bearing his cross.
  • Romans 16:13Paul greets "Rufus chosen in the Lord, and his mother and mine." He may be Simon's son.
  • Acts 2:10; 6:9; 11:20; 13:1Men of Cyrene are present at Pentecost, oppose Stephen, preach at Antioch, and lead in the church there.

The world around them

Cyrene was a Greek city in North Africa, in what is now eastern Libya. It had a large Jewish community. Jews from Cyrene were in Jerusalem at Pentecost and had their own synagogue there (Acts 2:10; 6:9). Roman soldiers could legally force civilians to carry loads for them for a distance, and Jesus refers to this practice when he speaks of going the second mile (Matthew 5:41). A condemned man usually carried the crossbeam of his own cross. Jesus, weakened by scourging, may not have been able to carry it all the way, though the Gospels do not say why Simon was chosen.

Tradition

Some suggest that the Rufus greeted in Romans 16:13 is Simon's son, and that "Simeon that was called Niger" in Acts 13:1 is Simon himself. These are possibilities, not certainties. An early teacher named Basilides claimed that Simon was crucified in Jesus' place. The church rejected this.

Words and places

  • Cyrene. A city of North Africa with a large Jewish community (Acts 2:10; 11:20).
  • Compel. To force someone into service. Matthew uses the same word as Jesus does about going the extra mile (Matthew 27:32; 5:41).
  • Golgotha. Aramaic for "the place of a skull." The KJV of Luke calls it Calvary (Mark 15:22; Luke 23:33).
  • Alexander and Rufus. Simon's sons, named as if the readers knew them (Mark 15:21; Romans 16:13).
