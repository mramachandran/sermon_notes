Simon the sorcerer

The magician of Samaria who tried to buy the power of the Holy Ghost.

Simon was the most famous man in Samaria. For a long time he had amazed the people with sorcery, and they called him "the great power of God." Then Philip came preaching Christ, with real signs and healings. The crowds turned to Jesus, and Simon himself believed and was baptized. He followed Philip everywhere, amazed at the miracles. But when Peter and John arrived and the Holy Ghost was given through the laying on of their hands, Simon offered them money for the same power. Peter's rebuke was sharp: God's gift cannot be bought. Simon asked the apostles to pray for him, and there the story ends. His name lives on in the word "simony," the buying or selling of church offices and spiritual things.

The life of Simon the sorcerer

Dates are approximate (AD). The events follow the scattering after Stephen's death, c. AD 33–35. Acts does not tell us what became of Simon.

  1. Before Philip comesSamaria

    Amazes the people

    For a long time Simon uses sorcery and bewitches the people of Samaria. He claims to be "some great one," and everyone from the least to the greatest listens to him.

    Acts 8:9–11
  2. c. AD 33–35Samaria

    Believes and is baptized

    Philip preaches the kingdom of God and the name of Jesus Christ. Men and women believe and are baptized, and Simon himself believes too. He stays close to Philip, amazed at the signs.

    Acts 8:12–13
  3. Samaria

    Watches the apostles

    The church in Jerusalem sends Peter and John. They pray for the Samaritans and lay hands on them, and the believers receive the Holy Ghost. Simon sees it happen.

    Acts 8:14–18
  4. Samaria

    Offers money

    Simon offers the apostles money, saying, "Give me also this power, that on whomsoever I lay hands, he may receive the Holy Ghost."

    Acts 8:18–19
  5. Samaria

    Rebuked and asks for prayer

    Peter tells him his heart is not right before God and calls him to repent. Simon answers, "Pray ye to the Lord for me, that none of these things which ye have spoken come upon me."

    Acts 8:20–24

Key activities

Christians differ on whether Simon's faith was real. Some think he was a true but immature believer who sinned badly. Others think his faith was only on the surface, because Peter says his heart was "not right in the sight of God." Acts leaves the question open.

  1. 1

    Practised sorcery

    He "used sorcery, and bewitched the people of Samaria, giving out that himself was some great one."

    Acts 8:9
  2. 2

    Believed and was baptized

    "Then Simon himself believed also: and when he was baptized, he continued with Philip."

    Acts 8:13
  3. 3

    Tried to buy spiritual power

    Seeing the Holy Ghost given, he offered the apostles money.

    Acts 8:18–19
  4. 4

    Heard a hard rebuke

    Peter said, "Thou hast neither part nor lot in this matter: for thy heart is not right in the sight of God."

    Acts 8:20–21
  5. 5

    Was called to repent

    Peter urged him to repent and pray, for he was "in the gall of bitterness, and in the bond of iniquity."

    Acts 8:22–23
  6. 6

    Asked for prayer

    He asked the apostles to pray for him. Acts does not say what happened next.

    Acts 8:24

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Simon see that made him offer money (Acts 8:18–19)?
  2. Understand: What did Simon misunderstand about God's gifts?
  3. Apply: Where are you tempted to use faith to gain attention or status?
  4. Apply: Who could you ask to pray for you this week about a struggle you face?
