Thomas

The loyal, blunt disciple whose doubt ended in the Gospel's highest confession.

Thomas is often called "Doubting Thomas," but that nickname is not in the Bible, and it tells only part of his story. John's Gospel shows him as a man of courage and honesty. When Jesus decided to return to Judea, where enemies wanted him dead, Thomas said, "Let us also go, that we may die with him." When Jesus spoke of the way to the Father, Thomas admitted he did not understand. And when the others said they had seen the risen Lord, Thomas refused to believe without seeing the wounds. A week later Jesus met him and invited him to touch. Thomas answered with the fullest confession of faith in the Gospels: "My Lord and my God."

The life of Thomas

Dates are approximate (AD). The crucifixion is dated AD 30 or AD 33; this study uses c. AD 30. Only John tells stories about Thomas; the other Gospels list him.

  1. c. AD 28Galilee

    Chosen as an apostle

    Jesus names the Twelve, and Thomas is among them. Nothing else is known of his life before this.

    Luke 6:13–16
  2. c. AD 30Beyond Jordan

    Ready to die with Jesus

    When Jesus decides to go back to Judea after Lazarus dies, the disciples warn him of danger. Thomas urges them to go and die with him.

    John 11:7–16
  3. c. AD 30The last supper

    Asks the way

    Jesus says the disciples know the way. Thomas answers that they do not even know where he is going. Jesus replies, "I am the way, the truth, and the life."

    John 14:4–6
  4. c. AD 30Easter evening

    Absent and unconvinced

    Thomas is not with the others when Jesus appears. He says he will not believe unless he sees and touches the nail prints and the wound in Jesus' side.

    John 20:19–25
  5. c. AD 30Eight days later

    Sees and believes

    Jesus appears again with Thomas present and invites him to touch his wounds. Thomas confesses, "My Lord and my God."

    John 20:26–29
  6. c. AD 30Sea of Tiberias

    Goes fishing

    Thomas is among the seven who meet the risen Jesus by the sea.

    John 21:1–2

Key activities

Scripture never says whether Thomas actually touched Jesus' wounds. Jesus invited him to, but John records only his confession.

  1. 1

    Followed as one of the Twelve

    He was chosen and named in every list of apostles.

    Matthew 10:3; Acts 1:13
  2. 2

    Spoke with courage

    He said, "Let us also go, that we may die with him."

    John 11:16
  3. 3

    Asked an honest question

    He said, "Lord, we know not whither thou goest; and how can we know the way?"

    John 14:5
  4. 4

    Demanded evidence

    Unless he could see "the print of the nails" and touch the wounds, he said, "I will not believe."

    John 20:25
  5. 5

    Confessed Jesus as Lord and God

    Faced with the risen Christ, he gave the Gospel's clearest confession.

    John 20:28
  6. 6

    Received a blessing for others

    Jesus told him, "blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed."

    John 20:29

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Thomas say he needed before he would believe, and what did Jesus offer him (John 20:25–27)?
  2. Understand: Jesus blessed "they that have not seen, and yet have believed." What does that mean for readers of John's Gospel today?
  3. Apply: Thomas was honest about his questions. What doubt or question could you bring into the open with God or with your group?
  4. Apply: Thomas missed the first meeting with the risen Lord. How can you keep from missing your gathering this week?

The world around them

Thomas lived in a world where death by crucifixion was final and public. Many Jews, like Martha, hoped for a resurrection "at the last day" (John 11:24). They did not expect one man to rise ahead of everyone else. That makes Thomas's doubt understandable. He asked for the same evidence the others had already received, for Jesus had shown them his hands and side (John 20:20). John places his story at the end of the Gospel, just before stating why the book was written.

Words and places

  • Didymus. A Greek word meaning "twin," John's translation of Thomas (John 11:16; 20:24).
  • Print of the nails. The marks left in Jesus' hands by crucifixion (John 20:25).
  • Faithless. Unbelieving. Jesus said, "be not faithless, but believing" (John 20:27).
  • Sea of Tiberias. Another name for the Sea of Galilee (John 21:1).
