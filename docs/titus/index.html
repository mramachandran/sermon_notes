Titus

The Greek co-worker Paul trusted with hard places: a test case in Jerusalem, a peacemaker in Corinth, and a builder in Crete.

Titus was a Gentile believer and one of Paul's most trusted partners. Acts never mentions him, so everything we know comes from Paul's letters. As a young man he went with Paul to Jerusalem, where the church leaders did not require him to be circumcised. That made him a living test of the gospel's welcome to Gentiles. Later Paul sent him to the troubled church at Corinth, and his return with good news lifted Paul out of deep distress. He then organized the collection for poor believers in Jerusalem. Finally Paul left him in Crete to appoint elders, and wrote him the letter that bears his name. Titus is a picture of a reliable servant sent into difficult places.

The life of Titus

Dates are approximate (AD). Galatians 2:1 may describe the famine visit (Acts 11:30) or the council (Acts 15), so the first date could be c. AD 46–49.

  1. c. AD 46–49Jerusalem

    A test case

    Paul takes Titus, a Greek, to Jerusalem with Barnabas. The leaders there do not compel him to be circumcised. Paul treats this as a defense of the gospel's freedom for Gentiles.

    Galatians 2:1–5
  2. c. AD 55–56Troas

    Missed by Paul

    Anxious about Corinth, Paul finds an open door to preach at Troas. Yet he has "no rest in my spirit" because Titus has not arrived, so he moves on to Macedonia.

    2 Corinthians 2:12–13
  3. c. AD 55–56Macedonia

    Brings good news

    Titus reaches Paul with news of the Corinthians' sorrow, repentance and renewed love for Paul. Paul is comforted, and Titus himself is refreshed by them.

    2 Corinthians 7:5–16
  4. c. AD 56Corinth

    Completes the collection

    Paul sends Titus back to finish gathering the gift for the poor believers in Jerusalem. Titus goes eagerly, "of his own accord."

    2 Corinthians 8:6, 16–23
  5. LaterCrete

    Setting things in order

    Paul leaves Titus in Crete to "set in order the things that are wanting, and ordain elders in every city."

    Titus 1:5
  6. LaterNicopolis

    Called to Paul

    Paul asks Titus to join him at Nicopolis, where Paul plans to spend the winter.

    Titus 3:12
  7. c. AD 64–67Dalmatia

    Sent on

    In his final letter Paul notes that Titus has gone to Dalmatia.

    2 Timothy 4:10

Key activities

Acts never names Titus, and no one knows why. Everything we know about him comes from Paul's letters.

  1. 1

    Stood as a test case

    As an uncircumcised Greek believer, he went to Jerusalem and was accepted.

    Galatians 2:1–3
  2. 2

    Handled a painful situation

    After a sorrowful letter from Paul, he went to Corinth and found the church repentant.

    2 Corinthians 7:8–13
  3. 3

    Comforted Paul

    God "comforted us by the coming of Titus," Paul wrote.

    2 Corinthians 7:6–7
  4. 4

    Loved the Corinthians

    His affection for them grew as he remembered how they received him.

    2 Corinthians 7:15
  5. 5

    Led the collection

    He began the gift for Jerusalem's poor and was sent back to finish it.

    2 Corinthians 8:6
  6. 6

    Handled money with integrity

    He traveled with trusted brothers so the gift was above suspicion. "Did Titus make a gain of you?" Paul asked.

    2 Corinthians 8:16–23; 12:18
  7. 7

    Organized the churches of Crete

    He appointed elders who met clear standards.

    Titus 1:5–9
  8. 8

    Taught and modeled sound teaching

    He was to teach every age group and to be "a pattern of good works."

    Titus 2:1–8
  9. 9

    Went where he was sent

    He was last seen heading to Dalmatia.

    2 Timothy 4:10
