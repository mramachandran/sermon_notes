Uriah the Hittite

A foreign-born soldier who kept faith with Israel's army while Israel's king broke faith with him.

Uriah was a Hittite of foreign descent, yet he was listed among David's mighty men. His name means "the LORD is my light," probably a sign that he or his family worshipped Israel's God. While Uriah was fighting for David at Rabbah, David took his wife, Bathsheba. To hide it, the king called Uriah home and urged him to go to his house. Uriah refused comforts his fellow soldiers could not have. So David sent him back to the front carrying his own death warrant. Uriah's quiet integrity shines against the king's scheming, and Scripture never forgets it.

The life of Uriah the Hittite

Scripture gives no dates or ages for Uriah. These events fall in the middle of David's reign (c. 1010–970 BC), during the war with Ammon.

  1. Earlier

    A mighty man

    Uriah the Hittite is listed among David's mighty men, his most honored warriors. He is married to Bathsheba, and his house in Jerusalem is within view of the palace roof.

    2 Samuel 23:39; 11:2–3, 8
  2. Spring

    At war

    "At the time when kings go forth to battle," David sends Joab and the army to besiege Rabbah. Uriah goes with them, while David stays in Jerusalem.

    2 Samuel 11:1
  3. Called home

    Refuses his comforts

    David calls Uriah back, asks about the war, and tells him to go home. Uriah sleeps at the palace door instead. The next night David gets him drunk, but he still will not go home.

    2 Samuel 11:6–13
  4. The letter

    Carries his own death warrant

    David writes to Joab: put Uriah where the battle is hottest, then pull back from him. Uriah carries the sealed letter back to the camp himself.

    2 Samuel 11:14–15
  5. At Rabbah

    Killed in battle

    Joab sends Uriah near the city wall. Some of David's soldiers fall, and "Uriah the Hittite died also." Joab sends word to David.

    2 Samuel 11:16–25

Key activities

David's mighty men were a band of his best warriors, many of whom had been with him since his days as a fugitive. Uriah is the last name on the list in 2 Samuel 23, which ends, "Uriah the Hittite: thirty and seven in all."

  1. 1

    Served a foreign king faithfully

    Though a Hittite by descent, he fought among David's best soldiers.

    2 Samuel 23:39; 1 Chronicles 11:41
  2. 2

    Stood with his comrades

    He slept at the king's door, saying he would not go home to eat, drink and be with his wife while the ark and the army were in tents.

    2 Samuel 11:9–11
  3. 3

    Kept his word

    He swore, "as thou livest, and as thy soul liveth, I will not do this thing."

    2 Samuel 11:11
  4. 4

    Held firm when drunk

    Even when David made him drunk, he did not go down to his house.

    2 Samuel 11:13
  5. 5

    Obeyed the king to the end

    He carried David's letter to Joab and went where he was sent.

    2 Samuel 11:14–17

For discussion

  1. Observe: What reasons did Uriah give for not going home (2 Samuel 11:11)?
  2. Understand: Why do you think the writer lets Uriah, a foreigner, show more integrity than the king of Israel?
  3. Apply: Uriah refused comforts his friends did not have. Where could you stand with people who are going through a hard time?
  4. Apply: Uriah's goodness is remembered in Scripture even though he was wronged. When have you had to keep doing right while someone treated you unfairly?

The world around them

The Hittites once ruled a great empire in what is now Turkey, which fell about 1180 BC. Genesis also speaks of Hittites living in Canaan, such as Ephron, who sold Abraham a burial cave (Genesis 23). Scholars differ on how these groups were related. By David's day, some people of foreign descent lived in Israel and served its king. Another Hittite, Ahimelech, was with David during his years in the wilderness (1 Samuel 26:6).

Words and places

  • Hittite. A people named among the nations of Canaan (Genesis 15:20; Deuteronomy 7:1).
  • Mighty men. David's elite warriors, listed by name (2 Samuel 23:8–39).
  • Rabbah. The Ammonite capital, today Amman in Jordan (2 Samuel 11:1).
  • Urias. The Greek form of his name used in the KJV New Testament (Matthew 1:6).
