The widow of Zarephath

A poor foreign widow who shared her last meal and saw God keep her alive.

The widow of Zarephath lived in the country of Sidon, the homeland of Queen Jezebel and a centre of Baal worship. During the great drought of Ahab's reign she was down to her last handful of flour. She was gathering sticks for one final meal when Elijah, a stranger and an Israelite, asked her to feed him first. She did, and her flour and oil lasted until the rain came. Later, when her son died, Elijah prayed and the boy lived again. Scripture never tells us her name. Yet Jesus chose her story to show that God's mercy reaches beyond Israel, and his hometown was furious.

The life of The widow of Zarephath

Dates are approximate. The drought falls in Ahab's reign (c. 874–853 BC), probably in the 860s BC. Kings gives no exact year.

  1. During the drought

    God's command

    When the brook Cherith dries up, God sends Elijah to Zarephath. He says he has "commanded a widow woman there to sustain thee." She does not yet know it.

    1 Kings 17:7–9
  2. At the city gate

    Meets Elijah

    She is gathering sticks when a stranger asks for water and a piece of bread. She has only a handful of flour and a little oil, and she expects to eat it with her son and then die.

    1 Kings 17:10–12
  3. That day

    Bakes for the prophet first

    Elijah tells her not to fear. She should make him a small cake first, trusting God's promise that her flour and oil will last until the rain. She does as he says.

    1 Kings 17:13–15
  4. Many days

    The jar does not run out

    Through the long drought the flour and oil keep lasting. They feed her, her household and Elijah, "according to the word of the LORD, which he spake by Elijah."

    1 Kings 17:15–16
  5. Later

    Her son dies

    Her son falls sick until there is no breath left in him. In her grief she asks whether the prophet has come to call her sin to remembrance and slay her son.

    1 Kings 17:17–18
  6. The same day

    Her son is raised

    Elijah carries the boy to his upstairs room, stretches himself on him three times, and cries to God. The child revives, and she confesses that Elijah is a man of God.

    1 Kings 17:19–24

Key activities

She was a Gentile living in Baal's own country. Yet she speaks of "the LORD thy God" (1 Kings 17:12) and acts on the word of his prophet.

  1. 1

    Helped a stranger

    When Elijah asked for water, she went to fetch it, though water was scarce.

    1 Kings 17:10–11
  2. 2

    Told the truth about her need

    She did not pretend. She had "not a cake, but an handful of meal in a barrel, and a little oil in a cruse."

    1 Kings 17:12
  3. 3

    Gave first

    She made Elijah's cake before her own, trusting a promise she could not yet see.

    1 Kings 17:13–15
  4. 4

    Opened her home

    Elijah stayed in an upstairs room of her house for many days.

    1 Kings 17:15, 19
  5. 5

    Brought her grief to the prophet

    When her son died, she poured out her grief and fear honestly to Elijah.

    1 Kings 17:17–18
  6. 6

    Confessed her faith

    Receiving her son alive, she declared that the word of the LORD in Elijah's mouth was truth.

    1 Kings 17:23–24

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names the widow.

Old Testament

  • 1 Kings 17:8–24The only account of her life
  • Obadiah 1:20Zarephath as a boundary in a promise of return
  • Echo: 2 Kings 4:1–37Elisha multiplies a widow's oil and raises a son
  • Echo: Deuteronomy 10:18God defends the widow and loves the stranger

New Testament

  • Luke 4:25–26"Unto none of them was Elias sent, save unto Sarepta"
  • Luke 4:28–29Nazareth's synagogue, enraged, tries to throw Jesus off a cliff
  • Echo: Luke 7:11–17Jesus raises a widow's only son at Nain
  • Echo: Mark 12:41–44A poor widow gives "all her living"

For discussion

  1. Observe: What exactly did Elijah ask the widow to do, and what did he promise (1 Kings 17:13–14)?
  2. Understand: Jesus used her story in his hometown sermon (Luke 4:25–29). Why would a foreign widow be such a pointed example?
  3. Apply: She gave from almost nothing. What is one thing, however small, that you could give this week before you feel you have enough?
  4. Apply: In her grief she blamed herself and the prophet (1 Kings 17:18). How do you tend to react when trouble comes even after you have obeyed God?
