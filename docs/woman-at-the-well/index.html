Part 11 · People Who Met Jesus · Chapter 136

The woman at the well

A Samaritan woman came for water and went home to tell her town about the Christ.

At midday a Samaritan woman came to draw water at Jacob's well near Sychar. A tired Jewish traveller sitting there asked her for a drink. It was Jesus. In one conversation he crossed three walls: Jew and Samaritan, man and woman, teacher and a woman with a troubled past. He offered her "living water," told her the truth about her life, and plainly said he was the Messiah. She left her waterpot and ran to tell the town. Scripture never names her, yet she brought a whole town to Jesus.

The life of The woman at the well

The date is approximate (AD) and follows the house chronology, with the crucifixion c. AD 30. John places the story soon after Jesus' first Passover in Jerusalem.

  1. c. AD 28–29The sixth hour

    A request for water

    On his way from Judea to Galilee, Jesus rests, wearied, at Jacob's well. A woman of Samaria comes to draw water, and he says, "Give me to drink." She is astonished that a Jew would ask this of her.

    John 4:1–9
  2. At the well

    Living water

    Jesus offers "living water" that becomes a well springing up into everlasting life. She asks for this water so she need not keep coming to draw, still thinking of the well.

    John 4:10–15
  3. At the well

    Her life laid bare

    Jesus tells her to call her husband. When she says she has none, he names her five husbands. She calls him a prophet and raises the old question of where to worship.

    John 4:16–20
  4. At the well

    The Messiah revealed

    Jesus says true worshippers will worship the Father "in spirit and in truth." When she speaks of the coming Messiah, Jesus says, "I that speak unto thee am he."

    John 4:21–26
  5. The same day

    She tells the town

    She leaves her waterpot and goes into the city: "Come, see a man, which told me all things that ever I did: is not this the Christ?" The people go out to him.

    John 4:27–30
  6. Two days

    Many believe

    Many Samaritans believe because of her words. Jesus stays two days, and many more believe his own word, saying he is "the Saviour of the world."

    John 4:39–42

Key activities

John does not explain her five marriages. Some readers assume a sinful past. Others note that a woman could be widowed or divorced by her husbands with little choice of her own. Jesus names her situation plainly, and the text records no word of condemnation.

  1. 1

    Came to draw water

    She came to Jacob's well at about the sixth hour, when Jesus was sitting there.

    John 4:6–7
  2. 2

    Questioned the stranger

    She asked why a Jew would ask her for a drink, and whether he was greater than "our father Jacob."

    John 4:9–12
  3. 3

    Asked for living water

    "Sir, give me this water, that I thirst not, neither come hither to draw."

    John 4:15
  4. 4

    Told the truth

    She said, "I have no husband," and Jesus said she had spoken well.

    John 4:17–18
  5. 5

    Raised the question of worship

    She asked whether God should be worshipped on the Samaritans' mountain or in Jerusalem.

    John 4:19–24
  6. 6

    Spoke of the Messiah

    "I know that Messias cometh, which is called Christ."

    John 4:25–26
  7. 7

    Left her waterpot

    She went back into the city in such haste that she left her waterpot behind.

    John 4:28
  8. 8

    Brought her town to Jesus

    Her simple testimony led many Samaritans to believe and to come and hear him.

    John 4:29–30, 39–42

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names the woman at the well.

Old Testament

  • Genesis 33:18–20; 48:22Jacob's land near Shechem, given to Joseph (John 4:5)
  • 2 Kings 17:24–41Where the Samaritans and their mixed worship came from
  • Echo: Genesis 24; 29:1–12Meetings at wells: Rebekah and Rachel
  • Echo: Jeremiah 2:13God, "the fountain of living waters"

New Testament

  • John 4:1–42The main account
  • John 7:37–39Living water again, explained as the Spirit
  • Luke 10:30–37; 17:11–19A good Samaritan and a grateful Samaritan leper
  • Acts 8:5–25The gospel reaches Samaria, and many believe
  • Echo: Revelation 22:17Take the water of life freely

For discussion

  1. Observe: What does the woman do after Jesus says, "I that speak unto thee am he" (John 4:26–30)?
  2. Understand: Why do you think Jesus began with a request for water rather than a sermon?
  3. Apply: She left her waterpot behind. What everyday concern might you set down this week to pay attention to Jesus?
  4. Apply: Her testimony was simple: "Come, see." Who is one person you could invite to church or to your group this week?
