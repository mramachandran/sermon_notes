Zerubbabel

The prince of David's line who led the exiles home and rebuilt the temple, "not by might, nor by power."

Zerubbabel, a grandson of King Jehoiachin, led the first large group of exiles home with Jeshua the high priest. Together they rebuilt the altar and laid the foundation of a new temple, while old men wept and the young shouted for joy. Then opposition stopped the work for about sixteen years. In 520 BC Haggai and Zechariah stirred him to begin again, and God promised his hands would finish it. The temple was completed in 516/515 BC. He appears in both family lines of Jesus, a sign that God kept David's line alive through the exile.

The life of Zerubbabel

Dates approximate (BC): a start under Cyrus, a long delay, completion under Darius I. Chronicles lists Zerubbabel as a son of Pedaiah, Shealtiel's brother.

  1. c. 538/537 BCAfter Cyrus's decree

    Leads the exiles home

    Zerubbabel heads the leaders who return from Babylon. The company numbers 42,360, besides 7,337 servants.

    Ezra 2:1–2, 64–67
  2. c. 537/536 BC7th month

    The altar rebuilt

    With Jeshua he rebuilds the altar, and daily sacrifices begin again, though the people fear their neighbours.

    Ezra 3:1–6
  3. c. 536 BC2nd year

    The foundation laid

    Priests blow trumpets; many shout for joy, while old men who saw the first temple weep aloud.

    Ezra 3:8–13
  4. c. 536–520 BCYears of delay

    Work stops

    He refuses help from the local peoples, who then frighten the builders and hire counsellors against them.

    Ezra 4:1–5, 24
  5. c. 520 BCDarius's 2nd year

    Back to work

    Haggai rebukes the people for neglecting God's house. Zerubbabel, Joshua and the people obey and begin again.

    Haggai 1:1–15; Ezra 5:1–2
  6. c. 520 BC9th month

    God's signet

    God promises to make Zerubbabel "as a signet." Zechariah adds that his hands will finish the house.

    Haggai 2:20–23; Zechariah 4:6–10
  7. c. 516/515 BCDarius's 6th year

    The temple finished

    The house is dedicated with joy. Zerubbabel is not named there, and his later life is not recorded.

    Ezra 6:14–16

Key activities

Ezra 5:16 says that Sheshbazzar laid the temple's foundation, while Ezra 3 and Zechariah 4:9 credit Zerubbabel. Some think they were the same man with two names. Others think Sheshbazzar was the first governor and Zerubbabel led the actual work.

  1. 1

    Led the first return

    He is named first among the leaders who brought the exiles home.

    Ezra 2:1–2
  2. 2

    Put worship first

    Before any walls or houses, he helped rebuild the altar and restore the daily offerings.

    Ezra 3:2–6
  3. 3

    Laid the foundation

    He organised the Levites to oversee the work and led the celebration.

    Ezra 3:8–11
  4. 4

    Refused a compromise

    He told the local peoples, "Ye have nothing to do with us to build an house unto our God."

    Ezra 4:1–3
  5. 5

    Obeyed God's word

    When Haggai spoke, Zerubbabel, Joshua and the people "obeyed the voice of the LORD their God."

    Haggai 1:12–14
  6. 6

    Took courage

    God told him, "be strong, O Zerubbabel … for I am with you."

    Haggai 2:4
  7. 7

    Finished the work

    The temple rose in spite of an official inquiry, completing what his hands had begun.

    Ezra 5:2–5; 6:14–15; Zechariah 4:9

Across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Zerubbabel.

Old Testament

  • Ezra 2:2; 3:2, 8; 4:2–3; 5:2He leads the return and the rebuilding
  • Nehemiah 7:7; 12:1, 47Remembered as leader of the first return
  • 1 Chronicles 3:17–19In David's royal line through Jehoiachin
  • Haggai 1:1, 12, 14; 2:2, 4, 21–23Governor and God's chosen signet
  • Zechariah 4:6–10"Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit"
  • Echo: Zechariah 4:14"Two anointed ones," often taken as Zerubbabel and Joshua

New Testament

  • Matthew 1:12–13; Luke 3:27"Zorobabel," ancestor of Jesus in both lines
  • Echo: 1 Corinthians 3:10–11The foundation "which is Jesus Christ"

For discussion

  1. Observe: What did Zerubbabel and Jeshua build first, and why do you think they started there (Ezra 3:1–6)?
  2. Understand: Why would God say "Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit" to a man leading a building project?
  3. Apply: Zerubbabel's work stalled for sixteen years. Is there something God gave you to do that you have set aside? What would restarting look like?
  4. Apply: Some wept because the new temple looked small. Where are you tempted to despise "the day of small things"?
