Josiah

The boy king who found the Book of the Law and turned Judah back to God.

Sixteenth king of Judah, grandson of Manasseh — the most wicked of Judah's kings — and remembered as its most wholehearted reformer. Named in prophecy some three centuries before his birth, he ruled in the last decades before Babylon's conquest.

Main passages: 2 Kings 22–23 and 2 Chronicles 34–35

Name
Hebrew Yoshiyahu, usually rendered “the LORD supports” or “the LORD heals”
Family
Son of King Amon; mother Jedidah, daughter of Adaiah of Bozkath 2 Kings 22:1
Crowned
Age 8, c. 640 BC, after his father was assassinated
Reign
31 years in Jerusalem, c. 640–609 BC
Death
Age 39, wounded in battle against Pharaoh Neco at Megiddo
Sons who reigned
Jehoahaz (Shallum), Jehoiakim (Eliakim), Zedekiah (Mattaniah) 1 Chronicles 3:15
Key people
Hilkiah the high priest, Shaphan the scribe, Huldah the prophetess, Jeremiah, Zephaniah
New Testament
Named once, in the genealogy of Jesus Matthew 1:10–11
“And like unto him was there no king before him, that turned to the LORD with all his heart, and with all his soul, and with all his might, according to all the law of Moses; neither after him arose there any like him.”2 Kings 23:25, KJV

The life of Josiah

Dates are approximate (BC). Years are counted from the start of his reign, as Kings and Chronicles count them.

  1. c. 930 BCAbout 300 years before

    Prophesied by name

    At Jeroboam's rival altar in Bethel, a man of God from Judah announces that a son of David named Josiah will one day burn the bones of its priests upon it.

    1 Kings 13:1–3
  2. c. 640 BCAge 8

    Crowned king

    His father Amon is assassinated by his own servants. The people of the land execute the conspirators and set young Josiah on the throne.

    2 Kings 21:23–26; 22:1; 2 Chronicles 33:25; 34:1
  3. c. 632 BCAge 16, 8th year

    Begins to seek God

    While still a youth, he begins to seek the God of David his father — a personal turning before any public reform.

    2 Chronicles 34:3
  4. c. 628 BCAge 20, 12th year

    Purges the land

    Tears down high places, Asherah poles and carved images in Judah and Jerusalem, and carries the purge north into Manasseh, Ephraim, Simeon and Naphtali.

    2 Chronicles 34:3–7
  5. c. 627 BC13th year

    Jeremiah is called

    The word of the LORD comes to the young prophet Jeremiah. Zephaniah also prophesies in these days.

    Jeremiah 1:1–2; 25:3; Zephaniah 1:1
  6. c. 622 BCAge 26, 18th year

    The Book is found

    During temple repairs Hilkiah finds the Book of the Law. Shaphan reads it to the king, who tears his clothes and sends to Huldah the prophetess.

    2 Kings 22:3–20; 2 Chronicles 34:8–28
  7. c. 622 BC18th year

    Covenant and Passover

    Reads the Book to all the people, renews the covenant, cleanses the land, and keeps a Passover unmatched since the days of the judges and Samuel.

    2 Kings 23:1–25; 2 Chronicles 34:29–35:19
  8. c. 609 BCAge 39, 31st year

    Death at Megiddo

    Goes out against Pharaoh Neco despite Neco's warning. Disguised, he is struck by archers, dies, and is buried in Jerusalem. Jeremiah laments him.

    2 Kings 23:29–30; 2 Chronicles 35:20–27

Twelve acts of a reformer

Kings orders the reforms after the Book is found; Chronicles places the first purge six years earlier. Read both accounts side by side.

  1. 1

    Sought God while young

    At sixteen he began to seek the God of David — the inner turning that drove everything after.

    2 Chronicles 34:3
  2. 2

    Purged Judah and the north

    Broke down Baal altars, Asherah poles and images, ground them to dust, and burned the bones of idol priests on their own altars.

    2 Chronicles 34:3–7
  3. 3

    Repaired the Temple

    Collected offerings and paid carpenters and masons to restore the house of the LORD. The workers were trusted so fully that no accounting was asked.

    2 Kings 22:3–7; 2 Chronicles 34:8–13
  4. 4

    Heard the Book and wept

    When Shaphan read the rediscovered Book of the Law, Josiah tore his clothes, grieved that the nation had not obeyed it.

    2 Kings 22:8–13; 2 Chronicles 34:14–21
  5. 5

    Inquired of Huldah

    The prophetess confirmed judgment on Jerusalem — but because his heart was tender, it would not fall in his days.

    2 Kings 22:14–20; 2 Chronicles 34:22–28
  6. 6

    Renewed the covenant

    Gathered elders, priests, prophets and people, read the whole Book aloud, and pledged the nation to keep it.

    2 Kings 23:1–3; 2 Chronicles 34:29–33
  7. 7

    Cleansed the Temple

    Burned the vessels made for Baal, Asherah and the stars in the Kidron fields, and removed the Asherah image and the cult prostitutes' houses.

    2 Kings 23:4–7
  8. 8

    Ended the high places

    Deposed idolatrous priests, defiled high places from Geba to Beersheba, and tore down those Solomon built for Ashtoreth, Chemosh and Milcom.

    2 Kings 23:5, 8–9, 13–14
  9. 9

    Stopped child sacrifice and the occult

    Defiled Topheth so no child would pass through fire to Molech, and removed the horses and chariots of the sun, mediums and household idols.

    2 Kings 23:10–12, 24
  10. 10

    Fulfilled prophecy at Bethel

    Burned bones on Jeroboam's altar exactly as foretold — yet spared the tomb of the man of God who had predicted it.

    2 Kings 23:15–20; 1 Kings 13:2
  11. 11

    Kept the great Passover

    Restored the ark and the Levites' order and gave thousands of lambs and bulls. No Passover like it since Samuel.

    2 Kings 23:21–23; 2 Chronicles 35:1–19
  12. 12

    Fell at Megiddo

    Went out against Pharaoh Neco, who said God had sent him. Josiah would not listen, was shot by archers, and died.

    2 Kings 23:29–30; 2 Chronicles 35:20–24

Josiah across Scripture

Direct references come first. Thematic echoes are study connections, not places where the text names Josiah.

Old Testament

Prophecy before his birth

  • 1 Kings 13:1–3A man of God names Josiah as the king who will defile Bethel's altar
  • 2 Kings 23:15–18The fulfilment, roughly three centuries later

The main narratives

  • 2 Kings 21:23–23:30Accession, the Book, reforms, Passover, death
  • 2 Chronicles 33:25–35:27Parallel account, with his early seeking and the Passover in detail

Genealogy

  • 1 Chronicles 3:14–15Son of Amon; father of Johanan, Jehoiakim, Zedekiah and Shallum

Prophets dated to his reign

  • Jeremiah 1:2; 25:3Jeremiah called in Josiah's 13th year
  • Jeremiah 3:6; 36:2Messages given in the days of Josiah
  • Zephaniah 1:1Prophesies in his reign; 1:4–6 echoes the purge of Baal

Remembered after his death

  • 2 Chronicles 35:24–25All Judah mourns; Jeremiah composes a lament
  • Jeremiah 22:10–19His justice held up against his sons' failures
  • Zechariah 12:11Great mourning in the valley of Megiddon — often linked to his death

New Testament

Direct mention

  • Matthew 1:10–11Josiah in the royal line from David to Jesus. “Jechoniah” here is his grandson — Matthew compresses the generations

Worth noticing

  • Luke 3:23–38Luke traces Jesus through David's son Nathan, so Josiah does not appear

Thematic echoes, not direct references

  • John 2:13–17; Mark 11:15–17A son of David cleansing the temple
  • Luke 24:27, 32Scripture opened and hearts burning
  • Luke 22:7–20; 1 Corinthians 5:7The Passover renewed and fulfilled
  • Luke 4:16–21A reading from the scroll and good news for the poor

The Law he found: Deuteronomy links

  • Deuteronomy 17:18–20The king is to read the Law all his days
  • Deuteronomy 12:1–14; 16:1–8One place of worship; the Passover
  • Deuteronomy 18:10–12Forbids child sacrifice and mediums

Deuterocanonical, for completeness

  • 1 Esdras 1:1–33; Sirach 49:1–4Later retellings and praise of Josiah
